# The royal court

Every kingdom now has a court. Five offices, held by real people whose skills matter, help run the realm. A ruler does not have to see to every small matter in person: they can send others to deal with the affairs of the realm, hold court in their own halls to hear petitions, and must win the oath of every house when they come to the throne. As a vassal you can be offered an office, hold court in your own fiefs and swear (or not) to each new ruler.

## The kingdom screen

The kingdom screen (**K**) has two new tabs after Diplomacy: **Crown**, for what the crown itself holds and gives, and **Realm**, for the houses of the realm and their dealings with one another. The usual tabs are unchanged, and with War Sails the Fiefs tab still shows your ships.

The **Crown** tab lists:

- **The Crown**, picked first: the ruler's portrait, the realm's law of succession, the ruler's [right to rule](succession.md) as a bar with the words the lords use for it, the portraits of the heir and of the strongest pretender, the houses with a [claim to the throne](succession.md#claims-to-the-throne) and why, and the [laws of the crown](crown-laws.md#the-crown-screen) in force, with what each does to the houses' regard and the buttons to change them;
- the five **offices**, with each holder's portrait and name, or *Vacant*. Pick one to see who holds it, a bar with how much of the office's full effect their skill brings (their skill under it, with words such as *"a sure hand"*) and what it gives right now. As ruler, **Dismiss** relieves a holder; you name someone by speaking with them or sending an envoy (see [Naming your court](#naming-your-court-as-ruler));
- **Marshals of the Fronts**, in war: who leads the realm's lords against each enemy realm, and, as ruler, **Take a command away** (see [Command your armies](command-your-armies.md));
- **Petitions**: how many people are waiting for you to [hold court](#holding-court), and whether your fiefs have begun to suffer for the wait;
- **Oaths of fealty**: while the houses are swearing to a new ruler, where each one stands;
- **Titles**: the duchies and counties of the realm, its themes or its ulus, and its landed knights (see [Dukes, counts and castellans](titles.md)); in a realm without titles, **Landed knights**;
- under **Tributaries**, the realms tied to yours: your vassal realms, your overlord, and an enemy that demands your fealty (see [Vassal realms and annexation](vassal-realms.md#tributaries-in-the-crown-tab)).

The **Realm** tab is described below.

Kingdoms Alive adds to the game's screens instead of replacing them (it needs [UIExtenderEx](../getting-started.md#requirements)), so other mods that add to the same screens keep working beside it.

### The Realm tab {#the-realm-tab}

Everything that sets the houses of a realm against one another, in one place: who answers to whom, who is loyal and who is not, the feuds, the plots and causes, the rights to fiefs, the assembly, what waits for your word and the envoys you have on the road. It is the last tab of the kingdom screen, after Crown. It looks the same whether you rule the realm, serve it as a vassal or fight for it for pay; only what is open to you changes. Each section opens with a line from your household on what it holds and what you can do there in your place. An empty list always says why it is empty, and every greyed action says why it cannot be done.

The tab adds no rules. Every button in it does what the same deed does in person or by envoy, with the same answer, and every conversation in person is still open to you.

**On the left**, the realm you are looking at (the arrows by its name go through the other realms of Calradia), the sections, with how many entries each holds, and **Your standing**: your relation with your ruler, your claim to the throne, the traitor's mark, your offices, your oath, your bonds and allies, your lands and the grievances (see [Your house at a glance](house-tab.md#your-house-in-the-realm-tab)). Wars within, Causes, Claims and Assembly list their entries under the section's name; pick one and it fills the middle of the screen.

#### Fealty

The realm as a tree: the ruler at the root, each house under the lord it answers to (see [Liege and Vassals](titles.md#liege-and-vassals)). Each card shows the house's banner with its [mark of rank](titles.md#marks-of-rank), its head and title, the landed knights it has, and, in gold under the title, the first office of the court or command one of its people holds. A legend over the tree names each mark of rank in the realm's own words. Click a card to pick a house; drag to move the tree.

What is yours bears the gold frame of the game, here and in every section, and comes first: your own house, its vassals and knights, and whatever aims at your house. Your own house's panel holds your lord, your titles, your knights, your dues and the grant of land to a knight (see [Your house at a glance](house-tab.md#your-house-in-the-realm-tab)).

Five buttons over the tree colour the cards, each with its legend:

- **Liege**: the tree as it is, who answers to whom up to the crown.
- **Loyalty**: each house as a friend of the crown, neither friend nor foe, or an enemy of the crown, as the ruler's page in the encyclopedia lists them, and the traitors marked. In your own realm, pick a house to read its mood and grievance.
- **Plots**: your own realm only. Each house's stance on the plots you know of, and *unknown* where you have not found out.
- **Feuds**: the houses at feud, the claimants in one colour and the holders in another, with a line from the head of the claimants to the head of the holders; a feud being fought and one only threatened or laid aside are told apart.
- **Claims**: a line from each house to the land of another it has a right to, close-set for a firm right and sparse for a weak one.

Under the tree, a box reads out what the button says of the house you picked. None of this shows anything you could not already learn in another screen: a plot you have not found out stays hidden here too, and looking at another realm, you see only what is known beyond its borders.

**On the right**, the house you picked: its head in 3D, traits, relation, liege, friends and foes, its people in the realm's service, and under them **What you can do with the {house}**, what can be done now first and what cannot after it:

- **Send an envoy...**: the envoy windows of the encyclopedia, exactly as from the lord's page (see [Envoys](envoys.md#sending-an-envoy)).
- **One line for each errand** that has a bearing on the realm's quarrels: win the house over to your plot, keep it loyal to the crown (only if you rule), win it to your cause, ask it to ride with you in your feud, press a right against it by the sword, petition the throne, ask for a pact between your houses, call an ally to war, swear fealty to a new ruler, offer an office at court or a command, demand fealty, or release a landed knight. Each sends an envoy to that house with the errand in mind: you choose who goes, and it is the first matter offered when the envoy is heard.
- **Seek him out** (or **Seek her out**), or **Stop seeking**.

Only what the lords of Calradia also do has a line of its own. What only you do (asking for a meeting, sounding a lord out, a gift, offering your own sword in a feud, and the like) is still there in conversation, and for your envoys to raise once they stand before the lord.

#### Wars within, Causes, Claims and Assembly

- **Wars within**: the feuds between the houses of the realm and the [warnings before arms](factions.md#warning-before-arms), with **Yield** and **Withdraw** where they are yours to use.
- **Causes**: the houses' mood, the plots against the crown you know of, a plot to [break away](civil-war.md#breaking-away-yourself) and the charters, with **Raise the banner**, **Seek independence** and **Strike for the throne** when they are yours.
- **Claims**: your house's [rights to fiefs](fief-rights.md) and titles and the rights of others to yours, with **Press by the sword** and **Petition the throne**.
- **Assembly**: the reckonings of your realm and how the [assembly](assembly.md) is like to judge them once it gathers.

They tell of your own realm; with another realm picked, a line says so. While your house rides with a feud's host, or serves for pay, these sections say what you can follow from where you stand.

#### How it stands

Beside each action that weighs on the realm's quarrels, a line **How it stands: ...** opens a block with what a lord in your place would weigh before doing the same: your side against theirs, whether your host could reach the walls, the men you could still raise, a war abroad that holds lords back, a right the crown has judged, a lord's regard for you and for the ruler, their grievances, their character. Each one in words, with a bar where a bar says it better, and in the voice of your household. It never decides for you. In the other sections the blocks stand under the entry you picked, headed **How things stand**. Only one is open at a time.

A lord whose stance you have not sounded keeps their reasons to themselves, and the block never names who is in a plot.

#### Pending

Everything that waits for your word, in one list: a letter of a feud, a charter laid before you, a case of the assembly, an oath, a lord's plea, a sentence, an envoy's letter with a price, terms of peace, a vote of your realm's council, an envoy who waits before a lord to speak for you, and the rest of what the realm asks of you. Each line says what it is and who asks it, and when something holds it back right now, it says so. Pending only lists: the windows come as before, and nothing is answered by looking at the list.

#### Envoys

Your envoys on the road (to whom, and about when they will be back), those who wait before a lord to speak for you (**Waits to speak for you**), the letters that wait for your answer, those riding home, and the meetings agreed. Envoys sent from this tab are the envoys of always: someone of your house, or a [rider from your party](envoys.md#a-rider-from-your-party), really rides to the lord, and you speak through them [when they arrive](envoys.md#when-the-envoy-arrives).

#### Let me look into it first

Most windows that ask your word on the realm's quarrels have one more choice: **Let me look into it first**.

- The window closes **without an answer**: neither the yes nor the no.
- Time stops, and stays stopped while you look.
- The kingdom screen opens on the Realm tab, at the matter itself when it has an entry (the feud, the warning, the charter, the reckoning), or at its house. An envoy's letter opens **Envoys**. The encyclopedia, the clan screen and every tab are yours to read.
- When you go back to the map, the same window comes again at once, before any time has passed. If the matter was settled meanwhile, it does not come again.

Escape still does what it did before. While you look, **Create army** is greyed, and only one matter can be looked into at a time. The choice is not offered in a battle, a scene or a hall, on the events that come up on the map (an incident between realms, a challenge, a hunt, a happening in your house), nor on a notice that only says **OK**.

#### Seek him out

Some things are said face to face. **Seek him out** opens an entry in your journal, *"Seek out {lord}"*, with where they were last seen, and marks the lord on your map. What you say when you find them is the conversation of always. One lord at a time; the entry closes when you speak with them, if they die or are taken, or with **Stop seeking**.

## The offices

| Office | Skill | Full effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Marshal** | Leadership and Tactics (the average) | More militia and security every day in every town and castle of the realm, whoever holds it, and a little more militia in the villages. Also leads the realm's armies and carries the ruler's orders and aims to the lords (see [Command your armies](command-your-armies.md)) |
| **Steward** | Steward and Trade (the average) | More tax in the towns and castles of the ruler's own clan |
| **Master of Works** | Engineering | Faster building in the towns and castles of the ruler's own clan |
| **Chancellor** | Charm | More influence every day for the ruler's clan; and a house loses less of its goodwill toward the ruler over the [crown's wrongs](factions.md#lords-remember): a good Chancellor spares a fair part of it, only a truly exceptional one a great deal (a small wrong may then cost no goodwill at all, now and then; the grievance itself is remembered just the same). With the old [discontent of large kingdoms](civil-war.md#discontent-off-by-default) turned on, less of that too |
| **Warden of the King's Peace** | Scouting and Leadership (the average) | More security every day in every town and castle of the realm, whoever holds it |

!!! note "The Warden and the King's Writ"
    A free man-hunter who carries an AI ruler's [writ](kings-writ.md) holds that realm's Warden seat for as long as the writ lasts, without being of the realm. AI rulers never give the Warden seat to your house any other way.

- **Skill decides how much.** A holder with little skill gives nothing; a master of the craft gives the full effect. In between it grows steadily.
- **A place at court is an honour.** The clan of each holder gets a little influence every day (once per clan, however many offices it holds). The ruler's own clan does not get this.
- **The Marshal leads the armies.** Whatever their skill, the Marshal is the one who takes the ruler's war aims and carries them to the lords; as ruler you give your orders through them, and as Marshal you take up your liege's aims for the war. See [Command your armies](command-your-armies.md).
- **No landed knight sits at court.** A [landed knight](landed-knights.md) serves the lord who granted his land and sits on no council of the crown: he holds none of the five offices, yourself included. One who becomes a landed knight while holding an office leaves it, and the realm is told.
- One office per person. A holder who is a prisoner, in hiding or away on another task keeps the office, but it gives nothing until they are back. A Marshal whose house rides in a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses)'s host counts as away: while the realm is at war with another, an AI ruler names another, and you cannot name a lord riding in a host. Someone [sent](#sending-others-on-the-realms-business) on an affair that belongs to their own office keeps working while away.
- You see the bonus in the game's usual tooltips, for example *"Marshal (Name)"*.
- An office falls vacant when its holder dies, leaves the realm, becomes ruler, becomes a landed knight or (a companion) leaves the ruler's household. You are told: *"The office of {office} of {realm} is vacant..."*

### Naming your court (as ruler)

Only the ruler names the court. Anyone adult in the realm can serve: nobles of any house of the kingdom (your own included) and, at your court, your family and **companions**. Mercenaries and landed knights cannot. The **Marshal** is the one exception among the rest: only the head of a house can raise the realm's hosts, so only the head of a house can be Marshal, never a kinsman or companion who does not lead one (see [One Marshal](command-your-armies.md#one-marshal)).

You offer an office in person or by envoy:

- **In person.** Say *"I would have you serve at my court."* (for a lord of another house it is under *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*), then pick the office: *"As my {office}."*, or *"As my {office}, in place of {holder}."* Hovering shows what they would give. Your family and companions are always asked this way.
- **By envoy**, to a lord of another house, wherever they are: *"Offer an office"*, from the lord's page in the encyclopedia or from their house under Fealty in the [Realm tab](#the-realm-tab). You choose the office in the conversation once your envoy stands before the lord; the answer is the lord's, as if you asked in person. See [Envoys](envoys.md).
- **By rider, for the Marshal.** From any town or castle of your realm, or through a companion or family member riding with you, a rider can offer the office of Marshal to a lord of another house, wherever they are. See [Riders](command-your-armies.md#riders).
- **Dismiss** a holder from the office's entry in the Crown tab.

A command against one enemy realm (a [Marshal of a Front](command-your-armies.md)) is offered the same ways: in person (*"I would have you lead the realm's lords in one of my wars."*) or by envoy (*"Offer a command"*).

Who says no:

- Family and companions always accept.
- A lord who thinks poorly of you refuses: *"An honour, but I would rather not serve you just now. Ask someone else."*
- A lord secretly in a plot does not give himself away: he accepts the office.

What it costs or gives:

- **A place at court warms a house to its ruler** for as long as it lasts: the head of a house thinks better of the ruler for each office one of his house holds, and the warmth goes when the office does. It never builds up: naming the same house again and again adds nothing.
- Clan leaders of more renown than the house you chose, who are not your friends, feel passed over and think a little less of you.
- **Putting someone of another house out of an office** while they serve, by dismissing or replacing them, leaves their house thinking less of the ruler for a good while, or until one of it is named again; each office lost counts, and it never builds up beyond that. The same for an AI ruler. None if they could not serve at the time. Dismissing your own family or companions costs nothing. The known leader of a plot leaves the court at no cost.
- **One house with more than one office** is resented: while it holds them, every other house of the realm thinks less of the ruler and of that house (if it is the ruler's own, of the ruler). An AI ruler is loath to give a second office to a house that already holds one.
- An office given as a term of a house's oath is kept while that oath binds it.

When you come to the throne, you inherit the old ruler's court and can keep it, dismiss its holders in the Crown tab, or name others in person or by envoy.

### AI rulers and their courts

- Every AI ruler looks over their court once a month, choosing the most skilled nobles of the realm, with a preference for those they like. They pass over houses involved in a plot, houses that refused them the oath, and landed knights.
- They replace a holder only for someone clearly better, or one who has been unable to serve for about a month.
- For **Marshal** they name only the head of a house, and prefer one who leads their own party in the field over one who keeps to their hall, whatever the skill; they look elsewhere only if no such lord is to be had. When the Marshal's seat falls empty, or its holder no longer heads his house, the ruler fills it at once, not at the next monthly look.
- They dismiss a holder who leads a plot once it is talked about in the realm, at no cost. Replacing anyone else costs them the same little regard it costs you.
- A new AI ruler keeps the old court for a while before changing it.
- An empty office does not wait for the monthly look: an AI ruler fills it within a few days, once a new ruler has settled in. If the best choice is of your house while an office is already being offered to you, the seat goes to the best of the others instead of staying empty.

### The Marshal's burden

When a fief of the realm falls to a siege and no army of the realm marched to it or lingered near it, nor any lord fought for it or stood within its walls, its house blames the crown and the **Marshal** too: a smaller grudge against the Marshal's house, and the head of the house thinks less of the Marshal (less than the ruler loses for it, a good Chancellor or not). Not when the Marshal is of the house that lost it, or of the ruler's own house or family: then the crown already answers for it. If you are the Marshal, you will hear of it. See [Factions and grievances](factions.md#lords-remember).

**Offers to you.** If you or a noble of your family is the best choice for an office, the ruler sends a letter, **"An office at court"**, saying what the office would give. **Accept** for the office and the ruler's warmer regard. **Decline** costs some of it, and that ruler will not ask again for a while. Your companions are never offered offices by an AI ruler, and no office is offered to you while you are a landed knight.

**Holding an office for an AI ruler.** Your office works for the whole realm according to your skill, and your clan gets the daily influence of a place at court. As **Marshal** you also take up your ruler's aims for the war (see [Command your armies](command-your-armies.md#your-lieges-aims)), and you can ask your ruler for that office yourself. A ruler never replaces you as Marshal with someone better suited: only if you have been unable to serve (a captive, or away) for about a month, or no longer may hold it (you leave the realm, for instance). The [affairs of your kingdom](realm-affairs.md) that belong to your office come to you as the ruler's charge: the messenger rides from the ruler, and the rewards are bigger. Leaving a charge undone as Marshal or Steward costs relation with the ruler.

### Laying down an office { #laying-down-an-office }

An office you hold at the court of an AI ruler need not be yours for good. Speak to the ruler: for each office you hold there is a line, *"My liege, I would lay down the office of {office}."* (hovering over it says what it costs). The ruler asks whether you mean it, in their own voice (*"You wish to lay down the office of {office}? Then I must find another for it. Are you certain?"*): answer *"It is my wish. I lay it down."* or *"No, forget I spoke of it."*

- The ruler thinks a little less of you and names someone else to the office at once.
- From then on, that office is offered to no one of your house, at the ruler's monthly look, at the [assembly](assembly.md) or by letter, until you ask for it again.
- **Asking for it again.** Speak to the ruler: *"My liege, I would serve you again as {office}."* The ruler weighs you against whoever holds it now, as at their monthly look: *"Very well. You are my {office} again."*, or a refusal that gives the real reason (the holder serves them better, your house has not sworn to them, they know of your plot, or the holder sits there by the terms of their house's oath). For the Marshal, ask as always: *"My liege, let me lead the realm's armies as your Marshal."*
- The **Warden of the King's Peace** is not laid down this way: that seat comes with the [King's Writ](kings-writ.md#warden-of-the-kings-peace) and goes back with it, at no cost, when you give the writ back.
- Only you lay an office down: the lords of the AI keep theirs until the ruler relieves them or they can no longer serve.

## Sending others on the realm's business

When an [affair of your kingdom](realm-affairs.md) comes up (a free company, a revolt, a hungry town, bad blood between two houses), a messenger brings you the news on the road and you can answer, as with the game's own quests, that someone else will see to it:

- *"One of my companions or kin will see to it."*: someone riding in your party goes, with men you pick in the game's troop screen.
- *"My vassals will see to it."* (if you rule): a house of your realm sends one of its lords (never its head) with soldiers from its own garrison. The list shows your relation with the head of each house, the chance it accepts and the influence it costs, less the better you stand with them. A house on bad terms with you may refuse; one that does not dislike you never does.
- Only those who can go are listed, and the holder of the fitting office first: Marshal for sellswords and revolts, Steward for hunger, Chancellor for bad blood.

Whoever goes is away for some days and has an entry in your quest log until they are back. How it goes depends on their skill: a skilled hand sees it done safely, a poor one takes longer and may fail. What you entrust never gives you more than doing it yourself. The work of the realm cannot be entrusted. Full details: [Someone else sees to it](realm-affairs.md#someone-else-sees-to-it).

## Holding court

People come to ask you for things, and you hear them by holding court in one of your own towns or castles.

### Who comes, and how often

- As **ruler**, a new request arrives about once a month, from anywhere in the realm. As a **vassal** with fiefs of your own, far less often, only from your own fiefs, and never the requests marked "ruler" below.
- You are told who asks and for what, for example: *"{Who} has come to ask you for {what}, and waits to be heard at {place}."* As ruler, a lord's request reads *"... Hold court in one of your own towns or castles and they will ride there to be heard."*
- Only a handful can wait at once. The Crown tab lists them under **Petitions**, by where they come from.
- While your realm is at war, no new request comes, and the waiting of those already there does not count: nobody gives up and nothing is held against you. A ruler with no town or castle of their own gets no new requests, and the waiting does not count either.

### "Hold court"

In the menu of one of **your own** towns or castles, choose **"Hold court"**. Hovering over it shows who is there now, which notables have a task for you, who would ride there and in about how many days, who cannot come and why (*"at war with us"*, *"with an army"*, *"hosting guests"*...), and about how long it should take.

- Riders go out to every lord who asked to be heard, **however far** they are. A lord with a party rides there; one without travels on their own. When one arrives you are told: *"{Lord} has arrived at {place} and waits to be heard."*
- You wait in the hall with the game's progress bar. The hall takes a couple of hours to make ready. Then each petitioner comes before you **in turn, as they arrive**: the conversation opens by itself, and the clock stops while you talk. Each request has two to four answers.
- **As ruler**, everyone else with a request (notables and common folk from any town or castle of the realm) comes to whichever of your halls you sit in. **As a vassal**, each is heard in the town or castle their request belongs to.
- **Then the notables.** Notables of the town and its villages who have one of the game's own quests to offer come before you too, with the game's usual quest conversation: accept it, turn it down or send a companion, as in the base game.
- The court ends by itself when everyone who could come has been heard, or when the bar runs out (those still on the road go home). **"Rise from court"** ends it early, and leaving the place ends it too.
- With nobody to hear, the button is greyed out and says why: *"Nobody is waiting to be heard."*, *"Nobody here asks to be heard. Those waiting are at: ..."* or who cannot come now.
- It cannot be held while the place is under siege.

### Court in the hall {#court-in-the-hall}

While you hold court, **"Go into the hall and hear them in person"**. In the lord's hall, those waiting to be heard who are there (lords, the notables of the villages, common folk) stand in a line before your seat, the one who has waited longest first. Hear them by speaking to them, in any order: the one you heard leaves the line and the line moves up. A herald and a scribe stand by your seat and guards along the walls. The notables of the town itself keep to the square: you hear them from the wait, as before. When you come out, the court goes on with its bar, and the days pass as before.

### The requests

| Request | Who asks | Your answers |
|---|---|---|
| **a fief** (ruler) | the head of a house of your realm without land | Promise the next fief the crown grants (they think well of you, and far worse if it goes to someone else or none comes in good time), put them off kindly (a Charm roll; a little resentment if it fails), or refuse (they resent it). |
| **gold to raise troops** (ruler) | a lord whose party is under half strength | Give gold, more for a greater house (they think better of you), or refuse (a little resentment). |
| **abuses of a lord** (ruler) | a village notable, against the lord who holds their town or castle | Reprimand the lord yourself (the lord resents it; the place grows more loyal and the notable grateful), send it back to the lord (the notable resents it), or have your **Marshal** look into it: after some days the complaint may turn out false. |
| **freedom from dues** | a merchant of your town | Grant a season without dues (less tax there but more prosperity each day, shown as *"Merchants' exemption"*, and a grateful merchant), lend gold to be repaid later (a little gratitude), or refuse (resentment). |
| **a quarrel over land** | two notables of one place | Give the land to one or the other (one grateful, one resentful; the two grow a little closer to each other), split it (both a little grateful, and the two grow much closer), or let them settle it. Heard or not, the same two do not bring it again for a long while. |
| **a widow's pension** | a soldier's widow, after a battle you fought | Pay a pension (the place grows a little more loyal) or refuse. |
| **a prisoner's freedom** | the house of a lord you hold prisoner | Free them (the head of the house is grateful), ransom them for twice the usual price, or keep them (resentment). |
| **a place in your guard** | an old soldier | Take him: a seasoned veteran of your culture joins you. |
| **works for the town** | a master builder, in your town or castle | Pay most of a building's cost: it goes into the town's building fund and that building goes first. |
| **refuge in your realm** (ruler) | a lord of a foreign kingdom at war with another | Shelter them: their ruler is grateful, but tension with the enemy kingdom rises (see [Events between kingdoms](events-between-kingdoms.md)). Or stay out of it. |
| **a place in your house** | a distant kinsman (needs a free companion slot; not at every court) | Take them in: a new companion of your culture joins your party. |
| **help with a marriage** (ruler) | two unmarried lords of different houses | Speak to both houses: a Charm roll; on success they marry and both house heads think better of you. |
| **a fief of yours their house claims by right** (ruler) | the head of a house of your realm with a [right](fief-rights.md) to one of your own towns or castles; most often one near its own lands, and a firm right more often than a weak one | Grant it: the fief is theirs, and they are very grateful (not your last town or castle, nor one under siege). Put it off: a Charm roll, a little resentment if it fails. Refuse: they resent it, and the house holds it against the crown (see [Lords remember](factions.md#lords-remember)). Heard or left waiting, the same house does not ask for the same fief again for a long while. |

Charm rolls show their odds in the answer: the more Charm, the better, up to a ceiling.

### Leaving them waiting

- A request left unheard for about a month lapses, usually with a small loss of relation: *"{Who} has given up waiting to be heard at {place}."*
- Once requests have waited a good while since the oldest arrived (or since your last court, if that is later), **each of your towns and castles slowly loses loyalty**. It shows as **"Petitions unheard"** in the loyalty breakdown of each fief, and the Crown tab says your fiefs are losing loyalty. Now and then the lords still waiting think a little less of you, and a message tells you who waits where, which fiefs are losing loyalty and what to do.
- Before that, the **Petitions** panel of the Crown tab tells you how many days have gone by and when the loss would start.
- Holding court and hearing at least one petitioner starts the count again.

### The Chancellor hears them for you

As ruler, with a Chancellor able to act, the Crown tab offers **"Let the chancellor hear them ({N})"**. Your chancellor decides by their own character (a generous one pays, a merciful one forgives, a mean one refuses), and only a generous one spends your gold, never much of it at one hearing. You see their answers first, in a window **"From your chancellor"**: **"Let it be done"** or **"I will hear them myself"**. Through the chancellor, relation gains are smaller, and their Charm decides the rolls. The old soldier and the kinsman will only speak to you in person. A ruler with no town or castle of their own can only have petitions heard this way.

## The oath to a new ruler

When a kingdom gets a new ruler (by succession, by election or by winning a civil war), every house of the realm has a short time to swear fealty. A brand-new kingdom has no oath. Nor is there one when a [vassal realm](vassal-realms.md)'s council chooses its overlord's ruler: the realm is joined to the overlord's and its houses swear no oath.

A new ruler's [claim to the crown](succession.md) weighs in every house's answer: a ruler with a strong claim finds most houses ready to swear, one with a weak claim hears more demands and more refusals.

Each AI house weighs it up: its relation with the new ruler, its Honor, whether it voted for them, whether it stood against them for the throne or fought them in a civil war, how close it is to plotting, and kinship.

| A house... | What happens |
|---|---|
| **swears** | It thinks a little better of the ruler, and is less inclined to plot for a good while. |
| **asks for something** | An empty office, or a fief of the ruler's own clan. If the ruler grants it, the house swears on those terms, and the ruler thinks a little less of it for having to buy its oath (an honourable or valiant ruler more so, a generous or calculating one hardly at all). |
| *(houses riding in a feud's host)* | swear like the rest, within the same days. [Landed knights](landed-knights.md) swear no oath: they are their lord's men. |
| **refuses**, or lets the time pass | It thinks less of the ruler; the house is **marked** for a long while: far more inclined to plot, and kept out of that ruler's court. Nobody leaves the realm. |

**AI rulers almost never give up a fief.** A ruler whose own clan holds only a few towns and castles never gives one, and the others very seldom, at most one fief per oath. Most of the time a house that asks gets an empty office instead. When the time for the oath ends, an AI ruler often wins back a house that refused, with gold or an office; otherwise it rebukes it, and the house thinks less of the ruler still.

### As a vassal

You are told: *"{ruler} is now ruler of {kingdom}. Every house is to swear fealty within {days} days: go to her/him in person to swear, ask for something, or refuse."* Go to the ruler in person, choose *"There is something I'd like to discuss."* and then *"I have come about my oath of fealty."*:

- *"I swear to be your faithful vassal..."*: the ruler thinks better of you, and you gain some influence.
- **By an envoy**: from the ruler's page in the encyclopedia you may send a family member or companion, who can swear in your name, with no terms (to ask for something, go in person). When the envoy stands before the ruler within your time, a window **"An oath to {ruler}"** asks you at once: **Swear in my name**, and your house is sworn that day: the same regard any house gets for swearing, but not the extra of kneeling in person, nor the influence; or **Not now**, and your time runs on as usual. Nothing is sworn without your word. If a battle or another window keeps that question from you, your house is not marked before it can be asked. See [Envoys](envoys.md#the-oath).
- *"I will swear, but I would ask something in return."* (once per oath): ask for an empty office, a fief of the ruler's own clan (only if they have one to spare by the rule above; much harder to argue), or gold, more for a greater house. Then you argue it out with the game's persuasion. Win and you get it and swear, but the ruler thinks a little less of you for it, as of any house that names its price. Lose and the ruler says *"No. You will have nothing of me for an oath you owe in any case."* (and thinks less of you); then swear without terms, refuse, or take more time.
- *"I will not swear to you."*: the ruler thinks far less of you, and your house is marked for a long while.
- Not going in time costs you the ruler's regard, and your house is marked.

### As the new ruler

A window, **"The oaths of fealty"**, lists every house as *likely to swear*, *wavering* or *cold to you*. When a house asks its price (**"A house asks its price"**), you can grant an empty office, one of your clan's fiefs or gold, more for a greater house, or answer *"Nothing: an oath is not for sale"* (it thinks again a few days later, less willing). The window warns you whether granting it would sit ill with you, by your character.

You can also talk to a house that has not sworn: persuade it (*"Hear me out before your house decides."*, once per house), offer it an office or a fief (accepted at once), or press it: *"You will swear, or answer for it."*, which costs influence and its regard either way, and makes it swear more often than not.

### Oaths in the hall {#oaths-in-the-hall}

While a realm swears to its new ruler and the ruler sits in a hall, the oath is a ceremony you can watch: the heads of the houses that have not yet sworn wait in a line before the throne, those who have sworn stand to one side and the rest to the other. A house that swears while you are there goes over to the sworn, and the hall applauds. A herald, a scribe, guards and a man-at-arms behind the ruler stand by.

## Taking a fief: decree, demand or reckoning {#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal}

No council votes on taking a fief from a house. There are three roads, as in the great feudal kingdoms, where taking back land was a lord's own act and not a matter for a vote.

- **The ruler, by decree.** As ruler, the button on the kingdom screen's Fiefs tab reads **Decree**. It opens a confirmation, **"Take {fief} by decree"**, that says what it will cost; there is no vote, and the fief passes into the crown's own hands, with no share-out. The ruler also decrees when backing a lord's plea (below), and the crown's verdict on a [reckoning](factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house) at the [assembly](assembly.md) takes a fief the same way.
- **A lord, by demanding it of his own vassal.** As a [duke or count](titles.md#a-lord-takes-back-a-vassals-fief), the same button reads **Demand** on the fiefs of houses that hold their land of you: a fief of your duchy, or a castle of your county. It goes back into your own hands.
- **House against house, only through a reckoning,** which the ruler judges at the assembly. A right alone does not open the button: it is greyed, and tells you the ways open to you (win lords over to a reckoning, or press the right by the sword).

### What it costs

Every time, with no limit and nothing coming back:

- The head of the house that loses the fief thinks far less of the ruler (or of its lord), and the head of every other vassal house of the realm (for a lord: each of his other vassals) a little less, wondering whose fief is next. If the house that gets the fief holds a firm [right](fief-rights.md) to it, the others hold nothing against it; the house that loses it still does. The confirmation counts the houses it will cost you.
- The influence the button asks.
- The house that loses it holds it against the crown (or against its lord), and keeps an old right to the fief.

### A fief taken for nothing {#a-fief-taken-for-nothing}

A ruler who takes a fief into the crown's own hands **with no cause the realm would own** commits what the realm calls a seizure: the house is no marked traitor and no felon of the crown, it leads no charter against the ruler in the open, and the ruling house holds no firm [right](fief-rights.md) to the fief. The confirmation tells you so before you seal it: *"...the realm will call this decree a seizure. It costs the crown far more of its standing, and the {house} are far readier to take up arms over it, alone or with any plot against you."*

- **The decree costs far more influence**, and far more goodwill with the other houses of the realm. The realm's policies that make a decree dearer or cheaper, such as Feudal Inheritance, move this price too.
- **The house is readier to defy it**: a war abroad does not hold it back, and its kin and friends see the crown in the wrong and ride with it more readily.
- **A plot it is sworn to rises over it more readily**, and the other houses of the plot are readier to rise with it.
- **A lord who demands a fief of his vassal with no such cause** (no traitor, no felon of his, no firm right of his house to it) pays the same higher price in influence.

The same for you and for the AI. An AI ruler moved only by bad blood with a house hardly ever does it. Taking a fief from a traitor, a felon, the leader of an open charter, or for a house with a firm right to it, costs only the usual price.

**Never a house's last town or castle.** Neither a decree of the ruler's own nor a lord's demand can take the last town or castle of a house, unless that house is marked as [traitors](realm-houses.md#houses-marked-as-traitors) or is a felon of the one taking it. Nothing is ever taken from the ruling house. The button is greyed with the reason, and the same holds for the AI.

The button is also greyed while the matter is still open (the house is weighing its answer, a reckoning is after it, it rides in a feud, your own feud or plea against that vassal is not settled, or you let it keep that fief when it last defied you), while the crown is not yet given after a ruler's death, and in a feud's host. Nothing is paid for a decree that cannot be carried out.

### AI rulers and lords

- **An AI ruler decrees only with a cause, and rarely:** a house he cannot stand, a house marked as traitors or felons of his, or his own coffers running low for a long time. His letter gives the true reason. He never does it during a civil war, nor against a house a reckoning is already after.
- **An AI lord demands only with the same causes, and seldom.** If you are the vassal, your lord's letter gives his true reason.
- **As ruler you decree at will**, with no cause needed.
- A lord never joins a reckoning against his own vassal: his only road to a vassal's fief is to demand it.

### How a house answers: yield or defy {#how-a-house-answers}

Every fief taken from a house, by the crown's decree, a lord's demand, the crown's verdict on a reckoning, a sentence of the assembly, a petition the emperor grants or the crown's judgment of a traitor, is answered the same way: the house **yields** or **defies**.

**Who defies.** Lords do not give up their land lightly. A house weighs its strength against those who would come to take the fief: its own men, and the part of the realm that would ride with it if it broke (its kin, its allies, the houses sworn to it, the houses plotting against that ruler), against the crown's and the claimants'. A bold or proud head, or one who hates the ruler, defies more readily; an honourable or calculating one, or one on good terms with the ruler, less. A house just beaten in a civil war hardly ever does. Against a lord's demand it weighs its own lord and those who stand with him, not the crown.

**A decree of the crown.** The house:

- **yields**: the fief passes at once to the crown, or to the house the ruler backed;
- **defies**: it keeps the fief and says so before the realm. Then the ruler decides:
    - **Look the other way.** The house keeps the fief, unpunished but not forgiven: the ruler thinks less of its head, and men say the crown's word can be defied, which costs the ruler some of his [right to rule](succession.md). If a house claimed the fief, it may still take it by the sword, in a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses) the crown lets run; if it has not the strength, the quarrel rests a while.
    - **Name them traitors.** The house breaks with the realm and rises against the crown: a civil war (see [A house that breaks with its realm](realm-houses.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm)).

    An AI ruler weighs it by the house's strength against his own, his regard for its head, his own character (merciful and calculating rulers look away more readily, honourable ones less) and how the realm's wars stand (a crown losing them looks away more readily); he looks away less readily from his own decree than from a sentence of the assembly.
- or **raises its plot**, if it is sworn to one (see [A fief taken can set a plot alight](civil-war.md#the-rising)); a house of a plot may also leave it and break alone, or yield.

**A lord's demand.** The vassal yields the fief to his lord, or **bars the gates**. Then the lord lets it lie (the vassal keeps the fief, the lord thinks less of him, and the quarrel rests a while, during which the lord demands nothing more of it) or takes up arms against him in a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses): the peace that ends it decides: if his lord takes the fief and grants no pardon, he answers for [felony](factions.md#felony); if the peace frees him, he no longer holds of that lord. A vassal never breaks with the realm over his lord's demand. If no feud can be fought that day, he keeps the fief.

**No fief is ever handed over by itself.** If a house that refused can no longer break away, or no sword can be drawn, it keeps the fief. An AI house named traitor while riding at your side (in your army, your battle or your siege, or while you are in one of its towns or castles) waits until it is no longer with you before it breaks away.

When a house does not yield to your decree or demand, you are told at once. As ruler, a window **"Defied before the realm"** asks you: **"Look the other way"** or **"Name the {house} traitors"**.

### A lord's plea {#a-lords-plea}

Under [High Crown Authority](crown-laws.md#high-crown-authority) a lord may not take up arms against his vassal. The vassal who bars the gates keeps the fief, and the lord takes his plea to the throne.

- **An AI ruler** judges it by its cause: against a vassal marked as traitors, or a felon of that lord, he nearly always backs the lord. Otherwise he keeps out of it, unless he has reason: the lord holds a firm right to the fief, the ruler holds the lord very dear, or he hates the vassal; the more reasons, the likelier. He never backs a lord against a vassal he holds very dear.
- **If you are the ruler**, the plea comes in the [feud panel](factions.md#the-feud-panel), the lord and his vassal face to face, with the lord's right to the fief and the law that keeps the sword out of it. It offers **"Back {lord} by decree"** or **"Keep out of it"**.
- **Backed**, it is a decree like any other: the vassal yields or defies, and the crown pays its usual price (but not with the lord who asked). No council has voted for it, so the decree also costs the ruler's house influence, you and the AI alike: an AI ruler without it keeps out of it, and your **"Back {lord} by decree"** is greyed with the reason.
- **Shielded**, the vassal keeps the fief, the lord holds it against the crown, and he may demand nothing of that vassal for a season, unless the vassal turns traitor or felon.

### If the fief is yours

- **"A decree of the crown"**: **"Give up the fief"** or **"Defy the decree"** (you keep it, and the ruler may look the other way or name your house traitors: then you break with the realm and rise against the crown; greyed with the reason if your house could not break with the realm). If you are sworn to a plot: **"Give up the fief"**, **"Raise the plot's banner"** or **"Renounce your oath"** (you leave the plot and break with the realm alone). If you choose to rise and it cannot be done after all, you keep the fief, are told why, and are asked again.
- **"Your lord's demand"**, with your lord's letter and reason: **"Give it up to your lord"** or **"Bar the gates"**, whose tooltip says what may follow. You cannot renounce your oath against your lord alone; if you are sworn to a plot, you also have **"Raise the plot's banner"** and **"Renounce your oath"**.
- The crown's verdict on a reckoning at the assembly: see [The assembly](assembly.md).

Every one of these windows has **Let me look into it first** (see [The Realm tab](#the-realm-tab)).

### In the Empire

In a realm of the [Empire](empire.md) the land is the emperor's to give: no lord demands a vassal's fief, and no house takes another's through a reckoning. A right is laid before the throne as a petition instead. The emperor decrees like any ruler.

## Turn it off

In the settings file (see [Settings](../settings.md)):

- `CourtOfficesEnabled` to `false`: the offices give nothing and nobody is named. The realm's armies then have a Marshal of their own (see [Command your armies](command-your-armies.md#one-marshal)).
- `CourtAiAppointEnabled` to `false`: AI rulers do not name their courts or offer you offices.
- `CourtChargeEnabled` to `false`: holding an office for an AI ruler does not bring you its affairs as the ruler's charge.
- `CourtResignEnabled` to `false`: you cannot lay down an office at an AI ruler's court.
- `AffairDelegationEnabled` to `false`: affairs of your kingdom cannot be sent to companions, kin or houses.
- `AudiencesEnabled` to `false`: no petitions and no "Hold court".
- `OathEnabled` to `false`: no oath of fealty when a ruler changes.
- `CourtSceneEnabled` to `false`: no court in the hall; you hear them from the wait.
- `OathSceneEnabled` to `false`: no ceremony in the hall while a realm swears.
- `FiefRevocationEnabled` to `false`: taking a fief from a vassal works as in the base game, with no lost relations and no revolt.
- `RevokeCrownDecreeAiEnabled` to `false`: AI rulers do not take fiefs by decree of their own accord (your Decree button stays).
- `FiefDefyEnabled` to `false`: a house of the AI always yields a fief taken from it (you still choose for yours); `BlindEyeRecordEnabled` to `false`: looking the other way costs a ruler nothing of his right to rule.
- `LookFirstEnabled` to `false`: no **Let me look into it first** on the windows of the realm's affairs.
- `VassalTreeEnabled` to `false`: no tree of the realm under Fealty in the Realm tab; Fealty shows your own house alone.
- `TitleLordDemandEnabled` to `false`: a duke or count cannot demand a vassal's fief; `TitleLordDemandAiEnabled` to `false`: AI lords never do it of their own accord (your Demand button stays).
