# Companies for hire

When a siege is about to fall and you need two hundred swords by next week, the tavern is where you look. Four sellsword companies sit in the towns of Calradia, waiting for someone rich and desperate enough to pay them. They are expensive, they take days to reach you, and they leave the day your silver runs out.

These are not the tavern mercenaries of the base game, nor the [Unsworn](mercenary-contracts.md) of the mercenary contracts, nor the [free company](realm-affairs.md#a-free-company) that squats in a village in the affairs of your kingdom. You hire a whole company at once, under its own captain, for a fixed term.

## The four companies

| Company | Troops |
|---|---|
| **Thornwall Company** | Pikes and crossbows. Thornwall Recruit, then Pikeman, Veteran Pikeman, Halberdier; or Crossbowman, Veteran Crossbowman, Arbalester. |
| **Grey Lances** | Heavy horse. Grey Lance Squire, then Lancer, Man-at-Arms, Knight; or Mounted Sergeant, Sergeant-at-Arms, Ironhand. |
| **Sons of the Wind** | Steppe riders. Wind Outrider, then Horse Archer, Bowrider, Wind Archer; or Wind Raider, Wind Lancer, Storm Lancer. |
| **Blackhand Company** | Axemen. Blackhand Hireling, then Axeman, Shieldbreaker, Ironwall (axe and shield); or Cleaver, Reaver, Headsman (great axe). |

- In the party screen every name carries the company's mark (Thornwall Pikeman, Grey Lance Knight, Wind Storm Lancer, Blackhand Headsman...).
- Each company has **170 to 250 men**, its captain included. Most are tier 3 and 4, with some tier 2 recruits and some tier 5 veterans.
- A company lives **in a town**, not on the map. Its captain drinks in that town's tavern.

## Finding them

Ask any tavern keeper in a town: **"I need swords. Many of them, and soon. Who is selling?"** They tell you what the road says of each company: where it is quartered and how many men it has, or that it is on the road, or that someone else has hired it, or that it will not take your coin.

When you take your leave with **"That is all I needed to know. My thanks."**, a journal entry opens, **"Swords for hire"**, which marks on your map the towns where a company would sign with you today: one that is free, will take your coin, and whose earnest for the shortest term you have in your purse. If none would, no entry opens and the tavern keeper tells you why. The news goes stale after **10 days**. Ask again for fresh news.

## Hiring in person

Go to the company's town. Find the captain in the tavern, or use the town menu option **"Seek out the captain of the Thornwall Company"** (or whichever company is there). Ask **"What are your terms, captain?"** and pick a term:

- **"I want you for 30 days."**
- **"40 days. Name your price."**
- **"50 days, the long term. What will that cost me?"**

Then answer **"Agreed. Here is your earnest."**, or haggle: **"That is robbery, and you know it. Knock something off and we have a deal."** You get **one try per visit**. Your chance is 25%, plus a quarter of your Trade or Charm, whichever is higher, up to 75%. If it works, the **earnest** is **15% lower**. The daily pay stays the same. If it fails, reloading the game will not give you another try that day.

## Hiring by rider

If you are in another town, the tavern keeper can send a rider instead: **"Send a rider to the Thornwall Company at..."**. This only works for a company within about **six towns' distance** of you. Choose the term, then pay with **"Here is the silver. Send your fastest rider."** You pay the earnest now, plus **300 denars** for the rider.

- The rider takes real time to get there, and the keeper tells you roughly how long. A journal entry, **"A rider to the Thornwall Company"**, follows him.
- If the company has gone when he arrives, or you have been taken prisoner, he rides back and **returns your earnest**. He keeps his fee.
- If you reach the company's town before your rider, you can seal the deal with the captain yourself: **"The terms are the ones my rider carries, silver and all. Take my hand on them now."**

## What it costs

- **Earnest**, paid when you sign.
- **Daily pay**, from the day they join you: for each man, the wage the game pays a regular soldier of his tier, **plus half**. This is about what the game charges for its own tavern mercenaries of the same tier.

The earnest goes up with the term: **120 denars a man** for 30 days, **150** for 40 days and **about 172** for 50 days. A longer term costs more up front but less per day.

The daily pay shows up as a line in your clan's daily finances: **"Pay of the Thornwall Company"**. The game takes it with the rest of your clan's day. When your gold drops below five days of pay for all your companies, a captain warns you. **On a day you cannot pay, the company leaves.**

**Example** (approximate): a company of about 210 men with the usual mix of tiers.

| Term | Earnest | Daily pay | Total for the term |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days | 25,200 | about 2,100 | about 88,500 |
| 40 days | 31,500 | about 2,100 | about 115,900 |
| 50 days | 36,225 | about 2,100 | about 141,700 |

A successful haggle cuts the 30-day earnest to about 21,400. At the end, **blood money** is due for the dead (see below): 40 men lost would cost another 2,400.

## The march

After you sign, the company **musters for 12 hours** in its town. Then it marches out as a real party on the map, with the company's name. The journal entry **"The Thornwall Company in your pay"** marks its column.

- It comes to wherever you are and keeps up with you if you move. Once it is close, or at the gate of the town you are in, it **falls in with your party**. If you meet the column on the road, you can tell the captain: **"Fall in behind my banner, captain."**
- It is a real party. Enemies can attack it on the way. If they cut it to pieces, the contract ends.
- **If you are inside a besieged town or castle**, the company will not march through the siege camp. It waits outside, out of the besiegers' reach, until you come out or the siege is lifted.
- **If you are at sea**, it waits on the shore.
- **Across the sea** (with War Sails): if there is open water between you and the company, its captain sends word that they will take ship, and the port is marked on your map. They sail from a harbour where a lord of their side could, or else from the coast by a port; they come ashore the same way on your side, never on a coast held or besieged by an enemy or watched by an enemy army. While at sea they cannot be seen or reached, and the voyage takes its real time. **You pay their passage**, as for the game's own ferry: **500 denars plus 10 a man**. If you cross back to their shore, they give up the passage and come by road.
- **The march has a deadline**: twice as long as the road to you should take, plus three days. Time spent waiting outside your siege does not count, and neither do up to 10 days of you being at sea. If you lead them on too long a chase, they give up and go home, and **half the earnest comes back** to you.
- If you are captured while they muster, you get the whole earnest back. If it happens while they march, you get half.

## In your service

Hired company men are with you, but they stay under their own captain:

- They **do not count against your party size**. The party size breakdown shows them as "Sellswords under their own captain". Your party is still slower for having more men, as in the base game.
- They **bring their own food**, so they do not eat from your stores.
- They bring **spare horses, one for every three of their men**. These help your foot soldiers ride, just like horses in your inventory would. You cannot sell or keep them. They never slow you down.
- They **never gain experience or upgrade**, and you **cannot move them** in the party screen. You also cannot leave them in a garrison.
- They **do not desert**, and they do not stop your own men from deserting either.
- **Your skills do not grow from them.** Their numbers add nothing to your Tactics, Leadership, Engineering, Scouting or Medicine experience.

Captains talk as the contract goes on. One speaks up after your first victory together, one warns you if you are **"spending us fast"**, and one reminds you **3 days before the term ends**.

## When it ends

- **Term served.** If you paid every day and lost fewer than a third of the men, the captain offers to **renew once**, in a window titled **"The Thornwall Company: the term is served"**. The terms: the same length again, **75% of the earnest** for the men left, and the same pay per man. Choose **"Pay the earnest and keep them"** or **"Let them go"**. If you give no answer within a day, they leave.
- **Released early.** Use **"Release the Thornwall Company from your service"** from the menu of any town, castle or village, and confirm with **"Release them"**. They keep the earnest, blood money is owed for the dead, and they will not take your coin again for a while.
- **Unpaid.** If you miss a day's pay, they leave that same day.
- **Heavy losses.** If more than **half** the men who joined you are dead, the captain ends the contract.
- **Blood money**: at the end, you pay **60 denars for each man who died** in your service. If you cannot pay it all, the captain takes what you have. You owe none if the column was broken before it reached you, or if you were captured.
- If a contract ends while you are **inside a besieged town or castle**, they will not walk out through the enemy lines. They stay on your walls, still paid, until the way is open. This does not apply when you could not pay them.

**Rest and grudges.** After a contract with you, a company will not sign with you again for **30 days**. The exception is a contract that ended through no fault of yours, such as your capture. If you left them **unpaid**, or **short of blood money**, they hold a grudge for **60 days**.

## Their men are never lost

Prisoners are not dead. If a company's men are captured anywhere, the company buys them back within a day, and it pays their captor the ransom. If you take company men prisoner yourself, or free some from an enemy, their quartermaster buys them back from you on the spot.

## Between contracts

- A company **wins back 2 men a day** in its town, or 4 a day while it has fewer than half its men. It **will not sign until it has at least 60% of its full size**.
- Every **60 to 90 days** it moves to another town. It prefers towns of realms at war and towns near a siege. If its town comes under siege, it leaves at once. Moves are not shown on the map, but they take as long as the march would.

## AI lords hire them too

Every day, each company for hire has a **1 in 10 chance** of being hired by a lord in need. Only clan leaders whose realm is at war and who are within a rider's reach can hire them, the most hard-pressed first (a besieged fief, a fief just lost, a realm much weaker than its enemy).

A lord only signs if, after the earnest, the whole term's pay and the blood money, they would still keep **200,000 denars**. In practice this means rulers and rich houses. The AI can have **at most 2 companies** at once. If the lord is of your realm or near you, you hear of it: *"Word on the road: ... has hired the Thornwall Company..."*. You may meet their column on the road, and if it is your enemy's, you can attack it.

## Turn it off
In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `CompanyHireEnabled`: nobody can hire the companies any more. A contract under way ends as usual.
- `CompanyAiHireEnabled`: AI lords no longer hire them. You still can.
- `CompanyCourierEnabled`: no riders. You must go to the captain yourself.
- `CompanyBloodMoneyEnabled`: no blood money for the dead.
- `CompanyRelocateEnabled`: companies stay in their town. They still leave it if it comes under siege.
- `CompanyRemounts`: no spare horses.
- `CompanySeaPassageEnabled`: companies never take ship. A company that could only reach you by sea is not offered, and one already marching waits on its shore while you are across the water.
