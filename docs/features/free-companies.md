# Companies for hire

When a siege is about to fall and you need two hundred swords by next week, the tavern is where you look. Nine sellsword companies sit in the towns of Calradia, waiting for someone rich and desperate enough to pay them. They are expensive, they take days to reach you, and they leave the day your silver runs out.

These are not the tavern mercenaries of the base game, nor the loose [Unsworn](mercenary-contracts.md) who sell themselves a few at a time in taverns or roam as bands, nor the [free company](realm-affairs.md#a-free-company) that squats in a village in the affairs of your kingdom. You hire a whole company at once, under its own captain, for a fixed term.

## The nine companies

| **Sons of the Wind** | Steppe riders. Wind Outrider, then Horse Archer, Bowrider, Wind Archer; or Wind Raider, Wind Lancer, Storm Lancer. |
| **Blackhand Company** | Axemen. Blackhand Hireling, then Axeman, Shieldbreaker, Ironwall (axe and shield); or Cleaver, Reaver, Headsman (great axe). |
| **The Last Cohort** | What is left of a broken imperial legion. Cohort Tiro, then Legionary, Veteran Legionary, Triarius; or Archer, Sagittarius, Palatine Bowman. |
| **The Sand Hawks** | Aserai horse. Sand Hawk Rider, then Faris, Mamluke, Heavy Mamluke; or Skirmisher, Jereed Rider, Desert Falcon. |
| **The Raven Fianna** | Battanian highlanders. Raven Kern, then Archer, Fian, Chosen Fian (longbows); or Falxman, Veteran Falxman, Champion. |

Unsworn Sellsword, then Hired Bow, Oathbreaker, Veteran (bows first, then axe and shield or great sword); or Horse Bow, Freerider, Free Lance (horse archers who rise to lancers). |

- Each company wears the arms of one people only, and never better than that realm's own soldiers of the same rank: what they sell is numbers, ready at once, not finer steel. The Unsworn Free Company is the exception: they carry whatever they brought with them from every realm, a Sturgian helm over an Aserai coat, but still nothing finer than the soldiers of those realms wear at the same rank.

- In the party screen every name carries the company's mark (Thornwall Pikeman, Grey Lance Knight, Wind Storm Lancer, Blackhand Headsman, Unsworn Free Lance...). The **Unsworn Free Company**, led by its Unsworn Free Captain, is not to be confused with the Unsworn bands of the mercenary contracts.
- Each company is a couple of hundred men, its captain included. Most are seasoned soldiers of the middle ranks, with some green hands and a few veterans.
- A company lives **in a town**, not on the map. Its captain drinks in that town's tavern.

## Finding them

Ask any tavern keeper in a town: **"I need swords. Many of them, and soon. Who is selling?"** They tell you what the road says of each company: where it is quartered and how many men it has, or that it is on the road, or that someone else has hired it, or that it will not take your coin.

When you take your leave with **"That is all I needed to know. My thanks."**, a journal entry opens, **"Swords for hire"**, which marks on your map the towns where a company would sign with you today: one that is free, will take your coin, and whose earnest for the shortest term you have in your purse. If none would, no entry opens and the tavern keeper tells you why. The news goes stale after some days. Ask again for fresh news.

## Hiring in person

Go to the company's town. Find the captain in the tavern, or use the town menu option **"Seek out the captain of the Thornwall Company"** (or whichever company is there). Ask **"What are your terms, captain?"** The captain names the daily pay and the earnest for each of three terms, a short one, a middle one and the long term, and you pick one.

Then answer **"Agreed. Here is your earnest."**, or haggle: **"That is robbery, and you know it. Knock something off and we have a deal."** You get **one try per visit**, and your Trade or Charm, whichever is better, helps. If it works, the **earnest** comes down; the daily pay stays the same. If it fails, reloading the game will not give you another try that day.

## Hiring by rider

If you are in another town, the tavern keeper can send a rider instead: **"Send a rider to the Thornwall Company at..."**. This only works for a company not too many towns away from you. Choose the term, then pay with **"Here is the silver. Send your fastest rider."** You pay the earnest now, plus the rider's fee.

- The rider takes real time to get there, and the keeper tells you roughly how long. A journal entry, **"A rider to the Thornwall Company"**, follows him.
- If the company has gone when he arrives, or you have been taken prisoner, he rides back and **returns your earnest**. He keeps his fee.
- If you reach the company's town before your rider, you can seal the deal with the captain yourself: **"The terms are the ones my rider carries, silver and all. Take my hand on them now."**

## What it costs

- **Earnest**, paid when you sign. It is reckoned man by man, and a veteran counts for more of it than a green hand, as his pay does.
- **Daily pay**, from the day they join you: for each man, what a soldier of his rank is paid **and somewhat more**, the same rate the taverns' sellswords ask. Under the [recruitment by the land](recruitment.md#wages-and-rising-in-rank), the pay of a soldier rises steeply with his rank, so a company heavy with veterans is dear.

A longer term costs more up front but less per day. The captain's terms tell you the exact figures before you sign.

The daily pay shows up as a line in your clan's daily finances: **"Pay of the Thornwall Company"**. The game takes it with the rest of your clan's day. When your gold runs low against the pay of all your companies, a captain warns you. **On a day you cannot pay, the company leaves.**

At the end, **blood money** is due for the dead (see below).

## The march

After you sign, the company **musters for some hours** in its town. Then it marches out as a real party on the map, with the company's name. The journal entry **"The Thornwall Company in your pay"** marks its column.

- It comes to wherever you are and keeps up with you if you move. Once it is close, or at the gate of the town you are in, it **falls in with your party**. If you meet the column on the road, you can tell the captain: **"Fall in behind my banner, captain."**
- It is a real party. Enemies can attack it on the way. If they cut it to pieces, the contract ends.
- **If you are inside a besieged town or castle**, the company will not march through the siege camp. It waits outside, out of the besiegers' reach, until you come out or the siege is lifted. If the place is a harbour and the company already has ships, it sails in to you where a lord of your side could.
- **Across the sea, or out at sea** (with War Sails): if there is open water between you and the company, or you are sailing, its captain sends word that they will take ship, and the harbour is marked on your map. The column marches to a harbour on its own shore, through the town or castle itself where a lord of your side could use it, or else to the coast beside it, and **hires real ships there**, of that harbour's people, as few as will carry its men. Then it goes by land and sea as a lord's party does, in plain sight on the map: it follows you at sea and joins you there, or comes ashore on your side, never on a coast held or besieged by an enemy or watched by an enemy army. **You pay the shipmasters**, as for the game's own ferry. At sea the column is a party like any other: storms and enemy fleets can catch it, and if it loses its ships it drifts ashore and looks for more. The ships are hired, not yours: they go back to their masters when the company joins you, and an enemy who sinks them takes no ships as loot. If you come back to their shore before they sail, they give up the passage and come by road.
- **The march has a deadline**, a good deal longer than the way to you should take. Time spent waiting outside your siege does not count. If you lead them on too long a chase, they give up and go home, and **half the earnest comes back** to you.
- If you are captured while they muster, you get the whole earnest back. If it happens while they march, you get half.

## In your service

Hired company men are with you, but they stay under their own captain:

- They **do not count against your party size**. The party size breakdown shows them as "Sellswords under their own captain". Your party is still slower for having more men, as in the base game.
- They **bring their own food**, so they do not eat from your stores.
- They bring **spare horses**, enough for a good share of their men. These help your foot soldiers ride, just like horses in your inventory would. You cannot sell or keep them. They never slow you down.
- They **never gain experience or upgrade**, and you **cannot move them** in the party screen. You also cannot leave them in a garrison.
- They **do not desert**, and they do not stop your own men from deserting either.
- **Your skills do not grow from them.** Their numbers add nothing to your Tactics, Leadership, Engineering, Scouting or Medicine experience.

Captains talk as the contract goes on. One speaks up after your first victory together, one warns you if you are **"spending us fast"**, and one reminds you a few days before the term ends.

## When it ends

- **Term served.** If you paid every day and did not lose too many of the men, the captain offers to **renew once**, in a window titled **"The Thornwall Company: the term is served"**: the same length again, a smaller earnest for the men left, and the same pay per man. Choose **"Pay the earnest and keep them"** or **"Let them go"**. If you give no answer within a day, they leave.
- **Released early.** Use **"Release the Thornwall Company from your service"** from the menu of any town, castle or village, and confirm with **"Release them"**. They keep the earnest, blood money is owed for the dead, and they will not take your coin again for a while.
- **Unpaid.** If you miss a day's pay, they leave that same day.
- **Heavy losses.** If more than half the men who joined you are dead, the captain ends the contract.
- **Blood money**: at the end, you pay a sum for each man who died in your service. If you cannot pay it all, the captain takes what you have. You owe none if the column was broken before it reached you, or if you were captured.
- If a contract ends while you are **inside a besieged town or castle**, they will not walk out through the enemy lines. They stay on your walls, still paid, until the way is open. This does not apply when you could not pay them.

**Rest and grudges.** After a contract with you, a company will not sign with you again for a while. The exception is a contract that ended through no fault of yours, such as your capture. If you left them **unpaid**, or **short of blood money**, they hold a grudge for longer.

## Their men are never lost

Prisoners are not dead. If a company's men are captured anywhere, the company buys them back within a day, and it pays their captor the ransom. If you take company men prisoner yourself, or free some from an enemy, their quartermaster buys them back from you on the spot.

## Between contracts

- A company **wins back men** in its town every day, faster while it is badly under strength. It **will not sign until it is back to most of its full size**.
- Every couple of months or so it moves to another town. It prefers towns of realms at war and towns near a siege. If its town comes under siege, it leaves at once. Moves are not shown on the map, but they take as long as the march would.

## AI lords hire them too

Now and then a free company is hired by a lord in need. Only clan leaders whose realm is at war and who are within a rider's reach can hire them, the most hard-pressed first (a besieged fief, a fief just lost, a realm much weaker than its enemy).

A lord only signs if, after the earnest, the whole term's pay and the blood money, they would still have a great deal of gold left. In practice this means rulers and well-off houses. The AI never holds more than a few companies at once. The claimants of a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses) may hire them too once they have declared it, by the same reckoning, but they always leave a few companies free for you. If the lord is of your realm or near you, you hear of it: *"Word on the road: ... has hired the Thornwall Company..."*. You may meet their column on the road or at sea, and if it is your enemy's, you can attack it.

## Turn it off
In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `CompanyHireEnabled`: nobody can hire the companies any more. A contract under way ends as usual.
- `CompanyAiHireEnabled`: AI lords no longer hire them. You still can.
- `CompanyCourierEnabled`: no riders. You must go to the captain yourself.
- `CompanyBloodMoneyEnabled`: no blood money for the dead.
- `CompanyRelocateEnabled`: companies stay in their town. They still leave it if it comes under siege.
- `CompanyRemounts`: no spare horses.
- `CompanySeaPassageEnabled`: companies never take ship. A company that could only reach you by sea is not offered, and one already marching waits on its shore while you are across the water.
