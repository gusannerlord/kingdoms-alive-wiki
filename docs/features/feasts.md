# Feasts and the grand tournament

In times of peace the lords of a kingdom gather under one roof. Houses grow closer or fall out, marriages are agreed, and you find the whole council in one hall, ready to be talked to. Every feast held in a town has a tournament in its arena, and a ruler's feast is the grand tournament of the realm. You can attend, and hold feasts of your own.

## When the AI holds a feast

- A kingdom must have been at peace with every other kingdom for a few days.
- Now and then, on a day of peace, one of its clans calls a feast. After a feast ends, that kingdom holds no other for some weeks.
- The host is most often the ruling clan. Otherwise it is one of the other clans, the greater houses more likely.
- The host pays for each clan invited, and only calls a feast with a good deal more than that in hand. They must be leading a party of their own, not be away with an army and not be on their way to your court.
- No feast is called while a [hunt](hunts.md) of the kingdom is announced or under way, nor for a few days after it ends. AI hunts are normally fitted into the waiting time between feasts, so they rarely hold a feast back.
- Nor while the realm's [assembly](assembly.md) is summoned or sitting, nor for a few days after it rises.
- **The assembly comes first.** After a peace, the realm owes its first assembly of that peace, and no feast is called until it has been held. An AI ruler summons it as soon as he can, so the feasts wait only a few days. If you rule, your lords will not feast until you have summoned it and it has sat, though not for ever. A realm that cannot hold it for a lasting reason (a ruler in chains, no hall of the crown's, a new war) does not keep its lords from feasting meanwhile.
- The feast is held in the host's richest town. If they hold no town, it is held in one of their castles.

If the feast is in your own kingdom, a message on the map tells you who calls it and where: *"{Host} bids the lords of the realm to a feast at {place}. The tables will be laid in ... days."* A feast in a town also names its tournament and the champion's purse (see [The tournament of a feast](#the-tournament-of-a-feast)).

## Notice, duration and who comes

- The tables are laid some days after the feast is called, and the feast lasts three evenings.
- Lords of the kingdom who lead a party ride there if they are within a few days' travel and would arrive before it ends. The host comes from any distance. A lord with something more urgent to do, such as a siege, still does that, and a lord you have called to your court (see [The royal court](royal-court.md)) goes there instead.
- When the tables are laid, a banner tells you if it is your own feast (*"Your feast at {place} has begun: join your guests in the hall."*) or a feast of your realm you are bidden to (*"The feast at {place} has begun: {Host} awaits the lords of the realm in the hall."*).
- **Clan leaders without a party** who are idle in a town or castle also travel there, if they are near enough to arrive with an evening left. Governors stay at their posts. Once at the feast, these lords stay until it is over.
- Mercenary clans are not invited.

## What happens at the tables

Every evening:

- The host grows a little closer to each clan sitting at the table.
- Two of the houses present (never yours) are thrown together. Usually they part a little closer; sometimes they quarrel instead and part worse. If you are in the hall you see it: *"Hard words pass between … over the wine, and the hall goes quiet."*
- Now and then two unmarried lords of different clans in the hall agree a match and marry. The couple is picked by the game's own marriage odds. Nobody from your clan is ever married off this way.

Apart from your own feasts (see below), a feast never raises a relation past warm regard.

What brings the lords together at a feast is the feast itself: while a feast or an [assembly](assembly.md) is announced or held in a town or castle, the game's **Good Lodging** and **Parade** perks bring no relation between two lords there.

## War breaks it up

- If the kingdom goes to war with another kingdom, the feast ends at once: *"War has come: the feast at {place} breaks up and the lords ride back to their men."*
- A siege of the venue also ends it, and so does the host losing the venue or leaving the kingdom.
- If it is called off before the tables are laid, the host gets their money back and the feast does not count towards the wait for the next one.

## Attending as a guest

When a feast is called or being held in a town or castle you are in, the menu shows **Join the feast**. It takes you to the feast's menu, which tells you who is hosting, how many days are left, which houses have arrived so far and what the tournament offers.

![The feast menu in the hall](../assets/feast-hall.jpg)

- **Stay at the feast** lets you wait in the settlement, with the menu kept up to date as the guests arrive. The wait stops by itself, with time stopped, when something opens for you: when the tables are laid (back to the feast), when the lists of its tournament open (back to the town, where the arena is; in a town both come in the same hour) and when the feast ends or breaks up. The game's own **Wait here for some time** in the venue also stops once, when the feast begins. **Leave the hall** takes you back.
- You count as present on any evening you are in the settlement.
- The host thinks a little better of you for each evening you are there (if the host is there too). Stay for every evening and you also come a little closer to one other house you spent the last evening with (*"You spend the evening in the company of the {clan}."*, also noted in the quest log).
- When the feast is over, its quest log entry names the houses you came closer to (*"You ride away on warmer terms with the {clans} than you came."*). At your own feast it tells you how many.
- **Feasts of other kingdoms:** you can join if your faction is not at war with them and the host does not dislike you. Otherwise the option is greyed out: *"{Host} keeps this table for friends, and does not count you among them."*
- **Easier persuasion:** at a feast of your own kingdom, the clan leaders in the hall are **one step easier** to win over about a war, a peace or a fief (see [Winning the council over](council.md)). Lords at the same feast are also easier to draw into a plot (see [Civil war](civil-war.md)). A [hunt](hunts.md) gives the same help, but never on top of a feast.

## A word over the wine

Once the tables are laid, the town or castle menu (and the feast's) shows **Go to the lord's hall: the feast of {host}**, with how many houses sit at the tables and who they are. It takes you into the game's own hall, where the guests are. If the hall is closed to you, the option is greyed out with the game's own reason.

Speak to any lord of another house in the hall and choose *"A word, over the wine... (Feast)"*. He greets you by how he regards you and by his nature, and you can say:

- *"To your house, and to its health!"*: a toast to the head of a house of the feast's realm, **once a feast** for each. It warms him to you a little, never past warm regard. A lord who dislikes you drinks, but not with you, and nothing comes of it.
- *"What is being said at the tables tonight?"*: what the hall really talks of: two heads of house at the tables who can hardly bear each other, or two thick as thieves, and whether he himself is unhappy to see a house there. A lord who dislikes you tells you nothing; a quiet table has nothing worth repeating.
- *"Is there a match to be made between our houses?"*: only to the head of a house, and only when both houses have someone unwed. He tells you who of his house would suit whom of yours, by the game's own rules for a marriage, or that nobody would. If there is a match, *"Then let us speak of it in earnest."* takes you into the game's own marriage talk. A lord who dislikes you will not hear of it.
- *"There is a matter of war and peace I would raise with you."*: the [council's persuasion](council.md), with the feast's easier step: only with the head of a house of your own realm, and not while you serve as a mercenary. Landed knights have no vote, so it is not raised with them, nor by you if you are one.

## Holding your own feast

In a town or castle that belongs to your clan, the menu shows **Hold a feast here**. You need:

- to be in a kingdom (as a vassal or as ruler, not as a mercenary);
- your kingdom at peace with every other kingdom;
- no other feast of your kingdom already called, and no hunt of your kingdom announced or under way, nor one that ended only days ago;
- no [assembly](assembly.md) of your kingdom summoned or sitting, nor one that rose only days ago; and, after a peace, the realm's first assembly of that peace already held (see [When the AI holds a feast](#when-the-ai-holds-a-feast));
- some days since the last feast in your kingdom, whoever held it, and some weeks since your own last feast;
- enough denars for at least the modest table.

If something is missing, the option is greyed out and the tooltip says why.

You choose one of three tables: **a modest table**, **a generous table** or **a lavish table**. The cost is paid for **each other clan of your kingdom** (mercenaries aside), whether or not it comes, and the window shows it before you choose. The richer the table:

- the more the guests warm to you over the feast;
- the higher it can raise a relation: a modest table only to fair terms, a lavish one to real friendship;
- the more influence you gain for each clan at the tables.

- Your feast is **marked on your map** from the moment you call it until it ends or breaks up.
- The relation goes to each clan that sits through every evening, and **only if you preside in person** on those evenings.
- When the feast ends you gain influence for every clan present on every evening you were there.
- **A host who is away gains nothing:** *"A feast without its host is soon forgotten: yours has won you nothing."*
- If war breaks out before the tables are laid, you get your denars back, the champion's purse too.
- In a town, a second window asks for the champion's purse (see [Your own feast's tournament](#your-own-feasts-tournament)).

!!! tip
    Only a lavish table raises relations to true friendship. Use the modest one when you just want the council in one room for persuasion.

## The tournament of a feast

Every feast held in a town, in every realm, has a tournament in the town's arena, the AI's and yours alike. A castle has no arena, so a feast in a castle has only its tables. If the town already had a tournament of its own, it gives way to the feast's: there are never two.

- The lists open when the tables are laid, with the lords who came to the feast on them, and stay open as long as the feast. You enter at the arena as you would any tournament.
- If you do not fight it out, the lords settle it among themselves when the feast ends, or when it breaks up for any reason but a siege. A siege of the town takes the tournament down unfought.
- **The champion's purse.** The host puts up a purse for the champion when he calls the feast, besides the prize. An AI host puts up what he can spare, and may put up none: then the champion fights for the prize alone. The purse never stops a feast. If nobody wins it, it goes back to the host.

### A lord's tournament

The feast of any host but the ruler has a tournament with the game's usual prize, and a modest purse.

### The grand tournament of the realm

A ruler's feast in a town is always the **grand tournament of the realm**, in every kingdom. Its champion takes:

- **a prize of the finest gear** of the kingdom's own culture: a weapon, a horse or a piece of armour;
- **a rich purse**, paid to the head of the champion's house, far greater than a lord's;
- **great renown, and influence** (any other tournament gives a little renown and no influence).

### Your own feast's tournament

When you hold a feast in one of your towns, a second window asks **What purse will you put up for the champion?**: none, or a quarter, a half, three quarters or all of the full purse (the grand tournament's if you rule the realm, a lord's otherwise); those you cannot pay on top of the table are greyed out. You pay it with the table. It goes to whoever wins (you, if you do), and back to you if nobody does.

### The prize of a champion away from you

When the champion is a lord of the AI, or one of your house who is not riding with you, the game pays his house the value of the prize in gold instead of the piece itself. Here that payment is capped, so a lucky lord does not grow rich on one tournament; if it is one of yours, you are told what the cap took off.

## The lord's hall {#the-lords-hall}

In the base game, the lords in a settlement wander the whole keep, up the stairs and into the bedrooms, and those with no place left all pile up at the door. With this mod, in any visit to a lord's hall:

- **The lords keep to the great hall.** Nobody is sent upstairs; whoever has no seat of his own takes a free chair or stands with others in talk. A ruler and consort who are both in the town are both in the hall.
- **The hall lets in as many lords as it can hold**, which is many more than before. When it is truly full, the lords who come in are picked each time you enter; your own clan, the feast's host, a lord you asked to speak to from the menu, a lord waiting for you at a meeting and lords needed by a quest always come in. The others are still in the settlement, and you can talk to them from the menu as usual.

## The feast in the hall {#the-feast-in-the-hall}

While a feast is held, the hall is full of it:

- **Every guest has a place.** The host takes the high seat when the ruler is not there. The seats nearest it go to the houses of most standing and friendship with the host, and yours is kept for you by the same rule: what you see is where you stand. Lords who hate each other are seated apart; the rest stand in groups of friends.
- **Families.** The guests' wives, husbands and grown kin who ride with them come into the hall too (the base game shows only the lord who leads each party).
- **Musicians** of the host's people play the music of their halls, with a dancer at the grandest feasts, and some guests gather round to listen and applaud.
- **Servants.** Serving women go from guest to guest with their jugs.
- **Laid tables.** The hall's great tables are laid with the food and cups of the host's people, richer at a grander feast, with candles at night.
- **Retinues.** A man-at-arms stands behind the host and behind the ruler.
- **The sound of the feast**: the murmur of a full hall.
- **Moments.** The host rises to welcome you when you come in of an evening, unless he hates you; the lords near you cheer your [toast](#a-word-over-the-wine); and what happened at the table that evening shows in the hall: two lords who quarrelled are seen arguing, a newly wed pair stand together with their kin.

## Turn it off

In the settings file (see [Settings](../settings.md)):

- `FeastsEnabled`: `false` turns off all feasts, for the AI and for you, and their tournaments with them.
- `HallOrderEnabled`: `false` lets the lords wander the keep as in the base game; the life of a feast in the hall needs it on.
- `FeastSeatingEnabled`, `FeastFamiliesEnabled`, `FeastMusiciansEnabled`, `FeastServantsEnabled`, `FeastTablesEnabled`, `FeastAmbienceEnabled`, `FeastRetinueEnabled`, `FeastMomentsEnabled`: `false` removes that part of the feast in the hall.
- `GrandTournamentEnabled`: `false` keeps the feasts but removes the grand tournament: a ruler's feast then has a lord's tournament, or none if `FeastTournamentsEnabled` is off too.
- `FeastTournamentsEnabled`: `false` removes the tournament from the feasts of everyone but the ruler.
- `FeastHallTalkEnabled`: `false` removes the way into the hall and the words over the wine.
- `HallCrowdLimitEnabled`: `false` lets every lord into the hall again, as in the base game.
- `GatheringLodgingQuietEnabled`: `false` lets the game's lodging perks bring relation between lords at feasts and assemblies again.
