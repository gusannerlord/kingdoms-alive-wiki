# The King's Writ

Ride as a man-hunter with a ruler's seal: no soldier, no vassal, only the law on their roads. Every outlaw band you break, every den you burn and every wanted chief you drag to trial in the king's lands is paid by the crown, and only there. It is a life of its own between the mercenary and the lord, and it is best in peacetime.

## Who can carry a writ

- Your clan must be **free**: in no kingdom, neither a vassal nor a mercenary, and you must not be serving a lord as their [squire](squire.md). A king of your own cannot carry another king's writ.
- **One writ at a time**, from one realm.
- The ruler must know your name: your clan needs **50 renown** (clan tier 1), or you must already have cleared an outlaw den in their lands, with or without a writ (*"I hear you have been clearing my roads"* is the spirit of it).
- Not while you are at war with that realm, and not while its guards want you for a moderate or severe crime: *"Clear your name with my law first; then ask me to make you its hunter."*
- A writ you lost by your own fault (see [When the writ ends](#when-the-writ-ends)): no new one from that ruler for **120 days**.

## Asking for it

Speak to the ruler, or to a lord of the ruler's own house, and choose *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*, then:

*"Your roads are thick with outlaws. Give me your writ, and I will hunt them in your name, for the price on their heads."*

If you qualify, they agree in their own voice, honourable, cruel, generous or cunning. If not, they tell you why. A journal entry opens, **"The King's Writ of {kingdom}"**, with your title and what the crown owes you. You start as **King's Hunter**.

### Warden of the King's Peace

The writ carries a seat at court. Every kingdom now has a fifth office in its [court](royal-court.md), **Warden of the King's Peace**, the keeper of the roads. When no hunter holds it, the ruler gives it to a noble like the other offices (skill: Scouting and Leadership). It adds a little security every day to every town and castle of the realm, according to the holder's skill.

When the ruler gives you the writ, the seat is yours, and it shows in the Court tab. A noble who held it before gives it up *"and not gladly"*: they think worse of you (**−8**). When the writ ends, the seat falls vacant and the ruler fills it within a few days.

## Paid only in the king's lands

Nothing needs a quest. The crown pays for outlaws beaten **in its own lands**: the nearest village, castle or town to the fight must belong to the realm (any of its houses), and not be too far off, about the distance between two neighbouring towns. Out of those lands, a fight that would have paid tells you so: *"These are the lands of {other kingdom}. Nobody here will pay you for this: {kingdom}'s writ ends at {kingdom}'s borders."*

| What | What the crown owes |
|---|---|
| An outlaw band of **12 men or more** at the start of the fight | per man: 3 for looters, 9 for outlaws of the hills, woods and steppe, 12 for sea raiders (and for any band beaten at sea, with War Sails), 14 for deserters. At most **800 a day** from bands. Their surrender counts. |
| An outlaw den cleared (also with "Send troops") | 800, plus 25 per defender, at most **2,000**. The same den pays again only after **30 days**. |
| One of the game's own outlaw quests done for someone in the king's lands (a bandit base nearby, outlaws on a merchant's road, deserters squeezing a village, poachers, smugglers, a caravan ambush) | **500** on top of the quest's own reward |
| Outlaws in your chains handed to the sheriff | the game's own ransom, as a broker pays, plus the same again from the crown (that extra at most **500 a day**) |

Your rank raises everything but the prisoners (see [Ranks](#ranks)).

### The king's sheriff

You collect at the sheriff of **any town of the realm**: in the town menu, **"Call on the king's sheriff"**. When you ride into one of the realm's towns with money owed, you are told: *"The sheriff of {town} has the crown's purse ready."* At the sheriff's stone house by the gaol you can:

- **"Collect what the crown owes you"**;
- **"Hand over the outlaws in your chains"** (they go down to the town's cells; you also learn some Roguery, as when selling prisoners);
- see the **wanted** chiefs on the wall, with where they were last seen and their price;
- bring a captured chief before the king's justice, or show the ring of a dead one;
- pay an informer, have your prisoners questioned (see [Wanted](#wanted-the-chiefs-with-a-name));
- **"Ask whether the crown has work for a hunter"**: the next contract comes sooner, or the sheriff tells you there is none to be had near you;
- **"Hire manhunters from the sheriff"** and, from the second rank, ask for a patrol;
- as Master of the King's Hunt, **"Send word to your captains"**.

## A hunting company, not a host

The writ is for a hunt, not a war, and the ruler says so: *"Keep your company to a score and ten riders: I am sealing a hunt, not a war."*

| Rank | Company the writ covers |
|---|---|
| King's Hunter | 30 men |
| Hunter of the March | 50 |
| High Justice of the Roads | 70 |
| Master of the King's Hunt | 90 |

Everyone in your party counts, heroes and wounded included, but not prisoners.

- **Too large:** the sheriff pays nothing for bands, dens or the crown's extra on prisoners, and they do not count towards your rank: *"The King's writ names you a hunter of outlaws, not a lord with a host."* The king's contracts and the wanted chiefs are still paid. Each day over the limit adds up (each day under it takes one away). After **30 days** in all, a letter from the ruler: *"My lords tell me you ride my roads with half an army. That is not what I set my seal to."* After **30 more**, the writ is lost. The Master of the King's Hunt only loses the pay.
- **Sworn writ-men:** full pay only if at least **half your soldiers** are manhunters (the troop tree below). With fewer, the crown pays your sworn men's share, never less than 30%: *"Half your company are hired swords and levies, not sworn writ-men."* The other half can be your own archers and crossbowmen.

Manhunters fight to take men alive: their maces knock men down instead of killing them, also when a fight is resolved by the game without you on the field. You end with more prisoners, and a wanted chief is taken alive more often.

## The manhunters

The writ's own troops, as in Warband: hard riders and gaolers with maces, clubs and shields. **No bows, no crossbows, no throwing weapons.** Two branches grow from the Manhunter:

| Tier | Mounted (the Warband line) | On foot |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manhunter | |
| 2 | Slave Driver | Slave Guard |
| 3 | Slave Hunter | Slave Keeper |
| 4 | Slave Crusher (needs a war horse) | Slave Warden |
| 5 | Slaver Chief (needs a war horse) | Slave Master |

- **Hire them from the sheriff**: 5 men a week for the whole realm as King's Hunter, 10 as Hunter of the March, 15 as High Justice, at the price of a mounted hireling. Never beyond the size your writ covers.
- **Free them from the outlaws**: some outlaw bands near you hold manhunters captive (Manhunters, now and then Slave Drivers). Beat the band and free them in the prisoner screen after the battle.
- Their wages are those of mercenaries.

## The king's contracts

About **6 days** after you get the writ, and then every **12 to 20 days**, a rider of the crown brings a contract: **"Take the contract"** or **"Not this one"**. One at a time. Refusing or failing costs you nothing but the pay.

| Contract | Time | The crown owes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Unsworn's den**: a hideout held by the Unsworn, marked on your map. "Send troops" works too. | 30 days | 3,500 |
| **Three bands of the Unsworn** riding the realm's villages | 40 days | 2,000 |
| **Deserters on the roads**: only within 40 days of a peace of the realm, three bands of its own deserted soldiers | 40 days | 2,200 |
| **A prisoner for the king's justice**: at the sheriff of one town, **"Take {name} in charge for the road to {town}"**, then bring him to trial there. *His Friends* ride to free him on the way. | 15 days | 2,000 |

Rank raises the gold. A contract done also gives **+2** with the ruler and **2 renown**. These two Unsworn tasks were the mercenary's: now they belong to the writ (see [Mercenary contracts](mercenary-contracts.md)).

## Wanted: the chiefs with a name

While you carry the writ, **two or three** outlaw chiefs with names and nicknames of their own (*"the Widower"*) ride the realm at once, each with his band. Each has his entry in your journal, **"Wanted: {name}"**, and his poster on the sheriff's wall.

- **Their price** starts at **1,500** and rises by 150 every 5 days, up to **4,000**. Their band grows too.
- About every **12 days** a chief falls on a village of the realm: it loses hearths, and you hear of it.
- The lords of the map leave them alone: they are yours.

**Finding him.** Without a lead you only know the last village he was seen near (marked on your map). A **lead** shows his band on the map for **5 days**:

- at a village he raided, **"Ask the villagers which way {name}'s band rode"** (for some days after the raid);
- at the sheriff, **"Pay one of the sheriff's informers for word of {name}"** (300 denars);
- at the sheriff, with at least 3 outlaws in your chains, **"Have the outlaws in your chains questioned"** (once a day, a good chance).

A chief's band will not talk: you can only fight it or leave it be.

**Alive or dead.** When you beat his band:

- **Taken alive** (wounded, or his band yielded): he goes into your chains and cannot be sold, ransomed or recruited. Bring him to trial.
- **Dead**: you keep his signet ring. At the sheriff, **"Lay {name}'s signet ring on the sheriff's table"** pays half his price.
- **Fled**: if he was still standing when his band broke, he may get away, and he is back with new men a few days later.

### The trial

At the sheriff of any town of the realm, choose **"Bring {name} before the king's justice"**. You are taken to the **lord's hall**, where the King's Justiciar sits with the accused (and the ruler, if they are there: *"under the eyes of {ruler} herself/himself"*). Speak with the justiciar. The writ gives you the first word:

| Your word | What follows |
|---|---|
| *"Let him swear never to lift a hand on the king's roads again, and go."* | Half the price. Mercy rises. A merciful or generous ruler likes it (**+3**), a cruel one does not (**−3**). |
| *"The dungeon, for as long as the king pleases."* | The full price. He goes to that town's dungeon. +1 with the ruler. |
| *"The rope. Let every outlaw in the realm hear how he ended."* | The full price. Mercy falls, your fame rises. A cruel ruler likes it (+3). |

If the hall cannot be used, the same choice comes in a window, **"The king's justice"**.

As **High Justice of the Roads**, when you take a chief alive a letter offers **"The right of the rope"**: hang him there and then, for his full price, or keep him for trial.

## Fame among the outlaws

Each realm's outlaws learn your name: every band broken adds a little, every den more, a wanted chief a lot, a hanging too. It fades slowly when you stop. As it grows, in this order:

1. **Small bands** (fewer than 25 men) in the king's lands dare not come at you.
2. Talking to a weaker band, a new line: *"I ride under {ruler}'s writ, and you know my name. Lay down your arms in the king's name, and you will live to see a court."* The more fame, the likelier they yield: *"...We know who you are."* Otherwise: *"Writ or no writ, a rope is a rope."*
3. **Bribes.** A boy presses a wanted chief's purse into your hand: **"An outlaw's purse"**. **"Keep the purse"** gives you the gold and costs you Honor, but for the next **60 days** the sheriffs may hear of it, and claiming that chief's price later is a great risk. Found out, the writ is torn. **"Throw it in the mud"** gives you Honor and fame.
4. **A price on your head.** The outlaws pool their coin, and a band of **Hired Blades** rides for you: **"A price on your head"**, followed on your map for 12 days. Beat them and their captain's purse is yours. It can happen again every month or so.

## Ranks

Dens, bands, chiefs and contracts earn the writ's trust. The ruler writes to you at each step:

| Rank | Pay | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| **King's Hunter** | normal | the writ, a company of 30 |
| **Hunter of the March** | +15% | a company of 50, a patrol of the garrison, more manhunters to hire |
| **High Justice of the Roads** | +30% | a company of 70, the right of the rope, more manhunters to hire |

### A patrol of the garrison

From **Hunter of the March**, at the sheriff: **"Ask the garrison of {town} for a patrol to ride with you"**. **20 men** of the town's garrison and their sergeant, *"{town} Watch"*, ride with you for **10 days** as a party of their own, with an arrow on the map (journal: **"The garrison's men"**). Then they go home to their walls. Once every **20 days**, and not from a thin garrison.

- They join your fights against **outlaws only**, never against lords.
- They wait on the road when you go into a village, an enemy place or to sea.
- When you clear a den with the watch close by, they have surrounded it: some of those who try to slip away end in your chains.
- Their losses are theirs. Your own men are never touched.

## The Outlaw King

After **4 wanted chiefs** taken or killed in the same realm, its outlaws crown a king of their own: **"The Outlaw King"**, in a great den marked on your map, with the best men left to them. Bring him down for **8,000** (raised by your rank), **25 renown**, **+10** with the ruler, **+3** with every head of a house of the realm, a horse fit for a lord and much of the writ's trust. Once per realm.

## Nobles freed from the outlaws

Outlaws sometimes carry lords as captives. When you, your watch or your captains beat such a band, the lord goes free: *"Tell the king's hunter that my house does not forget."* Freed by your watch or captains, they and their house think better of you (**+8**, unless their realm is at war with you; freed in your own fight, the game gives its own). And they [remember it for ever](memories.md): *"There is the hunter who freed me from the outlaws. You are always welcome at my fire."* Even an enemy lord says so: *"We are not friends, you and I, not now. But I have not forgotten who cut my ropes."*

## Master of the King's Hunt

The end of the road, and a long one. As **High Justice**, after holding the writ for **240 days** with **6 contracts**, **4 wanted chiefs** and **8 dens** behind you, and if the ruler's house holds at least 3 towns and castles with a castle to spare, a herald finds you: **"Summoned by {ruler}"**, with the ruler marked on the map. Go and say *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*, then *"You sent for me"*.

The ruler offers you a castle of their house, their hand as your liege and the title **Master of the King's Hunt**:

- *"I kneel, and I swear. My sword and my roads are yours."*: you become their vassal (as with the game's own oath), lord of the castle, and you keep the writ. Your company may be 90 strong, and a larger host only costs you the writ's pay.
- *"You honour me. But a hunter rides best with no lord but the law."*: you stay free with the writ, and the offer comes again in **60 days**.

If there is no castle to spare when you arrive, the ruler says so and sends for you again when there is.

### Captains of the King's Hunt

As Master, you may name **up to 3 captains**: companions or kin who already lead a party of your clan (made in the clan screen, as usual), with at least half manhunters. Talk to them: *"I would have you ride as one of my captains of the King's Hunt."* Release one with *"I release you from your captaincy."*

- Each keeps the march around the nearest town of the realm, falls on the outlaw bands there that they can beat, and now and then clears a den of that march. Each has a journal entry, **"Captain of the King's Hunt: {name}"**, following their party.
- The crown owes you **half** of what it would pay you for their work. A wanted chief they take goes to the nearest dungeon, for his full price (half, with his ring).
- When their company is worn down, they rest a few days in a town, heal and refill with manhunters, paid from your purse (you are told how much).
- At the sheriff, **"Send word to your captains"**: hunt a wanted chief, see to the crown's contract, or go back to the march.

## When the writ ends

| How | What follows |
|---|---|
| You give it back: *"I have come to give you back your writ. I will hunt no more in your name."* | Closed with thanks. |
| You become a vassal or mercenary of any realm, a squire, or the ruler of a realm of your own | Closed with thanks, in the ruler's voice. |
| The realm is destroyed | Closed. |
| You leave the realm as Master of the King's Hunt | Closed; your captains ride home. |
| You go to war with that realm | **Lost.** |
| A bribe is found out | **Lost.** |
| Too large a company for too long | **Lost.** |

**Closed:** a rider of the sheriff brings you everything still owed, and every wanted chief still in your chains is paid and goes to a dungeon. **Lost:** what is owed is forfeit, **−5** with the ruler, and no writ from them for 120 days. Either way the contract, the patrol and the summons end with it.

## Turn it off
In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `WritEnabled`: the whole feature. No writ can be asked for, and one you carry is laid aside quietly.
- `WritRewardsEnabled`: no pay for bands, dens and prisoners without a contract.
- `WritNativeQuestBonusEnabled`: no extra for the game's own outlaw quests.
- `WritOutsideNoticeEnabled`: no word when a fight was out of the king's lands.
- `WritContractsEnabled`: no contracts. The Unsworn's den and bands go back to the mercenary's tasks. (With `UnswornTroopsEnabled` off, those two contracts are never offered.)
- `WritWantedEnabled`: no wanted chiefs.
- `WritTrialEnabled`: no trial in the hall; the verdict comes in a window.
- `WritFameEnabled`: no fame among the outlaws. `WritAssassinsEnabled`: no price on your head. `WritBribesEnabled`: no bribes.
- `WritCompanyRulesEnabled`: no limit on your company and no need of sworn writ-men.
- `WritRanksEnabled`: no ranks.
- `WritPatrolEnabled`: no patrol of the garrison.
- `WritTroopsEnabled`: no manhunters to hire or free.
- `WritOutlawKingEnabled`: no Outlaw King.
- `WritVassalOfferEnabled`: no summons and no Master of the King's Hunt.
- `WritCaptainsEnabled`: no captains.
