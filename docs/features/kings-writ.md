# The King's Writ

Ride as a man-hunter with a ruler's seal: no soldier, no vassal, only the law on their roads. Every outlaw band you break, every den you burn and every wanted chief you drag to trial in the king's lands is paid by the crown, and only there. It is a life of its own between the mercenary and the lord, best early in a campaign and in peacetime.

## Who can carry a writ

- Your clan must be **free**: in no kingdom, neither a vassal nor a mercenary, and you must not be serving a lord as their [squire](squire.md). A king of your own cannot carry another king's writ.
- **One writ at a time**, from one realm.
- The ruler must know your name: your clan needs **50 renown** (clan tier 1), or you must already have cleared an outlaw den in their lands, with or without a writ.
- Not while you are at war with that realm, and not while its guards want you for a moderate or severe crime: *"Clear your name with my law first; then ask me to make you its hunter."*
- A writ you lost by your own fault (see [When the writ ends](#when-the-writ-ends)): no new one from that ruler for **120 days**.

## Asking for it

Speak to the ruler, the ruler's heir, or the noble who keeps the king's peace at court (the Warden, below), choose *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*, then:

*"The roads of your realm are thick with outlaws. I would hunt them under the king's writ, for the price on their heads."*

If you qualify, they agree in their own voice, honourable, cruel, generous or cunning. If not, they tell you why. Any other lord of the realm tells you whom to ask. A journal entry opens, **"The King's Writ of {kingdom}"**, with your title and what the crown owes you. You start as **King's Hunter**.

With the writ come **15 manhunters** from the sheriff's yard (once a campaign). Those who do not fit in your party wait in the yard until you send for them at any sheriff of the realm.

### Warden of the King's Peace

The writ carries a seat at court. Every kingdom has a fifth office in its [court](royal-court.md), **Warden of the King's Peace**, the keeper of the roads. When no hunter holds it, the ruler gives it to a noble like the other offices (skill: Scouting and Leadership). It adds a little security every day to every town and castle of the realm, according to the holder's skill.

When you get the writ, the seat is yours, and it shows in the Court tab. A noble who held it before gives it up *"and not gladly"*: they think worse of you (**−8**). When the writ ends, the seat falls vacant and the ruler fills it within a few days.

## Paid only in the king's lands

Nothing needs a quest. The crown pays for outlaws beaten **in its own lands**: the nearest village, castle or town to the fight must belong to the realm (any of its houses), and not be too far off, about the distance between two neighbouring towns. Out of those lands, a fight that would have paid tells you so: *"These are the lands of {other kingdom}. Nobody here will pay you for this: {kingdom}'s writ ends at {kingdom}'s borders."*

| What | What the crown owes |
|---|---|
| An outlaw band of **12 men or more** at the start of the fight | per man: 3 for looters, 9 for outlaws of the hills, woods and steppe, 12 for sea raiders (and for any band beaten at sea, with War Sails), 14 for deserters. At most **800 a day** from bands. Their surrender counts. |
| An outlaw den cleared (also with "Send troops") | 800, plus 25 per defender, at most **2,000**. The same den pays again only after **30 days**. |
| One of the game's own outlaw quests done for someone in the king's lands (a bandit base nearby, outlaws on a merchant's road, deserters squeezing a village, poachers, smugglers, a caravan ambush) | **500** on top of the quest's own reward |
| Outlaws in your chains handed to the sheriff (also deserters and Unsworn you took from outlaw bands) | the game's own ransom, as a broker pays, plus the same again from the crown (that extra at most **500 a day**) |

Your rank raises everything but the prisoners (see [Ranks](#ranks)).

## The king's sheriff

Every town of every realm has a **king's sheriff**, *"{name} the Sheriff"*, a notable of the town like its merchants and artisans. Find him among the town's people and talk to him. When you ride into one of the realm's towns with money owed, you are told: *"The sheriff of {town} has the crown's purse ready."* If a sheriff dies, the court sends another within a few days, who writes to you in his own voice; what the crown owes you is never lost.

Only the sheriffs of your writ's realm deal with you. Another crown's sheriff tells you your writ is *"only wax"* there. Without a writ, ask him *"How does one come by the king's writ, then?"*

**He counts your company first.** If any soldier with you is not a sworn manhunter, he says so, and the crown pays you nothing until they are gone (see [Sworn writ-men only](#sworn-writ-men-only)). You can answer *"Then I will leave the men who are not sworn to the writ with the town watch."*: they go up on the walls with the garrison, or, if the walls have no room, he pays them off and sends them home. A hired company must end its contract with its own captain first.

Then, in your own words:

- *"I have come for what the crown owes me."*
- *"Take the outlaws in my chains off my hands."* or *"Let me choose which of my prisoners go to your cells."*
- *"Who is on your wall these days?"*: the **wanted** chiefs, where they were last seen and their price.
- *"I have brought {name} in chains. Let the king's justice sit."*, or *"{name} is dead. Here is his signet ring."* (see [Wanted](#wanted-the-chiefs-with-a-name)).
- *"I will pay one of your informers for word of {name}."* and *"Have your men question the outlaws in my chains."*
- *"Has the crown any work for a hunter?"*: the next contract comes sooner, or he tells you why not (nothing near you, a wanted chief to bring in first, or your last contract was only just paid).
- *"I need men who know the work. Who is waiting in your yard?"*: hire manhunters (see [The manhunters](#the-manhunters)).
- *"Show me what the crown's armoury has on its racks."* and, in one town, the master-at-arms (see [The sheriffs' master-at-arms](#the-sheriffs-master-at-arms)).
- For a contract: take a prisoner in charge, or lead a raid; after a sentence, walk a chief to the gallows; with captains, *"I need riders sent to my captains of the hunt."*

The more you are paid at a sheriff's table, and the more chiefs and contracts you bring home to his town, the more he trusts you (up to a point), and the more men he lets you take from his yard.

## Sworn writ-men only

The writ is for a hunt, not a war. Your company may be as large as the game lets your party be, but **every soldier in it must be a sworn manhunter** (the troop tree below). You and your companions do not count; hired swords, levies, any other soldiers and the companies for hire do.

With even one such man in your company, the crown books nothing for your work (bands, dens, contracts, wanted chiefs, the extra on prisoners), and no sheriff pays out: *"The King's writ names you a hunter of outlaws, and you ride with men who never swore to it."* What was owed before waits in the sheriff's strongbox, and the prices of chiefs you brought down are kept for you. A line in the journal tells you each time your company stops or starts being a hunter's again. The Master of the King's Hunt rides with whom they please.

Manhunters fight to take men alive: their maces knock men down instead of killing them, also when a fight is resolved by the game without you on the field. You end with more prisoners, and a wanted chief is taken alive more often.

## The manhunters

The writ's own troops, as in Warband: hard riders, gaolers and crossbowmen with maces, clubs and small round shields. No blades. Three branches grow from the Manhunter:

| Tier | Mounted | On foot | Crossbow |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manhunter (on foot) | | |
| 2 | Slave Driver (needs a horse) | Slave Guard | Tracker |
| 3 | Slave Hunter | Slave Keeper | Hamstringer |
| 4 | Slave Crusher (needs a war horse) | Slave Warden | Runner-Stopper |
| 5 | Slaver Chief (needs a war horse) | Slave Master | Quarry Warden |

- **Mounted**: the Warband line, to run them down.
- **On foot**: the shock line, with a two-handed mace besides mace and shield, and the heavier armour (the riders carry a two-handed mace too).
- **Crossbow**: a light crossbow to drop a runner, a mace and a small round shield, in padding and leather, a step lighter than the foot line.

**Hiring them.** Only the holder of the writ can hire manhunters: no lord, no garrison. Men come to the realm's sheriffs' yards over time, about one every two days as King's Hunter, one a day as Hunter of the March, a little under one and a half as High Justice, up to 6, 12 or 18 waiting in all. Some arrive as tier 2 of one of the three branches (more of them at higher ranks). Talk to a sheriff of the realm, or use the town's own **Recruit** screen, where he stands with the other notables: how many you may take from him depends on how far he trusts you and on your rank, once a day. The price is the game's own for a hireling.

**Freeing them.** Some outlaw bands near you hold manhunters captive. Beat the band and free them in the prisoner screen after the battle.

**Wages**: the same as regular soldiers of their tier.

## The sheriffs' master-at-arms

The ruler will not have their hunter *"riding about dressed like a tinker"*. With the writ, a journal entry sends you to the town where the **Sheriffs' Master-at-Arms** keeps the crown's armoury. Tell the sheriff there *"I am told the sheriffs' master-at-arms has a kit put by for me."* and choose a kit, free:

- the rider's (with a horse), the footman's or the crossbowman's;
- the **first** kit is a tier-3 manhunter's, given with the writ;
- better ones are put by for you as your work for the crown adds up. A rider brings word when one is ready. If the armoury's town is lost or besieged, it is carted to another town and you are told where.

**The crown's armoury.** At any sheriff of the realm, *"Show me what the crown's armoury has on its racks."* opens a trade screen with every piece the manhunters wear, weapons and shields too, at the town's market price. A few of each piece are always there; the racks fill again every week.

## The writ in your journal

The writ's journal entry carries your rank in its title, *"The King's Writ of {REALM} (Hunter of the March)"*, and ends with the game's progress bars:

- **"Toward Hunter of the March"**, then **"Toward High Justice of the Roads"**: the next rank.
- The court's reckoning of the [king's summons](#master-of-the-kings-hunt): **"The king's trust"**, **"Days in the king's service"**, **"Contracts kept"**, **"Named chiefs brought in"** and **"Dens cleared"**. When all of them are full, you are High Justice and the king has a castle to spare, he sends for you.

Each time a piece of work counts, a line says so: *"Set down to your name at court: the den you cleared. (Toward Hunter of the March: 14/25)"*.

| Work | Counts |
|---|---|
| A band of 12 or more broken | 1 |
| A den cleared, or one of the game's outlaw errands done | 3 |
| A contract kept, or a rescue at the gallows beaten off or ridden down | 4 |
| A wanted chief brought in | 6 |
| The Outlaw King | 18 |

Bands, dens, contracts, gallows and errands go untold at court while men not sworn to the writ ride with you; a named chief is always told.

## The king's contracts

About **6 days** after you get the writ, and then every **12 to 20 days**, a rider of the crown brings a contract: **"Take the contract"** or **"Not this one"**. One at a time, and they take turns: never the same kind twice running, and the Unsworn's den comes up more often than the rest. Refusing or failing costs you nothing but the pay.

| Contract | Time | The crown owes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Unsworn's den**: a hideout held by the Unsworn, marked on your map. "Send troops" works too. | 30 days | 3,500 |
| **Three bands of the Unsworn** riding the realm's villages | 40 days | 2,000 |
| **Deserters on the roads**: only within 40 days of a peace of the realm, three bands of its own deserted soldiers | 40 days | 2,200 |
| **A prisoner for the king's justice**: at the sheriff of one town, take him in charge for the road to another, then bring him to trial there. *His Friends* ride to free him on the way. | 15 days | 2,000 |
| **A raid on {town}**: a gang hiding inside one of the realm's towns (below) | 20 days | 1,800, plus 12 for every man of the gang taken or killed |

Rank raises the gold. A contract done also gives **+2** with the ruler and **2 renown**. The two Unsworn tasks were the mercenary's: now they belong to the writ (see [Mercenary contracts](mercenary-contracts.md)).

One big task at a time: no new contract while a newly posted wanted chief is still at large, no new chief while a contract is under way, and a few days of rest between them.

### The raid

Go to the town, marked on your map, and tell the sheriff *"I am ready. Ring the bell: we go in after {chief}'s gang."* The best of your company go in with you: up to **10 sworn writ-men** and **3 companions**, with **4 to 6 men of the watch**. The rest wait at his door. Then **"Go in"**, or **"Not yet"**.

You fight in the town's own streets. The gang waits in **one or two knots** not far from the gate, and the townsfolk go about their business until steel comes out; then they run for their doors (at night the streets are near empty). Once a knot is stirred, some of its men run for the gate, and so does the chief when the fight goes against him: cut them off.

- The chief taken or killed and the gang broken: the full pay, plus the heads. The living are yours to hand over.
- You go down, but the chief is taken or dead: part of the pay.
- The chief gets out by the gate, you break off the raid, or you leave the streets: no pay, and no blame.

!!! note "How big the outlaws are"
    The writ's own bands and dens (the contracts, the wanted chiefs, the hired blades, the Outlaw King) are sized the way the game sizes the outlaws of its own quests: smaller early in a campaign, bigger as it goes on, whatever the size of your party. Expect bands of about 35 to 40 men early on, a chief's band up to about 55, and dens of about 35 to 45. The raid and the gallows are sized on the few who go into the streets with you.

## Wanted: the chiefs with a name

While you carry the writ, up to **three** outlaw chiefs with names and nicknames of their own (*"the Widower"*) ride the realm, each with his band and his trade: some burn villages, some tax them, some waylay the market carts, some drive off the herds. Each has his entry in your journal, **"Wanted: {name}"**, and his poster on the sheriff's wall.

- **Their price** starts at **1,500** and rises by 150 every 5 days, up to **4,000**. Their band grows too.
- About every **12 days** a chief falls on a village of the realm, and you hear of it.
- The lords of the map leave them alone: they are yours.

**Finding him.** Without a lead you only know the last village he was seen near (marked on your map). A **lead** shows his band on the map for **5 days**:

- at a village he struck, **"Ask the villagers which way {name}'s band rode"** (for some days after);
- at the sheriff, pay an informer (**300** denars);
- at the sheriff, with at least 3 outlaws in your chains, have them questioned (once a day, a good chance).

A chief's band will not talk: you can only fight it or leave it be.

**Alive or dead.** When you beat his band:

- **Taken alive** (wounded, or his band yielded): he goes into your chains and cannot be sold, ransomed or recruited. Bring him to trial.
- **Dead**: you keep his signet ring. Shown to a sheriff, it pays half his price.
- **Fled**: if he was still standing when his band broke, he may get away, and he is back with new men a few days later.

### The trial

At the sheriff of any town of the realm, say *"I have brought {name} in chains. Let the king's justice sit."* You are taken to the **lord's hall**, where the King's Justiciar sits with the accused (and the ruler, if they are there). Speak with the justiciar. The writ gives you the first word:

| Your word | What follows |
|---|---|
| *"Let him swear never to lift a hand on the king's roads again, and go."* | Half the price. Mercy rises. A merciful or generous ruler likes it (**+3**), a cruel one does not (**−3**). |
| *"The dungeon, for as long as the king pleases."* | The full price. He goes to that town's dungeon. +1 with the ruler. |
| *"The rope. Let every outlaw in the realm hear how he ended."* | The full price (if his gang comes for him, only once he hangs: see below). He hangs at dawn in the square. Mercy falls, your fame rises. A cruel ruler likes it (+3). |

If the hall cannot be used, the same choice comes in a window, **"The king's justice"**. The sheriff will not call the court while men not sworn to the writ ride with you.

As **High Justice of the Roads**, when you take a chief alive a window offers **"The right of the rope"**: **"Hang him here"**, for his full price, or **"He will stand trial"**.

### The gallows of {town}

Sentenced to the rope, a chief's gang may come for him: about **1 time in 3**, the sheriff hears that they are in the town and mean to cut him down on the way to the gallows. A journal entry, **"The gallows of {town}"**, gives the day.

- In the small hours before dawn, at the sheriff's: *"It is time. I will walk {name} to the gallows myself."* The best of your writ-men and companions walk the cart with you and the watch. The sheriff asks: **"Bring him out"**, or **"Not yet: put it off a day"** (twice at most; after that, **"Leave it to the watch"**).
- In the street the gang falls on the cart from the alleys, and some try to drag him off to the gate. Hold them, and he hangs: his price, plus 12 for every man of the gang taken or killed.
- If they get him out by the gate, a band rides for a den in the hills with him, marked on your map: **catch them on the road** and he hangs after all. If they reach the hills, he is free and his poster goes back up.
- Not there? The watch may hold them, or may not.

## Fame among the outlaws

Each realm's outlaws learn your name: every band broken adds a little, every den more, a wanted chief a lot, a hanging too. It fades slowly when you stop. As it grows, in this order:

1. **Small bands** (fewer than 25 men) in the king's lands dare not come at you.
2. Talking to a weaker band, a new line: *"I ride under {ruler}'s writ, and you know my name. Lay down your arms in the king's name, and you will live to see a court."* The more fame, the likelier they yield: *"...We know who you are."* Otherwise: *"Writ or no writ, a rope is a rope."*
3. **Bribes.** A boy presses a wanted chief's purse into your hand: **"An outlaw's purse"**. **"Keep the purse"** gives you the gold and costs you Honor, but for the next **60 days** the sheriffs may hear of it, and claiming that chief's price later is a great risk. Found out, the writ is torn. **"Throw it in the mud"** gives you Honor and fame.
4. **A price on your head.** The outlaws pool their coin, and a band of **Hired Blades** rides for you: **"A price on your head"**, followed on your map for 12 days. Beat them and their captain's purse is yours. It can happen again every month or so.

## Ranks

Dens, bands, chiefs and contracts earn the writ's trust (the bar in your journal). The ruler writes to you at each step:

| Rank | Pay | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| **King's Hunter** | normal | the writ |
| **Hunter of the March** (25) | +15% | more manhunters in the sheriffs' yards |
| **High Justice of the Roads** (70) | +30% | the right of the rope, **one captain** of the King's Hunt, more manhunters |
| **Master of the King's Hunt** | as High Justice | a castle and an oath (below), up to **three captains**, and nobody counts your men |

## The Outlaw King

After **4 wanted chiefs** taken or killed in the same realm, its outlaws crown a king of their own: **"The Outlaw King"**, in a great den marked on your map, with the best men left to them. Bring him down for **8,000** (raised by your rank), **25 renown**, **+10** with the ruler, **+3** with every head of a house of the realm, a horse fit for a lord and much of the writ's trust. Once per realm.

## Nobles freed from the outlaws

Outlaws sometimes carry lords as captives. When you or your captains beat such a band, the lord goes free: *"Tell the king's hunter that my house does not forget."* Freed by your captains, they and their house think better of you (**+8**, unless their realm is at war with you; freed in your own fight, the game gives its own). And they [remember it for ever](memories.md): *"There is the hunter who freed me from the outlaws. You are always welcome at my fire."* Even an enemy lord says so: *"We are not friends, you and I, not now. But I have not forgotten who cut my ropes."*

## Master of the King's Hunt

The end of the road, and a long one. As **High Justice**, with **"The king's trust"** full, after holding the writ for **120 days** with **6 contracts**, **4 wanted chiefs** and **8 dens** behind you (a den cleared for a crown contract counts among them), and if the ruler's house holds at least 3 towns and castles with a castle to spare, a herald finds you: **"Summoned by {ruler}"**, with the ruler marked on the map. Go and say *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*, then *"You sent for me"*.

The ruler offers you a castle of their house, their hand as your liege and the title **Master of the King's Hunt**:

- *"I kneel, and I swear. My sword and my roads are yours."*: you become their vassal (as with the game's own oath), lord of the castle, and you keep the writ. No sheriff counts your men any more.
- *"You honour me. But a hunter rides best with no lord but the law."*: you stay free with the writ, and the offer comes again in **60 days**.

If there is no castle to spare when you arrive, the ruler says so and sends for you again when there is.

### The hunters' castle

Your castle can raise three works of its own, built from its **Manage** screen like any other project, three levels each:

- **Hunters' Lodge**: now and then manhunters come to its garrison, more often as it grows.
- **Gaol Tower**: a larger dungeon, and the crown's cart takes the outlaws you leave there and books their price to your name.
- **Watchtower**: a safer castle, the outlaw bands about it marked on your map, and earlier word of any host coming for you.

They work while the castle is yours and you are Master of the King's Hunt.

### The outlaws' host

Every month or so, the outlaws of the realm gather a great host under a warlord to take back the roads from you. A rider of the ruler brings word some days ahead: **"A Host at the Gates of {castle}"** opens in your journal with your castle marked. You are always given time to ride home.

Inside the castle, choose **"Man the walls against the outlaws' host"** and wait for them. When the horns sound, **"To the yard!"**: they break in through the gate in waves, the warlord with the last, and you hold the courtyard with your company and half the garrison. Win and the crown pays well, the ruler is pleased and the warlord is yours, alive or dead. Lose and the castle bleeds, but it is never taken. If you are not there when they come, the garrison fights alone and the villages suffer; if you are a captive or riding in another lord's army, the host loses its nerve and scatters.

## Captains of the King's Hunt

From **High Justice of the Roads** you may name **one captain**, and as Master **up to three**. You are told when the right comes. A captain is a companion or kin who already leads a party of your clan (made in the clan screen, as usual), with at least half manhunters in it; your clan must be large enough to field another party. Talk to them: *"I would have you ride as one of my captains of the King's Hunt."* Release one with *"I release you from your captaincy."*

- Each keeps the march around the nearest town of the realm, falls on the outlaw bands there that they can beat, and now and then clears a den of that march. Each has a journal entry, **"Captain of the King's Hunt: {name}"**, following their party.
- The crown owes you about **half** of what it would pay you for their work, less if their company is not all sworn men. A wanted chief they take goes to the nearest dungeon, for his full price (half, with his ring).
- When their company is worn down, they rest a few days in a town, heal and refill with manhunters, paid from your purse (you are told how much). They take no other recruits: no village or town volunteers, none of their prisoners, and captives they free go home.
- A captain rides at the crown's den only with a company at least as strong as the outlaws in it. If the den holds against them, they keep the march for a few days to mend and then try again on their own.
- At the sheriff, *"I need riders sent to my captains of the hunt."*: hunt a wanted chief, see to the crown's contract, or go back to the march.

## When the writ ends

| How | What follows |
|---|---|
| You give it back: *"I have come to give you back your writ. I will hunt no more in your name."* | Closed with thanks. |
| You become a vassal or mercenary of any realm, a squire, or the ruler of a realm of your own | Closed with thanks, in the ruler's voice. |
| The realm is destroyed | Closed. |
| You leave the realm as Master of the King's Hunt | Closed; your captains ride home. |
| You go to war with that realm | **Lost.** |
| A bribe is found out | **Lost.** |

**Closed:** a rider of the sheriff brings you everything still owed, and every wanted chief still in your chains is paid and goes to a dungeon. **Lost:** what is owed is forfeit, **−5** with the ruler, and no writ from them for 120 days. Either way the contract and the summons end with it, and the men still waiting in the yard go looking for another captain.

## Turn it off
In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `WritEnabled`: the whole feature. No writ can be asked for, and one you carry is laid aside quietly.
- `WritRewardsEnabled`: no pay for bands, dens and prisoners without a contract.
- `WritNativeQuestBonusEnabled`: no extra for the game's own outlaw quests.
- `WritOutsideNoticeEnabled`: no word when a fight was out of the king's lands.
- `WritSheriffEnabled`: no sheriffs in the towns; a **"Sheriff's House"** in the town menu does their work instead.
- `WritContractsEnabled`: no contracts. The Unsworn's den and bands go back to the mercenary's tasks. (With `UnswornTroopsEnabled` off, those two contracts are never offered.)
- `WritCastleBuildingsEnabled`: no Hunters' Lodge, Gaol Tower or Watchtower. `WritHordeEnabled`: no outlaws' host against your castle.
- `WritRaidEnabled`: no raids in towns. `WritRaidTownsfolkEnabled`: no townsfolk in the raid's streets; the curfew bell rings and the streets are empty.
- `WritWantedEnabled`: no wanted chiefs.
- `WritTrialEnabled`: no trial in the hall; the verdict comes in a window.
- `WritGallowsEnabled`: no rescue at the gallows.
- `WritFameEnabled`: no fame among the outlaws. `WritAssassinsEnabled`: no price on your head. `WritBribesEnabled`: no bribes.
- `WritCompanyRulesEnabled`: soldiers who are not manhunters may ride with you.
- `WritBandVanillaScaling`: the writ's bands and dens are sized against the strength of your own company instead.
- `WritRanksEnabled`: no ranks. `WritRankBarEnabled`: no rank bar in the journal. `WritSummonsBarsEnabled`: no bars for the king's summons.
- `WritTroopsEnabled`: no manhunters to hire or free. `WritWageAsRegular`: manhunters cost the wages of hired swords.
- `WritGearGiftsEnabled`: no kits from the master-at-arms. `WritArmouryEnabled`: no crown's armoury (`WritArmouryWeapons`: armour only).
- `WritOutlawKingEnabled`: no Outlaw King.
- `WritVassalOfferEnabled`: no summons and no Master of the King's Hunt.
- `WritCaptainsEnabled`: no captains.
