# The Empire's own ways (very soon in 2.0.0)

The three empires do not live like the other kingdoms. As in Byzantium, the land, the commands and the justice belong to the emperor: lords do not make war on each other or band together to take a fief, they petition the throne. In return the empire has its own dangers: a general at the head of a theme may march on the palace, and each emperor claims the whole Empire from the other two.

## Which realms are imperial

A realm of the Empire is any kingdom of imperial culture: the Northern, Western and Southern Empires, and any new kingdom of that culture (yours too, if your house is imperial). Who sits on the throne does not change it. Two exceptions keep the rules of any other kingdom while they last: the host of a feud between houses, and the rebel side of a civil war still being fought.

## No reckonings, feuds or vassal votes on fiefs

In a realm of the Empire:

- **No Reckoning.** Lords never band together in a [faction](factions.md) to take a fief from one of their own.
- **No feuds.** No house takes up arms against another, for a fief or for anything else: "in {REALM} a lord who takes up arms against another is a rebel against the throne."
- **No vassal Annex.** Only the emperor may put a lord's fief before the council. The emperor's own Annex, the share-out of conquered fiefs, plots against the crown, the Charter, calls to abdicate, houses breaking with the realm and the judgment of traitors all work as in any kingdom.

These rules have no switch: they are simply how the Empire works. If your save from an older version had any of them under way in an empire, they end within the hour, with a message if they concern you. A feud already being fought ends with nobody winning, and everything taken in it is given back.

## Petitions to the emperor

In the Empire, a right is not pressed by the sword or by the council: it goes to the throne. A house with any [right to a fief](fief-rights.md) held by another house of the same realm (a lost fief, a forged claim, an old claim of its line, a claim by marriage) can lay it before the emperor.

**How you petition** (only as head of your house, and not as the emperor yourself):

- **In person**, talking to the emperor: "I would lay a petition before the throne: a fief held against my house's better right." Then pick the fief. The line only shows when you have a petition the throne can hear.
- **By an envoy**: "Carry a petition to the throne: a right to a fief" (see [Envoys](envoys.md)).
- **From the kingdom screen**: in the Factions tab, under your house's rights, the button **"Petition the throne"** next to the right.

The envoy errand and the button are always visible, greyed out with the reason when the throne cannot hear it now. It cannot be heard against the emperor's own house, during a civil war, while the fief is under siege or waiting for the council's grant, if your house already has a petition before the throne, or if the throne refused this one less than 60 days ago. A foreign war does not stop it.

**The hearing.** The emperor's judges hear both houses for **5 days**. Your petition gets a journal entry, "A petition for {FIEF}", and then the answer comes:

- **Granted**: the fief passes to the petitioner by the throne's decision. The house that lost it keeps no right to it, and holds it against the crown (a grievance as heavy as a fief taken). A house wronged like this may plot or break with the realm.
- **Refused**: the petitioner holds it against the crown (a grievance like a claim denied) and cannot bring the same petition again for **60 days**.
- **It comes to nothing** if things changed before the answer: the fief changed hands, a house left the realm or took the crown, the right was lost, or civil war broke out.

**How an AI emperor decides.** A firm right counts for more than a weak one. The emperor favours the house whose head they like better, and their own kin. An honourable emperor respects a firm right and turns away a weak one; a calculating one favours the stronger house. With no strong feelings either way, a firm right is granted about half the time and a weak one about 3 times in 10. Nothing is ever certain: the chance stays between 5% and 90%.

**As emperor**, every petition comes to you in a window, "A petition before the throne", with the right and where it comes from. You choose: "Grant it: {FIEF} goes to the {CLAIMANT}" or "Refuse it: the {HOLDER} keep {FIEF}". The tooltip of each says what it will cost you.

AI houses bring about 6 petitions in each realm of the Empire over 900 days.

## Pronoia

In the Empire only the throne swears [landed knights](landed-knights.md). It grants them a **pronoia**: the rents of a village, given in return for service.

- The crown may grant the pronoia of a village of **any castle of the realm**, whoever holds the castle, one knight to a castle, as many knights as the realm has castles. No other house of the Empire swears knights.
- The house that holds the castle loses the village's rents while the pronoia lasts, with no grievance and no loss of regard.
- A pronoia belongs to the throne: when another house takes the crown, every knight passes to the new emperor. If you lose the throne, your knights go to the new emperor.
- If the castle leaves the realm (conquest, a house breaking away, a civil war, a lord going over to another emperor), the knight stays with the crown but loses the village, and gets it back if the castle returns.
- As emperor, the House tab of the clan screen has the button **"Grant a pronoia to a worthy knight"**: a new knight of the realm takes the pronoia of a village of a castle with no knight. You can do this once every **20 days**, and the button is greyed out with the reason when you cannot.
- As a knight in the Empire, you ask the emperor: "My sword is the throne's, your majesty, if the throne would grant me a pronoia to keep it."

## Themes and their strategoi

In a campaign started with the mod, each pair of imperial towns is a **theme**, commanded by a **strategos** whom the emperor names and can relieve. The strategos is not a duke: the command is not inherited, and the land stays the emperor's. Details on the [titles page](titles.md).

## Strategos coups

When the empire is in trouble, a strategos may try to seize the throne for themselves. Nobody acclaims them by right of blood: their army does.

**What drives it.** Towns and castles lost in the empire's current wars (minus those it took) push hardest. An emperor with a weak claim to the throne, or whose own house holds few of the empire's towns and castles, adds to the pressure, though much less while the empire is not losing a war. With no trouble, no coup begins, and one already under way fades after six weeks of calm. A strategos with a strong theme, Valor or a calculating mind is more likely to try; one who likes their emperor much less so. Expect about one coup in the whole of Calradia per campaign, and not all of them strike. A weak or losing empire is not spared: coups are its danger.

**Who joins.** Mostly the strategos's kin and the houses that hold land in their theme; others only rarely. If you are one of them, a rider may bring you "A word from the strategos": answer "Send word: we stand with the strategos" or "Burn the letter and say nothing". The letter tells you what failure would cost you.

**Leading one yourself.** If you are a strategos and the empire is in trouble, tell a lord of the realm: "The empire is in trouble, and {RULER} cannot save it. My army would follow me to the palace. Would you?" Win over your kin and the houses of your theme in person or through an envoy. Your journal and the Factions tab show how much of the realm stands behind you. After two weeks, with enough of the realm behind you, press **"Strike for the throne"** in the Factions tab. You can strike at war or at peace, but not while you ride in an army.

**How it strikes.**

- **With less than nearly half the realm: a blow at the palace.** If it lands, the strategos is crowned at once. If it fails, every house of the coup is judged as a traitor (see [Traitors](realm-houses.md)), and its leader's life is forfeit unless the throne shows mercy. A bigger share of the realm, the emperor away in the field, a war going badly and friends holding offices at court all help it land.
- **With nearly half the realm or more: "The army at the gates".** The emperor is called on to lay down the crown. A calculating emperor yields more easily, a brave one less. If the emperor yields, the strategos reigns without bloodshed. If not, it is a [civil war](civil-war.md), foreign war or no foreign war, and if you were in the coup you are asked whether you ride. A coup's civil war never splits the empire: its peace crowns the strategos, pardons the rebels or imposes the crown's terms.

**The emperor's suspicion.** Once a coup is talked about at court, the emperor may hear of it each week (more likely if they are calculating, less if they trust the strategos). An AI emperor then relieves the strategos of their theme and their offices at court, and the coup collapses. As emperor, you get "Whispers of a coup" with the name of the strategos: "Relieve the strategos of the theme" or "Let it be".

After a coup, the realm has 150 days without another, or 300 days after one that crowned its strategos.

## One Empire, one emperor

The three empires are what was left when the Empire split after Arenicos, and each emperor claims all of it.

- **A cause for war.** Between two imperial realms, the claim to the whole Empire is a reason for war: "there is one Empire, and {RULER} is not its emperor". It mostly replaces other reasons (the rulers' ill will, lost land) and adds few new wars.
- **Yielding the purple.** In the [Terms of Peace](war-and-peace.md) between two empires, one emperor may agree to "Yield the purple to {RULER} of the {EMPEROR}". The beaten realm ends: its ruling house and its lords go over to the winner and keep their towns and castles. A lord who loves their old emperor more may leave or rise with their land, as when a vassal realm is annexed. This is only possible for an emperor clearly beaten by that rival in this war, and it is the costliest term of all, so in practice only an empire that is almost destroyed accepts it. It cannot happen during a civil war or while either realm has vassal realms or is one, and never within 300 days of another such union anywhere on the map. You can ask for it or be asked; the term is greyed out with the reason when it is not possible.
- **Lords who change emperors.** A house on bad terms with its own emperor and on better terms with another goes over to that other empire more readily than lords elsewhere. This works the same for your vassals when you are emperor, and for a rival emperor's lords you try to bring over.

## Turn it off

In the [settings file](../settings.md):

- `ImperialPetitionEnabled`: petitions to the throne (default `true`).
- `PronoiaEnabled`: only the crown swears knights in the Empire, on any castle of the realm (default `true`).
- `StrategosCoupsEnabled`: strategos coups (default `true`; they also need the themes, `ImperialThemesEnabled`).
- `WarImperialClaimEnabled`: the claim to the one Empire as a cause for war (default `true`).
- `ImperialUnionEnabled`: yielding the purple at a peace (default `true`; needs the negotiated peace and vassal realms).
- `ImperialDefectionEnabled`: lords going over more readily to another emperor (default `true`).

The absence of Reckonings, feuds and vassal Annex in the Empire has no switch.
