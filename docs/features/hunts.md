# Hunts

If a feast is the big gathering of a peace, a hunt is the small one. A lord asks a few friendly houses to hunt in the woods of one of their fiefs for a couple of days. Houses grow closer or fall out, someone takes the best kill, someone takes a fall, and once in a long while somebody does not come back. You can be asked, you can hold hunts of your own, and a hunt is a good place for quiet words.

The game has no wild animals to hunt in a scene, so the hunt is told on the campaign map: each day you are out with it, a window asks you what you do about something that happens in the woods.

## When the AI holds a hunt

- A kingdom must have been at peace with every other kingdom for **5 days**.
- Each day there is a **1 in 10** chance that one of its houses calls a hunt (1 in about 7 with the **Hunting Rights** policy).
- After a hunt ends, that kingdom holds no other for **15 days**.
- Any house of the kingdom can host, the higher-tier houses a little more often. Unlike feasts, it is not mostly the ruler. The host must be leading a party of their own (not with an army), have **3,000 denars**, and be able to reach the fief in time.
- The host pays **1,000 denars for each house invited**.
- The hunt is held from one of the host's towns or castles: one with a village in or near a forest if they have one, otherwise the most prosperous. The woods of that village are the hunting grounds.

All in all hunts are rare: only a few in the whole map every 100 days.

### Hunts never meet feasts

A hunt and a feast of the same kingdom never happen at the same time, and a hunt never holds back the next feast:

- No hunt is called while a feast of the kingdom is announced or under way, nor in the **3 days** after one ends.
- After a feast, a hunt is called only while the feast's 25-day wait is running, and only if the whole hunt (5 days of notice, 2 days of hunting, 3 days of rest) is over before that wait ends.
- If the peace has lasted **20 days** (12 with Hunting Rights) and no feast has been called in it, the kingdom may hunt instead.
- In the other direction, no feast is called while a hunt is announced or under way, nor in the 3 days after it ends.

So both can happen in the same peace if it lasts long enough.

## Notice, duration and who comes

- The hunt rides out **5 days** after it is called and lasts **2 days**.
- Up to **4 houses** of the kingdom are invited. They must be within about **3 days** of travel and at **−10 relation or better** with the host; the better the relation, the likelier the invitation. Mercenary houses are not invited.
- The invited lords ride to the town or castle and wait there. Clan leaders without a party who are idle in another town or castle travel there too, if they arrive with at least one day left, and stay until it is over.
- A lord you have called to your court stays for that instead.
- If the host leads a plot in the realm, they prefer to invite lords they hope to win over (see [Quiet words](#quiet-words)).

## What happens each day

On each of the two days:

- The host grows a little closer to each house that came (up to **+1** over the whole hunt, **+2** with Hunting Rights).
- One lord takes the **best kill of the day**: good archers, throwers and riders are likelier. Their house gains **2 renown**. About **3 times in 10** another lord grows jealous (**−1** between them).
- Two houses out with the hunt are thrown together. Usually they part **1 point** closer. About **3 times in 10** they quarrel instead and lose **2 points**.
- There is a **1 in 10** chance that a lord takes a fall and is badly hurt.

No hunt raises a relation above **40** (your own hunts go up to 45).

If you are out with the hunt you hear about it: *"{Lord} takes the best kill of the day."*, *"{First} and {Second} ride home apart, each sure the other spoiled the chase."*, *"{Lord} is carried back to {place} on a hurdle, cursing a fallen tree and a nervous horse."*

## A death in the woods

Very rarely, a lord does not come back. The chance is **1.5% per hunt**, and only when at least **2 AI lords** are out with it. Older lords and poor riders are likelier to fall. Rulers can die too, at **half** the chance of another lord. You and your house never die this way.

*"A riderless horse comes back to {place} at dusk: {lord} has died in the woods."* The death is recorded in the game as **"died in a hunting accident"**. If someone out with the hunt hated the dead lord (relation −30 or worse), *"Some whisper that it was no accident."*: the dead lord's heir loses **10** relation with that lord.

Across the whole map, this is about one death every couple of thousand days.

## War breaks it up

- If the kingdom goes to war with another kingdom, the hunt ends: *"War has come: the hunt at {place} breaks up and the lords ride back to their men."*
- A siege of the town or castle ends it too, and so does the host dying, leaving the kingdom or losing the fief.
- If it is called off before it rides out, the host gets their money back and it does not count towards the 15-day wait.

## Being invited

You can be asked to a hunt:

- **by a lord of your own kingdom**, if your relation with the host is −10 or better and you are within about 3 days. Your house is twice as likely to be picked as another;
- **by a lord of another kingdom**, if you are not sworn to that kingdom, your relation with the host is **20 or better**, you are at peace with them, your own realm is at war with no kingdom and you are within about 3 days. This happens at about **3 in 10** of the hunts where it could.

*"{Host} invites you to hunt in the woods of {place} in {N} days. The place is marked on your map."* The marker stays until the hunt is over. You are only ever asked to one hunt at a time.

A guest from another kingdom is released, with no penalty, if war comes between you and the host, if your own realm goes to war, or if your own realm calls a feast, or a hunt that includes you.

If a hunt of your kingdom does not include you, you just see a message: *"{Host} has bidden a few friends to hunt in the woods of {place}."*

### At the lodge

In the town or castle, the menu shows **Join the hunt** (greyed out if you were not asked). The lodge tells you who hosts, when it rides out, who is already there and who is still on the road. Its options:

- **Wait at the lodge for the hunt to begin** (before it starts);
- **Ride out with the hunt** (once it has started): a wait with a progress bar that ends when the hunting is done: *"The hunting at {place} is done, and the guests ride home."*;
- **Arrange an accident...** (see below);
- **Leave the lodge**.

You count as out with the hunt on any day you are in that town or castle. Ride out **both days** and you gain **+1** with the host, if the host is out too. Never come at all and the host loses **1** relation with you: *"{Host} waited for you at {place} and you never came."*

## Holding your own hunt

In a town or castle of your clan, the menu shows **Hold a hunt here**. You need:

- to be in a kingdom (as a vassal or as ruler, not as a mercenary);
- your kingdom at peace with every other kingdom;
- no feast of your kingdom called, and **3 days** since the last one ended;
- no other hunt of your kingdom, **3 days** since its last one, and **20 days** since your own last hunt;
- not to be bidden to another hunt already;
- at least one house that would come;
- **2,000 denars** for one guest.

If something is missing, the option is greyed out and the tooltip says why.

You pick up to **4 houses** from a list that shows each one's relation with you and how many days away it is. Houses of your realm can be asked, and so can friends from kingdoms at peace with yours (relation 20 or better). Only houses with a lord who would really arrive in time are listed. Each guest costs **2,000 denars**.

- Your hunt is **marked on your map** until it is over.
- While you wait, you are told as each guest arrives (*"{Lord} of the {clan} has arrived at {place} for your hunt."*) and when all are in. The lodge shows how far away each missing guest is, or that they *may not come*.
- At the end, every house that hunted **every day with you there** gains **+2** relation with you (up to 45), and you gain **0.5 influence** for each house and day. Four houses for two days give 4 influence.
- **A host who stays at home gains nothing:** *"A hunt whose host is not out with it wins him nothing: yours has won you nothing."*
- If it is called off before it rides out, you get your denars back, and so for a foreign guest who has to go home before it starts.

## The windows

Each day you are out with a hunt, a window from the game's event screen comes in the morning (from about 9 o'clock) with something that happens in the woods. If you are not in the town or castle by then, the chance is lost. You do not see the same event twice in one hunt, and the next hunts prefer ones you have not seen lately.

Some answers are a sure thing. Others are a skill roll, shown in the option's tooltip with the chance and what happens either way. The chance is **20% + skill ÷ 3** (in %), **+10%** with the skill's hunting perk and a little more or less for a fitting trait, between **10% and 90%**. Some rolls are made by the companion in your party best at that skill. A successful roll also gives **150 experience** in that skill.

| Event | When | Your choices |
|---|---|---|
| **The stag breaks cover** | the host is out | Take the shot (Bow or Throwing): +5 renown, −1 with the host; fail −2. Leave it to the host: +4, Generosity. Send a companion to turn it (Scouting): +3 with the host and +1 with everyone else. |
| **A boar at bay** | a guest is out | Spur in with the boar spear (Polearm): +8 with the guest, +5 renown, Valor; fail +4 and you are hurt. Loose an arrow (Bow): +5; fail the guest is hurt, +1. Rein back: −3, Valor down. |
| **Not back by dusk** | a guest is out | Lead the search (Scouting): +6 with the guest, +2 with the host; fail +1. Send your best tracker: +4. Wait: −2, Calculating. |
| **Too close** | a lord who dislikes you is out | Laugh it off (Charm): +3 with them and +1 with everyone else; fail −2. Accuse them: −10, +3 renown, Honor. Say nothing: Calculating. |
| **The hound** | the host is out | See to the wound (Medicine): +5 with the host, Mercy; fail +2. Speak of the dog's courage (Charm): +3. Ride on: −3, Mercy down. |
| **A wager by the fire** | a guest is out | Take the wager (Bow or Riding): +4 renown, −1 with the guest. Leave them the field: +2. |
| **The headman's fields** | the grounds are a village | Ride round the fields: +5 with the village, −2 with the host, Mercy. Ride on: −5 with the village (−8 with Hunting Rights), +1 with the host. At someone else's hunt, promise to speak for the village (Charm): +3 with the village; fail +1 and −1 with the host. |
| **The ditch** | any day | Jump it (Riding): +3 renown; fail you are hurt. Go round: nothing. |
| **Talk in the saddle** | a lord of your realm is out | Listen (Charm): learn where their house stands on any plot in the realm (or +2 if there is none); fail +1. Keep to horses and hounds: +2. |
| **The honours of the day** | the host is out | Accept: +5 renown, one other lord −1. Give them to the host: +4, Generosity. Give them to your ruler, if they are out: +5, Generosity. |
| **The huntsman's glade** | the host is out | Follow him (Scouting): +6 renown, −2 with the host. Send him to his master: +3, Honor. |

Events with the host only come at someone else's hunt. As ruler, if you accuse a lord who is in a plot against your crown, their part in it comes out.

## Arranging an accident

At the lodge, once the hunt is out and before its last day, **Arrange an accident...** lets you pick a lord out with the hunt (an AI lord of another house; rulers too). It happens on the next day, if you ride out: if you are not there, it comes to nothing. You can try once per hunt, and then not again for **120 days**.

- **Chance it works:** 30%, +0.15% per point of Roguery (yours or that of the best rogue in your party, whichever is higher), +0.08% per point of your Bow, −0.1% per point of the victim's Scouting, between **10% and 60%**.
- **Chance it is found out:** 35%, +5% for every other lord out with the hunt, −0.1% per point of your rogue's Roguery, between **15% and 75%**, and half as much again if it failed.

Both chances are shown when you choose. What follows:

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Dies, nobody knows | *"Everyone calls it a terrible accident. You alone know better."* Your Honor goes down. |
| Survives, nobody knows | They are badly hurt, keep well away from you, and lose **10** relation with you. |
| Found out | Taken as murder: a **blood feud** with their house, as when you execute a lord, with the relation losses that go with it; **30 crime** in the host's kingdom (unless you rule it) and **−10** with the host. |

If the true dangers of the woods kill your victim first, nobody is to blame and your arrangements are quietly dropped.

## Quiet words

Long rides make for quiet words. Once a hunt has begun, while you and a lord are both out with it at the lodge:

- they are **one step easier** to persuade about a war or a peace (see [Winning the council over](council.md)), about a fief, and about joining or leaving a plot (see [Civil war](civil-war.md)). This never adds up with a feast, since the two never happen at the same time;
- for the AI, plot recruitment between two lords out with the same hunt is **1.5 times** as likely to succeed;
- on the last day of an AI hunt, a host who leads a plot (or, half the time, one who is in it) tries once to win over each wavering guest. A ruler who knows of the plot does the opposite and tries to win back each guest who is in it.

## Turn it off

In the settings file (see [Settings](../settings.md)):

- `HuntsEnabled`: `false` means no new hunts, for the AI or for you. Hunts already called run their course.
- `HuntForeignInvitesEnabled`: `false` stops invitations between kingdoms, both ways.
- `HuntWindowsEnabled`: `false` removes the daily windows.
- `HuntSkillXpEnabled`: `false` means no skill experience from the windows.
- `HuntAccidentsEnabled`: `false` means nobody dies in a hunt (falls still happen).
- `HuntAccidentsCanKillRulers`: `false` means rulers never die in a hunt.
- `HuntPlotAccidentEnabled`: `false` removes "Arrange an accident...".
- `HuntPlotCanTargetRulers`: `false` means you cannot arrange a ruler's accident.
- `HuntPlotRecruitEnabled`: `false` means no easier persuasion and no plotting at hunts.
- `HuntingRightsEnabled`: `false` means the Hunting Rights policy no longer changes hunts.
