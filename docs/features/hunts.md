# Hunts

If a feast is the big gathering of a peace, a hunt is the small one. A lord asks a few friendly houses to hunt in the woods of one of their fiefs for a couple of days. Houses grow closer or fall out, someone takes the best kill, someone takes a fall, and once in a long while somebody does not come back. You can be asked, you can hold hunts of your own, and a hunt is a good place for quiet words.

The game has no wild animals to hunt in a scene, so the hunt is told on the campaign map: each day you are out with it, a window asks you what you do about something that happens in the woods.

## When the AI holds a hunt

- A kingdom must have been at peace with every other kingdom for a few days.
- Now and then one of its houses calls a hunt, a little more often with the **Hunting Rights** policy.
- After a hunt ends, that kingdom holds no other for a couple of weeks.
- Any house of the kingdom can host, the greater houses a little more often. Unlike feasts, it is not mostly the ruler. The host must be leading a party of their own (not with an army), have some silver to spare, and be able to reach the fief in time.
- The host pays for each house invited.
- The hunt is held from one of the host's towns or castles: one with a village in or near a forest if they have one, otherwise the most prosperous. The woods of that village are the hunting grounds.

All in all hunts are rare: only a few in the whole map in a season.

### Hunts never meet feasts

A hunt and a feast of the same kingdom never happen at the same time, and a hunt never holds back the next feast:

- No hunt is called while a feast of the kingdom is announced or under way, nor for a few days after one ends.
- After a feast, a hunt is called only while the kingdom waits for its next feast, and only if the whole hunt (its notice, its days of hunting and the rest after it) is over before that wait ends.
- If the peace has lasted a good while (less with Hunting Rights) and no feast has been called in it, the kingdom may hunt instead.
- In the other direction, no feast is called while a hunt is announced or under way, nor for a few days after it ends.
- No hunt is called while the realm's [assembly](assembly.md) is summoned or sitting, nor for a few days after it rises.
- **The assembly comes first.** After a peace, no hunt is called until the realm's first assembly of that peace has been held, as with the feasts (see [Feasts](feasts.md#when-the-ai-holds-a-feast)).

So both can happen in the same peace if it lasts long enough.

## Notice, duration and who comes

- The hunt rides out some days after it is called and lasts two days.
- A few houses of the kingdom are invited. They must be within a few days' travel and not on bad terms with the host; the better the relation, the likelier the invitation. Mercenary houses are not invited.
- The invited lords ride to the town or castle and wait there. Clan leaders without a party who are idle in another town or castle travel there too, if they can arrive with a day left, and stay until it is over.
- A lord you have called to your court stays for that instead.
- If the host leads a plot in the realm, they prefer to invite lords they hope to win over (see [Quiet words](#quiet-words)).

## What happens each day

On each of the two days:

- The host grows a little closer to each house that came, a little more with Hunting Rights.
- One lord takes the **best kill of the day**: good archers, throwers and riders are likelier. Their house gains some renown. Sometimes another lord grows jealous of it.
- Two houses out with the hunt are thrown together. Usually they part a little closer; sometimes they quarrel instead and part worse.
- Now and then a lord takes a fall and is badly hurt.

A hunt never raises a relation past warm regard (your own hunts a little further).

If you are out with the hunt you hear about it: *"{Lord} takes the best kill of the day."*, *"{First} and {Second} ride home apart, each sure the other spoiled the chase."*, *"{Lord} is carried back to {place} on a hurdle, cursing a fallen tree and a nervous horse."*

## A death in the woods

Very rarely, a lord does not come back, and only when more than one lord of the AI is out with the hunt. Older lords and poor riders are likelier to fall. Rulers can die too, though less often than other lords. You and your house never die this way.

*"A riderless horse comes back to {place} at dusk: {lord} has died in the woods."* The death is recorded in the game as **"died in a hunting accident"**. If someone out with the hunt bitterly hated the dead lord, *"Some whisper that it was no accident."*: the dead lord's heir thinks much worse of that lord.

Across the whole map, such a death is a rare thing, once in many years.

## War breaks it up

- If the kingdom goes to war with another kingdom, the hunt ends: *"War has come: the hunt at {place} breaks up and the lords ride back to their men."*
- A siege of the town or castle ends it too, and so does the host dying, leaving the kingdom or losing the fief.
- If it is called off before it rides out, the host gets their money back and it does not count towards the wait for the next one.

## Being invited

You can be asked to a hunt:

- **by a lord of your own kingdom**, if the host is not on bad terms with you and you are within a few days' ride. Your house is likelier to be picked than another;
- **by a lord of another kingdom**, if you are not sworn to that kingdom, the host counts you a friend, you are at peace with them, your own realm is at war with no kingdom and you are within a few days' ride. This happens at some of the hunts where it could.

*"{Host} invites you to hunt in the woods of {place} in {N} days. The place is marked on your map."* The marker stays until the hunt is over. You are only ever asked to one hunt at a time.

A guest from another kingdom is released, with no penalty, if war comes between you and the host, if your own realm goes to war, or if your own realm calls a feast, or a hunt that includes you.

If a hunt of your kingdom does not include you, you just see a message: *"{Host} has bidden a few friends to hunt in the woods of {place}."*

### At the lodge

In the town or castle, the menu shows **Join the hunt** (greyed out if you were not asked). The lodge tells you who hosts, when it rides out, who is already there and who is still on the road. Its options:

- **Wait at the lodge for the hunt to begin** (before it starts): the wait stops by itself when the hunt rides out, and you are back at the lodge with **Ride out with the hunt** open; if the hunt is called off before it rides out, it stops too and you are told why;
- **Ride out with the hunt** (once it has started): a wait with a progress bar that ends when the hunting is done: *"The hunting at {place} is done, and the guests ride home."*;
- **Arrange an accident...** (see below);
- **Leave the lodge**.

You count as out with the hunt on any day you are in that town or castle. Ride out **both days** and the host thinks a little better of you, if the host is out too. Never come at all and the host takes it ill: *"{Host} waited for you at {place} and you never came."*

## Holding your own hunt

In a town or castle of your clan, the menu shows **Hold a hunt here**. You need:

- to be in a kingdom (as a vassal or as ruler, not as a mercenary);
- your kingdom at peace with every other kingdom;
- no feast of your kingdom called, and a few days since the last one ended;
- no [assembly](assembly.md) of your kingdom summoned or sitting, and a few days since the last one rose; and, after a peace, the realm's first assembly of that peace already held;
- no other hunt of your kingdom, a few days since its last one, and a few weeks since your own last hunt;
- not to be bidden to another hunt already;
- at least one house that would come;
- the silver for one guest.

If something is missing, the option is greyed out and the tooltip says why.

You pick a few houses from a list that shows each one's relation with you and how many days away it is. Houses of your realm can be asked, and so can friends from kingdoms at peace with yours. Only houses with a lord who would really arrive in time are listed. Each guest costs you the same.

- Your hunt is **marked on your map** until it is over.
- While you wait, you are told as each guest arrives (*"{Lord} of the {clan} has arrived at {place} for your hunt."*) and when all are in. The lodge shows how far away each missing guest is, or that they *may not come*.
- At the end, every house that hunted **every day with you there** comes closer to you, and you gain some influence for each house and day.
- **A host who stays at home gains nothing:** *"A hunt whose host is not out with it wins him nothing: yours has won you nothing."*
- If it is called off before it rides out, you get your denars back, and so for a foreign guest who has to go home before it starts.

## The windows

Each day you are out with a hunt, a window from the game's event screen comes in the morning with something that happens in the woods. If you are not in the town or castle by then, the chance is lost. You do not see the same event twice in one hunt, and the next hunts prefer ones you have not seen lately.

Some answers are a sure thing. Others are a skill roll, shown in the option's tooltip with the chance and what happens either way. The better your skill, the better your chance, and the skill's hunting perk and a fitting trait help too; nothing is ever certain, nor hopeless. Some rolls are made by the companion in your party best at that skill. A successful roll also gives experience in that skill.

| Event | When | Your choices |
|---|---|---|
| **The stag breaks cover** | the host is out | Take the shot (Bow or Throwing): renown, but the host is put out, and worse if you miss. Leave it to the host: he is pleased, and Generosity. Send a companion to turn it (Scouting): the host and everyone else are pleased. |
| **A boar at bay** | a guest is out | Spur in with the boar spear (Polearm): the guest is much in your debt, renown, Valor; fail, and you are hurt for less thanks. Loose an arrow (Bow): the guest is grateful; fail, and the guest is hurt. Rein back: the guest thinks less of you, and your Valor suffers. |
| **Not back by dusk** | a guest is out | Lead the search (Scouting): the guest is much in your debt, and the host pleased; fail, a little thanks. Send your best tracker: the guest is grateful. Wait: the guest takes it ill, Calculating. |
| **Too close** | a lord who dislikes you is out | Laugh it off (Charm): they and everyone else warm to you; fail, they think worse of you. Accuse them: a bitter enemy, but renown and Honor. Say nothing: Calculating. |
| **The hound** | the host is out | See to the wound (Medicine): the host is grateful, Mercy; fail, a little thanks. Speak of the dog's courage (Charm): the host is pleased. Ride on: the host takes it ill, and your Mercy suffers. |
| **A wager by the fire** | a guest is out | Take the wager (Bow or Riding): renown, but the guest is put out. Leave them the field: the guest is pleased. |
| **The headman's fields** | the grounds are a village | Ride round the fields: the village is grateful, the host put out, Mercy. Ride on: the village resents it (more with Hunting Rights), the host is pleased. At someone else's hunt, promise to speak for the village (Charm): the village is grateful; fail, a little thanks and a put-out host. |
| **The ditch** | any day | Jump it (Riding): renown; fail, you are hurt. Go round: nothing. |
| **Talk in the saddle** | a lord of your realm is out | Listen (Charm): learn where their house stands on any plot in the realm (or warm to each other, if there is none); fail, a little goodwill. Keep to horses and hounds: they warm to you. |
| **The honours of the day** | the host is out | Accept: renown, but one other lord is jealous. Give them to the host: he is pleased, Generosity. Give them to your ruler, if they are out: the ruler is pleased, Generosity. |
| **The huntsman's glade** | the host is out | Follow him (Scouting): much renown, but the host is put out. Send him to his master: the host is pleased, Honor. |

Events with the host only come at someone else's hunt. As ruler, if you accuse a lord who is in a plot against your crown, their part in it comes out.

## Arranging an accident

At the lodge, once the hunt is out and before its last day, **Arrange an accident...** lets you pick a lord out with the hunt (an AI lord of another house; rulers too). It happens on the next day, if you ride out: if you are not there, it comes to nothing. You can try once per hunt, and then not again for a long while.

- **Whether it works** rises with Roguery (yours or that of the best rogue in your party, whichever is better) and a little with your Bow, and falls with the victim's Scouting. It is never certain, and never hopeless.
- **Whether it is found out** rises with every other lord out with the hunt, and falls with your rogue's Roguery; a failed attempt is likelier to be found out.

Both chances are shown when you choose. What follows:

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Dies, nobody knows | *"Everyone calls it a terrible accident. You alone know better."* Your Honor goes down. |
| Survives, nobody knows | They are badly hurt, keep well away from you, and think much worse of you. |
| Found out | Taken as murder: a **blood feud** with their house, as when you execute a lord, with the relation losses that go with it; a heavy crime in the host's kingdom (unless you rule it), and the host turns against you. |

If the true dangers of the woods kill your victim first, nobody is to blame and your arrangements are quietly dropped.

## Quiet words

Long rides make for quiet words. Once a hunt has begun, while you and a lord are both out with it at the lodge:

- they are **one step easier** to persuade about a war or a peace (see [Winning the council over](council.md)), about a fief, and about joining or leaving a plot (see [Civil war](civil-war.md)). This never adds up with a feast, since the two never happen at the same time;
- for the AI, drawing another lord out with the same hunt into a plot is easier too;
- on the last day of an AI hunt, a host who leads a plot (or, sometimes, one who is in it) tries once to win over each wavering guest. A ruler who knows of the plot does the opposite and tries to win back each guest who is in it.

## Turn it off

In the settings file (see [Settings](../settings.md)):

- `HuntsEnabled`: `false` means no new hunts, for the AI or for you. Hunts already called run their course.
- `HuntForeignInvitesEnabled`: `false` stops invitations between kingdoms, both ways.
- `HuntWindowsEnabled`: `false` removes the daily windows.
- `HuntSkillXpEnabled`: `false` means no skill experience from the windows.
- `HuntAccidentsEnabled`: `false` means nobody dies in a hunt (falls still happen).
- `HuntAccidentsCanKillRulers`: `false` means rulers never die in a hunt.
- `HuntPlotAccidentEnabled`: `false` removes "Arrange an accident...".
- `HuntPlotCanTargetRulers`: `false` means you cannot arrange a ruler's accident.
- `HuntPlotRecruitEnabled`: `false` means no easier persuasion and no plotting at hunts.
- `HuntingRightsEnabled`: `false` means the Hunting Rights policy no longer changes hunts.
