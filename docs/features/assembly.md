# The kingdom assembly

Once in a long peace the ruler summons every house of the realm to his hall. The lords ride in as to a feast, and for a few days the crown sits in judgment over the quarrels the realm really has: a house's right to a fief the crown keeps, a kinsman killed, a fortress stormed, a wrong of the last war, an empty office. You can lay your own case, speak for a friend, work the hall the night before, or stay away and be marked for it. As ruler, you preside and judge.

## Each realm in its own way

Every realm holds its assembly in the form of its people, and each form has its own customs (see [Customs of each form](#customs-of-each-form)):

| Realm | Assembly |
|---|---|
| Sturgia, and Nordvyg (with War Sails) | **the thing** |
| The Khuzaits | **the kurultai** |
| The Aserai | **the majlis** |
| Battania | **the oenach** |
| Vlandia | **the curia of the peers** |
| The three empires | **the Senate** |
| Any other realm | **the great council of the realm** |

## When an assembly is summoned

- The realm must have been at peace with every other kingdom for **30 days**, and it meets **once in each peace**: the next assembly waits for the next peace.
- Not during a civil war, nor while houses that broke with the realm are at war with it, nor while the realm has an army in the field.
- Not while a [feast](feasts.md) or a [hunt](hunts.md) of the realm is called or under way, nor in the **3 days** after one. Feasts and hunts wait for the assembly in the same way while it is summoned or sitting, and for 3 days after it rises.
- Not with an enemy lord's host of 80 men or more in the open field within about a day's ride of the hall (bandits and outlaw bands do not count).
- **An AI ruler** summons it to a town or castle of the crown's own: his most prosperous town, or a castle if he holds no town. Each day the rules allow it he may call it, an honourable ruler more readily.
- **As ruler**, in a town or castle of your own: **Summon the realm to an assembly here** in the settlement's menu. When it cannot be called, the option is greyed out with the reason (*"The peace is too young: the lords will not leave their men while the last war is still fresh. Give it 12 more days."*). Then **Send out the riders**.

The houses of the realm hear of it on the map (*"{Ruler} summons the houses of the realm to the thing at {place}. It sits in 4 days, and a house that can ride is expected to come."*), and a journal entry opens with the hall marked on your map.

## Who comes

- It sits **4 days** after it is called, and lasts **5 days**.
- The lords of the realm within about **5 days** of travel ride there, as they would to a feast. Heads of houses without a party of their own who are idle in a town or castle travel there too. A house that has a case to bring before the crown rides from any distance, and far more eagerly.
- **The ruler goes to his hall and stays there** while he presides. He leaves it only to defend his own land, if one of his towns or castles is besieged or one of his villages raided.
- Mercenary clans and [landed knights](landed-knights.md) are not summoned.
- **Houses in a feud are left alone.** A house in any [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses), on either side and at any stage, is never drawn away from it, is never held to have missed the assembly, and is a side in no case while the feud lasts (an accusation against it waits for another assembly). The assembly never judges a feud.

### Staying away

A house that never comes to the hall, and had no good reason to stay away on at least half of its days, loses **5** relation with the ruler, and the ruler remembers it for a while (see [Lords who remember you](memories.md)). Good reasons: its head is a prisoner, with an army, in a battle or a siege, defending its own land, away on an errand, or too far to come. This applies to you too: *"{Ruler} summoned the realm to {place}, and your house never came. The crown has marked your empty seat."* As ruler, you are told which houses never came, and you think the less of them for it.

## At the assembly

In the town or castle, **Attend the assembly** takes you to the hall, which tells you who presides, what is before the court and how many days are left. From there:

- **Lay a petition before the crown**, **Bring an accusation before the crown**, **Ask the crown to make good a wrong it did your house** (see [The cases](#the-cases)).
- **Speak for a house in its case**: stand with one side of a case still to be heard. Your word counts like that of any house in the hall, if you are there when it is heard.
- **Speak against the khan** (at a kurultai) and **Step onto the sand of the arena** (a trial by combat at a thing).
- **Go to the lord's hall** to talk to the lords there (see [Talk in the hall](#talk-in-the-hall)).
- **Stay at the assembly**: let the hours pass among the houses. Time runs as in the game's own wait, and **the wait stops by itself** and brings you back to the assembly when something new opens for you: the roll of the court is drawn, tomorrow's case is set and the lords can still be swayed, your own case is called, the ruler waits for you in the hall, a window of the assembly is put before you, or an option of the menu becomes usable. A line says what is new. When the assembly rises, you are back in the town or castle.
- **Leave the hall**.

Only the head of your house can lay its cases. A house that serves for pay, or holds its land as a [landed knight](landed-knights.md), lays no case and answers none.

### How a day goes

- **The second day**, the **roll of the court** is drawn from what the realm really has: *"Before the court: the petition of the Osticos for Pravend, which the crown holds; the accusation of the Rhagaea against the Vizartos: they stormed Amprela, which was theirs."* A case is only put on the roll if the house that brings it is in the hall. If there is nothing, the court says so: *"Nothing is laid before the court: the realm has no quarrel it would bring."*
- From the next day, **one case a day** is heard, at most **3** at an assembly, and only while the ruler is in the hall: *"no case is heard until the ruler takes the seat"*.
- A house is a side in one case at most, and the crown answers one case of its own (a petition or a plea for redress against it) at each assembly.
- A house that brings a case and is not in the hall when it is called loses it: *"... is called, and nobody of the {clan} is in the hall to press it. The court moves on."*

## The cases

Every case comes from something that happened in your campaign.

- **A petition for a fief of the crown.** A house with a [right](fief-rights.md) to a town or castle the ruling house holds asks for it before the realm. Never the hall the assembly sits in, never the seat of the crown's own house (*"It is the seat of the crown's own house. No ruler gives that away."*), never one under siege, and never when the crown would be left with fewer than 3 towns and castles of its own. Granted, the fief passes to the house as a gift of the crown. Refused, the house holds it against the crown as a claim denied.
- **An accusation** between two houses of the realm (never the crown's own) for a grave wrong of the last 120 days, done while both were of the realm: a kinsman fallen to the other's host or put to death at their hands, or a town or castle of theirs stormed. What is done in battle between the two sides of a feud while it lasts (deaths in the field, walls stormed) is no crime; an execution or a murder still is. Each wrong is heard once. A house of the AI brings one only against a house it is not on good terms with.
- **A wrong of the war** one house holds against another: *"they rode off to a quarrel of their own while the realm's foe was at its walls"*, they went over to the realm's foe, their host stood nearby and never relieved a fief that fell, as marshal they sent no host to it, as allies they refused the call to arms, as their liege they did not come to defend it or stood aside when the house was attacked, they broke the vote they pledged at the peace. It is judged as an accusation. Upheld, the grievance is laid to rest; dismissed, it festers more, and the same wrong is not brought again (a new one can be).
- **A plea for redress**: a house asks the crown to make good a wrong the crown did it (a fief that fell with no host of the crown near it, passed over when a fief was given out, a fief the crown kept for itself or took from it, a shameful peace, a title or ulus passed over, a claim or petition turned away, a charter broken...). Granted, the crown gives it an empty office of the court if its head may hold one, or else, for a wrong that cost the house land, perhaps a town or castle of the crown's own, which the ruler weighs as closely as a petition for land (below), or else gold, if the crown has enough of it in hand; the grievance is laid to rest. Refused, it festers the more for being heard.
- **The service of a house in the late war**: the house of the realm that stormed the most towns and castles and won the most battles in the wars since the last peace is called to be answered. The ruler may honour it with an office, gold or influence; if he will not, the house takes it ill, and so, a little, does the hall. A ruler with nothing in hand praises it before the realm, and nobody holds that against him. It can be your house.
- **An empty office of the court** (see [The royal court](royal-court.md)): the ruler names one of the heads in the hall, above all one who has stood by him in the field. If it is you, you may take it or decline it before the hall.
- **A vacant title** (see [Dukes, counts and castellans](titles.md)), when you are the ruler: you grant it before the assembly, or let it wait.
- **Before the Senate only:** *the charge that a house plots against the throne*, brought against a house that really is in a plot by a house not on good terms with it; and *an officer of the court called to account* for the villages of his own lands that lie burned, who may lose his office.

## How the ruler judges

An AI ruler judges by his character, and when a ruling gives a reason, it is the true one, in his own voice. He weighs:

- how he regards each head, and blood ties;
- the right of it (an honourable ruler), the stronger house (a calculating one), and in an accusation the wronged house's claim on his honour, the more so for a kinsman put to death;
- the houses in the hall that stand with each side, by their regard for the two heads and their kin;
- what each side said in the hall (a good speaker counts, and so does service the ruler remembers);
- at a majlis, the counsel of most of the houses.

**A petition for land** is weighed more closely, because the crown gives up what it holds. A firm right counts, a generous ruler gives more readily and a calculating one keeps what he has; the ruler's regard and the words said in the hall only go so far; and the ruler weighs what the fief is worth to the crown: a town far more than a castle, and the more of the crown's own income it brings, the harder it is to part with. When that is what decides it, he says so: *"I will not strip the crown of {fief}. It is worth too much to the throne."*, or by his character, *"I have counted what {fief} brings the crown. No favour in this hall is worth that much to me."*

### Sentences

- **An accusation** is dismissed, or upheld with **amends** paid to the wronged house: **3,000** denars, half from a merciful ruler and half again more from a cruel one, never more than the guilty head has.
- **Rarely, a fief taken.** Only a hard-handed ruler (cruel, or calculating and without mercy) takes land, and only from a house with at least 2 towns and castles, for a fief stormed or a kinsman's death, or for a wrong of the war that cost a fief. It is the very fief stormed if the accused still holds it, or else their least prosperous castle (a town only if they hold no castle), and it goes to the wronged house. The house answers it as a [decree of the crown](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal): it yields and holds it against the crown, breaks with the realm and founds its league, or the [plot](civil-war.md) it is sworn to rises over it; if it is your house, you are asked.
- **The losing side** bows to the crown's word if its head is honourable and does not dislike the ruler; any other holds it against the crown (see [Factions and grievances](factions.md)).

### Defying the sentence

A house ordered to pay amends, or convicted of plotting before the Senate, may **defy the crown** instead. That is war: the house breaks with the realm and founds its [league](realm-houses.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm), alone, out of any plot, even in a civil war (a sentence that takes a fief is answered as a decree, above). The assembly breaks up when that war begins. A house of the AI defies only if its head is proud (brave, or without honour), on bad terms with the ruler, strong enough to stand against the crown's house, and holds land to defend; the bolder and the angrier, the likelier, but never more than 7 times in 10.

If the head who defies is in the hall, it is up to the ruler:

- **An honourable ruler**, or one who is neither cruel nor calculating, **keeps the assembly's peace**: the lord rides out under safe-conduct straight to the fief the sentence takes (with only a fine, to his house's best town or castle), and it is war when he gets there, or after **4 days** wherever he is.
- **A ruler without honour who is cruel or calculating seizes him** in the hall. His house goes to war without its head, and every house in the hall likes the ruler **5** less for breaking the assembly's peace.

## Customs of each form

- **The thing:** where it sits in a town with an arena, an accused who expects the ruling to go against him may demand **trial by combat**: each house's champion, the best fighter of the house in the hall, meets the other on the sand, to the death, as in a [duel](duels.md). The side whose champion falls has lost, and the gods' verdict stands for the court's: no grievance against the crown. An accused who loses pays the case's amends, and cannot defy the gods' verdict. An AI accused demands it only when he likes his champion's chances (a brave one takes worse odds). As the accused, you can demand it yourself (*"Put your cause to the sword: demand trial by combat"*); as either side, you fight in person (**Step onto the sand of the arena**), and if you fall you are only badly hurt, but your side has lost. Never stepping onto the sand before the thing rises loses your cause.
- **The kurultai:** on the first day the khan sits, every house in the hall on bad terms with him (**-10** or worse) speaks against him to his face, and he likes each of them **2** less. With **2** voices or more, those houses lean harder towards a [plot](civil-war.md) against the khan for **60 days**: a plot is likelier to be born and to grow, and if there is one already, they weigh joining it at once. You can rise and speak against the khan yourself (**Speak against the khan**), at the same cost.
- **The majlis:** the emir must hear the counsel of the houses before he judges, and the side most of them lean to weighs in his ruling. If he judges against what most of them counsel, every house in the hall likes him **3** less.
- **The oenach:** a kinsman killed or put to death is answered with the **blood price**, **5,000** denars instead of the usual amends.
- **The curia of the peers:** a lord is judged by his peers. The crown may fine him, but takes no land from him unless most of the peers in the hall vote for it (each by its regard for the two heads; honour votes for it and mercy against; never against its own kin).
- **The Senate:** the senators in the hall vote on a sentence that takes land, on a charge of plotting and on an officer called to account. A calculating or cruel emperor may override them, and every senator who voted the other way likes him **5** less. A house convicted of plotting pays the amends; if it leads the plot, the whole court learns who leads it, and if it only follows, it must break with the plot. Acquitted, the houses of its plot are bolder for it.
- **The great council:** the ruler hears the cases with the lords about him, and judges as he sees fit.

## Talk in the hall

Between hearings, the lords who came are in the game's own **lord's hall** of the town or castle. **Go to the lord's hall** (in the town or castle menu and the assembly's) says what awaits: *"tomorrow the court hears the petition of the Osticos for Pravend, and the lords there can still be swayed"*. The lords worth talking to carry the game's quest marker, and the journal names tomorrow's case.

Talk to the head of a house: *"About tomorrow's case before the court..."*. Then:

- *"Stand with my house before the court tomorrow."* (or with the side you speak for);
- *"Do not stand with {side} tomorrow. Keep your peace."*;
- *"Where will you stand when the court hears it?"*: he tells you, and whether he could be moved (*"Ask me, and you may find me willing."*, *"Though every voice in this hall has its price."*).

He answers by his character and his ties, and says why in his own words:

- He will not stand against his own blood, nor do anything at the asking of someone he hates, nor, if he is honourable, lend his name to the side that is in the wrong. A lord who has given his word to another keeps it, unless he has no honour.
- A good friend agrees for nothing (*"For you, my friend, gladly."*), and so does an honourable lord asked for the side in the right, or one who leans your way already.
- In between, he weighs his regard for both heads and for you, which way the ruler leans, his kin, a [house alliance](factions.md#alliances-between-houses), the grievances of his house, the right of it, the stronger house (a calculating lord) and mercy for the accused; the graver the case, the harder he is to move. A calculating lord names his price: gold (*"Nothing in this hall is given for nothing. Give me 2,000 denars, and you have my voice tomorrow."*, more on a grave matter and the further he leans away), or your word that your house will stand with his when he calls on it. Any other hesitates, and you argue it out with the game's persuasion; an argument that goes very badly costs a little relation.

What you win is his house's word, and it counts in the hearing exactly as any house that stands with a side. If you paid with your own word, your house owes his a favour: when his house has a case of its own before the court, at this assembly or a later one, it may call on yours to stand with it. Keep it and you are quits; break it, or stay away without good reason, and its head likes you **10** less. A favour never called on lapses after a while, at no cost. Breaking a word you gave in the hall costs the same at once. It works the other way too: a house that gives you its word for yours owes you.

The two sides of the AI canvass the hall the same way before each hearing, and may draw you aside with a window: *"A house seeks your voice"*. Each side of a case can only ask so many houses in all (the houses you ask for a side count for it); the hall tells you when you have spoken to as many lords as you can for it.

## Your own case

**Under an AI ruler**, when your case is called and you are in the hall, a window asks what you say: plead your cause (the better you speak, the more it counts), remind the crown of what your house has done for it (it helps only if the ruler thinks well of you), demand trial by combat (an accused, at a thing), or say nothing. If you are in the town or castle, the ruler **waits for you in the lord's hall** for some hours instead: *"I have come to answer the court on my case."* There you plead in person, hear the ruling and its real reason, and answer it. You can also ask him *"Why did you rule as you did on {case}?"* about the last case he judged. If you do not go to him, the windows come as usual.

Sentenced to amends or to lose a fief, or convicted of plotting, you choose: **Bow to the crown's ruling**, **Defy the crown** (greyed out with the reason when you cannot, for example while you are sworn to a plot, unless the charge is that plot), or, when a fief is taken, **Raise the banner of the plot you are sworn to**. If you defy in the hall, the ruler's character decides: a safe-conduct (a journal entry tells you where to ride and by when, the fief marked on your map) or seizure. Your house's war waits, as any rising does, until you are out of a battle or siege and not inside another house's town or castle; an army holds it at most 2 days.

**As ruler**, you preside: be in the hall, or no case is heard, and an assembly whose ruler stays away **2 days** in a row breaks up. Each case comes to you in a window (*"The thing: your judgement"*) that shows who stands with each side, what each answer does and whether the losing side will bow, hold it against you or may defy you. At a majlis it shows which way most houses counsel; at a curia or before the Senate, how the vote goes. For an office, the heads in the hall who may hold it, and whether they have stood by you in the field. If a lord defies you in your hall, you choose: **Keep the assembly's peace: let {lord} ride out**, or **Seize {lord} in the hall**.

## When it breaks up

War with another kingdom, a civil war, an enemy at the walls, a strong enemy host in the field near the hall, a ruler away too long, a new ruler, or a house that goes to war over a defied sentence: the assembly breaks up at once (*"War has come: the thing at {place} breaks up, and the lords ride back to their men."*). Nobody is held to have missed it, and the cases not yet heard are set aside (a defiance already made still means war). At its normal end, the houses ride home: *"The thing at {place} is over, and the houses ride home with the crown's word."*

## Turn it off

In the settings file (see [Settings](../settings.md)):

- `AssemblyEnabled` to `false`: no assemblies.
- `AssemblyHallTalkEnabled` to `false`: no talk with the lords in the hall about tomorrow's case (cases are heard from the second day).
- `AssemblyHallKingEnabled` to `false`: the ruler does not wait for you in the hall; your case is always answered in windows.
- `AssemblyKingStaysEnabled` to `false`: the ruler is not held at his hall while he presides.
- `AssemblyPlaintiffPullEnabled` to `false`: houses with a case ride to the assembly no more eagerly than the others.
- `AssemblyWarWrongsEnabled`, `AssemblyRedressEnabled`, `AssemblyRewardEnabled` to `false`: no wrongs of the war, no pleas for redress, no reward for the war.
