# Rights to fiefs

Every house holds its towns and castles by right, and remembers the ones it lost. Other houses may claim a fief too: by blood, by marriage, by having led the siege that took it, by the [title](titles.md#rights-of-a-duchy-and-of-a-county) of the duchy or county it lies in, or with a claim forged at the holder's own court. A house may also claim a [duke's title](titles.md#claims-to-a-title) itself. A right weighs when the council shares out a fief and when the ruler judges a [reckoning](factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house) at the assembly, and it is what a reckoning needs before it can be raised.

## Firm and weak rights

A right is either **firm** or **weak**.

- A firm right weighs fully, in a share-out and at the assembly. A weak one weighs half.
- A right belongs to the head of the house who held it. When that head dies, a firm right passes to the next head as a weak one, and a weak right is forgotten. A claim by marriage belongs to the spouse instead (it dies with them).
- The holder of a fief always has a firm right to it. A fief just taken and waiting for the council to share it out has no rightful holder until then.

## How a house comes by a right {#how-a-house-comes-by-a-right}

| How | Strength |
|---|---|
| **It lost the fief to a foe**: to a siege, a peace, a feud between houses, or its own townsfolk rising | Firm |
| **It lost the fief by a decision of its realm**: a decree (a lord's sentence for felony included), a reckoning the ruler found for at the assembly, or a petition granted at the assembly (one granted by the emperor leaves no right at all) | Weak |
| **The realm took it back**: a fief lost to a foe, retaken by the realm and given to another house. Both the house that now holds it and the house that lost it have a firm right | Firm |
| **It led the siege** that took the fief, and the council gave it to another house | Weak |
| **By blood**: a house died out, and the nearest houses of its kin (by marriage first) take up its claims | Weak |
| **By marriage**: the head of a house, its heir or you marry; the house that takes in the spouse claims the two towns and castles of their house of birth nearest its own lands on the wedding day, for as long as the spouse lives (see below) | Weak |
| **A forged claim**: found, or made, at the holder's own court (see below) | Weak |
| **Of our duchy**: a duke or noyan, to a county (by its town) or castle of his duchy held by a house of his realm that does not answer to him (see [Rights of a duchy and of a county](titles.md#rights-of-a-duchy-and-of-a-county)) | Weak, for as long as that lasts |
| **Of our county**: a count, to a castle of his county held by a house of his realm that does not answer to him | Weak, for as long as that lasts |

A house that gives a fief away, leaves its realm, or dies out keeps no right to it. Nor does a house whose fief is taken by the judgment of a beaten rising or by a sentence of the assembly.

## No old claims at the start {#old-claims-of-a-line}

A campaign starts with no claims dealt out: every house holds what it holds, and the only rights are the ones the campaign itself makes, for the AI as for you. Every claim in the realm has a story you can follow.

## A claim by marriage

When the head of a noble house, its heir (the one who would be its next head), or you yourself, marry into another noble house during the campaign, the house that takes in the bride or groom gains a weak claim to the two towns and castles of the spouse's house of birth nearest its own lands on the wedding day. This goes for your house as much as for the AI: marry well, and you marry into claims. The same goes for the heir of your own house. Marriages of other sons, daughters and kin bring nothing, and neither do marriages that already existed when the campaign started.

The claim lasts as long as the spouse lives, and dies with them; it never passes on.

When one of your own marries out this way, you hear that the other house now claims the fiefs of yours nearest its lands.

You can press a claim by marriage whenever you like (in a [reckoning](factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house), or by the sword), though your in-laws will not forgive it easily. The AI houses press theirs only when the heads of the two houses are on bad terms: a house will raise a reckoning against its in-laws over a fief it claims through that marriage only on bad terms with them. Against blood kin, or in-laws over any other right, an AI house weighs heavily against it and very seldom does it, the more seldom the better they get on.

## A claim forged at a foreign court

Leave a companion or a member of your family in a town or castle of another house with the game's own **"+"** in the town or castle panel. As in the base game, they win friends among the notables and lords there. Speak with them there (find them in the town or castle, or pick their portrait at the top of its menu) and ask them to **forge a claim to that fief**: *"Their archive must be full of old parchment. Find me a claim to {fief}, one the council will have to hear."* Only towns and castles, only the fief they are in, and never one of a kingdom at war with yours.

- **An honourable guest refuses**, in their own words, and goes on making friends.
- **Forging.** It cannot be hurried. Every day there is a small chance that they find (or make) the grant, and the chance depends on their **Charm**: a charming courtier may manage it in a season or two, one with little Charm may take years, or never. There is no time limit, and asking them how it goes gives you an idea in their own words. While they work on the parchment they make no new friends among the notables there; tell them to leave it be and they go back to it.
- **When it can be done**, a letter tells you what it will cost: clerks, witnesses and seals are not cheap. The price follows what the fief itself is worth in a trade between kingdoms: a great deal of silver for a castle and a small fortune for a rich town. Pay, and your house has a weak claim to the fief. Do not pay (or cannot), and they keep looking: the chance comes again some other day, as in Crusader Kings. They stay where they are until you call them back through the clan screen.
- **It stops** if they leave the fief, are taken or die, if the house loses the fief, if your house comes to hold it or a claim to it by other means, or if your kingdom and theirs go to war. You hear of it in their words when it matters to you.
- **The journal** keeps an entry for each of them while they forge, **"{name} seeks a claim to {fief}"**, with the fief marked on the map.

## A claim to a title forged at its seat {#a-claim-to-a-title-forged-at-its-seat}

The same can be done for a **duke's title**: leave your companion or kinsman at the seat of a duke (or a noyan) of your own realm, and ask them there for an old grant of his title to your house (*"Their lord holds {title}. Find me an old grant of it to our house, one the realm will have to hear."*). Never a title of your own house or of the ruler's, nor one you already claim, and your house must hold a town or castle somewhere (not necessarily in that duchy). It works like a claim to a fief, but takes far longer, and its price is that of the duchy's chief town. The claim is weak, and pressed only by the sword, in a [feud for the title](titles.md#a-feud-for-a-title). Houses of the AI forge such claims now and then too, to a duchy near their own lands.

## When others forge claims to your fiefs

AI houses forge claims too. Now and then a house whose head is not honourable quietly works on a claim to one of the fiefs nearest its own, in any kingdom, yours included; a house that bears a grudge against the holder is likelier to choose that house's fief, and one very seldom works against its own kin, the more seldom the better they get on. They pay the same price as you, from the purse of the house's head, and wait until they have it. You see nothing of it until it succeeds. If the fief is yours, word reaches you: *"the {house} have found an old grant to {fief} in their name, sealed and witnessed, though nobody had heard of it before."* That house can now press it against yours: in a [reckoning](factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house) or by the sword, and it will weigh in a share-out. And if you are the ruler, in peace, the head of that house may come to [your court](royal-court.md#the-requests) to ask you for it.

## In the council and at the assembly {#in-the-council}

- **Sharing out a fief.** A house with a firm right counts double when the council picks the candidates and weighs them; a weak right counts half as much again. Honourable lords care most about this. It is never a sure thing: a house that already holds a lot is still weighed down by it, and the ruler has the last word.
- **No vote takes a fief from a house.** The council votes on a fief only to share one out. A right is pressed in a [reckoning](factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house), which the ruler hears and judges at the realm's [assembly](assembly.md), or by the sword. For a vassal the kingdom screen's **Annex** is greyed, and tells you the roads open to you (win lords over to a reckoning, or join one, or press the right by the sword). The ruler takes a fief by decree, and a lord demands one of his own vassal, with no vote (see [Taking a fief](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal)). This holds in war as in peace.
- **A reckoning's hearing.** When the ruler judges a reckoning, the right of its houses counts, firm in full and weak by half, and the more honourable the ruler, the more. See [Heard at the assembly](factions.md#heard-at-the-assembly).
- **Joining a reckoning.** A right draws lords to it, honourable ones most.
- **In the empires** there are no reckonings and no feuds: a right to a fief held by another house of the realm is laid before the throne as a **petition** instead, in person, by an envoy or with the **Petition the throne** button of your house's rights in the Realm tab. See [The Empire's own ways](empire.md).
- **A feud.** If claimants take up arms over a fief and their right is only weak (or they have none), honourable houses with no ties to either side may stand with the holder, whose right is firm. Never a house that counts the holder an enemy (no love for its head, or an old grudge against its house). When one does, you hear it in their own words. It doesn't always happen, more often for a very honourable lord. A claim with a firm right draws nobody in this way.

At the realm's [assembly](assembly.md), a house may also petition the crown for a fief the crown keeps for itself, and lay its right before the ruler.

## Where to see it {#where-to-see-it}

- **Encyclopedia.** A house's page says what it claims beyond what it holds, how firmly, and why. A fief's page says who holds it by right, and who else claims it and why.
- **Kingdom screen, Realm tab.** Under **Claims**, **Your house's rights**: your rights (to titles too), who of your house is forging a claim at a foreign court, who claims your fiefs and titles, and the lords who will back you when the next fief is shared out. Each of your rights against a house of your realm has a **Press by the sword** button (see [Three ways to press a right](factions.md#three-ways-to-press-a-right)); in the empires, **Petition the throne** instead. A right the ruler judged against at the assembly is marked *"judged by"* that ruler: while he reigns, raising it again defies the crown (see [A right the crown has judged](factions.md#a-right-the-crown-has-judged)).
- **Clan screen, House tab.** Under **Claims**, the same rights of your house and the rights others hold to yours, only to read.

## Turn it off

In the [settings file](../settings.md), set to `false`:

- `FiefRightsEnabled`: all of it (rights are still kept, but count for nothing, and a reckoning needs none);
- `FiefRightsVotesEnabled`: rights in the council's share-outs;
- `FiefRightsForgeEnabled`: forging claims at a foreign court;
- `FiefRightsMarriageEnabled`: claims by marriage;
- `FiefRightsReckoningNeedsRight`: a reckoning needs a right;
- `FiefRightsAiForgeEnabled`: AI houses forging claims too;
- `FiefRightsFeudHonourEnabled`: honourable houses standing with the better right in a feud;
- `FiefRightsEncyclopediaEnabled`: rights in the encyclopedia.
