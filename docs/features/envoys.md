# Envoys

Send someone of your house to a lord anywhere in Calradia, with a message you would otherwise have to carry yourself. The envoy really rides there and back, and when the message needs persuading, they argue it as you would, with their own skills and character: a spouse with a ready tongue may do better than you, a rough companion worse. For a message that needs no persuading, a horseman of your party can ride instead (see [A rider from your party](#a-rider-from-your-party)).

## Sending an envoy

Envoys are sent from the **encyclopedia**: open the page of the lord you want to reach and press **Send an envoy**. They can also be sent from the [Realm tab](royal-court.md#the-realm-tab) of the kingdom screen: choose the lord's house under Fealty, then **Send an envoy...** or one of the errands listed there, with the same windows. The button is there for any living lord outside your house; when nothing can be sent to them now, it is greyed and its tooltip says why (they are a prisoner, too young, there is nobody to send...). The encyclopedia stays open, the game pauses, and the windows open over it:

1. **What is the message?** Only the errands that make sense with this lord are listed. Those that cannot be asked right now are greyed, with the reason.
2. **The errand's own choice**, for some errands: the matter before the council, whose hand to offer, how much gold, which right to press, the terms of peace...
3. **Who should go?** Everyone of your house who could ride, each with their portrait and:
    - for an errand that needs persuading, the game's own **"% risk of failure"**, as when you send a companion to solve a quest;
    - the days of riding to reach the lord;
    - *"some risk of capture"* if the road passes close to an enemy town or castle.

    The safest choices come first. The tooltip says, in words, how that person would put it to the lord, and whether better, about as well, or worse than you would yourself. Those who cannot go are greyed, with the reason. Below them, always, **A rider from your party**: greyed, with the reason, for the errands only one of your house can carry, or when you have no horseman to spare.
4. **Send {envoy}?** A summary: the days there and back, what the envoy will say, the odds in words, and a warning if the road crosses enemy land. Press **Send**.

News comes back in letters starting *"A letter from {envoy}"* or in the envoy's own words, and every envoy on the road has an entry in your quest log, and in the Envoys section of the Realm tab, until they are home. When a letter asks for your answer (a price to pay, an offer to take or leave), it has one more choice, **Let me look into it first**: it closes without an answer, time stops, and the kingdom screen opens on your envoys in the Realm tab; the same letter comes back when you return to the map.

## Who can go

Any grown member of your family or any of your companions who is **riding in your party, or in the same town or castle as you**, and is not:

- already away on another errand, a prisoner, or wounded;
- a woman of your family who is with child;
- the governor of a town or castle, or riding with another party;
- busy with another task, or bound to fight a [duel](duels.md) as your champion;
- the one whose own marriage is being proposed.

You cannot send anyone while you are a prisoner, in a battle, in a conversation, or walking a town's streets or a keep (send them once you are back on the map or in a town's menu).

One envoy at a time rides to the same lord, and after an envoy's errand that lord may not hear another of the same kind for a while.

### A rider from your party {#a-rider-from-your-party}

Some messages are not worth sending one of your own: a lord only has to hear them. For those, one of the mounted soldiers of your party can ride with your word instead. In **Who should go?** he is listed as **A rider from your party**, with his troop's name, his risk of failure, his days of riding and the risk of capture, like everyone else.

- **Who goes.** A horseman of your party who is not wounded, of the lowest rank you have. He leaves your party when he rides off and joins it again when he is back.
- **What he cannot carry.** An oath of fealty, a marriage, terms of peace, a meeting, a pact between houses and a demand that a crown bow to yours are acts of your house, put by one of your own: for these the rider is greyed, with the reason.
- **How he speaks.** A plain soldier has no gift of speech, no character of note and no blood ties to anyone. Where the errand needs persuading he argues it as best a soldier can, and does worse than a kinsman or companion would; before a ruler who is no good friend of yours, he does a little worse again, just as a hired companion would. Where the answer is the lord's own whatever the messenger, he does as well as anyone: *"A horseman of your ranks would carry your word faithfully; how it is answered does not hang on who brings it."*
- **On the road.** He rides as fast as any envoy and runs the same risks. If he is taken, he becomes a prisoner of the town or castle that took him, and you will not see him again; a gift he carried is lost with him.
- **Coming back.** He returns to your party. A rider learns nothing of Charm or Roguery from his errand.

The lord takes him for what he is: letters and journal entries speak of *"your rider"*. One envoy at a time rides to the same lord, rider or not, but several riders may be out at once to different lords.

## How the envoy's speech counts

How much it matters who goes depends on the errand.

**Persuasions** (the council, a plot, a voice or a vote over a fief, winning a house over to your realm, demanding that an enemy ruler bow to you). The envoy argues it exactly as the game's persuasion would if they were the one talking: each argument uses their own skill and suits or grates on the lord's character by their own traits, and their own perks count instead of yours. What belongs to your house counts whoever goes: your house's relation with the lord (friendship is between houses), the lord's grudges, what is at stake. Only two things are added on top of the game:

- **Blood helps a little.** Whoever speaks to a lord of their own blood (a parent, a child, a brother or sister, or someone born into the lord's house) lands each argument a little more often. This counts for you in person too, in these same talks.
- **A hired companion before a ruler.** A ruler who is not a good friend of yours cares little to hear a hired companion, and that envoy does a little worse.

An envoy does not share the ease of a feast or a hunt you attend: that only helps when you talk there yourself.

**Meetings, sounding a lord out, and winning a house to your charter or reckoning.** The envoy's Charm (little of it hurts, much of it helps), how their character sits with the lord's (shared traits help, opposite ones hurt) and blood ties count, against your own. A hired companion before a ruler who is not your friend does a little worse, except when winning a house to a cause.

**Everything else** is a message: the answer is the lord's own, for their own reasons, whoever brings it. Only the ride and the road matter. The tooltip says so: *"{envoy} would carry your word faithfully; how it is answered does not hang on who brings it."*

## The errands

The names below are as the game shows them, with the lord's or house's name in place.

| Errand | To whom | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Ask {lord} to meet you halfway** | Any lord of a realm | The lord rides to a town or castle about halfway and waits for you (see *Meetings*). |
| **Sound {lord} out** | Any lord of a realm | The envoy learns where the lord stands (see *Sounding a lord out*). |
| **Carry a gift of gold to {lord}** | The head of a house not at war with you | Gold for goodwill (see *Gifts*). |
| **Propose a marriage between your house and the {house}** | The head of a house not at war with you | Offers your hand, or that of a lord of your house (see *Marriages*). |
| **Win the {house} over to your realm** | The head of a house of another realm, not its ruling house, even at war with yours | The game's own talk for bringing a lord over to your kingdom, with its four parts. If the head is won, they name the game's price for changing sides, and you pay or decline when the letter comes. |
| **Win {lord} over in the council** | The head of a house of your realm | Same as [winning the council over](council.md) in person, for a war or a peace with a realm you pick. Won over, that house votes your way on it. |
| **Win {lord} over to your plot** / **Keep {lord} loyal to the crown** | A lord of your realm | Same as in person: see [Civil war](civil-war.md). |
| **Ask {lord} for their voice when the next fief is shared out** | The head of a house of your realm | Same as in person: see [Winning the council over](council.md). |
| **Win {lord} over to your cause** | The head of a house of your realm | Wins a house to your charter or reckoning (see [Factions and grievances](factions.md)), by the same rules as in person. |
| **Swear your house's fealty to {lord}** | A new ruler of your realm, while the houses are swearing | Swears in your name, with no terms (to ask for something, go in person). Your house is sworn the day the envoy arrives; greyed if it would arrive too late. See [The oath to a new ruler](royal-court.md#the-oath-to-a-new-ruler). |
| **Ask {lord} to swear that your houses stand together** | The head of a house of your realm (greyed unless your houses are good friends) | Asks for a sworn pact between your houses, as in person (see [Factions and grievances](factions.md)). The answer is the house's own. |
| **Ask the {realm}, your allies, to join one of your wars** | A lord of a realm allied to yours | Pick the enemy (*"Against whom?"*, with the cost of each). When the envoy arrives, the ally names the game's cost of the call; pay it when the letter comes and the gold goes to their war chest, and they declare war. |
| **Offer the {house} a sellsword's contract with your realm** | The head of a minor house (you must rule your realm) | The game's own hiring of a minor house, with its refusals and its rate. If they will take it, you accept or decline when the letter comes. |
| **Carry your terms of peace to {lord}** | An enemy ruler, or the head of the other side in a civil war or feud (you must lead your own side) | The [Terms of Peace](war-and-peace.md#terms-of-peace) screen opens; set your terms and press **Send these terms** (only terms the ruler would accept now). The ruler answers when the envoy arrives, as the war then stands, and terms they take go to the vote of a council, as at the table. Free, as in person. |
| **Demand that {lord} bow to your crown** | An enemy ruler (you must rule your realm) | Demands fealty, or the crown itself where the rules allow it, as face to face (see [Vassal realms](vassal-realms.md)). If the ruler wavers, the envoy argues it for you. |
| **Offer your swords to the {house} in their feud** | A house at feud in your realm | Offers your sword (as a mercenary, your company for their silver). Whether they take it is up to them. See [Factions and grievances](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses). |
| **Ask the {house} to ride with you in your feud** | The head of a house free to take a side, while you head a side of a feud | As in person: the house decides for its own reasons; if it says yes, you pay the compensation for its men and it rides with you. |
| **Carry your defiance to the {house}: press a right by the sword** | The head of a house of your realm holding a fief or title your house has a right to | Once delivered, your house's purpose is known, as if you had said it to their face, and the [warning before arms](factions.md#warning-before-arms) begins: rumours, then your letter, then the feud. If the right can no longer be pressed when the envoy arrives, they write to say why. |
| **Carry a petition to {lord}: a right to a fief** | The emperor of your realm (Empire only) | Lays your right to a fief before the throne. See [The Empire's own ways](empire.md). |
| **Release {lord} from their oath to your house** | One of your [landed knights](landed-knights.md) | Takes back the knight's land: gently if you are on fair terms, as a seizure (with a grudge) if you are at odds. |
| **Offer {lord} an office at your court** | A lord of another house of your realm who could take an office (you must rule your realm) | Pick the office. The answer is the lord's, as if you asked in person (see [The royal court](royal-court.md#naming-your-court-as-ruler)). |
| **Offer {lord} command of the realm's lords in one of your wars** | A lord of your realm who could lead a front (you must rule your realm, at war) | Pick the war: the lord would lead the realm's lords against that foe as its Marshal of the Front, while that war lasts. The answer is the lord's, as in person (see [Command your armies](command-your-armies.md)). |
| **Ask {lord} for command of the realm's lords in one of the wars** | Your ruler | Asks to be named Marshal of the Front in a war you pick, weighed as if you asked in person. |

While your house rides with a feud's host, errands that need a voice in the realm's council, or would bring a house into the realm, are greyed: your house has no voice in the council until the feud is settled.

### Marriages
Choose **whose hand you offer**: your own, if you are unmarried, or that of a lord of your house. As between families in the base game, the other house chooses whom of theirs to give: its head if they suit, otherwise the eldest of its lords who does. You are told who before you send, and the dowry (only once the lord has been sounded out; otherwise *"price unknown"*). There is no courtship: the families settle it.

When the envoy arrives and the house still offers the same person, the game's **marriage offer** window opens as soon as you are free on the map, with both portraits, their ages, houses, occupations, relations, traits and skills. **Accept** pays the dowry and the marriage is made as the game makes it between families; you are told who joins which house, and whether the two houses will grow closer. **Decline** costs nothing.

If anything changes on the way or before you answer (they choose someone else, one of the two marries or dies, war breaks out between you), the match is off and a letter tells you so.

### Gifts
Three amounts, small, middling and large, each with a word on how much it would warm the lord. A gift is gold paid over in a bargain, as the game counts it: it buys only a little relation (more with the Charm perk *Tribute*), and each step dearer the further the lord already is from indifference, friend or foe. A lord who neither likes nor dislikes you is cheap to warm; one who already feels strongly about you takes the largest gift for a single step; what is left over may buy one more by chance. A gift is always received. After it, as after any bargain, the lord takes no other gift and will not bargain with you for a while; if you bargained with them lately in person, they will not take it and the envoy brings the gold back.

### Sounding a lord out
The envoy writes back with what they learnt:

- the lord's regard for their liege (loyal, dutiful, cool or bitter);
- for lords of your realm: their mind on each war and peace before the council, and their house's stance on a plot you know of;
- the price to join your realm;
- the dowries of their house for anyone of yours they could marry.

Each item is usually learnt, more or less often by the envoy's Charm, character and blood ties against yours, and by their **Roguery** against yours (little hurts, much helps, a master's helps more). What the envoy learns is always true; what they miss is listed as not found out. Prices and dowries then show in the envoy windows for a good while.

### Meetings
Whether a lord agrees depends on how they feel about you, before the envoy's effect: one who likes you nearly always comes, one who neither likes nor dislikes you usually does, and the more they dislike you, the less likely they are to come; one who hates you very seldom does.

The place is a town or castle at peace with both of you and not under siege, and it is marked on your map. The envoy's letter says in about how many days the lord should be there, and you get a message when they arrive. A lord who is late but still on the road is given a few more days; only a lord who never comes calls it off. Once there, the lord waits a few days, a little more with a good envoy, a little less with a poor one. A lord in an army, a governor, one riding in someone else's party, and one giving a feast or hosting a [hunt](hunts.md) cannot come.

- You talk to the lord there as usual. Meeting them warms them to you a little.
- If they wait in vain, they leave offended and think less of you.
- The same lord will not agree to another meeting for a while.

## Failure
A refusal costs nothing but time: the lord will not hear the same errand again for a while (every errand keeps the same waits as when you ask in person).

In the talks that can go badly in person (the council, plots, and a voice or vote over a fief), an envoy's failure can go badly too, about as often as their own talk would end in a critical failure: then the lord thinks less of your house and the wait is longer, exactly as after a critical failure in person. Winning a house over to your realm never goes badly: the talk only ends.

An envoy who wins a lord over learns from it (Charm experience); one who sounds a lord out learns Roguery.

## Travel and danger
- An envoy rides alone, far faster than a lord's party. Expect a day or two to a lord of your own realm, a few days across the map. The same again to come back.
- If the lord has moved on, the envoy follows for a few more days; farther than that, they turn back.
- If the road passes close to a town or castle of a realm at war with you, the envoy may be **taken prisoner** there, on the way out or on the way back, though not often. Gift gold is lost with them.
- An envoy received by an enemy ruler rides home under that ruler's **safe-conduct**: that realm's towns and castles are no danger on the way back, though other enemies still are.
- The envoy comes back to your party, or to the town they left from.
- If your character dies, every family member away as an envoy comes home at once, so your heir is not on the road.

## Turn it off
In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `EnvoysEnabled`: all envoys.
- `EnvoyRidersEnabled`: soldiers of your party as envoys. Off, only your kin and companions can go; a rider already on the road comes back as usual.
- One switch for each errand: `EnvoyMeetingEnabled`, `EnvoySoundEnabled`, `EnvoyGiftEnabled`, `EnvoyMarriageEnabled`, `EnvoyRecruitEnabled` (winning a house over to your realm), `EnvoyCouncilEnabled`, `EnvoyPlotJoinEnabled` and `EnvoyPlotLoyalEnabled` (the two plot errands), `EnvoyFiefClaimEnabled` (a voice when a fief is shared out), `EnvoyFactionWinEnabled` (a house won to your cause), `EnvoyPactEnabled` (a pact between houses), `EnvoyCallToWarEnabled` (an ally called to war), `EnvoyHireEnabled` (a sellsword's contract), `EnvoyPeaceTermsEnabled` (terms of peace) and `EnvoyDemandFealtyEnabled` (demanding fealty).
- The other errands go with their features: `FactionFeudPlayerJoinEnabled` (offering your swords in a feud), `FeudCourtEnabled` (asking a house to ride with you), `FactionFeudPressRightEnabled` (pressing a right by the sword), `ImperialPetitionEnabled` (the petition to the emperor), `OathEnabled` (swearing fealty), `LandedKnightsEnabled` (releasing a knight), `CourtOfficesEnabled` (offering an office) and `CaptainsEnabled` (offering or asking for the command of a front).
- `SpeakerKinEnabled`: blood ties no longer help in any persuasion, yours or an envoy's.
