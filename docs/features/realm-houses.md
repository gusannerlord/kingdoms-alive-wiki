# Traitors and the houses of a realm

In the base game a kingdom is as strong as the number of its houses, and a house only ever disappears. Kingdoms Alive lets a crown punish the houses that rose against it, makes a house that breaks with its realm rise against its crown, raises new houses when a realm has more land than lords, and lets lords go when it has more lords than land.

## After a civil war the crown wins

This follows a civil war the crown wins outright, or one it ends with a peace that grants the rising no pardon (see [Civil war](civil-war.md#the-outcomes)).

### The leader of the rising

The head of the house that led the plot is **put to death**. An AI ruler nearly always does it; a merciful one may spare the life. If you are the ruler, a window asks you: *"Put him to death"* or *"Spare his life"*. The sentence is carried out once the leader is in the crown's hands or free in the realm, never in the middle of a battle, and wherever you happen to be. If he flees abroad with his house, or it cannot be carried out before his house's traitor mark runs out, the sentence lapses. A death by the realm's sentence does not start a blood feud.

**The sentence comes before the judgment of his house.** While it stands, his house can only be pardoned, and a pardon lifts the sentence; any other judgment waits until the sentence is carried out, lifted or lapses. The same goes for the house the rising would have crowned, if it was not the leader's: it is judged right after it, and as hard, though its head is not condemned.

If **you** led the rising and lost, the same awaits you: you are brought before the ruler and, unless the ruler spares you, executed. Your heir takes over your house as after any death in the game; with no heir, the campaign ends.

His house is not banished: it comes back to the realm with its towns and castles, under its new head, and is marked as traitor like the others.

### Houses marked as traitors {#houses-marked-as-traitors}

Every rebel house that comes back is marked as traitor for a season, and the house that led the rising is remembered as such. While the mark lasts, the ruler may judge it **by decree**: no vote of the council, no influence spent, and the rest of the realm does not hold it against the ruler.

- **Pardon**: the house keeps everything and its head is grateful.
- **Rebuke before the court**: the house keeps its lands, but its head thinks less of the ruler and the house holds the shame against the crown (*"the crown shamed our house before the whole court for the part we had in the rising"*).
- **Take a town or castle**, or **all of them**: they go before the council to be granted anew (or to a new house, below), but not to another house of the same rising while the traitor marks last and anyone else can take them. The house stays in the realm and holds a grievance against the crown. With land, a duke loses his duchy and a noyan his ulus.
- **Banish**: like an expulsion by the council, but with no vote and no influence to spend: its towns and castles go before the council to be granted anew and the house leaves the realm with nothing. Its head, and its kin and friends among the realm's houses, think less of the ruler, though less than the lords who vote an expulsion. If no other kingdom takes it in, it fades away before long, as any house without land or kingdom does in the base game.

**Traitors forfeit land and titles.** For a while after it loses a rising, whatever the crown's judgment of it, pardon included:

- **No new land.** It is given no town or castle when the realm shares one out, wherever the fief came from, while any other house can take it; neither is a landed knight who rode with it.
- **Only a weak right** to a fief the crown took back from it in the rising, and it does not grow firm while that time lasts (see [Rights to fiefs](fief-rights.md#how-a-house-comes-by-a-right)).
- **No vassals.** The houses that would hold of it hold of the lord above it, or of the crown, and of it again once that time is over (see [Lords and vassals](titles.md#lords-and-vassals)).
- **No land, no title.** A house that comes home from a lost rising with no town or castle left loses its duchy or ulus at once, keeping only a weak claim to it.

A house that has just lost a war **rarely dares to defy** the judgment: its men came home beaten and few would follow it against the crown so soon. Now and then one still defies it, weighing its strength and that of the houses that would ride with it, its character and how it gets on with the ruler; the other houses beaten in the same war count for neither side. Then the ruler looks the other way or names it traitors, and it [breaks with the realm](#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm), as when any fief is [taken](royal-court.md#how-a-house-answers). Banishment is defied more readily, the loss of one fief least. A house that is to lose land may instead **leave the realm with nothing**: it gives up every town and castle it holds, which go before the council, and goes into exile like a banished house. A bold house (Valor), or one that bears the ruler ill will, takes that road more often, though seldom. If it bows and the sentence is carried out, its kin and friends among the realm's houses think a little less of the ruler; an exile costs the ruler the same.

**A house sworn to a plot** answers the judgment as it would a [decree](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal): its plot may rise over it, it may leave the plot and break alone, or it bows. A house just beaten in a civil war, with a weak plot behind it, rarely rises again; a strong plot still does.

A house that holds a [duchy or an ulus](titles.md#a-title-without-its-land) loses it with a sentence that takes land or banishes it, but only if the sentence is truly carried out; it keeps it with a pardon or a rebuke, unless it came home with no land at all (above). In an empire, a [strategos](titles.md#themes-of-the-empires) who rose has already lost his command. A [landed knight](landed-knights.md) rode with its lord and is not judged on its own.

**An AI ruler** judges the houses **one at a time**: the house that led the rising the day after the war, then another every few days, the one with the most towns and castles first (sooner, if the mark would run out before all are judged). It judges by its character, and it is hardest on the house that led the rising:

- **The house that led it**: now and then pardoned, more often by a merciful ruler; otherwise a cruel ruler takes everything and others take one town or castle; it is banished only now and then, and only from a realm of many houses.
- **The houses that followed**: often pardoned, more often by a merciful ruler, never banished and never stripped of more than one town or castle (and that only now and then). Each house that already lost land in the same judgment makes the ruler less likely to take from another; and a ruler with only a few landed houses to serve it does not take land at all. When it spares their land, it rebukes them before the court instead.
- **A house with no land** to take is rebuked, the house that led the rising too (the death sentence on its head stands apart).
- **A costly judgment softens the ruler**: every house that defies it or leaves for exile makes the ruler readier to pardon the rest, and slower to take land from those that followed.

**If your house is judged by an AI ruler**, the same rules apply. A pardon or a rebuke is simply announced. Otherwise a herald brings the sentence in the window *"The crown's judgment"*: *"Bow to it"*, *"Leave the realm with nothing"* (when land is taken from you) and *"Defy the crown"*. Like an AI house, you can defy only if your men would follow you: most of the time the option is greyed out (*"Your men came home beaten from the last war and want no more of it: none would follow you against the crown now."*). It is greyed out too if you have no town or castle left to hold. If you defy, the ruler may look the other way or name your house traitors, and then you break with the realm. If your house is sworn to a plot, *"Defy the crown"* means leaving the plot and breaking alone, and you may instead *"Raise the banner of the plot you are sworn to"*, by the same odds an AI house has. While your house is marked, or you are condemned, you cannot leave the kingdom nor ask to be released from your oath.

**As ruler**, a window asks you about each house when the war ends, the house that led the rising first, and tells you whether it led or followed: *"Rebuke the {Clan} before the court"*, take a town or castle, take them all, banish, pardon, or *"Not yet"* to decide later, then speak to the head of the house: *"Your house rose against the crown. Hear my judgment."* Your choices are your own, whether the house led or followed; the houses you judge may defy you or leave for exile by the same odds as under an AI ruler, and when one defies you, you choose to look the other way or name it traitors. You can also banish a marked house from the **Clans** tab of the kingdom screen: while the mark lasts, its **Expel** button reads *Banish*, costs no influence and needs no vote.

## New houses {#new-houses}

When an AI kingdom has a town or castle to share out (taken in a war, taken from a traitor, or left by a house expelled or gone; never a fief voted away from a house, nor one you took) and holds many more fiefs than houses to hold them, its ruler **raises a new house** and grants it that fief instead of putting it to the vote: *"{Ruler} has raised {Lord} to the nobility of {Kingdom}. The new house of the {Clan} holds {Fief}."*

- It happens only when the kingdom holds far more towns and castles than its houses, counting the one to share out (a balanced map at the start of a campaign rarely sees one). A kingdom of very few houses always raises one with its next fief.
- Every new house makes the next one harder: each house needs its own share of land, and a kingdom of many houses needs ever more land for one more.
- Not often in any one kingdom, and never during a civil war.
- The house is born from nothing, as in the game's own rebellions: a few lords of the kingdom's culture, one of them its head, a house of middling rank with two parties, with some silver to raise its men.

If the kingdom has a [landed knight](landed-knights.md) ready to rise, the fief goes to that knight's house first, and no new house is raised for it.

Your own kingdom is left to you: as in the base game, you make a new house by granting a fief to a companion.


## A kingdom takes in a house only with land for it

An AI kingdom does not take in a noble house (sellswords are not affected) if, with it, it would hold too little land for its houses. A banished house, or one cut loose when its kingdom falls, finds a new kingdom only where there is land to spare; otherwise it fades away.

## Knights of a house

A rich AI house with at least one town or castle, and fewer grown lords than the parties its tier allows, takes a **knight** into its service: a new lord of its culture who leads one of its parties. Not often, and only a few per house.

A knight is never the heir of the house and never weds. When the last of the blood dies, the house dies out as in the base game, and its knights with it.

## Too many lords for the land

An AI kingdom of many houses with fewer towns and castles than houses lets one landless house go at a time, now and then: *"There is no land in {Kingdom} for every lord who serves it. The {Clan} have taken their leave of {Ruler} to seek their fortune elsewhere."* The house that leaves is a landless one: first one of another culture than the kingdom's, then the one of the lowest clan tier, and between equals the one that likes the ruler least; never the ruler's, yours, a sellsword, or one in a plot, a faction or a feud. It leaves in peace and seeks another kingdom. Houses that leave of their own will (going over to another kingdom, rising) are another matter and can take a kingdom below that.

## A house that breaks with its realm {#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm}

A house that breaks with its realm (one the crown names traitors when it [defies](royal-court.md#how-a-house-answers) a decree, a verdict or a judgment, one that defies a sentence of the [assembly](assembly.md) or the crown's word in a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses), one that refuses to be expelled, or one that renounces its oath) **always raises its banner against the crown: a civil war** of its realm, with the house at its head and no plot needed before it. It is fought and ended like any [civil war](civil-war.md#the-rising), on the war score.

- **Who rides with it.** Its kin, its allies, the houses sworn to it as their lord, and the houses already plotting against that ruler may join it when it rises, never of their own accord. Each weighs it: whether the crown or the house was in the wrong (a fief taken for nothing, or a firm right barred, is the crown's wrong; a lawful vote, a sentence or an oath broken of its own will is the house's), how strong the rising is against the crown, what it thinks of the ruler and of that house, its own oath and its grievances. Blood comes first: kin heed only a wrong of the crown. A house that stayed with the crown may think again during the war, when the fortunes of the two sides or its feelings have changed by much.
- **If the rising wins**, the houses that rose choose among themselves who wears the crown, each by its right to the throne, its house, its kin and its friendships. A house of another culture or faith than its realm's may instead stand as a realm of its own; otherwise the peace joins the realm again.
- **If the crown wins**, the house comes back to the realm with what it has left; its head is condemned, and the house is marked and judged as a beaten rising (above).
- **The fief it broke over.** If the fief was being taken for another house (by the crown's decree, a lord's demand, the assembly's verdict), it goes to that house whenever the crown has it back: retaken in the war, sent back to the crown by the peace, or taken by the judgment that follows. It is not shared out. It waits while the fief is under siege, while the crown is not given, or while that house rides in a feud's host; if that house can no longer hold it, the fief is shared out as ever. A fief the crown was taking for itself goes to whoever then wears the crown.
- **It costs** the goodwill of every house of the realm it leaves (not the houses sworn to it, nor its own landed knights), once; a house that goes over to the rebels of a civil war or to another king does not pay it.
- **It leaves its plot** in that realm first; if it led it, the plot is over. The realm's other plots end with the civil war, and the calm after a civil war follows it.
- **During a civil war,** a house that breaks, AI or you, goes over to the rebels already in arms, with its fiefs.
- **To a foreign king.** Now and then a house that breaks goes over instead, with all its fiefs, to a king at war with its realm who is on good terms with it and has room for it (never to your realm), and hardly ever to one whose land lies far from its own.
- **A house with no town or castle** rises only if a house that holds one rides with it. If none does, it leaves alone, at war only with the realm it left, and may not make peace with it, serve a realm at peace with it, or go back to it for a long while.

**You.** Defying in any of those windows, if the ruler names your house traitors, or renouncing your oath, raises your banner: you lead the civil war, and the houses tied to you weigh riding with you. So does the kingdom screen's **Leave Kingdom** with **Keep all holdings**: a confirmation says so first. You cannot leave while your house is marked as traitors or you are condemned, nor while every town and castle of the realm is yours, nor while you ride with rebels in arms against the crown. "Relinquish all holdings", and leaving a contract as a sellsword, work as in the base game. If a house you are tied to breaks away, a window asks whether you ride with it; if it is your own lord, you are called as when a lord rises against the crown (see [When your lord rises](civil-war.md#when-your-lord-rises-against-the-crown)).

## Houses that change realms {#houses-that-change-realms}

**Far-off kingdoms.** An AI house with towns or castles very rarely goes over to a kingdom whose land lies far from its own, by land or by sea: no help could reach it there. A lord you try to win over from far away says so, and asks a great deal for the risk. Your own house is free to go where it will.

**A house without a realm** that holds towns or castles and looks for a kingdom to join favours one near its land, though a kingdom of its own culture still comes first.

## A house expelled by the council

When the council votes a house out of a kingdom (the game's **Expel**), its towns and castles go before the council to be granted anew, as in the base game and as a banished traitor's do. The house may refuse, as a banished traitor may: it breaks with the realm and [rises against the crown](#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm), by the same odds as a banishment. No vote to expel a house is put while its head is under sentence of death. A house with no town or castle cannot refuse, nor one in a plot, nor the ruling house, nor mercenaries, nor a house whose refusal would leave the realm with no other fief, nor one riding in your army (it is simply expelled); neither does yours. Only you ever put an Expel to the council: the AI never proposes one.

## Turn it off

In the [settings file](../settings.md), set to `false`:

- `CrownWinsExecutesLeaderEnabled`: the leader of a failed rising is not put to death; his house comes back like the others;
- `TraitorsEnabled`: no houses marked as traitors;
- `TraitorLandForfeitEnabled`: a house of a lost rising keeps its share of new land, its vassals, its firm rights and its duchy as any other (only the fiefs its judgment took are kept from the houses of the rising), and the fief broken over is shared out as ever;
- `RealmJoinNeedsLandEnabled`: AI kingdoms take in houses as in the base game;
- `RealmNewHousesEnabled`: no new houses;
- `HouseKnightsEnabled`: no knights;
- `RealmShedHousesEnabled`: no kingdom lets a house go;
- `ExpelRefuseEnabled`: a house expelled by the council always goes;
- `RuptureJoinEnabled`: a house that breaks rises alone, with nobody weighing whether to ride with it;
- `DefectFarEnabled`: far-off kingdoms are no harder to go over to; `IndependentNearEnabled`: a house without a realm does not favour near kingdoms.
