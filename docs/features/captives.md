# Captives

A lord in your baggage train is no longer just a ransom waiting to be paid. Their house may come to bargain, send hired swords after you or set knives in the streets. And the captive talks: how you keep them, at your table, in irons or as a laughing-stock for your men, is remembered long after they ride home.

## Part one: the house reacts

### How it starts

- Your **own party** takes a lord of another house prisoner in battle (not a companion, not a bandit chief).
- About **1 in 3** times (35%), the captive's house does something about it, **1 to 3 days** later, once the news has reached them.
- Only **one such matter at a time**. While one is under way, other captures bring no reaction.
- It needs a war: the captive must still be in your hands (your party, the dungeon of one of your fiefs, or a party of your house), and their realm still at war with yours.

What the house does is drawn by chance, leaning on the character of the head of the house:

| Reaction | Base chance | More likely if the head of the house is... |
|---|---|---|
| A messenger: "name your price" | about 40% | calculating |
| Hired swords hunting your party | about 35% | brave |
| Knives in the streets of the towns | about 25% | dishonest |

### "Name your price"

A messenger of the house stops you on the road (only out on the map, never in a town, battle or menu). An honourable lord asks "as one of rank to another"; a calculating one is "ready to listen to anything reasonable, and to a few things that are not"; others ask plainly what you want. Answer:

- *"Let us talk, then. Here is what I would have for {captive}..."* opens a window, **"The price of {captive}"**, with the offers below.
- *"Tell {lord} that {captive} stays where she/he is."* is a refusal. Closing the talk without an answer counts as a refusal too.

While the bargain is pending, the game's own ransom offers for that lord wait, and the captive cannot escape.

| Offer | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Coin** | About **1.5 times** the game's ransom value for that lord, or all their house can raise. |
| **A marriage** | Up to four matches the game's own marriage rules allow: an unwed member of their house with you or an unwed member of your house (you first). The offer warns you if your kin would leave your house to join theirs. |
| **A piece from their armoury** | Up to three pieces of their culture (armour, weapons, shields or horses), each worth about **4 times** the ransom. It goes into your inventory. |
| **Where a rich caravan rides** | Word of a caravan of their realm carrying at least **6,000** in coin and goods, close enough to catch. Offered only when one is really on the road nearby. |
| *"Nothing. {captive} stays my prisoner."* | A refusal. |

Any bargain struck sets the captive free and gives **+5 relation** with the head of their house. If the marriage cannot be made after all, the window opens again.

If you refuse, or the messenger cannot reach you within **5 days**, there is an even chance the house turns to force **2 to 4 days** later: hired swords or knives in the towns, as below. Otherwise the matter ends there.

!!! note
    While the captive rides in your party, it may be the captive who makes the caravan offer instead of a messenger (about **2 in 5** times): *"Let me go, and I will tell you."* Answer *"Very well. Tell me where it rides, and you are free."* or *"No. You will go home when your house pays, and not before."* Refusing is the same as refusing the messenger. This bargain does not raise your relation.

### The caravan: truth or lie

The caravan is marked on your map with an arrow, and a journal entry opens: **"The ransom of {captive}: a caravan on the road"**, saying where it is bound. You have **3 to 10 days** to catch it, depending on how far it is, and before it rolls through the gates of its destination.

- **The truth**: the packs are heavy, the quest is a success.
- **A lie**: the lord pointed you at their realm's poorest caravan. You only learn it when you take it: *"You have been played for a fool, and you will not forget it."* You lose **10 relation** with the liar.
- An **honourable** lord never lies. Otherwise there is about a **15%** chance, more for a calculating or dishonest one (at most 80%), and only if a poor caravan is about to point at.
- The tip ends if the caravan reaches its town, the time runs out, someone else takes it, or peace is made.

### Hired swords on your trail

Only while the captive rides **in your party**. A company of one of the game's minor mercenary companies (of the captive's culture when there is one), named **"Sellswords of the {house}"**, is hired against you:

- They are about **as strong as your party, up to 1.3 times**, counted by the worth of each man (from 15 up to 400 men).
- They ride out of the house's nearest fief within about 4 days' ride, or else appear near you, out of sight. Your scouts tell you where they come from.
- An **arrow** on the map points to them, and a journal entry opens: **"Sellswords of the {house} come for {captive}"**.
- They ride a little faster than an average lord. They **attack** whenever they are at least three quarters as strong as you and you are out in the open; otherwise they shadow you. Other lords leave them alone, and they do not parley: *"We are paid to bring a man home, not to talk. Stand aside, or don't."*
- They give up about **8 days** after they could have reached you.

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| You beat them | The captive stays yours; the quest is a success. Survivors who flee scatter. |
| They beat you | They take the captive back. If they take you prisoner too, they let you go: they came for their lord, not for you. |
| You put the captive in one of your dungeons, or hand them to another party of your house riding elsewhere | The sellswords lose the trail and ride home. |

### Knives in the streets

For **20 days**, men of a mercenary company wait for you in the towns. A journal entry opens: **"Knives in the streets: the {house} want {captive} back"**.

- Enter the town where the captive lies **in your dungeon**: a **9 in 10** chance they are waiting. That town is marked on your map.
- Enter **any town** while the captive rides **with you**: about **1 in 3**.
- A captive in a **castle** dungeon is out of their reach; they lie low and come back if the captive rides with you again before their time is up. The journal follows the captive as you move them.
- Never while you are wounded or the town is besieged.

A window titled **"Ambush!"** opens, and you fight in the streets: you and your healthiest companion or family member against **3 to 5** men (alone, against 3). Nobody of yours dies in it.

- **Win**: **2 renown**, and the house gives up.
- **Lose**: they break the captive out of the dungeon, or take them from your camp.

### When it all ends

Any of these ends the matter at once: peace with their realm, the captive escaping, dying, being freed or ransomed, or leaving your hands some other way.

## Part two: captives who talk

While you carry noble prisoners **in your own party**, every **3 to 5 days** one of them (held at least a day, the one who has spoken least lately) speaks to you on the map. What they say depends on how they have been kept and on their character: a brave one dares you to give them a sword, a calculating one wonders aloud what they are worth to you, a merciful one asks after their men, a starving one asks if you mean to starve them to death.

Your answer is how you treat them. Each captive keeps a tally of their keeping:

| Your answer | Effect |
|---|---|
| *[Have food brought from your own stores, and share your table with her/him.]* | Uses one of your best foods, and one wine if you have any. Treatment **+2**, **+1** more with wine. A little **Generosity**. Only if you carry food. |
| *[Have her/him put in irons...]* | Treatment **-1.5**, and **-0.2** each day in irons. Their chance to escape, and the game's ransom offers for them, drop to about **40%** of usual. |
| *[Have the irons struck off.]* | Treatment **+1**. |
| *[Drag her/him out before your men and make sport of...]* | Treatment **-4**. Party morale **+3**. You lose some **Mercy**, and **2 relation** with the head of their house. They never forget it. |
| *"Tell me about your realm. Where are its walls thin, and its lords weak?"* | See below. Once every **8 days** per captive. |
| *[Say nothing, and leave her/him to her/his thoughts.]* | Nothing. |

A **starving** party costs every noble captive **1** point of treatment a day.

### What they tell

Whether they talk at all depends on how you have kept them and who they are: a well-treated captive talks more; an **honourable** or **brave** one less; a humiliated one less. A cowardly one kept badly may talk to save their skin. When they talk, it is about the world as it is right now:

- **A weak fief**: a town or castle of their realm held by fewer than half the men (garrison and militia) of an average one of its kind, with a rough count.
- **Their realm's strength**: bled white (less than 60% of its enemies' strength) or with men to spare.
- **An army gathering**, where, under whom, and its target if it means to besiege. Only if they were taken in the last **5 days**.
- **Where their lord is**: the head of their house, or their ruler. Also only if taken lately.

!!! warning
    Captives can **lie**, and the lies are about real places: the best-defended fief named as weak, a realm said to be weak when it is not, an army "gathering" at a quiet castle, a lord "at" the far end of the realm. An **honourable** captive never lies. A calculating or dishonest one, or one you have treated badly, is far more likely to; one who is grateful, less.

### When they leave your hands

Freed, ransomed, escaped, traded, handed over or sent home by a peace, the captive settles their account with you:

- **Treated well** (treatment **+3** or more): they are grateful. Relation **+3**, plus their treatment, up to **+15**, and **+2** with the head of their house. If they escaped, the good treatment counts half.
- **Treated badly** (**-3** or less): they hold a grudge. Relation **-3**, minus their treatment, down to **-15**.
- In between, nothing.

A captive with a grudge may then **swear a feud and become your rival** (see [Your rival](rival.md)). The base chance is about **30%**, higher the worse you treated them, and much higher if you **humiliated** them or they are merciless, brave or dishonest; lower if they are merciful. Lords who can never be your rival (rulers, ruling houses, your family) only keep the grudge.

!!! tip
    [Lords who remember you](memories.md) also bring up how they left your hands: ransomed for a piece of their armoury, freed for word of a caravan, or broken out by their house's knives. A captive freed by a marriage remembers the wedding instead.

## Turn it off

In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `CaptiveDealsEnabled`: houses never react to a captive taken (no messenger, sellswords or knives).
- `CaptiveDealOffer`: no "name your price" bargains, from the messenger or the captive.
- `CaptiveMarriageOffer`: no marriages among the offers.
- `CaptiveCaravanTip`: no caravan tips among the offers, and captives never offer one.
- `CaptiveRescueParty`: no sellswords hunting you.
- `CaptiveTownThugs`: no knives in the towns.
- `CaptiveTalksEnabled`: captives never speak to you, and irons have no effect.
- `CaptiveTalkNews`: you cannot ask captives about their realm.
- `CaptiveGrudges`: captives are neither grateful nor bitter when they leave, and none becomes your rival.
