# Landed knights

Below the great houses of a kingdom stand its **landed knights**: houses of a single knight who hold the land of one castle (one of its villages, or both) of the lord of that castle, and owe that lord their sword. **One knight to a castle**: a house may have as many landed knights as it holds castles, and none without a castle (towns do not count). The same rule binds you and every lord of the AI. A house that loses castles keeps the knights it has, but takes no more until it is back under its number, and no knight ever swears to a house that has no room for him, however he comes to it (see *Losing the land*). The one exception is the lord who takes **you** as his knight (below). They are a step between a nobody and a house of the realm, for the AI and for you. In the Empire only the throne grants such land, as a **pronoia** (see [In the Empire: the pronoia](#in-the-empire-the-pronoia)).

## What a landed knight is

- A house of **one knight**, at **clan tier 1**. The encyclopedia shows the knight alone, with a line saying whose knight he is and which villages he holds. The page of his lord's house names its landed knights.
- He is dressed and mounted as the young nobles of his culture are when they come of age: a lord's harness, not a common soldier's. He has the face, hair and beard of a noble of his culture too, as does every new lord the mod makes (the knights a rich house takes into its service, the lords of a new house raised by a ruler).
- He holds **villages, and nothing else**, all of them of the same castle and lord. The game cannot give a village an owner of its own, so the village still belongs to the castle or town it trades with. The knight holds it **of the house that owns that castle or town**, and that house is his lord.
- The village's **taxes go to the knight**, not to his lord: that is what pays for his men. When his purse runs low the kingdom's treasury helps him only a little (at most 200 a day; a poor house of the realm gets far more), so he must live within his land.
- He fields **one party** and never calls an army of his own (he rides in other lords' armies).
- He has **no voice in the council**: no vote and no proposals. He never stands for a castle or a town, never claims the throne, never leads a plot, a reckoning, a charter or a feud, and holds no rights to fiefs. He can hold an office of the royal court, but not that of Marshal.
- The AI's landed knights **never wed**. You may.
- He follows his lord: if the lord breaks with the kingdom, rises with a plot or goes over to another crown, his knights go with him. In a feud between houses he rides with his lord: into his lord's host when his lord takes the field, and home again with him. He takes no side of his own, his men count in his lord's strength, and he is never judged for it (no felony, no brand, no grievance). A castle he takes in the feud goes to his lord. If the other side takes the castle of his village from his lord in that feud, he keeps faith with his lord and his land until the feud is done.

## How one is made

Every so often (about once every 20 days in each kingdom, while some lord has room for another), an AI lord grants a village of a castle that has no knight yet to a new knight of his culture. An AI lord only raises a knight when his house has **100,000** denars or more, and keeps him only while it can afford it: a house that stays under **30,000** for **30 days** in a row lets one of its knights go, the one its head likes least, and another 30 days later if it is still that poor. A knight let go for want of silver loses his land and a little of his regard for his lord, but bears him no grudge, and seeks another lord. The more prosperous the village, the likelier it is the one granted; a lord who already has landed knights of his own is less likely to make another, so they tend to spread among the houses with land. The lord gives the new knight some silver to raise his men, or what he has if his purse is thin.

A lord who comes to think highly of his knight may grant him **a second village**: the other village of the same castle, never one of another (a castle with a single village has no second to give). You can do the same with yours: *"You have served my house well. I would grant you another village to hold of me. (Landed knight)"*. As a landed knight yourself, you may ask your lord for one: *"I have held your land faithfully, my lord. Would you trust me with another of your villages?"* He answers as he would grant it unasked: only if he thinks highly of you and has a village to spare, and he chooses which. Turned down, do not ask the same lord again too soon.

As a lord you can also grant a village to a landless knight of your kingdom (*"I hear you hold no land now..."*); he then counts among the knights sworn to you.

You let one of your knights go yourself, in person or from afar:

- **Speaking to him.** With one you have come to despise: *"You have given me cause enough. I am taking back the land you hold of me."* He will not forget it: he holds it against you and thinks much less of you. With any other: *"I can keep you no longer. I release you from your oath..."*: his land comes back to you, he thinks a little less of you, and there is no quarrel between you.
- **By an envoy:** *"Release {knight} from their oath to your house"*, from the knight's page in the encyclopedia (see [Envoys](envoys.md)). The envoy tells him in your name when he arrives, the hard way or the gentle one depending on how the two of you stand then.

Your purse is your own affair: unlike the lords of the AI, you never lose a knight for want of silver, and making a companion a knight or taking in a landless one asks no sum in your coffers.

You can make one too: as a lord of a kingdom with a castle that has no knight yet, speak to one of your companions: *"You have served me well. I would give you a village to hold of my house, and make you a knight of {your kingdom}. (Landed knight)"*. You choose the village; those you cannot grant are greyed, with the reason. The companion leaves your party and becomes a house of their own, sworn to yours, with some of your silver to raise their men.

### Raising a knight yourself

In the clan screen, **House** tab, under *Oaths*, the button **Grant land to a worthy knight** raises a new knight from the worthy men of your kingdom, the way an AI lord makes one. You choose the village (those you cannot grant are greyed, with the reason); the new knight swears to your house, rides under your colours, and you give him the gift a lord gives a new knight (**10,000** denars, or what you have if less). Unlike an AI lord, your house needs no great purse to raise one. The button is always there, greyed with the reason when you cannot use it:

- you must be sworn to a kingdom (not as a sellsword), free, and not riding in a feud's host;
- the kingdom must have a ruler, enough houses and no civil war;
- one of your castles must have no knight yet and a village to give, not under siege;
- at most one knight raised this way every **20 days** (a companion you make a knight does not count).

## In the Empire: the pronoia

In the realms of the Empire (of imperial culture), only the **throne** grants land for service. The emperor may grant the **pronoia** of a village of **any castle of the realm** that has no knight yet, whoever holds that castle: the knight swears to the throne and takes the village's rents, and the house that holds the castle has none of them while the pronoia lasts, without a grievance or any loss of regard. The throne may keep as many knights as the realm has castles. No other house of the Empire swears a knight of its own (as a lord of the Empire you cannot make one either, and the House tab says so).

- A pronoia belongs to the throne: when another house takes the crown, the knights go with it and swear to the new emperor (yours too, if you lose the throne).
- If its castle changes hands inside the realm, the pronoia stays. If the castle goes to another realm, the knight loses it, and has it back if it returns.
- Knights of a lord who goes over to the Empire with his castles pass to the throne.
- As a knight of low standing, you ask the emperor yourself: *"My sword is the throne's, your majesty, if the throne would grant me a pronoia to keep it."* As emperor, the button reads **Grant a pronoia to a worthy knight**.

More on the Empire's ways: [The Empire's own ways](empire.md).

## Losing the land

| What happens to the castle or town of his village | What the knight does |
|---|---|
| Another kingdom takes it | He loses that village and stays in his kingdom. If he still holds villages of the same lord elsewhere, he keeps them; if not, he is left without land. |
| His kingdom takes it back before he has new land | He holds that village again, of whoever now owns the castle, if that house has room for another knight and that castle has no other knight. |
| It passes to another house of the same kingdom | If it was all he held, he swears to the new owner, if that house has room for another knight (yours too: you are not asked). If it has none, he loses the village and seeks another lord as a landless knight. If his lord still has other villages of his, he stays with his lord and loses only that one, so he never serves two houses. |
| His lord leaves the kingdom with it | He goes with his lord. |
| His lord is put out of the kingdom without it (expelled, banished) | He stays, and swears to whoever is granted the castle or town. |
| The village is raided | It stays his; while it lies raided it pays nothing, as in the base game. |

A lord who comes to **despise** his knight may take his land back. The knight never forgets it.

A landless knight seeks another lord of his kingdom with room for him who will grant him a village, the ruler first, then those who like him best (never the lord who has just let him go for want of silver). If none does for **24 days**, his house comes to an end. When an AI knight dies, his house dies with him and his castle is free for another knight. **48 days** after a knight's house ends, neither it nor its people are listed in the encyclopedia any more. A landed knight never keeps a castle or town that comes to him some other way (as the heir of a house that died out, say): if he is ready to rise and the kingdom has land for one more house he rises with it; otherwise it goes to his lord, or to the ruler.

A kingdom never makes landed knights while it has only one house or is torn by a civil war, whoever makes them (you included). If ever nothing but its ruling house and its knights is left to a kingdom (yours too), the most renowned of the knights becomes a full house the next day, without land: his villages go back to his lord, and an AI knight gains two more lords of his culture. So the crown always has a house to pass to, and if the ruling house is already gone he takes the crown. Not in a rising still at war while its leader's house lives.

## Rising to a house of the realm

Once a landed knight has grown in renown to **clan tier 2**, he is ready to rise. He rises when his kingdom has a town or castle to share out and **land for one more house**: the same rule as for [new houses](realm-houses.md#new-houses) (more than 2.2 towns and castles per house, a little more over 11 houses, and always in a kingdom of fewer than 5 houses; at most one new house every 30 days), even with the new houses switched off. Landed knights do not count as houses for it. A ready landed knight goes before a house raised from nothing; if he took that fief himself, it is his.

He receives the fief, gives his villages back to his lord, and becomes a house of the realm like any other, with a voice in the council. The AI's gain two more lords of their culture, so that they are a full house.

In your own kingdom you decide: when you rule and there is land for another house, a ready landed knight appears among those who may receive the fief in the share-out.

## You as a landed knight

- **Knighted as a squire**: at the end of the [squire's road](squire.md), if you are still of clan tier 1 or lower, the lord who knights you gives you one of his villages, if his house has a castle for you and the well-filled purse a lord of the AI needs to raise any knight. If only the silver is lacking, he knights you all the same and tells you, in his own words, that his house cannot spare the rents of a village.
- **Asking a lord**: of clan tier 1 or lower, sworn to no kingdom (or its sellsword), speak to the head of a house that holds a castle: *"My sword is yours, my lord, if you would give me land to hold of your house. (Landed knight)"*. He answers in his own voice: he must think well enough of you, and his house must have the well-filled purse a lord of the AI needs to raise any new knight, you being no exception. If it has not, he tells you so in his own words: his house lives on the rents of its villages and cannot spare one now. If he agrees, he names the village he grants you, as a lord of the AI would choose it (the more prosperous, the likelier).
- **A full house makes room for you.** If the lord's house already has a knight for every castle, he takes you all the same and dismisses the poorest and weakest of his knights. You get a free village of his castles if there is one; if not, the dismissed knight's land passes to you at once, and his castle is yours alone. If you got a free village, the dismissed knight leaves as soon as he is free (not a prisoner, not in a battle, a siege or an army), giving up his land as a knight who loses it does, and you are told; until then the house has one knight more than its limit, and takes no other. The dismissal is called off if the house is back within its limit without it (another of its knights gone, or another castle), or if you stop holding its land: you swear to another house, leave the kingdom or are released from your oath. If you lose your village to another kingdom, the dismissal waits: should its castle or town come back to your lord's house, the village is yours again and the knight still leaves. Only you: a lord of the AI never dismisses a knight to take another.
- A house with no castle keeps no knights, and a lord who has no village to give you does not offer it either: the line does not appear. Neither does it when his house is already over its limit or still has a knight to dismiss, or, with a knight in every castle, when none of them holds the land of a castle alone that he could give you, or that castle is besieged. Ask another.
- You can still swear to a ruler as a vassal at tier 2, as in the base game: becoming a landed knight is another road, not the only one.

The people of your village treat you as their lord: its notables greet you as such, villagers offer you their service, you may settle a village's troubles with a lord's authority, its menu welcomes you to the village you hold, its people gossip about you as their lord, and the matters a village brings to its lord (a festival, families seeking land) come to you.

While you are a landed knight you follow the same rules as theirs: no vote, no council proposals, no army of your own, no castle or town, no plots, reckonings, charters, feuds, alliances between houses or claims to the throne (those lines of conversation do not appear), no forged rights. You may marry. If your lord leaves the kingdom with the castle or town of your village, you follow once you are free on the map. If you leave the kingdom on your own, your oath is broken and the land with it.

In a feud your lord rides in, you ride with him: you are never asked to take a side, a knight owes his lord his sword in his quarrels. Once you are free on the map (and not in an army, nor in the middle of a sparring bout), you are taken into his host with him, still of the realm and still holding your land, and you come home when he does; you cannot leave the host on your own. A castle you take riding behind him goes to him, and you are told so.

If you lose your land, or a lord of the AI lets you go for want of silver, the journal says so (*"A knight without land"*), and lords of your kingdom may send a rider offering you a village. If none comes for 24 days, your oath no longer binds you: at clan tier 2 or more you serve the crown as any other house; below it you leave the kingdom's service, free to seek your fortune where you will.

## Errands from your lord

As a landed knight, your lord remembers you between wars. Now and then a rider of his people finds you on the road with an errand from him: **one at a time**, the next some 20 to 30 days after the last one ended or you turned it down, and never the same kind twice running. The rider waits until you are free on the map (not in a town, a battle, a siege or at sea, nor a prisoner); after five days without finding you he rides back.

He tells you what your lord wants and what he will pay. Answer *"Tell my lord it will be done"* or *"Tell him I cannot do it"*. Accepted, the errand goes into your journal with what to do, where and by when, and its place, person or party is marked on your map.

| Errand | What you do |
|---|---|
| **A work in your village** | Raise in your village the cheapest of the [village works](village-works.md) it still lacks: go there, choose *See to the works of the village* and start it, paying the builders yourself. When it stands, your lord gives you 85% of what it cost. Only when your lord can pay, never while the village is raided or has a work under way, and not again for 120 days after the last one. If raiders loot the village while it is being raised, the work is lost with what you paid, and your lord pays nothing for it, though he does not blame you. |
| **The rents** | The rents of your village are due at your lord's castle or town. A few carters wait at the village until you come for them, then walk on only while your banner is in sight; nobody bothers them on the road but, more often than not, one band of highwaymen waiting for them. Get them through the gates. If they are destroyed, you leave them far behind, or you are taken, the rents are lost. |
| **Clear the road** | A band prowls about one of your lord's villages: deserters while his kingdom is at war, the outlaws of the land otherwise. Find it and destroy it in time. |
| **Men for the garrison** | His castle's garrison is thin: bring seasoned soldiers (tier 3 or higher, not wounded) and hand them over to the garrison's commander: choose *Leave ... seasoned men with the garrison, as ... asked* in his castle's or town's menu and speak with him. |
| **Within the walls** | While his kingdom is at war: stay two days inside his castle or town. The days count only while you are inside; *Keep watch on the walls* in its menu lets the time pass, with a bar showing how much is left. |
| **A letter or a gift** | Put a letter (a gift, from a generous lord) into the hands of the head of another house: an ally of his, or a house of his kingdom he would have closer or make peace with. Talk to that lord: *"I bring a letter from ..."*. If he rides in an army, its leader can take it for him; if he lies at a siege, the guards of the siege lines will carry it (an option in the siege's menu); if you are both inside a besieged place, give it to him from the siege's menu. Both lords think better of each other, and he thinks better of you only if you put it into his own hands. While he cannot be reached (at sea, shut in a besieged place, in a battle), the errand's days do not run. |
| **Judge in his name** | Two notables of one of his villages quarrel over land. Find out what really happened and judge between them: see [Judging a quarrel over land](#judging-a-quarrel-over-land). |
| **Recruits for the house** | Bring recruits of his own people (tier 2 or lower) to his castle or town and hand them over to the garrison's commander, from its menu. |
| **A debt** | A notable of his town or villages owes his house money. Talk to him: reason with him (your Charm; once), press him (he and, in a town, its security suffer) or pay the debt yourself (he will remember it). |

**Done**, your lord thinks better of you, pays what he promised (a generous lord more, a tight-fisted one less) and your renown grows a little. **Refused**, he is a little displeased; **failed** or out of time, more. His words, through the rider and afterwards, are in his own voice: an honourable lord speaks of your oath, a cruel one does not shout but remembers, a calculating one makes a note of it.

An errand is called off, without blame, if your lord dies, you are no longer his knight or lose your land, the place is no longer his, or the lord you carry a letter to is taken captive. If his castle or town is besieged while you are outside it, the garrison and recruits errands wait instead: break in and hand the men over from the siege's menu, or, to keep watch on the walls, help break the siege (a battle won there by your side, and the siege lifted, is the errand done). A debt waits while the debtor's town is besieged or his village raided. While an errand waits, its days do not run; if it has to wait about 20 days in all, it is set aside without blame. The journal says each time how the errand is to be done now, and why. A lord with empty coffers pays what he has, and says so; a debtor who has spent his silver pays what is left. Only you receive errands: the landed knights of the AI do not.

### Judging a quarrel over land

Behind every quarrel your lord sends you to judge there is something that really happened, and each of the two notables tells it in their own way. An honest one tells it as they know it, though they may simply not know the whole of it; one without honour may dress the truth up, or lie outright.

1. **Hear them both.** Go to the village and talk to each of them (*"... sends me to judge in his name the quarrel over land between you and ..."*). You cannot judge until you have heard both. What each one says goes into your journal.
2. **Look for the truth (if you want to).** In the village's menu, *Walk the bounds of the land ...* takes a few hours, waiting by the village (you can leave off and go back in, and pick it up later where you left it), and shows you what the ground has to say. If someone else of the village knows something of it (the headman, if he is not one of the two), the journal names them: ask them, and they will tell you what they know, truthfully. Everything you find is written in the journal.
3. **Press the one you doubt.** Once what you found shows that one of them is in the wrong, you may confront that one once, with the game's usual persuasion. They may own the truth, or hold to their story; if you leave off before they answer, that was your one chance.
4. **Judge, before them both.** Speak to either of them: give the land to one, to the other, or split it. When you have found a reason, you give it as you judge.

How it goes depends on whether you found the truth:

- **A true judgement:** the one in the right is grateful and rises in standing in the village; the other resents it, though less if they owned the truth when pressed. The two make their peace, and your lord hears that you judged well: he thinks better of you than for other errands, and pays in full.
- **An unjust judgement:** the one you wronged bears you a grudge and goes to your lord with the complaint. He thinks less of you, pays you less than he promised, and you gain no renown for it. The quarrel goes on, and for a while the village's fields and herds yield less.
- **Splitting the land** is only fair when both had some right to it. If only one of them was in the right, the one who was in the right takes it as an injustice.


## Troubles of your fiefs

Your landed knights can take the troubles of your own lands off your hands: the game's own quests that the notables of your towns, castles and villages offer, the ones you could hand to a companion. Nothing is made up: only troubles that are really there.

**Giving one to a knight.** When a notable of a fief of yours tells you of a trouble, beside the game's answer for sending a companion there is **"My knight will see to it."** (only if you have landed knights). The notable asks *"Which of your knights shall we look for?"* and lists them, each with how they stand with you and the game's **"% risk of failure"**, as for a companion; the tooltip says how many men the knight would bring, how long the ride and the work would take, and whether, on bad terms with you, they might refuse. *"I am thinking of another knight."* takes you back. Once you choose, the notable has a word on your choice, and you send them off: *"So be it. A rider will carry my word."* If none of your knights can go now, the line is greyed and its tooltip gives each knight's reason (no men under their banner, in a battle or a siege, at sea, no road there, too few men fit for it and how many it asks, already about another trouble, or on such bad terms with you that they will take no charge).

**What happens.** A rider carries your word; if the knight is at sea, the rider waits a few days for them to come ashore. The knight answers in their own voice: a knight who is cool towards you may refuse, and one who is no friend of yours surely does. Agreed, you pay what the work asks, as you would for a companion, and the knight rides there with their own men and stays the days the work takes. **The trouble comes first**: while it lasts, no host can call the knight away (they leave one they were riding with, unless it is in a battle or a siege), and the line asking them to join your army is greyed with the reason. The quest is in your journal, and while the knight is on it nobody else takes it: the notable says your knight is already seeing to it.

- **Done:** the reward and the notable's thanks are yours, as with a companion; the knight thinks better of you (**+5**), gains a little renown and the skill experience a companion would.
- **Refused:** the knight thinks a little less of you (**−2**). **Failed:** less still (**−6**); a knight who fails at dangerous work comes back wounded.
- Done or not, dangerous work may cost the knight some of their men, as it would a companion.
- **Called off without blame**, with your silver returned (except silver for grain, which stays with the village): if the notable is gone or no longer needs help, the fief is no longer yours, the place is besieged or raided, the knight is taken, killed or left without men, or they cannot reach the place in time.

**Of their own accord.** While your realm is at peace, each of your knights who bears you no ill will may, now and then (a small chance each day), take up the nearest trouble in your lands by themselves, if they are not riding in an army. It costs you nothing and brings you no reward: the knight sees to it as a lord of the realm would. You are told when they set out and how it went, and it changes how the knight feels about you as above. You can still give them a trouble yourself whenever you like.

**The AI does the same.** Lords of the AI send their own knights to the troubles of their lands while their realm is at peace, by rider, under the same rules, and only to knights who bear them no ill will. When you meet a notable whose trouble a knight is already seeing to, they tell you so.

## Where to see it

- **Your clan screen, Fiefs tab:** as a landed knight, your villages appear there while your house holds no town, with their hearths, militia, lord and taxes.
- **Your clan screen, House tab, "Oaths":** your oath, your lord and your villages. It also shows how many landed knights your house may have (one for each castle) and how many it has, and which of your castles still have none; the knights sworn to you are listed there too, and the tooltip of your towns and castles in the Fiefs tab says who holds which village.
- **Kingdom screen, Clans tab:** choose a knight's house to see whose knight it is and its village. The village is also shown among the house's fiefs.
- **Kingdom screen, Court tab:** every landed knight of the kingdom, with their village and lord. They are under "Titles" in kingdoms that use titles, and under "Landed knights" in the others.
- **Oaths and duties:** a knight and his lord swear, greet each other, ask for relief and answer the call to the banner as any vassal and lord do (see [Dukes, counts and castellans](titles.md#oaths-and-duties)).

## Turn it off

In the [settings file](../settings.md), set `LandedKnightsEnabled` to `false`, or in the in-game options under *The houses of a realm*. `PronoiaEnabled` set to `false` gives the Empire the same knights as any other realm. Your lord's errands alone: `LordErrandsEnabled` (*Errands from your lord*), or one kind with its own switch.

The troubles of your fiefs: `KnightIssuesEnabled` (all of it), `KnightIssuesPlayerAutoEnabled` (your knights taking them up of their own accord) and `KnightIssuesAiEnabled` (the AI's knights doing the same).
