# Your rival

Beat the wrong lord in a tournament, or let a hatred fester, and someone may swear a feud against you. Over the following weeks they come at you in their own way: taunts, tales told to their ruler, hired blades in a hideout, bands hunting you, ambushes in town streets. At the end, always, a duel to the death in the arena.

You have at most one rival at a time. Each rivalry opens a quest in your journal, **"The feud with {rival}"**, with your rival tracked on the map. If you meet your rival's party on the map, they greet you with a few sharp words before the usual talk.

## How a rival appears

- **A tournament.** Until your first rivalry, each tournament you win with lords in it has a **30%** chance that one of them swears a feud against you; after that, **1%**. The rival is the lord you beat in the final if they can be one; otherwise another lord who took part (cruel, brave or hostile ones likelier).
- **Hatred.** Every 10 days, if some lord stands at **-30 relation or worse** with you, there is a **3%** chance that one of them declares themselves your rival. The more they hate you, the likelier they are to be the one.
- **A lord you served badly.** If you walk out on the lord you serve as [squire](squire.md), or are sent away in disgrace, they may swear a feud: likely for a lord who is neither generous, merciful nor honourable. Such a lord can be of a ruling house (never the ruler).
- **A captive you mistreated.** A lord you held prisoner and treated harshly may swear vengeance when they go free; humiliating them makes it much likelier. See [Captives](captives.md).

There is no fixed wait between rivalries. A house that has already had a rivalry or a blood feud with you is about **10 times** less likely to produce another rival.

### Who can never be your rival

- A king or queen, or anyone of a ruling house.
- Your own family (parents, children, brothers and sisters, spouse), or anyone of a house where such a relative lives.
- A lord who likes you (relation above **+20**).
- If you rule a kingdom, anyone of your own kingdom.
- The lord you serve as [squire](squire.md), for as long as you serve them.

## The blood feud

When the rivalry begins, you hear how it came about, in words that depend on why they hate you, and your relation with the head of their house drops by **15**, and to **-20** if it was still higher. Their house enters the game's own **blood feud** with yours:

- If a party of their house captures you, **you will be executed**.
- Members of your clan they hold prisoner can be executed too.
- Parties of your clan led by others will execute any member of their house they capture.

A blood feud their house already had with you stays as it was and outlives the rivalry. If the head of their house changes, your relation with the new head also drops to -20.

### Ending the blood feud

- **Pay.** Go to a **ransom broker** in a tavern and choose *"I’m involved in a blood feud, and I would like to end it."* It costs **5,000 denars**, plus **50,000** for each member of their house you have executed beyond the members of yours they have executed. It ends the blood feud and the rivalry, and your relation with the head of their house goes back to what it was before you paid (never above -20), instead of the game's -49.
- **Make peace with their house.** If your relation with the head of their house climbs back to 0, the game ends the blood feud and the rivalry with it.
- **Kill your rival.** In the duel, in battle, or by execution: the blood feud ends with them.

!!! warning
    The first time you execute someone of their house yourself, you lose relation with other houses, as the game charges for any execution feud. If it is not your rival, the feud becomes an ordinary blood feud that **outlives the rivalry**. The same if they execute one of your clan, or if your hunting accident against their house is found out (see [Hunts](hunts.md)).

## The letter

One or two days after the feud is sworn, once you are out on the map, a messenger stops you and reads you your rival's letter, with a warning about the blood feud and a hint that the ransom brokers in the taverns can end it for a price.

Accept (*"Tell {rival} I have read it, and I accept. [...] We will see who is left standing."*: a little Valor, and they soon send back a taunt) or *"[Take the letter, tear it in two, and ride on without a word.]"*; closing the talk counts as silence too. Either way the rivalry goes on the same.

## The plan

Each rivalry draws its own plan, so no two go alike. The first step comes **3 days** after your answer, then one every **5 to 9 days**. The plan may hold:

| Step | How likely |
|---|---|
| Taunts (a mocking song in the taverns, a wooden sword sent as a gift, a wager at a feast that you will not last the year...) | 0 to 3 |
| A word to their ruler | 60% |
| A hideout of hired blades | 45% |
| Bands hunting you | none 25%, one 40%, two 25%, three 10% |
| A larger company | 40%, only after a band or the hideout |
| Street ambushes | none 40%, one 40%, two 20% |

A **brave** or **honourable** rival sends about 40% less of everything and comes to the duel sooner; a **calculating** or **cruel** one sends about 40% more killers. Never more than three bands and two ambushes.

The steps come in a shuffled order. While a threat is still out (a band on the road, the hideout still manned, a challenge waiting), the next one waits. Nothing happens while you or your rival are prisoners, or while your rival rides with you.

### A word to their ruler

Once per rivalry, your rival speaks against you to their ruler: you lose **3 relation** with that ruler. Never if you rule a kingdom yourself.

### The hideout of hired blades

Your rival hires **35 men**, their captain among them, who hole up in one of the hideouts near you. It is marked on your map and named after the company, such as **"Wolfskins Hideout"**. No ordinary bandits inside: their **Hired Captain** is the boss you face at the end, as in the game's own hideouts, and cannot be taken prisoner. Leave them alone for **15 days** and they come out as a band looking for you.

### Bands hunting you

**"Hired Blades of {rival}"** appear near you, just out of sight: **20 to 40 men** depending on how far your campaign has gone. An **arrow** at the edge of the map points to them, and they are tracked in your journal.

- They ride faster than a lord's party, so you cannot simply outrun them.
- They **chase** you while they are more than 1.2 times as strong as your party and you are on the open map; otherwise they keep on your heels. Lose men, and they will come.
- Other lords leave them alone, and they do not talk. After **6 days** they give up.
- While you have fewer than **15 healthy men** the band waits; after two such delays it is dropped. At sea it waits until you land.

### The larger company

After a band or the hideout, your rival may pay for more: **"{company} in the Pay of {rival}"**, **37 to 70 men** led by elite mercenaries. It works like a band and gives up after **8 days**.

### Street ambushes

Each time you enter a town while an ambush is planned, there is a **1 in 10** chance it waits there. A window titled **"Knives in the alley"** opens and you fight in the streets:

- You and your healthiest companion or family member against **3 to 5** of the company's men. Alone, you face 3.
- **Win**: **5 renown**. **Lose**: you are robbed of up to **500 denars**, but nobody of yours dies.
- At least **12 days** apart, never while you are wounded or the town is besieged.

### Hired companies

All of your rival's men come from one of the game's minor mercenary companies of their culture (Wolfskins for Battania, Lake Rats for Sturgia, Karakhergit for the Khuzaits, Skolderbroda for the Nords...); the later in the campaign, the more veterans.

## The duel

The last step is always a duel to the death in the arena, fought by your rival in person. A window titled **"A challenge"** opens, and it works as any duel (see [Duels](duels.md)): fight in person, send a champion, or refuse. Refusing, or not turning up, costs **25 renown** (no influence), and they challenge you again **30 days** later.

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Your rival falls (to you or your champion) | Your rival **dies**. You gain **40 renown**, and the rivalry and the blood feud are over. |
| You fall in person | You are badly wounded, not killed. You lose **20 renown**, their house gains 20, and the rivalry ends in their favour. The blood feud ends too. |
| Your champion falls | Your champion **dies**, with the same result as losing in person. |

!!! note
    If your kingdom and theirs are at **war**, there is no arena to meet in: the duel waits for peace.

## How a rivalry ends

- **The duel**, won or lost.
- **The blood feud ends** (see above).
- **Your rival dies** some other way, or becomes **family** (by marriage).
- **Your rival, or their house, comes to rule a kingdom**, or their house joins **your** kingdom while you rule it.
- **It goes cold**: **150 days** with nothing coming of it (say, a long war that keeps the duel away).
- Your heir takes over: the feud was with the one before.

A blood feud that began with the rivalry ends with it, unless it has become an ordinary blood feud (see the warning above). The quest closes as a success if you win the duel or the blood feud ends, as a failure if you lose the duel, and is cancelled otherwise.

## Turn it off

In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `RivalsEnabled`: no rivals at all. A rivalry under way ends quietly, with its blood feud.
- `RivalHideoutEnabled`: no hideout of hired blades.
- `RivalBountyEnabled`: no bands hunting you and no larger company (the hideout's men no longer come out after 15 days).
- `RivalTownAmbushEnabled`: no street ambushes.
- `RivalDuelEnabled`: no duel at the end. The rivalry then lasts until the blood feud ends, your rival dies, or it goes cold.
- `RivalSmearEnabled`: your rival never speaks against you to their ruler.
