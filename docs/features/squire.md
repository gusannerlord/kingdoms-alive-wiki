# Squire service

Kneel to no crown yet: ride instead at the side of a great lord as their squire. They dress you in the colours of their house, pay part of your company's keep and send you on errands by messenger. You ride to their wars, fight beside them, and one day kneel for your spurs, stay on at their side, or walk out and make an enemy of them.

## Offering yourself

Talk to a lord, choose *"There is something I'd like to discuss"* and then **"My lord, I would ride in your service, as your squire."** (*"My lady"* for a woman).

The lord must belong to a noble house of a kingdom (not a mercenary company or a minor clan), be free and not of your house. A ruler can take you too. If you cannot ask, the line is greyed out and tells you why:

- You are sworn to a kingdom, or serve **another** kingdom as a mercenary. (Already a mercenary of **their** kingdom is fine.)
- Your clan is still at **tier 0**.
- The lord dislikes you (relation below **0**).
- You are at war with their kingdom.
- The lord is your [rival](rival.md).
- You are still on a quest this lord gave you.

The lord states the terms, in a voice that fits their character. Answer **"Then I am yours to command. I will serve you faithfully, and your house with you."** or **"Let me think on it a little longer."**

## What you get

- **Their banner.** Your clan joins their kingdom as **mercenaries, without the crown's pay**: "my word is your wage". While you are a squire, the crown sends you no [mercenary contracts](mercenary-contracts.md); your lord sends you errands instead.
- **Their gear.** The battle gear of a **tier 3 noble troop** of their kingdom's culture, in the game's "receive items" screen. Only once per campaign: a second lord tells you to keep the harness you have.
- **Half your wages.** Every day the lord pays **half of what your men cost**, up to **200 denars a day**, and never more than they have. It shows in your daily income as **"Upkeep from {lord}"**.
- **A journal entry, "Squire to {lord}"**, with a bar *"Errands done for your lord"* out of **10**. The lord is marked on your map with the game's arrow, even out of sight, for as long as you serve them.

## Errands by messenger

A messenger brings the first errand about **3 days** after you swear, then one every **6 to 12 days**, never sooner than **3 days** after the last one ended. One errand at a time. The messenger waits until you are free on the map, and gives up after **5 days** with no blame.

The lord picks at random among the errands that make sense near you. Answer **"Tell her/him it will be done."** (it goes into your journal) or **"Tell her/him I cannot do this."**

| Errand | What to do | Time | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A tournament** | Fight in a tournament in their name and reach a given round, or win it (it depends on the game's quest difficulty). The towns holding one are marked. | 25 days | 1,500 |
| **The wager** (a lord without honour) | Reach the final of a tournament and **lose it**. Winning or falling sooner fails. Costs you some Honor. | 25 days | 3,000 |
| **A letter** | Find the lord it is for (marked on the map) and choose **"I bring you a letter from {lord}."** They think a little better of you. | the ride and a few days | 500 |
| **A threat** (a lord without honour) | The same, but the letter is a threat: the reader thinks less of you, and it costs you a little Honor. | the ride and a few days | 500 |
| **A feast** | Spend **12 hours** in the hall of a [feast](feasts.md) of their kingdom while it lasts. | until the feast ends | — |
| **Sellswords** | Hunt down a company of **Skolderbroda** sellswords loosed on their lands by their enemies, some of them veterans. Marked on the map. | 15 days plus the ride | 2,500 |
| **Taxes** | The game's own **Revenue Farming** quest, for one of their towns. | the game's | the game's |
| **Garrison** | The game's own **Lord Needs Garrison Troops** quest, for one of their thin garrisons. | the game's | the game's |
| **Drill** | Ride to one of their fortresses and choose **"Drill with the men-at-arms"**: a day in the yard gives experience in **3** weapon skills (150 to 300 each). | the ride and a few days | — |
| **A hideout** | Clear a bandit hideout near their lands. Marked on the map. | 15 days plus the ride | 2,000 |

Every errand done also gives **+3 relation** with the lord (+1 for the game's own quests, which pay their own reward) and a little renown. A **generous** lord pays a quarter more; a **closefisted** one a quarter less.

Errands that cannot be finished through no fault of yours lapse with no blame: you were a prisoner, the reader died, the feast was called off, someone else cleared the hideout.

### Failing your lord

| | Relation with the lord |
|---|---|
| Refusing an errand | −2 (−6 if it was one of their dirty tricks) |
| Failing an errand | −3 |
| Not coming to their host in time | −5 |
| Leaving their host, or fleeing a battle they fight | −8 |

**Three** of these in a row and the lord sends you away (a further **−10**). The second one brings a warning. Any errand done resets the count.

## To arms

Whenever your lord **raises a host or joins one**, a rider tells you at once and a journal entry **"To arms: {lord}'s host"** opens. Join it within **2 to 8 days** (depending on the ride): speak with your lord (*"I have come to ride at your side, as you bade me."*) or with the host's leader. Then stay until it breaks up.

- Riding it out (at least **half a day**) counts as an **errand done**.
- Leaving before it breaks up is **desertion**, unless you were captured, the host fell apart, or there were no ships for you to cross.
- No other errands come meanwhile, and one under way may go undone without blame.

### When your lord rides out alone

When your lord's own party (not a host) sets out to **raid** an enemy village, **besiege** an enemy town or castle, or **relieve** a place of their kingdom under siege or raid, a rider brings word in their voice. A journal entry opens, **"At {lord}'s side: the raid on {place}"** (or *the siege of*, *the relief of*), with the place and your lord marked. If they turn elsewhere, the entry follows.

Reach them in time (in their raid, their siege camp, their battle, or riding beside them) for **+1 relation**. If you cannot make it, nothing is lost. The word only comes when you are not already with them, not in a host, not a prisoner and not too far away. The same place is not called again within a week.

### When your lord fights without you

When your lord's party goes into battle against an enemy kingdom or clan and you are not there, you get the game's own quick notice, with their portrait: *"{lord} is raiding {village}."*, *"{lord} is storming the walls of {town}!"*, or the game's own text when they are attacked. Once per battle, never for fights with bandits.

## In battle at their side

- Your lord is **outlined in gold**, rider and horse, seen through walls and crowds, in every battle they fight on your side.
- Once deployment is over, you are moved **to their side** in field battles, siege assaults and sallies, wherever the ground allows (never at the foot of a wall or across it, and not while either of you is on a ladder or a siege engine). Not at sea. *"You take your place at {lord}'s side. Keep her/him in sight: her/his colours shine gold above the press."*
- **Stand over them** when they fall (within about 12 metres): **+4 relation**. Otherwise, stay near them for most of the fight: **+1**.

## Rescuing your lord

If your lord is taken, **2 days** later a messenger of their house finds you: *"Forgive me, I have ridden hard."* You have **40 days**, and the entry tells you how: break into a town's dungeon or see the town taken, take the castle, clear the hideout, or beat the party that drags them along. Answer **"I will get her/him out."** or **"I will do what I can. I promise nothing."**

Freeing them gives **3,000 denars**, **+10 relation** and counts as an errand. Failing costs nothing. While the rescue is on, your lord rarely escapes on their own. Other errands wait while they are held.

## Kneeling for your spurs

After **10 errands**, your lord sends word. If they rule (or serve no crown), they knight you themselves; otherwise they write to their ruler, and you must go there. Choose **"{lord} sent me to you, your Majesty. I have come to be knighted."** (or *"The errands are done, my lord. I have come for what you promised me."*), hear the speech, then:

- **"(Kneel) I swear to serve you and your realm, with my sword and my house, for as long as I draw breath."** You become a **vassal** in the game's own way, with its gifts of gear and troops (once per campaign) and influence. **+10 relation** with your lord and **+10 renown**.
- **"Not yet. Give me a little time before I bend the knee."** The word stands; come back later.
- **"I am honoured beyond words, but I would keep my sword my own."** You leave with your lord's blessing (**+5 relation, +5 renown**) and stay a mercenary of the kingdom, now with its usual pay.

If your lord dies after **7 or more** errands, their ruler keeps the promise. With fewer, the service ends with no blame. If you swear to the crown by your own word first, the service ends well. If your lord's house changes kingdom, you follow them.

## Staying on

When the errands are done, a window opens: **"A letter from {lord}"**. They offer the spurs, or to keep you at their side a while longer, without the oath. The buttons: **"I will ride to {ruler} for my spurs"** and **"I will stay at her/his side"**. While the knighting waits, you can also tell them in person: **"My lady/lord, I would rather not leave your side yet. Let me stay on as your squire."**

Staying gives **+5 relation**. "Squire to {lord}" closes as a success and **"In {lord}'s service"** opens:

- No more errands.
- Still called to every host, to their raids, sieges and reliefs, to fight at their side and to rescue them.
- They still pay half your wages, and they stay marked on your map.
- Deserting can still get you sent away.

Later, in their conversation:

- **"I think I am ready for my spurs now, my lord, if you will still speak for me."** The knighting is due again, by their hand or the ruler's. Until you kneel you still ride with them.
- **"It is time I rode my own road, my lord. I have come to ask your leave."** You part with their blessing: no anger, no rival, and you stay a mercenary of the kingdom with its usual pay. Leaving their kingdom after staying on counts the same way.

## Walking out

Before the errands are done, you can say **"I must ask you to release me from your service."** The lord warns you; **"My mind is made up."** confirms it. Ending your contract with their kingdom counts the same.

- **−20 relation**, and your contract with their kingdom ends.
- The lord may become your [rival](rival.md). The chance starts at **3 in 4**: each of **generosity, mercy and honour** they have lowers it by a quarter, and each they lack raises it a little. A lord who is all three almost never does.
- A lord who **sends you away** costs **−10 relation** but never becomes your rival for it.

If you leave the kingdom while the knighting waits (and you had not stayed on), the promise lapses quietly.

## They remember

A lord you served remembers it for good (see [Lords who remember you](memories.md)). What they say depends on what you have become (still their squire, a modest knight, a great lord, a ruler, a sellsword) and on how you get on now: proud, cool, or bitter. One you walked out on, or who sent you away, remembers that instead.

!!! tip
    Errands are sought near **you**, so ride close to your lord's lands to get the best of them. And stay near your lord when they are at war: a host or a siege called while you are far away is hard to reach in time.

## Turn it off

In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `SquireEnabled`: no lord takes a squire. One serving now is released within the hour, with thanks and no blame.
- `SquireTournament`: no tournament errands (nor the wager).
- `SquireLetter`: no letters to carry (nor threats).
- `SquireFeast`: no feast errands.
- `SquireBand`: no Skolderbroda sellswords to hunt.
- `SquireTaxes`: no Revenue Farming errand.
- `SquireGarrison`: no garrison errand.
- `SquireTraining`: no day of drill.
- `SquireHideout`: no hideouts to clear.
- `SquireArmyCall`: you are not called to your lord's hosts.
- `SquireRescue`: no word comes when your lord is taken.
- `SquireBattleSide`: no gold outline, and you start where the game puts you.
- `SquireStayOn`: no letter and no choice to stay on: after your errands you go for your spurs.
- `SquireMarkLord`: your lord is no longer marked with the map arrow.
- `SquireMarchCall`: no word when your lord rides out alone to raid or besiege.
- `SquireMarchDefend`: no word when your lord rides out to relieve a place of their kingdom.
- `SquireBattleAlarm`: no notice when your lord goes into battle without you.
