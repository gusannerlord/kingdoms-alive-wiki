# Recruitment by the land

In the base game, men are a resource that refills by itself: any lord, or you, can ride into any village, buy whoever is waiting and ride out again, and the village never notices. With Kingdoms Alive, soldiers come from the land and belong to it. A village's young men owe their service to the lord of that land, a thriving place sends more of them than a poor one, every man raised is missed at home, the best of them wait for their own lord, and a veteran is made on campaign, not bought at the well.

It is on from the start of a campaign. The same rules hold for you, the lords of the AI and the garrisons alike.

## Where the men come from

You recruit as in the base game, from the notables of villages and towns, with the game's own recruit screen. What changes is how many men come forward, and who may take them.

- **A rich place sends more.** A crowded, thriving village finds new volunteers faster than a thinly peopled one; a prosperous town faster than a poor one. The recruit screen says so under its title, in words: *"The village is thinly peopled; few young men are left to take up arms."*, *"The town thrives, and there is never a lack of men looking for a soldier's pay."*
- **War stops it.** A village being raided, or burnt and not yet rebuilt, gives no new men, and neither does a town under siege. Those already waiting stay. The screen tells you why: *"Raiders are in the fields; no man will leave his family to come forward now."*
- **No veterans wait at the well.** A man waiting with a notable grows a little more seasoned the longer he waits, as in the base game, but never into a veteran. The higher ranks are earned in your service.
- **Castles have men too.** Every castle now keeps a muster of its own (see [The castle's muster](#the-castles-muster)).

The priests of the [faiths](faiths.md) and the [sheriffs of the writ](kings-writ.md) keep their own rules for the men they give.

## Every man is missed at home

Each man you raise is a son taken from his family. A village loses a little of its hearths for every man raised there, and a town a little of its prosperity. It is not taken all at once: it is paid off over the next days, and it shows in the game's own breakdown of the village's growth as **"Men gone to war"**. A castle's men come from its villages, the bigger ones giving more.

Raise men in the same village again and again and it shrinks. Spread your recruiting over your lands, or let a village rest, and it grows back.

## The right of levy

The men of a land owe their service to its lord.

**Who is the lord of the land:**

- A village is its landed knight's, if one holds it (see [Landed knights](landed-knights.md)); otherwise it is the land of the house that holds its town or castle.
- A town or a castle is the land of the house that holds it.

**On your own lands**, you may take every man the notables there offer. So may your garrisons, and the landed knights sworn to you, in your lands. Even the best of them are yours: the young nobles (see [Young nobles](#young-nobles)) are kept for you.

**On anyone else's lands** you are an outsider, and so is everyone else: a lord of another realm, a sellsword, a house with no land there, and also the ruler and the great lords, outside their own fiefs. A duke's or a count's vassals hold their lands for him, but the men of those lands answer first to the vassal. An outsider:

- takes only the cheapest of each notable's men (a little more in a town than in a village), however much the notable likes him;
- never takes the young nobles waiting there: *"The well-born I keep for {lord}, their lord."*;
- pays a **fee for the right of levy** on top of the price, to the head of the lord's house. It shows on the recruit screen as **"The right of levy"**, and you are told what you paid: *"You paid ... to ...'s steward for the right to raise men in ..."*.

When others raise men on your lands, the fee comes to you: *"... raised ... men on your lands and paid ... for the right."*

The notables say it in their own words when a man is closed to you: *"These men owe service to {lord}. Their lord's own retinue comes first."*

**At war with the place**, nobody there gives you a man: *"This place is at war with you. No one here will go with you."*

**A notable who dislikes you** gives even the lord of the land only part of his men: *"The custom owes you some of these men, and you shall have them. The rest will not march for you while there is bad blood between us."* Mend the relation and the rest will follow you.

**Battanian clients.** In the Battanian hills a man follows those he is bound to by friendship as well as by land. In a Battanian village, a notable who thinks highly of you opens a few more of his men to you even where you are an outsider.

**Your skills still count.** The game's perks that open more of a notable's men (**Artisan Community**, **Combat Tips**, **Firebrand**, **Flexible Ethics**, and your engineer's **Engineering Guilds**) open as many more in lands not your own, after what an outsider may take, the young nobles too once the rest are yours. The lords of the AI use their own perks the same way.

**Serving for pay.** While your house serves a realm as mercenaries, you may take a little more of each notable's men than other outsiders.

## The castle's muster

Every castle has a **keeper of its muster**, a notable of the castle's people with the title his people give the office: the **Castellan** in Vlandia, the **Kastrophylax** in the Empire, the **Voivode** in Sturgia, the **Penteulu** in Battania, the **Darughachi** among the Khuzait, the **Qa'id** among the Aserai and the **Lendmann** among the Nords. You can speak with him: *"What does the keeper of a castle's muster do?"*, *"Are there men to be had?"*

Use **"Recruit troops at the castle's muster"** in the castle's menu. The young men of the castle's villages come to his yard and he drills them:

- He gives **regular soldiers**, already past their first rank, and the longer a man waits in the yard, the better soldier he is when he is taken, though never a veteran.
- In his dearer places, now and then, a **young noble** comes to serve, more often the more prosperous the castle.
- His yard fills as fast as the castle's villages allow: thriving villages send more, burnt or raided ones send none until they recover.
- The right of levy holds here too: the castle's lord takes everyone, an outsider only the greenest, with the fee. A keeper who dies is replaced after a few days.

## Young nobles

The game's noble troops, the knights and well-born riders of each realm, come from fewer and truer places:

- **The knights' manors in the villages**, around castles and around towns alike: a rural notable now and then has a young noble among his men, never too many waiting in one village at a time.
- **The castles' musters**, in the keeper's dearer places.
- **A town's merchants and artisans**, now and then, in their dearest place, more often the more prosperous the town.

A young noble waits for the lord of the land: an outsider cannot take him (except with the perks above, once the rest are his). If you want knights, hold land, or ride with the lord who does.

## Garrisons

- A garrison raises its own men on its lord's lands with his right, and takes the young nobles waiting there before the common men.
- A garrison that raises men on another lord's lands pays him the fee. When yours do, you hear of it: *"Your garrisons paid ... to ... for the right to raise men on his lands."*
- A lord taking men from his garrison takes its nobles first.
- A lord of the AI who comes to a town or castle of his own takes the knights of its garrison and leaves as many of his common soldiers in their place, so his host carries the best men and the walls keep their numbers. Your own garrisons are left to you: swap them yourself at the garrison screen.

## Wages and rising in rank

Every soldier who is not a hero is paid by his rank, and the higher the rank, the steeper the pay: hardly more than in the base game for the youngest, far more for veterans and elite troops. The same goes for rising in rank: every soldier needs more battles, and more gold, to rise, a little more at first and much more for the highest ranks. The gold shows in the upgrade's breakdown as **"Arms and harness of a higher rank"**. Veterans and elite men stay few, and dear.

By kind of soldier:

- **Regulars, nobles, militia and the rest**: the pay of their rank.
- **Swords for hire** (the taverns' sellswords, caravan guards, the [Unsworn](mercenary-contracts.md#the-unsworn-troop-tree)) ask somewhat more than a regular of their rank, the same rate as the [companies for hire](free-companies.md#what-it-costs).
- **The warriors of a faith** ask less: they fight for their god, not for coin.
- **The writ's manhunters** ask what a regular of their rank asks.

Garrisons pay their full wages, as a party in the field does. The AI's houses keep their garrisons a little larger than in the base game, and pay them the new wages.

## Men going home

Men do not vanish when they leave the ranks.

- **Men you let go** in the party screen take the road home: *"The men you let go take the road home to their villages."* What they cost their villages in hearths comes back over the next days (**"Men home from war"**), and the village's notables think a little better of you.
- **A host of the AI breaking up** in the field sends its men home the same way. One that breaks up at a town or castle leaves them in the garrison instead.
- **Deserters** go home too, though nobody is thanked for them.

Each party remembers how many of its men came from each village or castle, and when men fall, are taken or rise into another troop, the count goes down evenly across those villages. Men only go home to a village that is not at war with whoever let them go, and that has not been burnt. Men raised in a town do not give its prosperity back.

## What the AI does

The lords of the AI live by the same rules: the same right of levy, the same fees, the same hearths lost and the same wages.

- **They prefer their own lands.** A lord short of men would rather raise them in his own villages and castles, where the best men are his, when they are not far out of his way, and above all at his own castles and their villages, where his knights wait. On his own lands he takes the young nobles first, if his purse allows.
- **Sellsword houses serving a realm** keep raising men of their own on the march, as they do when free, and, if they can pay them, hire tavern sellswords, until their ranks are well filled. Until then they take no villagers; afterwards, a little more of each notable's men than other outsiders, as your house does while it serves for pay.
- **Garrisons and lords** swap knights and commons as described above.

## Turn it off

Recruitment by the land is on by default. Set `RecruitmentEnabled` to `false`, in the game's options or in the settings file, to go back to the base game in all of it: who may raise men where, how fast they come back, what they cost the land and what they are paid. Each part can also be turned off on its own. See [Settings](../settings.md).

Turned off in a campaign under way, the castles' keepers stay but raise no men and are no longer offered in the castle's menu, and the notables' men go back to the base game's as they are taken.
