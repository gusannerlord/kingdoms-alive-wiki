# Lords who remember you

The lords of Calradia keep in mind what you have done to them, beside them and to their families. Ride to a lord's aid and they will thank you months later; burn their village and they will not let you forget it; kill their brother and they will remember it for the rest of their life.

## When a lord brings it up

When you talk to a lord, they may open the conversation with a memory of you, in place of the comment the game would normally make. What you hear depends on what happened, on how they feel about you now and on their character.

- **One memory per conversation**, never more: the weightiest one they have. A kinsman you killed outweighs a feast you shared.
- **The game's own comment still wins** if it is about something more important.
- **Not at a first meeting**, not while they are your prisoner, and never from members of your own clan.
- **After a lord brings up a memory, they stay quiet for most of a day.** Two conversations in a row will not both open with the past.
- **Small things are said once.** A memory that lasts two months or less comes up only the first time it fits.
- **Big things come back.** A memory that lasts longer can come up again, but not within 20 days of the last time, and even then only about one conversation in three. A lord who brings it up again uses different words ("I still owe you for that day...").
- **Fresher, weightier memories come first.** A memory past half its life weighs a little less.

!!! note
    Lords judge your character, not your bloodline. If you die and your heir takes over, lords do not blame the heir for the deaths and executions you caused or the fiefs you handed out. They still hold your house to account for fiefs it stormed and for marriages made by other members of your house.

## What they remember, and for how long

| What happened | Who remembers | How long |
|---|---|---|
| You both rode in a tournament (you won, they won, or neither) | each lord who rode in it | 6 days |
| One of you put the other out of the lists | that lord | 10 days |
| You sat at the same [feast](feasts.md) | each lord in the hall | 10 days |
| You fought on the same side | each lord in the battle | a month |
| You fought on opposite sides | each lord in the battle | about 6 weeks |
| You beat them in a [duel](duels.md) and they lived, or they beat you | that lord | 3 months |
| You rode to their aid in a fight already under way, and won | that lord | 3 months |
| You took them prisoner | that lord, and their parents, children, siblings and spouse | 4 months (2 months for the family) |
| You let them go free | that lord | 6 months |
| You freed them from their captors (in battle or from a dungeon) | that lord | 8 months |
| You sold them back for a ransom | that lord | 2 months |
| They escaped your keeping, or their friends freed them | that lord | 2 months |
| A kinsman of theirs fell in battle against your side | close family (up to grandparents, uncles, aunts, nephews and nieces) | 4 months |
| You raided a village of their house | everyone of that house | 2 months |
| You stormed a town or castle of their house | everyone of that house | 8 months |
| You gave their house a fief (as ruler, or as a gift of your own) | everyone of that house | 8 months |
| As ruler, you gave a fief of theirs to someone else | everyone of that house | 4 months |
| You killed a kinsman of theirs, in battle or in a duel | the whole family and house | **forever** |
| You executed a kinsman of theirs | the whole family and house | **forever** |
| You, or someone of your house, married into their family | the whole family and house | **forever** |
| While you carried a [king's writ](kings-writ.md#nobles-freed-from-the-outlaws), you, your patrol or your captains freed them from outlaws | that lord | **forever** |
| You served them as their squire | that lord | **forever** |
| As their squire, you stood over them when they fell in battle, or let them lie | that lord | **forever** |
| As their squire, a talk by their fire they took well (or badly), or a bout in their yard | that lord | about 4 months (2 for a talk taken badly; 8 for a dirty win or yielding in the yard) |

Only lords who took part remember a battle, and only lords of a realm at war with you remember being captured. Ransoms remember how they were paid: sold to a ransom broker in a tavern, bought back by their house, an heirloom given in place of coin, or a tip about a caravan. Each has its own words.

!!! tip
    If you add the mod to a campaign already under way, lords pick up what the game itself has recorded: kinsmen you killed or executed, lords you captured, your own marriage, and recent battles and tournaments.

## What they say

There are about 250 lines. A lord's character changes what they say: honour, mercy, valour, generosity, cunning, and whether they like you or not. Some examples:

- A **friendly** lord you beat in a tournament: *"I still laugh about [place]. One moment I thought the bout was mine, the next I was looking at the sky and you were still on your feet. Well fought, my friend."*
- A **cunning** one: *"I have been thinking about how you beat me at [place]. You drop your guard on the left after a feint. I won't fall for it twice."*
- A **stingy** one you rode to help: *"Yes, yes, you saved my hide near [place]. I suppose I must be grateful. I am. Do not make me say it twice."*
- A lord you sold to a broker: *"You sold me to a ransom broker, [your name], like a sack of grain at market. I have not forgotten the smell of that tavern cellar."*
- A **merciful** lord whose village you burned: *"The people of [village] had done nothing to you. They were farmers. They were mine to protect, and you burned their homes over their heads."*
- A **cruel** one whose kinsman you executed: *"You executed my brother. Good. Now I know the rules of the game you play, and when you fall into my hands you will get the same."*
- A **cunning** one, years later: *"It was some years ago that you took my sister from me. I count the seasons, [your name]. I count them all."*
- A lord whose daughter you married: *"I gave you my child, [your name]. Do not make me regret it. Tell [her name] her old home misses her."*

Lords call their relatives what they are to them: my father, my niece, my cousin, my late wife.

## Before they attack

When an enemy lord stops you on the map to attack, they may first throw an old memory at you, if they have one that fits the moment. Grievances come out as threats:

- *"For my brother [name]! Today you pay what you owe my house!"*
- *"This is for [village], [your name]! For every roof you burned there!"*
- *"I have not forgotten your chains, [your name]. Today it is your turn."*

Old debts come out with regret: *"You spared me once near [place]. I am sorry it has come to this, [your name]. I truly am."*, or from an old comrade: *"We stood together at [place]. I take no joy in this, [your name], but our banners are at war, and I follow mine."*

## Your old lord, if you were a squire

If you served a lord as their [squire](squire.md), they never forget it. The memory is kept when you choose to stay on in their service, when you are knighted, or when you part with their blessing. They bring it up now and then, under the same rules as other lasting memories.

What they say depends on **what you have become**:

- still their squire by your own choice, back in their household for a second time, or another lord's squire now;
- a knight of a modest house;
- a great lord, with a strong house or several fiefs;
- the ruler of their own realm, or the ruler of another;
- a free sword or a mercenary.

It also depends on **how you get on now**: **proud** if they like you (relation +15 or more), **bitter** if they dislike you (−10 or worse) or your realms are at war, **cool** in between. Once you have outgrown it, they never call you "squire" again.

- Proud, you rule their realm: *"My liege. Forgive me my pride: I remember a squire who fumbled with my saddle straps, and now I bend the knee to you. I would not have it any other way."*
- Proud and cunning, you are a great lord: *"When they ask how you rose so far, I tell them I had the shaping of you. A little credit for the old master, eh?"*
- Bitter, you were knighted and became a sellsword: *"I trained a knight, and got a sellsword. I should have asked for my harness back."*

**Walking out.** If you leave their service before your spurs are won, or they send you away, the proud memory is gone. In its place they keep a bitter one, forever: *"Ah, the squire who ran. I tell the story at table sometimes. It always gets a laugh."* If you rise high after walking out, they notice that too: *"You left my service before your spurs were won, and now you wear a crown. I do not know whether to curse you or bow. I do both, quietly."*

## Turn it off

In the settings file, set `NobleMemoryEnabled` to `false`: lords stop noting what you do and stop bringing up memories. What they already remember is kept in your save, in case you turn it back on.

How long each memory lasts can be changed with the `Memory...Days` numbers (a negative number means never forgotten), and how often lasting memories come back with `MemoryRepeatDays`, `MemoryRepeatChance` and `MemoryQuietHours`.
