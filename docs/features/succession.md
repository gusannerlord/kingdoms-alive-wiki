# The crown and its claim

When a ruler dies, every kingdom now follows its own law of succession, taken from the history each culture is drawn from. And every lord has a **claim** to the crown: how good a right the realm thinks they have to rule. A ruler with a weak claim faces plots, a grudging oath, a reluctant council and neighbours who smell blood.

The game never shows the claim as a number. You hear it in words: *beyond question*, *secure*, *questioned in the halls*, *weak*, or *little better than a usurper's*.

## The law of each realm

Bannerlord opens in the year 1084 of its own calendar, and TaleWorlds drew its peoples from the years 600 to 1100. Each law follows what its real counterpart did in those centuries.

| Kingdom | Drawn from | Law | Who takes the crown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Empire | the Roman and Byzantine Senate | **The Senate** | The council votes among the three heads of houses with the best claim. Wealth, rank and land count for most; blood hardly at all. |
| Western Empire | the soldier-emperors | **Acclamation** | The three heads of houses with the best claim stand; strength and renown count for most. The army raises its general. |
| Southern Empire | the Macedonian dynasty | **The dynasty** | The eldest child of the dead ruler, daughter or son, inherits with no vote. With no child, the eldest of the house. |
| Sturgia | Kievan Rus | **Seniority** | The eldest man of the ruling house inherits with no vote: an uncle before a son. |
| Vlandia | the Normans and the Franks | **Primogeniture** | The eldest son inherits with no vote; then the eldest daughter; then the eldest man of the house. |
| Battania | the High Kings of Ireland | **Tanistry** | The ablest of the ruling house stands against the two best claims of other houses. The strongest hand usually wins. |
| Aserai | the Arab tribal confederacies | **The sheikhs' council** | The ablest of the ruling house stands against the two best claims of other houses. Wealth counts for most. |
| Khuzait | the Turkic khaganates | **The founder's blood** | The ablest of the ruling house is raised as khan. Another house may stand only if it has grown far stronger than the ruling one. |
| Nords (War Sails) | the Viking kings | **The Thing** | As with the Khuzaits: the ruler's kin are acclaimed unless another house has grown far stronger. |

- **New kingdoms** follow the law of their culture. A new imperial realm keeps the law of the realm it broke away from; one you found takes the law of the nearest imperial realm.
- **No one left to stand:** if the ruling house has no adult who can take the crown, the lords vote among the three best claims of the realm.
- **A contested heir:** under the three laws of inheritance, an heir with a hopelessly weak claim has to stand in a vote against the two best claims of other houses. This is very rare.
- Under the laws with a vote, the vote is the game's own: in your realm you see it on the kingdom screen as always, with the candidates the law allows. Each house backs the candidate with the best claim, its own head, its kin and those it likes.
- A realm your own house rules is not touched: when you die, the game lets you choose your heir as always.

A line in your messages tells you whenever a crown passes anywhere in Calradia, in the voice of the law: *"Vlandia: with Derthert gone, the crown passes to Morcon, as custom demands."*, *"The Senate of the Northern Empire has chosen Ascyron to wear the diadem."*

## What makes a claim

Every lord's claim is built from the same parts. Each law weighs them differently.

- **Blood**: being the heir by the law, or the ruler crowned by it, counts most. Being of the ruling house counts for half of that, and being married into it, or into a house that once ruled the realm, for a quarter. A ruler who took the crown by winning a civil war has only half the blood of a lawful one.
- **Strength, wealth, land and renown** of the lord's house, each measured against the best of the other houses of the realm.
- **Rank**: the tier of the house.

| Law | Blood | Strength | Wealth | Rank | Land | Renown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Senate | small | some | great | great | great | small |
| Acclamation | small | great | small | some | small | great |
| The dynasty | very great | some | some | some | small | small |
| Seniority, Primogeniture | very great | some | small | some | small–some | small |
| Tanistry | some | great | small | some | small | some |
| The sheikhs' council | some | some | very great | some | some | none |
| The founder's blood, The Thing | great | great | small | small | none | some |

**A woman** may inherit where the law prefers men, but her claim suffers for it: badly in Vlandia, Sturgia, the Aserai, the Khuzaits and the Nords; a little in the Northern and Western Empire and in Battania; not at all in the Southern Empire. This counts for you too if you play a woman.

### The ruler's own claim

On top of that, a ruler's claim grows or shrinks with the reign:

- **The years on the throne** settle it, slowly. Rulers already reigning when you start (or when you first load a game with this version) count as long established.
- **Victories and defeats**: every town or castle the realm takes in a siege raises it, and every one it loses to a siege or a rebellion lowers it.
- **The oath**: a new ruler to whom most houses [swore fealty](royal-court.md#the-oath-to-a-new-ruler) stands firmer than one most houses refused.
- **The lords' regard**: the ruler's relation with the heads of houses.
- **Sacred Majesty**, and the **Senate** policy in a realm of the Senate, add to it.
- **Usurpation**: a crown taken by winning a civil war starts with a heavy stain that fades over about four months.

## What a claim does

A ruler with a **weak** claim:

- faces lords who start a plot with less hatred for them: a lord with a better claim than the ruler needs 10 less bad blood to start one (and, with `PlotsOnlyInLargeRealms` on, a weak ruler can be plotted against even in a kingdom that is not [too large](civil-war.md#too-large));
- is a worse cause to swear to: more houses ask for something or refuse the oath;
- is slow to start a war, unless it would win back land of their own culture, and quicker to want peace;
- tempts neighbours who already have a quarrel with the realm, above all in the first months after a succession. A weak crown is never a reason for war on its own;
- finds the council slower to back their proposals of war, peace and laws.

A ruler with a strong claim is the opposite, more gently: lords lean away from plots against them, houses swear more readily and the council follows them a little more. Lords lean towards a plot whose pretender (the house it would crown) has a better claim than the ruler.

## Claims to the throne

A good claim to rule is not the same as a **claim to the throne**: only some houses could ever be put on the throne by a [plot](civil-war.md#claims-to-the-throne). A house has a claim to the throne when its head is close kin of the ruler at the head of a house of their own (father or mother, son or daughter, brother or sister, husband or wife), when it married into the royal house, when it once ruled the realm, or, under the Senate, acclamation, tanistry, the sheikhs' council and the Thing, when it is among the three houses with the best claim to rule besides the ruler's; under the hereditary laws and the founder's blood, the two houses with the best claim to rule besides the ruler's are of the old blood of the realm and have a claim too.

## Where you see it

- **The Crown**, the first entry of the Court tab of the kingdom screen, shows the law of your realm, your ruler's claim (or yours) as a bar with the words the lords use for it, and the portraits of the heir and of the strongest pretender. Below it, **"Claimants to the throne, should the crown ever be contested"** lists each house with a claim to the throne and its reason, in words: *"The {Clan}: {head}, brother to {ruler}."*, *"... son of {ruler}"*, *"... through marriage to the royal house"*, *"... of a house that once ruled {Kingdom}"*, or by the law of the realm, such as *"named among the worthy by the law of the Thing"* or *"a name the council of sheikhs would hear, should it gather to choose"*, or *"of the old blood of {Kingdom}, near enough to the throne to be named for it"*. Your own house is listed as *"Your own house, the {Clan}: ..."*. With no claimant, it says that no other house has a claim.
- **The Encyclopedia**: a clan's page opens with *"The {Clan} have a claim to the throne of {Kingdom}, {reason}."* when it has one.
- **Ask any lord** *"What do the lords say of {ruler}'s right to the throne?"*. Honest lords, plain-spoken ones, a rival with an eye on the throne and the ruler themselves all answer in their own way.
