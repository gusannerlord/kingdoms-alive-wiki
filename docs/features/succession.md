# The crown and its claim

When a ruler dies, every kingdom now follows its own law of succession, taken from the history each culture is drawn from, and its ruler may change it, against the custom of the realm and at a price. And every lord has a **claim** to the crown: how good a right the realm thinks they have to rule. A ruler with a weak claim faces plots, a grudging oath, a reluctant council and neighbours who smell blood.

The game never shows the claim as a number. You hear it in words: *beyond question*, *secure*, *questioned in the halls*, *weak*, or *little better than a usurper's*.

## The law of each realm

Bannerlord opens in the year 1084 of its own calendar, and TaleWorlds drew its peoples from the years 600 to 1100. Each realm begins under the law its real counterpart followed in those centuries: its **custom**.

| Kingdom | Drawn from | Law of custom | Who takes the crown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern Empire | the Roman and Byzantine Senate | **Senate** | The council votes among the three heads of houses with the best claim. Wealth, rank and land count for most; blood hardly at all. |
| Western Empire | the soldier-emperors | **Acclamation** | The three heads of houses with the best claim stand; strength and renown count for most. The army raises its general. |
| Southern Empire | the Macedonian dynasty | **Dynastic** | The eldest child of the dead ruler, daughter or son, inherits with no vote. With no child, the eldest of the house. |
| Sturgia | Kievan Rus | **Seniority** | The eldest man of the ruling house inherits with no vote: an uncle before a son. |
| Vlandia | the Normans and the Franks | **Salic Primogeniture** | The eldest son inherits with no vote; then the eldest man of the house. A woman never inherits, nor stands if the lords must choose. |
| Battania | the High Kings of Ireland | **Tanistry** | The ablest of the ruling house stands against the two best claims of other houses. The strongest hand usually wins. |
| Aserai | the Arab tribal confederacies | **Shura** | The head of the ruling house stands against the two best claims of other houses, and the council of the clans chooses. Wealth counts for most. |
| Khuzait | the Turkic khaganates | **Kurultai** | The ablest of the ruling house is raised as khan. Another house may stand only if it has grown far stronger than the ruling one. |
| Nords (War Sails) | the Viking kings | **Thing** | As with the Khuzaits: the ruler's kin are acclaimed unless another house has grown far stronger. |

- **New kingdoms.** Whoever founds a realm chooses its law among those of its people, and that law is its custom from its first day. If you found one, the governor asks you while you name it (*"And the crown itself, when you are gone: by which law of the {people} people shall it pass?"*); if your own rising stands as a realm apart, a window, **The law of the crown**, asks you, with what your counsellors advise. An AI founder takes what serves his house: a woman, or a lord whose heir is a daughter, takes the law that best lets her line inherit. An AI realm born of a rising of the Empire keeps the law of the realm it broke away from.
- **No one left to stand:** if the ruling house has no adult who can take the crown, the lords vote among the three best claims of the realm.
- **A contested heir:** under a law of blood, an heir with a hopelessly weak claim has to stand in a vote against the two best claims of other houses. This is very rare.
- Under the laws with a vote, the vote is the game's own: in your realm you see it on the kingdom screen as always, with the candidates the law allows. Each house backs the candidate with the best claim, its own head, its kin and those it likes.
- **A realm your own house rules:** when you die as ruler, the game lets you choose your heir as always. Under a law of blood, the heir you chose inherits with no vote if the lords hold his claim strong enough; otherwise the realm votes, as for a contested heir. You are never replaced by the heir the law would have named. Under Salic Primogeniture a daughter cannot take the crown: name her, and the lords choose another ruler, from another house.
- **Stepping down takes your whole house off the throne.** If you abdicate, none of your house stands in the vote that follows; the realm chooses by its law, and you stay on as head of your house. (Retiring is how a ruler hands the crown to an heir.)
- **While the crown waits for a vote.** When a ruler dies and the crown must be voted, the head of his house holds it for a few days, as a matter of form, until the realm is quiet. Its feuds are set aside, and nothing is done against or through the crown meanwhile: plots, charters and reckonings wait, no decree is given, no law of the crown is weighed, no title granted. Then the lords vote, and the feuds that still make sense take up again. The Crown entry of the Crown tab says who holds it, and that the lords will choose once the realm is at peace within.

A line in your messages tells you whenever a crown passes anywhere in Calradia, in the voice of the law: *"Vlandia: with Derthert gone, the crown passes to Morcon, as custom demands."*, *"The Senate of the Northern Empire has chosen Ascyron to wear the diadem."*

## The laws of succession {#the-laws-of-succession}

The law of succession is one of the [laws of the crown](crown-laws.md): it sits in the game's **Policies** tab, under **Laws of the Crown**, group **Succession**, with the crown on the law in force. Each realm knows only the laws of its own people, three or four of them; a law of another people is greyed: *"{Realm} has never known this law: its lords would not understand it, let alone swear to it."*

| Kingdom | Its laws (the custom first) |
|---|---|
| Northern, Western and Southern Empire | Senate, Acclamation, Dynastic (each Empire's custom as above) |
| Vlandia | Salic Primogeniture, Primogeniture, Feudal Elective |
| Sturgia | Seniority, Primogeniture, Veche, Cognatic Seniority |
| Battania | Tanistry, Primogeniture, Cognatic Primogeniture |
| Aserai | Shura, Designated Heir, Rule of the Strongest, Cognatic Designation |
| Khuzait | Kurultai, Designated Heir, Seniority, Cognatic Designation |
| Nords | Thing, Primogeniture, Cognatic Primogeniture |

The laws not in the first table:

- **Primogeniture**: the eldest son inherits with no vote; then the eldest daughter; then the eldest man of the house.
- **Cognatic Primogeniture**: the eldest child inherits, daughter or son alike; failing children, the eldest of the house.
- **Cognatic Seniority**: the eldest of the ruling house inherits, woman or man alike.
- **Designated Heir**: the ruler names an heir from the ruling house, a son as a rule before a daughter, and the heir takes the crown with no vote. An AI ruler names the one the game would make head of his house; you name yours when you die, as always.
- **Cognatic Designation**: the same, daughter or son alike.
- **Feudal Elective**: the heir of the ruling house stands against the two lords with the best claim, and the great lords choose. Land weighs most. Women stand as the realm's old custom allows.
- **Veche**: the three lords with the best claim, of any house, stand, and the lords choose. Wealth and renown weigh most.
- **Rule of the Strongest**: the heads of the three strongest houses stand, and the lords choose. Strength weighs above all.

Under every law of blood, an heir whose claim the lords doubt badly enough must still stand in a vote, as above.

### Changing the law

- **Only the ruler** brings another law of succession before the council, you and an AI ruler alike. The houses can ask for one thing only, through a [charter](factions.md#the-charter): a return to the realm's custom. Their other road is the sword. See [Laws of the crown](crown-laws.md#who-brings-them-before-the-council).
- **The council votes as the game votes on any law**, by each lord's character (a law of blood pleases the autocrats, a law of choosing the friends of the lords' councils, and a law where daughters inherit as sons a little the egalitarians), and by what the law means for his house: under a law of blood the other houses lose their chance at the crown, so they vote against it (the harder the better their own claim) and for a law that puts the crown to the lords; the realm's resistance to a woman on the throne, or to a broken order of the blood, weighs in their votes too. The ruler's house backs a law of blood.
- **The custom resists.** A law brought in against the realm's custom is resented by the heads of its houses, as a lower regard for the ruler and a grievance against the crown, and both fade as the law grows old:
    - handing the crown down by blood where the realm has always chosen is resented most;
    - changing who of the blood inherits (the eldest of the house, the ruler's eldest child, the heir he names, the dynasty) is resented, and the first ruler to inherit by the new law has a weaker claim while it is new: *"And there are those who say the crown should have gone to other kin of the blood, by the old order, and not to {ruler} by a law made yesterday."*;
    - letting a woman inherit where the custom bars her or puts her after the men is resented as much as the realm's people resent it: not at all in the Southern Empire, somewhat among the Nords, in Battania and in the Northern and Western Empire, badly in Vlandia, and bitterly in Sturgia, among the Aserai and the Khuzaits;
    - putting the crown to the lords where it has always passed by blood pleases the houses instead.
- **They add up**, and they fade: in full for a generation, half as much for as long again, and then the law is the **custom** of the realm and nobody holds it against the crown any more: *"{Law} has been the law of {realm} for so long that it is its custom now: no house holds it against the crown any more."* Until then a woman's claim is still weighed as the old custom weighs it.
- **A charter can only ask for the custom back**, and only while the new law is resented: once it has become the custom, the charter drops the demand. Going back to an old custom after that counts as a change against the new one.
- **A crown won in a civil war** does not change the law: the new ruler reigns under the law the realm had.
- **The Crown entry of the Crown tab** shows the law and whether it is the custom (*"Succession: {law}, against the custom of the realm ({custom})"*), and how much of the houses' regard it costs.

## What makes a claim

Every lord's claim is built from the same parts. Each law weighs them differently.

- **Blood**: being the heir by the law, or the ruler crowned by it, counts most. Being of the ruling house counts for half of that, and being married into it, or into a house that once ruled the realm, for a quarter. A ruler who took the crown by winning a civil war has only half the blood of a lawful one.
- **Strength, wealth, land and renown** of the lord's house, each measured against the best of the other houses of the realm.
- **Rank**: the tier of the house.

| Law | Blood | Strength | Wealth | Rank | Land | Renown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Senate | small | some | great | great | great | small |
| Acclamation | small | great | small | some | small | great |
| Dynastic | very great | some | some | some | small | small |
| Seniority, Cognatic Seniority, Primogeniture, Salic and Cognatic Primogeniture | very great | some | small | some | small–some | small |
| Designated Heir, Cognatic Designation | very great | some | some | some | small | small |
| Tanistry | some | great | small | some | small | some |
| Shura | some | some | very great | some | some | none |
| Kurultai, Thing | great | great | small | small | none | some |
| Feudal Elective | some | some | some | some | great | small |
| Veche | some | some | great | some | small | great |
| Rule of the Strongest | some | very great | some | small | small | some |

**A woman** may inherit where the law puts men first, but her claim suffers for it: under the realms' own customs, badly in Vlandia, Sturgia, the Aserai, the Khuzaits and the Nords, a little in the Northern and Western Empire and in Battania (a law brought in weighs her as it does wherever it holds); not at all in the Southern Empire, nor under a law where daughters inherit as sons once it is the custom. Under Salic Primogeniture she can neither inherit nor stand. This counts for you too if you play a woman.

### The ruler's own claim

On top of that, a ruler's claim grows or shrinks with the reign:

- **The years on the throne** settle it, slowly. Rulers already reigning when you start (or when you first load a game with this version) count as long established.
- **Victories and defeats**: every town or castle the realm takes in a siege raises it, and every one it loses to a siege or a rebellion lowers it.
- **The oath**: a new ruler to whom most houses [swore fealty](royal-court.md#the-oath-to-a-new-ruler) stands firmer than one most houses refused.
- **The lords' regard**: the ruler's relation with the heads of houses.
- **Sacred Majesty**, and the **Senate** policy in a realm of the Senate, add to it.
- **Usurpation**: a crown taken by winning a civil war starts with a heavy stain that fades over about four months.

## What a claim does

A ruler with a **weak** claim:

- faces lords who start a plot with less hatred for them: a lord with a better claim than the ruler needs 10 less bad blood to start one (and, with `PlotsOnlyInLargeRealms` on, a weak ruler can be plotted against even in a kingdom that is not [too large](civil-war.md#too-large));
- is a worse cause to swear to: more houses ask for something or refuse the oath;
- is slow to start a war, unless it would win back land of their own culture, and quicker to want peace;
- tempts neighbours who already have a quarrel with the realm, above all in the first months after a succession. A weak crown is never a reason for war on its own;
- finds the council slower to back their proposals of war, peace and laws.

A ruler with a strong claim is the opposite, more gently: lords lean away from plots against them, houses swear more readily and the council follows them a little more. Lords lean towards a plot whose pretender (the house it would crown) has a better claim than the ruler.

## Claims to the throne

A good claim to rule is not the same as a **claim to the throne**: only some houses could ever be put on the throne by a [plot](civil-war.md#claims-to-the-throne). A house has a claim to the throne when its head is close kin of the ruler at the head of a house of their own (father or mother, son or daughter, brother or sister, husband or wife), when it married into the royal house, when it once ruled the realm, or, under a law that has the lords choose (Senate, Acclamation, Tanistry, Shura, Thing, Feudal Elective, Veche, Rule of the Strongest), when it is among the three houses with the best claim to rule besides the ruler's; under the laws of blood and the Kurultai, the two houses with the best claim to rule besides the ruler's are of the old blood of the realm and have a claim too.

### How a house wins a claim {#how-a-house-wins-a-claim}

If your house is a vassal of the realm with no claim to the throne, the tooltip of the claim in your clan screen's [House tab](house-tab.md), and a line at the end of the claimants in the Crown tab (*"Your own house has no claim. ..."*), tell you how to come by one under your realm's law: a marriage into the ruling house, your head close kin of the ruler, a house that once ruled, or, where the law names them, a place among the first houses by their right to rule. For that last way they also tell you what the law weighs most, where your house stands (for example *"Your house stands fifth of the 9 houses of the realm by that right; the first 3 have a claim."*), and where it falls furthest short of the house with the most of it, or that it already has as much as any. Landed knights and a house serving for pay are not reckoned among them.

### A crown promised at a peace {#a-crown-promised-at-a-peace}

A [civil war](civil-war.md#the-outcomes) that ends in a peace with the rebels somewhat ahead can settle the next crown over the law. In a realm whose crown passes by blood, the pretender's house is named **heir**: when the ruler leaves the throne, by death or otherwise, its head is crowned. In a realm that chooses its ruler, the crown's house and the houses that stood by it **pledge their votes** to the pretender's house at the next choice; the vote is still real, and a house that pledged and votes for another breaks its word. The heir, or the house the votes are pledged to, is the one the Crown tab of the kingdom screen shows as next to follow the ruler.

In the realms of the Empire, the throne can also change hands by a strategos's coup, and the knights of the crown's pronoia pass to the new ruling house: see [The Empire's own ways](empire.md).

## Where you see it


- **The Crown**, the first entry of the Crown tab of the kingdom screen, shows the law of your realm, your ruler's claim (or yours) as a bar with the words the lords use for it, and the portraits of the heir and of the strongest pretender. Below it, **"Claimants to the throne, should the crown ever be contested"** lists each house with a claim to the throne and its reason, in words: *"The {Clan}: {head}, brother to {ruler}."*, *"... son of {ruler}"*, *"... through marriage to the royal house"*, *"... of a house that once ruled {Kingdom}"*, or by the law of the realm, such as *"named among the worthy by the law of the Thing"* or *"a name the council of sheikhs would hear, should it gather to choose"*, or *"of the old blood of {Kingdom}, near enough to the throne to be named for it"*. Your own house is listed as *"Your own house, the {Clan}: ..."*. With no claimant, it says that no other house has a claim.
- **The Encyclopedia**: a clan's page opens with *"The {Clan} have a claim to the throne of {Kingdom}, {reason}."* when it has one.
- **Ask any lord** *"What do the lords say of {ruler}'s right to the throne?"*. Honest lords, plain-spoken ones, a rival with an eye on the throne and the ruler themselves all answer in their own way.
