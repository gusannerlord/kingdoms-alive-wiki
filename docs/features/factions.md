# Factions and grievances

Lords do not only plot against the throne. They remember what the crown has done to them, and they band together behind a cause: a charter to bind the ruler's hands, the fall of a house that has grown too great, or a new ruler. A faction tries the council first, the lawful way. Only when the council fails it does it put its case to the ruler, and only when the ruler refuses does it think of arms.

All of this happens in every kingdom, not only yours. You can join a faction, lead one, betray one to the crown, or face them from the throne.

## Lords remember

Every house keeps a short list of wrongs, each with a name, a place and a date. Lords speak of them in conversation, and the worst of them show in the [kingdom screen's Plots tab](civil-war.md).

| What happened | Against whom |
|---|---|
| A town or castle of theirs fell to a siege and no other lord of the realm was within a short ride | the crown |
| A conquered fief they stood for was given to another house (twice running weighs double) | the crown, and a little the house that got it |
| The ruler's own house took a conquered fief they stood for | the crown |
| The council voted to take a fief from them | the crown, and whoever proposed it |
| The realm bought its peace with tribute | the crown |
| A kinsman was executed | the crown, or the house that did it |
| The crown shielded a house they wanted brought down, or spurned their charter | the crown |
| The crown took a conquered fief for itself while a charter forbade it | the crown |

Wrongs fade with time (an executed kinsman, much more slowly). When a new house takes the throne, the old grievances against the crown weigh only half.

A house's grievances make it readier to join a [plot against the throne](civil-war.md) and any faction against the crown. So does serving a ruler of another [faith](faiths.md), and a ruler who refused to step down when the lords asked.

## The Charter

Houses with grievances against the crown write a charter of up to three clauses:

- to strike down the crown's strongest laws in force (such as Royal Privilege, Sacred Majesty or War Tax);
- to make law one of the lords' own (such as the Senate or the Lords' Privy Council);
- **fiefs to the realm**: if the ruler has been keeping conquests for his own house, that for a while the ruler's house takes no share of conquered fiefs.

The faction brings its laws before the council one by one, and all its lords vote together. If the council turns it down, or a clause cannot be voted, it puts the whole charter to the ruler and waits for an answer.

The ruler may **sign it** (the laws change and the lords forget their grievances), **buy its leader** (he goes quiet for a good while, and the faction looks for another head), **strike at the leader** (take his office or put one of his fiefs to the council), or **refuse**. A refused charter asks once more after a while, and the second time it breaks up bitter, unless half the realm stands behind it and the realm is at peace: then it rises. A charter's rising is a [civil war](civil-war.md) in which the ruler keeps the throne if he loses, and signs.

## The Reckoning of a house

When a house grows too great (a quarter of the realm's strength or more, and not the ruler's), or gives too many others cause for grudges, lords band together to see it stripped of a fief: the one it holds by the weakest right, a castle before a town.

They propose taking it in the council and vote together. If the council keeps the fief with the house, they ask the ruler. A ruler who gives way puts it to the council himself, and its lords watch how he votes. A ruler who shields the house is remembered for it.

## Asking a ruler to step down

A [plot against the throne](civil-war.md) that has gathered a good part of the realm asks the ruler to lay down the crown before it rises. A ruler with a weak claim is likelier to agree. If he does, the realm chooses a new ruler by its [own law](succession.md), and the plot's houses vote together for its leader. If he refuses, the lords hold it against him, and the plot goes on.

If you are the ruler, you are asked too. You can step down and stay as a vassal with your lands, or refuse and take what comes.

## You and the factions

Talk to the head of another house of your realm:

- **What news of the realm, my friend?** A lord who is your friend tells you who is pressing whom: the factions, their aims, who stands with them, how far along they are, whether there is a darker plot against the throne, and what his own house holds against the crown. A good friend tells you more; an honourable one keeps the secrets of his own cause unless you are in it; a calculating one may remember more for a consideration. When a faction a friend is in comes to a head, he may write to you.
- **Join** a lord's charter or reckoning, or **leave** it.
- **Start a charter**, or **start a reckoning** against the house the lord you are talking to resents most. Win other lords over one by one; how likely they are to agree is said in words.
- **Tell the ruler.** If you know of a faction still in whispers, you can take it to the ruler. He acts at once, and thanks you. Whoever told you about it will find out.
- **Ask the ruler to step down**, if you lead a plot strong enough.

When you lead a faction, you bring its laws or its claim before the council yourself, from the kingdom screen; its lords vote with you. Your factions, and those that put their case to you as ruler, have their own entry in the journal.

## Not too many civil wars

In the AI kingdoms there is only one civil war at a time, and some time must pass between one and the next. A plot or a charter that could rise waits, and keeps pressing its case. Civil wars in your own kingdom do not count towards this.
