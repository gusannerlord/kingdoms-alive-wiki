# Factions and grievances

Lords do not only plot against the throne. They remember what the crown has done to them, and they band together behind a cause: a charter to bind the ruler's hands, or the fall of a house that holds what they claim. A charter tries the council first, the lawful way, and only when the ruler refuses it does it think of arms. A reckoning against a house is heard by the ruler at the realm's assembly, and only a verdict it cannot bear sends it to the sword.

All of this happens in every kingdom, not only yours. You can join a faction, lead one, betray a charter to the crown, or face them from the throne. What you know of them is in the **Realm** tab of the kingdom screen (**K**): the feuds under **Wars within**, the charters and plots under **Causes**, the rights of your house under **Claims**, the reckonings waiting to be heard under **Assembly**, and what waits for your answer under **Pending**.

## Lords remember {#lords-remember}

Every house keeps a short list of wrongs, each with a name, a place and a date. Lords speak of them in conversation, the worst of them show in the Realm tab, and those your house holds or suffers are under **Grievances** in the [House tab](house-tab.md) of the clan screen and under **Your standing** in the Realm tab.

| What happened | Against whom |
|---|---|
| A town or castle of theirs fell to a siege while an army of the realm, led by another house, lingered close by, free to come, and never fought for it | the house that led that army (the crown, if it was the ruler's army or his family's) |
| A town or castle of theirs fell to a siege, no army of the realm marched to it or lingered near it, and no lord of another house fought for it or stood within its walls | the crown, and the house of the realm's [Marshal](royal-court.md#the-marshals-burden) |
| A conquered fief they stood for was given to another house (twice running weighs double) | the crown, and a little the house that got it |
| The ruler's own house took a conquered fief they stood for | the crown |
| The crown found against them in a [reckoning](#the-reckoning-of-a-house) at the assembly, and they gave the fief up | the crown, and the house that led the reckoning |
| The ruler took a fief of theirs by decree, or their lord by his demand | the crown, or their lord (and the house that asked for it, if the ruler backed someone) |
| The ruler gave a fief of theirs to their rivals by decree, in a feud | the crown |
| The realm bought its peace with tribute | the crown |
| A kinsman was executed | the crown, or the house that did it |
| The crown shielded a house they wanted brought down, left their reckoning unheard, or spurned their charter | the crown |
| The crown took a conquered fief for itself while a charter forbade it | the crown |
| The ruler's house holds more towns and castles than its due (see below) and a large share of the realm's | the crown |
| A vassal house holds more towns and castles than its due and a sizeable share of the realm's | that house (not the crown) |
| They came to your court to ask for a fief of yours they claim by right, and you refused | the crown |

### New wrongs remembered {#new-wrongs-remembered}

| What happened | Against whom |
|---|---|
| The crown gave a town or castle of theirs to the enemy as part of the price of a [peace](war-and-peace.md) | the crown |
| They had to yield a town or castle to the crown at the peace that ended a [civil war](civil-war.md) | the crown |
| A house pledged its vote to theirs at the peace that ended a civil war, and gave it to another when the lords chose a ruler | that house |
| The crown rebuked them before the court for their part in a failed [rising](realm-houses.md) | the crown |
| A town or castle of theirs fell, and their own [lord](titles.md) brought his host close by and never fought for it, or never came though he was free to | their lord |
| They asked their lord to come to their aid in a feud, he would not though he was free, and the place fell | their lord |
| They were attacked in a feud, and you, their lord, stood aside | your house |
| A vacant duchy or ulus they hold land in went to another house | the crown, and a little the house that got it |
| They are a duke and the ruler keeps a town of their duchy for himself: held for as long as that lasts, and gone the day it ends | the crown |
| They are a duke, a noyan or a count, and another house of the realm holds land of their duchy, ulus or county without answering to them: held for as long as that lasts | that house |
| Another house won their duchy or ulus from them in a feud for the title | that house (the crown, if the ruler decreed it) |
| A vassal of theirs rode against them in a feud | that house |
| The emperor took the command of their theme from them, or the khan took back their ulus for treason | the crown |
| The emperor took a fief of theirs on another house's [petition](empire.md), or turned their own petition away | the crown |

**Too much land in one hand.** While the ruler's house holds more towns and castles than its due and a large share of the realm's, every other house (not the ruler's kin) holds it a little more against the crown as time goes by, the more so the further beyond its due. A vassal house with more than its due and a sizeable share of the realm's is begrudged the same way, against that house alone (its own kin excepted). A house's due grows with its tier, and the crown is due a little more than a house of its tier. It is one grudge per house that keeps growing while the hoard lasts, and fades like any other once it ends. The goodwill it costs is another matter, and fixed: while the hoard lasts, the head of each of those houses thinks less of the ruler (or of the head of that house) for each town or castle beyond its due. Take one more and it costs more; hold one fewer and that much comes back; once the house is back within its due (or under its share of the realm), all of it comes back. It never piles up with time. When a house gets a new head (the old one dies, retires or leaves it), what was taken from the old head is given back first, so the new head does not inherit it; the new head is then charged once like anyone else. It counts the same for you, as ruler or vassal, and a hoarding house is the natural target of a [reckoning](#the-reckoning-of-a-house). Your own house's towns and castles against its due, and whether they are begrudged, are under **Your lands** in **Your standing** in the Realm tab; every house's, beside its tier in the Clans tab of the kingdom screen.

**Grudges between houses.** When one house of an AI realm comes to hold a wrong against another, their heads fall out at once: a house passed over in a share-out thinks less of the house that got the fief; a house that left the realm alone with a foe at the walls during a feud loses the goodwill of every other house, and much more if it defies the crown to the end or goes over to the foe. The same holds against your house: a house you beat to a share-out, or leave alone against a foe in a feud, thinks less of you; and if your house defies the crown to the end, or goes over to the foe, in a feud, every house of the realm turns against you at once, as it would against any other. Your own feelings are never moved for you: whom you like is up to you.

**Who came, and who fought.** While a fief is besieged, the realm watches which armies come near it, and whether they really helped: one of their parties fought at that siege (breaking the besiegers from outside, sallying out with the garrison, holding the walls against the assault) or rode into the fief. An army that lingered close and did nothing is what the house remembers when the fief falls, and its head thinks less of whoever led it, you included. Only the hours it was free to come count: not while it was besieging another fief, fighting a battle, relieving or holding another besieged fief of the realm, still gathering its lords, or already marching to this one or chasing its besiegers. An army still on its way when the walls fall is not blamed. If the house had asked you in person to relieve the fief (see below) and you answered, your answer and what you did count instead. A lone lord riding past does not spare the crown; only a lord who fought for the fief does. A siege that is lifted leaves no grudge.

**A plea from your host.** When you lead an army and a fief of a house riding in it is besieged not far off, one of its lords (the head of the house, if they ride with you) rides up beside you in their war gear and, as one who marches under your banner, asks you to lead the army to its relief: once per siege and house, however many of them ride with you. Refuse and the house thinks less of you. Promise and it watches: if the fief falls and your host never fought for it nor rode into it, your word is remembered as broken, and that costs more than refusing; if you did help, the house is grateful when the siege ends, whether the fief held or not. If the siege is lifted without you, nobody minds. Help after refusing is remembered kindly too.

**Grudges against the crown.** A wrong a house lays at its ruler's door (a fief lost with no help sent, passed over, the king's share, a shameful peace, a ruler who shields their enemy or spurns them, a broken charter, a fief given away by the ruler's decree) also costs a little of its head's goodwill toward the ruler (a town of a duke's duchy kept by the crown costs none: it is how things stand, not something done to him), whether the ruler is of the AI or you. A little each time: a ruler who fails the same house again and again makes an enemy of it, and an enemy of the ruler is who starts a plot. A good [Chancellor](royal-court.md#the-offices) softens it: the more charming, the less goodwill the wrongs cost, down to half only with a truly exceptional one (a small wrong may cost none at all), though the house remembers the wrong just the same. An executed kinsman costs that goodwill the way the game already does, and a rebuke before the court costs it once, when it is given; neither costs it twice. Your goodwill toward a ruler, as a vassal, is never moved for you.

**Great realms breed rancour.** In a kingdom grown beyond its fair share of Calradia, every wrong against the crown weighs more when it is born, up to twice as much in a kingdom of twice its fair share, and costs the ruler that much more goodwill. The lords of such a kingdom also band together more readily: a charter or a reckoning asks less of a house to join it and to stay in it, and a smaller grudge is enough to begin a Reckoning (see [Great realms, restless lords](civil-war.md#great-realms-restless-lords)). Grudges between houses do not grow with the realm.

Wrongs fade with time (an executed kinsman, much more slowly). When a new house takes the throne, the old grievances against the crown weigh only half.

A house's grievances make it readier to join a [plot against the throne](civil-war.md) and any faction against the crown. So does serving a ruler of another [faith](faiths.md), and a ruler who refused to step down when the lords asked.

## The Charter {#the-charter}

Houses with grievances against the crown write a charter of up to three clauses:

- to strike down the crown's strongest laws in force (such as Royal Privilege, Sacred Majesty or War Tax);
- to make law one of the lords' own (such as the Senate or the Lords' Privy Council);
- **fiefs to the realm**: if the ruler has been keeping conquests for his own house, that for a while the ruler's house takes no share of conquered fiefs.

The faction brings its laws before the council one by one, and all its lords vote together. If the council turns it down, or a clause cannot be voted, it puts the whole charter to the ruler and waits for an answer.

The ruler may **sign it** (the laws change and each lord forgets the heaviest of his grievances), **offer its leader a purse** (if he takes it he goes quiet for a good while, and the faction looks for another head; he may send it back, and then the ultimatum stands), **strike at the leader** (take his office, or a fief of his by [decree](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal), never his house's last), or **refuse**. Striking does not end the ultimatum. When the time runs out without a yes, the charter rises if more than half of the realm stands behind it (somewhat less, if you lead it) and the realm is not already in a civil war, even while it is at war with another kingdom (an AI charter in a realm that stands weak in its wars rises far less often: see [Quarrels in a time of war](#quarrels-in-a-time-of-war)); if not, it asks once more after a while, and the second time it breaks up bitter. AI rulers seldom sign: charters get their laws mostly by winning the council's votes. A charter's rising is a [civil war](civil-war.md) in which the ruler keeps the throne whatever happens, and signs as much of the charter as the terms of the peace give it (see [The terms of a civil war](civil-war.md#the-outcomes)). If you lead it and it is its last asking, the window that asks you to raise its banner says so, and its other button reads **"Let the charter go"**.

- **The laws of the crown.** Where [High Crown Authority](crown-laws.md#high-crown-authority) is law, a return to Autonomous Vassals is always a charter's first demand; a Normal or High [tax obligation](crown-laws.md#the-tax-obligation) brought down is its second. Each vote or seal lowers the tax one step, and the clause is won only when the tax is truly below what the charter asked.
- **The law of succession.** Where the ruler has brought in a [law of succession](succession.md#the-laws-of-succession) against the realm's custom, the houses hold it against the crown (*"the crown has changed the very law by which it passes, against the custom of our fathers"*), and a charter may demand the old law back. It can ask nothing else of the succession, and only while the new law is resented: once the new law has become the custom, the charter drops that demand, and a charter left with nothing to ask is over.
- **A sealed charter binds the crown for a good while**, however it was won: a ruler who undoes one of its laws in that time breaks the charter, and the houses that sealed it remember. An AI ruler does not do it.
- **If you lead it**, the crown never buys you without asking: a window, **"The crown's purse"**, offers you its gold (**"Take the purse"**: you leave the charter and no lord hears you on a charter or a reckoning for a while; or **"Send it back"**). Your charter keeps time as an AI one does: if you let a long while pass without putting one of its laws to the council when you could, it counts as a vote lost, and it comes before the ruler or fades. A lord of the charter reminds you shortly before.
- **A house promised the crown** by the peace of a [civil war](civil-war.md#the-outcomes) seals no other house's charter, is not asked to, and takes its seal off one it is on; it may still lead a charter of its own.
- **If you are the ruler**, you may win houses back from a charter set against you, by the same odds an AI ruler has: speak to the head of a house that sealed it (*"Your seal is on a charter set against my crown. Take it off..."*), once in a while for each charter, never its head.

When the head of the house leading a charter or a reckoning dies, the heir leads it on with the house; it passes to another house only if this one can no longer lead it (it left the realm, took the crown, rode out to a feud and so on). The same holds for your heir. If the cause then falls to your house as the strongest left in it, a window asks whether you take its head; if you would rather not, another house leads it if any will.

**Not in a civil war.** No charter or reckoning can begin while the realm is in a civil war or in the calm after one, yours either: you are told, and the Realm tab says so.

## The Reckoning of a house {#the-reckoning-of-a-house}

Lords band together to take a fief from another house of the realm, but only a fief the house that leads them has a [right](fief-rights.md) to. The fief is always for that house: if another house takes the lead, the fief is for it. A grudge against that house, or its having grown too great, is what moves them; a firm right is reason enough on its own for a house that has no love for the holder. The ruler's own fiefs are never the target. A realm holds at most three reckonings at a time. **A lord never joins a reckoning against his own vassal**, AI or you, not even one another vassal of his leads: his only road to a vassal's fief is to [demand it](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal).

**Never in the Empire.** In a realm of the Empire (the three Empires, and any realm of imperial culture) the land is the emperor's to give: lords do not band together to take it from one another, neither the AI nor you. A house with a right takes it to the emperor as a [petition](empire.md) instead. Nor is there any reckoning with the realm's [assemblies](assembly.md) turned off, since only an assembly can hear one.

**How it grows.** A reckoning begins in whispers: you may hear a rumour of it, with no names. Its head wins other houses over, in person or by an envoy, in peace or in war, and once enough of the realm stands behind it, with at least one house besides its head, it comes out in the open: it is **declared**, and waits to be heard.

### Heard at the assembly {#heard-at-the-assembly}

A declared reckoning is never put to the council's vote: it is laid before the ruler at the realm's [assembly](assembly.md), and the ruler judges it.

- **First on the roll.** At the next assembly, the declared reckonings are heard before any other case, in the order they came out in the open, and the assembly sits a day longer for each. Their houses and the house they are after are drawn to the hall like the sides of any case. A reckoning whose house or head rides in a feud, or is already a side in another case, waits for the next assembly; so does one whose fief has changed hands or is under siege. If no lord of the reckoning is in the hall when it is called, it falls apart.
- **A ruler who will not hear it.** A ruler, of the AI or you, who lets a long while of peace pass with a declared reckoning waiting and no assembly called has refused it: its houses hold it against the crown, and may take up arms over it (their right is not judged). As ruler, its head warns you in his own voice a week before. An AI ruler calls the first assembly of every peace as soon as he may, and a realm with reckonings waiting may meet again later in the same peace.
- **How the ruler judges.** As any case between two houses: his regard for each head, blood, the stronger house (a calculating ruler), the **right** of the reckoning's houses to the fief (a firm right counts fully, a weak one half, and more with an honourable ruler), the houses in the hall that stand with each side (the reckoning's own houses always stand with it, yours too), what was said in the hall, and, in a small realm, his wish to lose no house over one fief (see below). Each people holds the hearing in its own form: in the **curia** of Vlandia the crown takes no house's land without the majority of the peers in the hall, and without it the claim fails, its right not judged and no grudge against the crown; the **majlis** weighs the counsel of the houses; at the **thing** the house that holds the fief may demand trial by combat, and the gods' verdict stands for the crown's. Under [High Crown Authority](crown-laws.md#high-crown-authority) it is the same: the verdict was always the ruler's.
- **What you see before the hearing.** Under **Assembly** in the Realm tab, and in the hall's menu while the assembly sits, each reckoning waiting says which houses in the hall stand with each side, by name, and, under a ruler of the AI, which way the talk in the hall says he leans and why, only what is true of him: *"The talk in the hall is that {ruler} looks kindly on the claim of {house}: it is said he holds their right to {fief} to be a good one."* At a curia, how the peers would vote. It changes as you win lords over and speak in the hall.
- **The verdict** gives what weighed most with the ruler and who stood with each side.

**If the ruler finds for the reckoning**, the head of the house that loses the fief thinks much less of him, the grievances over that fief are laid to rest, and the house must [yield or defy](#yield-or-defy). If it yields, the fief goes straight to the house the reckoning was for, and the reckoning has won; the house keeps a weak right to it and holds it against the crown and the reckoning's head. If it defies, the ruler looks the other way, at a cost to the record of his reign, and the reckoning's houses take up arms over it if they can (if they cannot, the house keeps the fief, the reckoning ends with no victor and the quarrel rests for a while); or he names it traitor, and it [breaks with the realm](civil-war.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-the-realm). A head who defies in the hall rides out under safe-conduct or is seized there, by the ruler's character, as for any [defied sentence](assembly.md#defying-the-sentence).

**If the ruler leaves the fief with its house**, every house of the reckoning holds it against the crown as the crown shielding its foe, and the ruler has [judged that right](#a-right-the-crown-has-judged). The reckoning breaks up, or takes up arms against the crown's word, which is defiance.

If a verdict ends in arms, the assembly breaks up within the hour: *"The court's verdict has put swords in the lords' hands..."*. What was left on the roll waits for the next one.

**A reckoning wins only if the fief truly reaches the house it was for.** If it went elsewhere, a foreign army took it or the crown kept it, it ends with no victor.

**As ruler**, each reckoning comes to you in the court's window, or in the [feud panel](#the-feud-panel) with the head of the reckoning face to face with the holder: **"Find for {house}: {holder} must give up {fief}"** or **"Leave {fief} with {holder}"**, each saying what follows in words (at a curia, how the peers vote). As its head or as the house it is after, you plead your case in the hall like any case; as the house that loses the fief, you **bow** or **defy the crown**; as one of its houses, you stand with the claimants.

### A right the crown has judged {#a-right-the-crown-has-judged}

When the ruler hears a reckoning and leaves the fief with its house, the matter is closed: the ruler has **judged** that right. The judgment falls on the house that led the reckoning and on every house of it with a right to the same fief, and the ruler lets it be known in his own voice. A reckoning the ruler never heard, or one the peers of a curia threw out, is not judged. As ruler, the hint of **"Leave {fief} with {holder}"** tells you that it judges their right.

- **While that ruler reigns**, raising the right again is defiance of the crown: a new reckoning over the fief, pressing it [by the sword](#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses) in any of the [three ways](#three-ways-to-press-a-right), or taking up arms at the moment of his verdict. It is weighed when it comes out in the open: a reckoning when it is declared, a right pressed by the sword when its rumours begin.
- **The ruler may look the other way**, as he may at [a defied ban](#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses): a ruler of the AI weighs the house's strength against the crown's, his own mercy, cunning and honour, his regard for its head and how the realm's wars go. If he does, the house goes ahead, his judgment binds it no more, he and its head think the less of each other, and it costs the record of his reign. If he does not, the house is counted traitor: it [breaks with the realm](civil-war.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-the-realm) and raises a civil war, and what it raised is over. A house that cannot break with the realm bows instead, and lets the matter drop.
- **A house of the AI** very rarely dares it: only a house very strong next to the crown, or one whose realm is doing very badly in its wars, and even then seldom.
- **You** are warned every time. The hints of every way to raise the right say what it would mean, and a window, **"A right the crown has judged"**, asks you to confirm: **"Defy the crown's judgment"** or **"Let it rest"**. If your house could not break with the realm, the choice is greyed with the reason, or kept out of the conversation. As ruler, a window, **"Your judgment defied"**, asks you whether to look the other way or **"Hold to my judgment"**.
- **The judgment lapses** when that ruler no longer reigns, when the fief passes into other hands, or when the house leaves the realm; you are told when one of yours does.
- Under **Claims** in the Realm tab, a right judged this way is marked *"judged by"* the ruler who judged it.

**A small realm spares its houses.** In a realm with fewer towns and castles than its [fair share](civil-war.md#how-large-a-kingdom-is), the ruler is slower to find for a reckoning: a small realm cannot afford to lose a house over one fief. The smaller the realm, the more he holds back. When that is what weighed most, he says so: *"This realm is too small to lose a house over one fief."*

**When you start one**, tell a lord *"There is a fief in this realm held against a better right than its holder's."*, then pick the house and the town or castle. Only fiefs your house has a right to are offered, each with how firm it is; the fief is for your house. How likely the lord is to agree is said in words, and depends on their grudge against that house and their bonds with it and with you. You can start one against a house led by a kinsman of the ruler too. Win houses over in person, by an envoy, or from their house under **Fealty** in the Realm tab; once it is declared, it waits for the assembly, and its entry under **Assembly** says whether it is *waiting for the assembly* or already *on the assembly's roll*. AI reckonings go after the fief their leader has the firmest right to. A reckoning whose houses no longer have a right to anything the house holds comes to an end. An AI house presses a claim [by marriage](fief-rights.md#a-claim-by-marriage) only when the two heads are on bad terms; you may press yours at any time.

**Against one's own blood.** An AI house joins a reckoning against its kin or in-laws only when it is on very bad terms with them and on very good terms with the reckoning's head, even when you ask it, and it seldom brings a reckoning against its kin itself, the more seldom the better they get on (in-laws over a fief claimed through the marriage are another matter: see [Rights to fiefs](fief-rights.md)); you decide for yourself.

In a long peace, the realm's [assembly](assembly.md) also hears the wrongs between houses of the last war and the crown's wrongs a house asks it to make good; a feud is never brought before it.

## Yield or defy {#yield-or-defy}

Whatever road takes a fief from a house (the ruler's [decree](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal), for the crown or for claimants in a feud, a reckoning found for at the assembly, a sentence of the [assembly](assembly.md) or a fief it [distrains for amends refused](assembly.md#refusing-the-amends), a lord's demand of his vassal, a petition the emperor grants, a felon's land, the khan's claim on an ulus, the crown's judgment of a traitor), the house answers it the same way: it **yields**, or it **defies**.

- **A house of the AI** defies the more readily the stronger its side looks next to the other: its own swords, and part of those of every house likely to ride with it should it break with the realm (its kin, its allies, the houses sworn to it, a plot against that king), against the crown (or the lord who demands it) and the houses that wanted the fief. A brave head, one on bad terms with the ruler, defies more; an honourable or calculating one less; and a realm weak in its wars abroad holds it back, except against a [fief taken for nothing](royal-court.md#a-fief-taken-for-nothing). A house beaten in a civil war seldom defies the crown's judgment.
- **A house in a plot** may answer with its plot instead: the plot [rises over it](civil-war.md#the-rising), or the house breaks with the realm alone, or it yields.
- **Some never defy**: the ruler's house, sellswords, a house with no grown head, one that holds no town or castle, and one whose loss would leave the realm with nothing. They yield.
- **If it defies, the ruler decides** (for a lord's demand, the lord): **look the other way**, or **act**. A ruler of the AI weighs it as he weighs a [defied ban](#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses), and looks the other way less readily at a defiance of his own decree.
- **Looked the other way**, the house keeps the fief. The ruler thinks less of its head and holds it against the house, and every blind eye to a defiance costs the record of his reign (see [The ruler's own claim](succession.md#the-rulers-own-claim)). If there are claimants (a reckoning found for, a decree for claimants, a sentence with a wronged house), they may take up arms for it in a feud, the crown letting the swords decide; if they cannot, the quarrel rests for a while. A lord who looks the other way at his vassal thinks less of him and may not demand that fief again for a while.
- **Acted on**, the ruler names the house **traitor**, and it [breaks with the realm](civil-war.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-the-realm): a civil war. A lord who acts takes up arms against his vassal in a feud instead (see [Felony](#felony)); a vassal never breaks with the realm over his lord's demand.
- **Rarely, a house named traitor goes over to another king** with all its fiefs: one at war with its ruler, near its own lands, whose ruler it likes and whose own it dislikes, the less often the more honourable its head; never to your realm.

**If the fief is yours**, a window asks you: for a decree, **"A decree of the crown"**, with **"Give up the fief"** and **"Defy the decree"**; for your lord's demand, **"Your lord's demand"**, with **"Give it up to your lord"** and **"Bar the gates"** (losing the feud that may follow is felony); for a sentence of the assembly, **"Bow to the crown's ruling"** and **"Defy the crown"**. Their hints tell you what you risk, without numbers. If you are in a plot, you may **"Raise the plot's banner"** or **"Renounce your oath"** (break with the realm alone) instead. What you cannot do is shown greyed with the reason; if you choose a road that cannot be taken after all, you keep the fief and are asked again. **As ruler**, a window, **"Defied before the realm"**, asks you whether to **"Look the other way"** or **"Name the {house} traitors"**.

An AI ruler seldom takes a fief for his own house out of mere bad blood: it is a thing he does rarely, and dearly paid for (see [A fief taken for nothing](royal-court.md#a-fief-taken-for-nothing)).

## Quarrels in a time of war {#quarrels-in-a-time-of-war}

A war with another kingdom does not hold back every quarrel of a realm. Plots and charters may rise, reckonings are won over and declared, and a house stripped of a fief may defy the crown, in the middle of a war as in peace. Feuds are another matter (see [Feuds frowned on in a war](#feuds-frowned-on-in-a-war)), and the assembly that hears the reckonings meets only in peace.

**A realm that stands weak** in its wars is far less ready to turn on itself. While it is at war with another kingdom and is either **losing** (much weaker than its foes, and behind in at least one of its wars) or **small** next to the average realm, every chance its AI lords have to start a war within is a small fraction of what it would be: a plot or a charter rising, a feud taken up, a house defying the crown. A realm grown far beyond its fair share is never held weak, even while it loses. At peace, or at war and not weak, nothing changes. The [coup of a strategos](empire.md) is never held back by this.

- Your own choices are yours: you may raise your plot, press your right or answer a decree as you will. Only what is left to your house's character weighs the realm's weakness, as for any house.
- **A charter** of the AI whose time runs out in a weak realm seldom rises; otherwise it asks again.
- **A feud being prepared** whose lords chose to take up arms while the realm was still sound weighs its weakness once more when it is ready to declare. Held back, it waits, ready, and weighs it again from time to time, or declares as soon as the realm stands sound again, while a feud's preparations may last. If you ride with them, their head tells you so in a letter.
- **A battle under way** delays a rising only a few hours, a day at most: a plot or a charter waits for its houses' battles to end, then rises; its lords leave the sieges and armies other houses lead. A feud about to begin waits for its houses' battles, or an assault on their walls, to end. If you lead a plot whose men are in a battle, your own **Raise the banner** is greyed meanwhile: *"the banner waits until the fighting is over."*
- With a foe besieging a town or castle of the realm, no feud begins.

### Feuds frowned on in a war {#feuds-frowned-on-in-a-war}

A realm at war with another kingdom has better uses for its swords than turning them on each other, and its ruler and lords know it. While the realm is at war abroad (its own civil war does not count):

- **The lords seldom take up a feud**, far more seldom than in peace, though a lord on bad terms with the ruler cares less; and they are slower to defy the crown's ban.
- **The ruler is readier to forbid a feud** between his houses, and says why: the realm's enemies come first. While the war lasts he may forbid it **even against a firm right** of the claimants.
- **A house that takes up a feud all the same** sours its ruler on its head. You are warned before you press a right by the sword or take up arms with a reckoning in a war. Not when the crown rides with the claimants, nor when the feud comes out of a lord's demand of his vassal (there the one who bars his gates is the one defying).
- **A quarrel forbidden in war rests a shorter while** before it may be pressed again.

At peace, nothing of this changes anything.

**A forbidden quarrel returns with peace.** A feud the ruler forbade while the realm was at war is not forgotten. On the first day the realm is at peace with every kingdom, such a quarrel may be taken up again, one a day in each realm, before any other: a house of the AI weighs it with its usual will, and if it was yours a window, **"An old quarrel, and peace"**, asks whether you take it up again or let it be. Taken up, it gives [warning](#warning-before-arms) like any other right pressed by the sword, and the crown answers it again at the ultimatum; it may forbid it once more, now in peace. Left alone for the first days of peace, or overtaken by a new war, it is forgotten.

## When a reckoning takes up arms: feuds between houses {#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses}

Houses of one realm may come to blows over a fief or a title: a **feud**. One begins when:

- **a right is pressed by the sword** (below), the commonest road;
- **a reckoning takes up arms**: when the ruler found for it and looked the other way as the house defied him, or never heard it, or when its houses defy his verdict and [the right he judged](#a-right-the-crown-has-judged);
- **a lord takes up arms against a vassal** who bars his gates against his demand (see [Titles](titles.md#a-lord-takes-back-a-vassals-fief)).

A realm can hold only so many feuds at a time; no house rides in two, nor is one fief fought over twice. If you lead the reckoning, you decide whether it takes up arms.

**A right pressed by the sword.** A feud needs no reckoning. Now and then a house of the AI with a [right](fief-rights.md) to a fief another house of its realm holds makes up its mind to take it, gives [warning](#warning-before-arms) and then comes for it, if its side is strong enough, counting the allies that would answer its call, the houses with a cause to join it, and what its purse could hire. You may do the same, in any of [three ways](#three-ways-to-press-a-right); the ruler's house is out of reach for you too, but whether your side is strong enough is yours to judge, and you may press a right against your own kin or a friend (you are warned what laying siege to them will cost you with them). A right to a [title](titles.md#claims-to-a-title) is pressed the same way, in a [feud for the title](titles.md#a-feud-for-a-title). A firm right, a bold head with little honour, and bad blood with the holder all make it likelier. A house of the AI weighs heavily against coming to this with its kin or a friend, the more so the warmer the friendship, and only very seldom does; no house presses the ruler's own, nor a lord a house sworn to him: he can only [demand its fief](titles.md#a-lord-takes-back-a-vassals-fief). Under [High Crown Authority](crown-laws.md#high-crown-authority) no right is pressed by the sword. If the fief is yours, the ultimatum asks you whether to fight or give it up; if the realm is yours, you answer as ruler.

**Never in the Empire.** In a realm of the Empire a lord who takes up arms against another is a rebel against the throne: no feud is fought there, by any road. A right is laid before the emperor as a [petition](empire.md) instead.

- **Allies called to arms.** Each [ally](#alliances-between-houses) of the head of either side is called first, and most answer; an honourable one, or one who likes the caller well, all the more. One allied to both heads stays out. An ally who refuses breaks the alliance, and the house that called holds it against him. If you are the ally called, you are asked.
- **Kin and friends who stay home.** A house that is kin or a close friend of a side's head and will not ride when called loses a little of that head's regard, you and the AI alike (bound to both sides and riding with neither, you lose nothing).
- **The two sides.** Each of the reckoning's houses may ride with its leader, the bolder the likelier (with a right pressed by the sword, the claimant rides alone at first). A house of the reckoning that stays home when its head takes up arms loses a little of that head's regard, you and the AI alike (not if you said you would ride and were then taken prisoner or shut in). The house under attack is joined by its kin and its close friends; an AI house that holds the fief fights for it, and gives it up at the ultimatum only very rarely, when far outmatched, and never its last town or castle. Other houses ride only if they are kin or close friends of one side: one bound to both goes with the one it likes best, and a house bound to neither stays home. Now and then an honourable house with no ties to either side, and no enmity with the holder, stands with the holder, when the claimants' [right](fief-rights.md) to the fief is weaker than the holder's; the more honourable, the likelier. A noble house with no town or castle may ride on either side like any other; a [landed knight](landed-knights.md) takes no side of his own, but rides in his lord's host, and a house that loses its last town or castle in the feud stays on its side to the end.
- **Your part in it.** When a feud breaks out in your realm, the head of a house that is your kin or your friend writes to ask your help: the claimants to ride with them, the holder to stand with them. Bound to both, you get both letters and choose one side, or neither. At any time while the feud lasts you may also offer your sword yourself: speak to the head of either house (or, while the head is a prisoner, to a grown lord of the house), or send an envoy with the offer. A head who likes you little turns you down; a calculating one takes help where he finds it. Joining earns you the ill will of the other side's head. If you serve the realm as a mercenary, you offer your company instead: the house pays you once, if its coffers can bear it, you leave the realm's service (an unfinished contract counts as broken), and you ride with its host as a sellsword, sharing its wars (the realm's foes are its foes), until the feud is done, when they let you go. As anywhere, a sellsword is given no fief: a castle your company storms goes to the house that leads the host.
- **The ruler's answer.** For a right pressed by the sword, the ruler answers when the ultimatum is sent. The ruler can let them settle it, forbid the feud, or stand with the claimants and give them the fief by decree. A ruler of the AI most often lets them be; he forbids it more readily the more merciful and honourable he is, and when he favours the holder; he stands with the claimants now and then, when he favours them, and less readily when their right is only weak. Forbidding a feud costs influence, and standing with the claimants a little less (you and the AI alike); only a ruler whose right to rule the lords hold secure can do either. The head of a house that bows to the ban thinks a little less of the ruler for it (you are told in the hint of **"Bow to the crown"**). **The crown respects a firm right:** when the claimants hold a firm [right](fief-rights.md) to the fief (or a firm claim to the title), the ruler, you included, may only let them settle it or stand with them; he cannot forbid the feud, save that a ruler at war abroad may still [forbid it](#feuds-frowned-on-in-a-war). Your warnings before you take up arms say what the crown may still do. Standing with the claimants is a [decree](royal-court.md#taking-a-fief-from-a-vassal) and costs what a decree costs (nothing with the claimants, who asked for it); the holder [yields or defies](#yield-or-defy) it as any decree, and if the ruler looks the other way, the feud goes on. **Defying the ban:** a leader whom the crown forbade may defy it, by a weighing of valour, honour, regard for the ruler and the strength his side could count on: a lord who loves his ruler almost never does. You may defy it too. A ruler of the AI may then look the other way, weighing the house's strength against the crown's, his own mercy, cunning and honour, his regard for its head, and how the realm's wars go (a crown losing them looks away more readily). If he does, the feud begins as if he had let it be: the influence spent on the ban is lost, he thinks less of the house's head and holds it against him, and it costs the record of his reign. If not, the house is named traitor and [breaks with the realm](civil-war.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-the-realm). Your own defiance of an AI ruler is weighed the same way (the hint says so); as ruler, a window asks you whether to look away or hold them to your word. While they wait, the crown's acts in a feud are listed under **Pending** in the Realm tab.
- **The fight.** For the length of the feud each side rides out as a host of its own, under the banner of its leading house, named after it and its realm (*Host of the dey Molarn (Vlandia)*): a kingdom in all but name, with the realm's laws and customs, that raises armies, lays sieges and rides to the relief of its walls like any other. The houses keep their lands, and their lords are still sworn to the crown: they still count as the realm's own, pay their dues, swear the oath to a new ruler, stand for vacant titles and keep their rights. The two hosts are at war only with each other, and with the realm's foes as before. No other kingdom may make war or peace with a host, nor ally with it, and no lord or sellsword of the AI takes service in one. A host holds no council: a fief it takes stays with the house that took it until the feud is settled. When the feud is over, the hosts are broken up.
- **To arms.** When a feud breaks out, both sides call up their men: for some days, lords whose companies are thin make recruiting their first care, riding to the towns and villages that would give them men and paying for them as always; the garrisons are left to hold the walls. Each host also hires free companies of sellswords, the nearest first, as long as its richest house can pay them; they ride with it until the feud is over. Once the feud is declared, the lords of both sides may also hire the [companies for hire](free-companies.md) of the taverns, as any lord at war does, if their purses can bear the whole term, taking turns so that neither side takes them all; nobody hires while a feud is still being prepared in silence. If you head a side, the nearest company you can afford offers you its service once, when the feud begins (**"Hire them (… denars)"** or **"Not now"**); after that, hire your swords by [envoy](envoys.md). A company so hired serves its whole term, even if the feud ends first. The lords of a host can gather an army with lighter companies than a realm would ask, and march it on the walls. Before the walls of the fief in dispute they need no more strength than the walls hold, where a realm's army would want far more, since a lesser house can look for little relief. Nor do they storm it while its walls stand: they let their engines batter them down first, for some days at most, and then storm it all the same. While the feud is fought, every lord's company of the houses riding in it holds more men than usual (yours too); once it is over they are back to their usual size, and the extra men drift away.
- **Gathering before the defiance.** A house of the AI that means to take up arms does not send its defiance at once. Its lords and their friends first gather their men in silence, making recruiting their first care, and only once their companies are nearly full and the walls are within their reach does the realm hear of it, in the rumours that begin the [warning](#warning-before-arms). If the men cannot be found in good time, the quarrel is let be, and nobody need ever know how close it came; however long a foe at the walls holds them back, preparations do not last for ever. A feud prepared in secret gets in nobody's way: the house it aims at knows nothing and goes about its business, and it does not count among the realm's feuds until the rumours begin. If its leader or its target is drawn into another feud first, the preparations are given up.
- **No hopeless feuds.** A house of the AI does not take up arms over a fief its side could never besiege, even with every man called up and every sellsword and company it could afford; a side that can bring most of what the siege needs may try, counting on the men it will still raise and hire. One pressing a right by the sword looks for another fief it could take instead.
- **A foe at the walls.** If an enemy besieges a place of the realm during the feud, the crown may call the houses to arms: see [The crown's call to arms](#the-crowns-call-to-arms) below. A feud already under way is not stopped by a war alone; when the realm declares a new war, the crown may [summon its host](#the-host-summoned-for-a-war-abroad).
- **More companies in a feud.** A noble house of the AI usually leads fewer companies than your house or a free company of the same standing. While it is out of its realm for a feud, on either side, it may lead as many as they do, so most houses can put one or two more in the field. Each still needs a lord of the house to lead it. Once the house is home again, the companies raised for the feud are disbanded: their lords ride home and their men join the garrison of the house's nearest castle or town (or the realm's, if it holds none); their stores are lost.
- **Other quarrels fall away.** A house that rides out to a feud, on either side, leaves behind whatever else it was part of in the realm; an AI house weighs its plot before riding into a feud of its own choosing, as you do. A charter or a reckoning goes on without it, under its strongest house if it was the one leading it, or comes to nothing; a plot against the throne it led falls apart. A reckoning against a house that rides out waits for it: it neither moves nor ends while the house is away, and takes up where it left off once the house is back. If you are asked to a feud, or offer your sword, while your house is part of such a cause, you are told what you would leave behind.
- **Winning houses over.** While a feud is prepared in secret, its claimants look for more friends; once the rumours are out, both sides do. Every few days the head of each side of the AI asks the house most likely to say yes. A house rides only for a cause of its own: its head is a good friend of the one who asks, or on bad terms with the other side's head, or it holds a grudge against the other house, or it is honourable and the one who asks has the better right. With none, it stays home. Silver does not buy it: once it has said yes, the head who asked pays it a compensation for keeping its men in the field (if it joined while the feud was still being prepared, once the feud begins), as one realm does when it calls an ally to a war (whatever he can spare). A house that says no is left alone for a while. Nobody is courted while the feud waits or a foe is at the walls. You may be written to with such a request, and told what you would be paid; if you head a side, you may ask the head of any house of the realm in conversation, by an [envoy](envoys.md) (*"Ask a house to ride with us in our feud"*), or from that house under **Fealty** in the Realm tab, and you pay the same way. A house that joins earns the other side's ill will.
- **The others remember.** A house that would not heed the crown's call with a foe at the walls, or went over to the foe, earns grievances from every other house.

A realm torn by a feud looks weaker to its neighbours, who are readier to make war on it. Open **Wars within** in the Realm tab to see a feud's two sides face to face.

### How a feud ends {#how-a-feud-ends}

A feud has no set end in days. The war between the two hosts is scored like any war, on the Diplomacy tab (see [The war score](war-and-peace.md#the-war-score)): battles, sieges and raids measured against the whole realm as it stood when the feud began, the **Prisoners** each side holds, and the **Objective**: holding the fief fought over (a landed knight's hold counts for his lord), or, in a feud for a title, clearly the greater part of the duchy's lands. Each day the side that holds it gains ground, the side that took it faster than the side that already held it. The journal entry **"The stakes of the war with the {enemy}"** tells you what the feud turns on, who holds it today and how ready both sides are for peace, with the fief marked on your map.

**Peace between the hosts.** The two hosts end it on the [Terms of Peace](war-and-peace.md#terms-of-peace) screen, with the same course as any war: no talk of peace in the first weeks unless one side is crushed; then terms may be set out, but so soon not for nothing: they must give the side ahead something, if only a sum in silver; then the lords grow weary of it, and at last sick of it. The head of each host sets any terms, at no cost; a lord who rides in a host he does not lead may only put a white peace, a pardon or a modest sum in silver before its council, paying the game's influence for proposing a peace. Both hosts vote in their councils, and each head has the last word over his own; a house that would hand over its own fief by the terms votes against them. If you lead a host, press **Negotiate terms** on the Diplomacy tab of the kingdom screen, speak to the other head in person, or send an [envoy](envoys.md); the AI's terms reach you by courier. The feud ends within the hour, on the terms agreed:

| Term | What it brings |
|---|---|
| **A white peace** | The fief goes back to whoever held it when the feud began, everything taken in it goes back, and the claim stands for another day. Nothing else goes beside it but a pardon, and not before the lords have grown weary of the war |
| **The fief** (or **the title**) | It passes to the claimants; the holders keep a firm right to it, as any house that loses a fief, and hold no grudge for it |
| **Their own right given up** | Only with the fief: the holders give up every right to it, for themselves and their heirs |
| **The right weakened** | The claimants keep their right, or their claim to the title, but a firm one becomes weak |
| **The right given up** | The claimants give it up for good, for themselves and the house they fought for |
| **The vassal freed** | In a feud over a [lord's demand](titles.md#a-lord-takes-back-a-vassals-fief): the vassal holds of the crown from then on and owes that lord nothing, even with the fief yielded to him in the same peace |
| **A pardon** | Each side may pardon those sworn to it who rose against it; without it, the side that comes out of the peace with the prize judges them for [felony](#felony) |
| **A sum in silver** | Modest, heavy or great, paid once as the peace is sealed by the house that leads the side that pays (greyed if it has not got it), to the house fought for or the holder |

No other town or castle changes hands at a feud's peace, and no tribute.

**What the AI gives and asks.** A host of the AI gives only what the war score has earned, and the side ahead asks for something. A side well ahead that holds the fief keeps it by the terms. A clear win asks at least the lighter form of what the feud is about: the fief, for the claimants; for the holders, the claimants' right weakened (or given up, when their right is only a weak one) or, against a vassal, his freedom. A crushing win asks the stronger form: the right given up, the vassal freed, or the fief with the holders' own right given up. It asks only what the other side could give, and silver only fills out the rest.

In a feud over a lord's demand, the vassal is free of his lord when the peace frees him, or when the lord's side is crushed at once.

**Ends at once.** A side whose leading house has lost the last of its towns and castles, and has seen the other side hold it for some days, loses as if crushed; so does a side left with nobody in arms, or whose head goes home. Every end with no winner (the houses make up, the feud falls out of reach, the crown's call to arms breaks it, a head is crowned or goes to another realm) is a white peace: what was taken goes back. What a house takes while the feud is being fought does not become its seat, does not change its lord, pays no dues and gives no title. A fief one side took from the other besides the one fought over goes back to its house at the end, as custom asks, though now and then the taker refuses (less likely an honourable or generous one); a house that keeps one makes an enemy of the house it took it from, which keeps its right to it. If it is you who took it, you choose.

**A pause.** A feud set aside (a foe at the walls, the lord's peace, the death of the ruler, a side with nobody in arms) keeps its war score, its days and its objective, and takes up with them when it resumes; meanwhile the days gain no one ground.

### Warning before arms {#warning-before-arms}

A right pressed by the sword (to a fief or to a [title](titles.md#a-feud-for-a-title)) does not fall on a house out of a clear sky. Between the moment a house makes up its mind and the first drawn sword, the whole realm has fair warning, the same for you and for the AI. A reckoning that takes up arms and a lord's demand of his vassal are not warned of this way: they come out of a quarrel the realm already knows.

- **Rumours.** For some days, talk of the quarrel goes round the realm: *"They say {lord} of the {house} has been counting swords and weighing friends of late, as one does who means to use them."* The rumour names the fief only when the house has a single right it could press, or the crown has judged it; otherwise it says only that it means to make good *"one of their old rights"*, and nobody can say which. From the rumours on, the quarrel counts among the realm's feuds, and neither its houses nor its fief can be drawn into another.
- **The ultimatum.** Then the head of the claimants writes to the holders: give it up, or it will be taken by the sword. *"I want {fief}. Hand it over, or I come and take it."* This is the formal demand, and the moment the crown gives its answer: let them settle it, forbid it, or stand with the claimants, as for any [feud](#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses). Kin, friends, allies and vassals are asked to take a side here too. If the crown forbids it and then lets it go on after all (a ban defied and a blind eye), the ultimatum takes up again with the days it had left.
- **Arms.** If the holders have not given it up when the time runs out, the feud begins, and only then do the hosts hire their sellswords.
- **The holder makes ready.** While the warning lasts, a threatened house of the AI asks the houses friendly to it, once each, to swear a [pact](#alliances-between-houses), and each answers as it would answer you in person, the less eagerly the stronger the claimants are; a new ally is called to its side. It also fills out the garrison of what is threatened by the game's own recruiting, up to what it usually holds and never beyond. If it is your house, the same doors are open to you: ask for pacts yourself, and see to your walls.
- **The claimants may think better of it.** A house of the AI that grows too weak for the quarrel, or can no longer reach the walls, lets it drop, and a warning held up too long by a foe at the walls or a realm with no ruler comes to nothing. The grudge stays.
- **One word for each moment.** The realm hears of it once when the rumours start, once when the ultimatum is sent, with all that came of it at that moment (the crown's answer, a defiance, a blind eye or a house breaking with the realm), once when it comes to arms, and once when it ends, whether you are part of it or not.

**If the fief is yours.** Your journal opens an entry, **"A quarrel over {fief}"**, with the fief marked on your map, as soon as the rumours name it. When the letter comes, a window, **"A letter from the {house}"**, asks you to **"Give it up"** or **"Refuse them"**; refused, the journal counts the days before they take up arms. At any time while the warning lasts, even after refusing, the **"Yield"** button on its entry under **Wars within** in the Realm tab gives the fief up and spares your house the feud; it is greyed with the reason while the rumours have not yet said what they want of you. Its **"How it stands"** tells you what a lord in your place would weigh.

**If the claim is yours.** You go through the same: your rumours, then your letter, under the same rules as the houses of the AI. The **"Withdraw"** button on its entry under **Wars within** lets the matter drop, in the rumours or under the ultimatum: there is no letter, or your demand is taken back, and there is no feud. Your right stays as it is, but the lords will not hear of the quarrel again for a while.

**Under Wars within**, every warning of the realm has its entry beside the feuds: the rumours (who is gathering their swords, and the fief only if the rumour named it), or the ultimatum (who demands what of whom, the time left to give it up, and the crown's answer, or that it is still awaited).

### Three ways to press a right {#three-ways-to-press-a-right}

Your house may press a right to a fief, or a claim to a [title](titles.md#claims-to-a-title), held by another house of your realm in three ways, all under the same rules the houses of the AI follow:

- **In person:** speak to the head of the house that holds it. The line appears only when the right can be pressed now; with more than one right against the same house, you pick which after the talk.
- **By an envoy:** *"Carry your defiance to the {house}: press a right by the sword"*, from the head of that house's page in the encyclopedia (see [Envoys](envoys.md)) or from that house under **Fealty** in the Realm tab. Your rights are listed, those that cannot be pressed greyed with the reason. When the envoy arrives it is weighed again; if it can no longer be pressed, the envoy writes to say why.
- **From the kingdom screen:** each of your rows under **Your house's rights**, in **Claims** of the Realm tab, has a **Press by the sword** button, greyed when it cannot be pressed, with the reason in its tooltip. It asks you to confirm, closes the kingdom screen and makes your purpose known. The **House** tab of the clan screen lists the same rights, only to read.

Whichever you choose, your house says it will make its right good, and the [warning](#warning-before-arms) begins: rumours first, then your letter, then arms. Each of them warns you when pressing it would mean riding against your own lord (see [Felony](#felony)), or souring your ruler in a time of war, and once the feud begins the journal says how it is won.

### What your stewards hear {#what-your-stewards-hear}

Before you choose, you are told what the AI would weigh in your place, in words and without numbers: when you confirm that you press a right by the sword (in person, by an envoy or with **Press by the sword**, whose **"How it stands"** carries the verdict), and when you bar the gates against your lord's demand. The windows that ask you to take a side in a feud show the [feud panel](#the-feud-panel) instead.

- **Who would ride**: the houses that would go with each side, a handful each, every one with *"will surely"*, *"are likely to"*, *"may"* or *"are unlikely to"*.
- **Aid from above**: whether the lord of the house attacked would send to his own lord for aid, and how likely that lord is to come.
- **The verdict**: *"Your host would outmatch theirs"*, *"You would be outnumbered"* or *"You would be evenly matched"*, and whether your host could take the walls.

It is only what your stewards hear: nothing changes for the AI.

### The feud panel {#the-feud-panel}

Whenever a feud between houses of your realm asks you to take a side (your reckoning taking up arms, the letters of kin, friends and allies, a house courting you, your lord's call, the house attacked, the crown's answer when the realm is yours, and the others), the question comes in a panel like the game's marriage offer, with the time stopped. As ruler, the same panel brings you **a reckoning to judge at the assembly** (the house that leads it against the holder, the rights of the houses behind it and who would get the fief) and **a lord's plea against his vassal** under [High Crown Authority](crown-laws.md#high-crown-authority) (the lord and his vassal face to face, the lord's right to the fief, and the law that keeps the sword out of it):

- **The two heads face to face**, the claimants on one side and the holders on the other, each with their portrait, house banner and traits as the game shows them (a lord you have not met yet you know only by name and repute), and your relation with each.
- **Their right**: who attacks to take the fief and who holds it, and what [right](fief-rights.md) each house of their side has to it, firm, weak or none known, and who would get it should the claimants win. In a feud for a title, their claims to the title.
- **Their house**: rank and culture, the towns and castles they hold, the men they have in the field.
- **Their ties**: the lord they are sworn to, their vassals, their allies, and their kin who head other houses. These are the houses that may answer their call; who really rides is not known until the feud is fought, and then you see who rides in each host.
- **What binds you to them**: alliance, kinship, friendship, your oath, or nothing.
- **What is at stake**: the fief (its realm, holder, walls, garrison and villages, as far as the encyclopedia would tell you) or the lands of the title, and other houses with a right to it.
- **The word that reaches you** and **your answer**: the letter or question itself, with the same choices, hints and greyed answers as before, and **"Let me look into it first"**, which closes it unanswered and opens the Realm tab on the matter, to be asked again once you are back on the map.

Every name is a link: the encyclopedia opens above the panel, and closing it brings the panel back.

### The lord's call {#the-lords-call}

In every feud, the head of each side calls his own **vassals**, the houses that hold their land of him; so does the lord of the house attacked when he comes to shield it, and a lord who comes to [aid](#aid-from-the-lord-above). Staying home is not one of the choices: each vassal **chooses a side**, with his lord or against him. Sellswords and the ruler's kin are not called; a vassal held prisoner chooses once he is free, if the feud is still on.

- **The vassals of the AI** ride with their lord unless they lean to the other side enough to risk their lives for it: bad blood with their lord, hatred above all, or warm ties with the other head. The fear of [felony](#felony) holds them back: more against a cruel lord, less against a merciful one, less when the other side is clearly the stronger (a calculating vassal sees it soonest), more when the lord's side is, and less for a valorous one. Even then it is never certain.
- **You:** a window, **"Your lord calls you to arms"**, tells you what is at stake and asks you to choose: **"Answer the call"** or **"Side with the {house}"**, with the warning that siding against your lord is felony.
- **Riding against one's lord** costs his regard, and he holds it against that house.
- **Counted before the defiance.** A house of the AI weighs every vassal the lords would call, on the side each would likely take, before it declares; your own choice counts once you make it.
- When a lord who is not a head rides in a feud, his vassals are likelier to ride with him, but are not called and owe him nothing for staying out.

### The lord's peace {#the-lords-peace}

A vassal never rides in two feuds at once: when his lord calls him, everything is folded into the lord's quarrel, as the old peace of the king and the Landfrieden did.

- **A vassal who only rides in another feud** (as one of its houses or an ally) leaves it, with no blame.
- **A vassal who leads a side of another feud:** that feud **pauses**. Everyone in it goes home, allies too, and its war score is kept as it stood; its journal entry reads *(waiting for a lord's quarrel)*. When the lord's feud is over it takes up again where it left off, unless the houses made up or the anger went out of it by then; if it ends instead, you are told why. One not yet begun is dropped (the right remains).
- **The other head of that paused feud**, if neither lord calls him, may **keep the truce** or **ride against** the vassal in the lord's feud, on the other side; never on the same side, and never forced. A head of the AI breaks it only with a grudge against that vassal, a leaning to the other side and little honour. If it is you, a window asks: **"Keep the truce"** or **"Ride against the {house}"**.
- **You are told of everything**: a message and a line in your journal whenever a vassal of your lord, or of a head of your feuds, chooses a side, and whenever a feud of yours pauses, takes up again, is dropped or ends while it waits.

### Aid from the lord above {#aid-from-the-lord-above}

When a house is attacked, its own lord shields it, except in the rare case of bad blood between them, and then he pays for it as you would; his own lord (the duke of a count shielding his castellan, say) does not come unasked, for the vassal of my vassal is not my vassal. The lord who shields, or one who came that way, may **send a rider to his own lord for aid**, as one sends for relief of a siege; never to the crown or its kin, and not to a lord the attacker is sworn to as well.

- **An AI lord** sends for aid when the other side is the stronger. **You** get a window, **"Ask your lord for aid?"**: **"Send a rider"** (greyed with the reason when it can no longer be done) or **"We hold with what we have"**.
- **When the rider arrives,** a lord who is not free (a prisoner, or caught up in a feud of his own) cannot come, and nobody blames him. An AI lord comes more readily when he is honourable or valorous and likes the one asking better than the attacker, and never if he hates the one asking. If it is you who is asked, a window says what each answer costs.
- **A lord who comes** joins the side of the house attacked, calls his own vassals, and may in turn ask his own lord.
- **A lord who will not come** though free loses the regard of the one who asked, and if the place falls, its holder holds it against him: *"our lords did not come to defend it."*
- **Counted before the defiance**, by the chance that he comes, and only when the attackers would be the stronger without him (which is when he would be asked): a lord who would rarely come does not deter an attack, a loyal and brave one does.
- You hear of the rider, the answer, the arrival and the grudge.

### Felony {#felony}

To rise in arms against one's own lord and lose is **felony**, as in the great feudal kingdoms:

- **Who:** a house that leads a feud against its own lord (for his title, or a fief of his), a vassal that holds out against [its lord's demand](titles.md), and every vassal who, called by his lord, chose the other side, when the lord comes out of the peace with the prize and its terms bring no pardon from him. A lord who loses the prize, or frees his vassal, judges nobody. A feud that ends at once, with one side crushed, judges those who rose against the lord if the lord's side won. A vassal who rose against his lord and left the feud before its end (he came home, or heeded the crown's call) still answers for it at the end if his lord's side wins; one who went to another realm is beyond his lord's reach. Nobody is judged before the feud is over.
- **The land fought over:** the house gives up to its lord what it holds in the land the feud was about, and only that: in a feud for a title, the duchy; in a feud for a fief, the lord's duchy (or county) the fief lies in. Its other towns and castles stay its own. What another house took of that land from it during the feud goes to the lord, not back to the felon. It may [yield or defy](#yield-or-defy) it, as any house that loses a fief.
- **The broken faith:** a vassal sworn to that lord also answers with his life or his purse. The lord may have him put to death: now and then, more often for a cruel lord and less for a merciful one, and most of all if he rose to take the lord's title. Spared, he pays his lord a fine from his house's gold. The AI lord decides by his character; as the lord you always choose.
- **You:** as the felon, a window, **"Your lord's judgment"**, tells you what you must give up and asks you to choose; before you ride against your lord, every warning says how his temper puts your life at stake.
- **Not when** the peace pardons them, the houses made up, or the feud broke up with nobody the winner (as when [High Crown Authority](crown-laws.md#high-crown-authority) ends it). A felon still riding in another feud is judged once that one is over.
- **The AI thinks twice:** a vassal of the AI turns against his lord only when he leans far enough the other way to risk his life (see [The lord's call](#the-lords-call)), and a house of the AI declares a feud against its own lord only when its side is strong enough; both weigh the land at stake and the very odds of death above.
- You are warned before you send a defiance that would make you a felon (in person, by an envoy, from the Realm tab), and in the journal while the feud lasts.

### The crown's call to arms {#the-crowns-call-to-arms}

If an enemy besieges a town or castle of the realm while a feud is fought, the ruler calls the houses at feud to arms if the crown has the influence for it (if you are the ruler, you choose). With no call, the feud goes on.

- **The heads answer first**, each for its whole side, and nearly always they heed the crown: an honourable head all the more, a calculating one a little less, and almost surely when the foe besieges a fief of their own. If a head refuses, each house of its side decides for itself, and a bitter, honourless house may, rarely, go over to the invader with its lands, if the invader is the stronger, and the less likely the further off his realm lies.
- **If every house heeds**, all come home and the feud waits.
- **If any house refuses**, nobody comes home yet: both sides stay in the field, still fighting each other, for **a few days of grace**. You may change your answer meanwhile: a window, **"The crown's peace"**, asks once (**"Heed the crown's call"** or **"Hold to your answer"**), and after that **"Heed the crown's call"** waits in the menu of any town, castle or village. At the head of your side, you answer for every house that rides with you. An AI house that refused thinks it over once, as the days run out (about half the time, more if it is honourable). If every house heeds before then, all come home at once.
- **When the days are out and one side heeded**, both sides lay down their arms and come home, and the feud waits. The houses that would not heed are remembered: every house of the realm outside the feud (the crown too) holds it against them, they lose its goodwill, and the ruler thinks less of their head. A house that heeded when others would not wins a little of the ruler's goodwill. Nobody is branded a traitor.
- **When neither side heeded**, the houses that heeded come home, and the crown judges each house that would not: name it **traitor**, and it [breaks with the realm](civil-war.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-the-realm) and raises a civil war, or **look the other way**. A ruler of the AI looks the other way more readily when the house is strong next to the crown, when he is merciful or calculating, when he likes its head, and when the realm's wars abroad go badly (a realm bleeding abroad cannot afford a war at home); less when he is honourable. If you are the ruler, a window asks you (**"Look the other way"** or **"Brand the {house} traitors"**); if you cannot answer in time, your council decides in your name and tells you. Looked over, the house fights on in its feud with no mark, but the ruler likes its head less and holds it against it, every house he lets off costs the record of his reign, and the houses that laid down their arms hold it against the crown. The feud goes on among those still riding in it; if one side has none left, it waits until the foe is gone; if none are left at all, it ends with nobody the winner.
- **If the foe is gone first** (at peace with the realm, or besieging nothing) before the days are out, the call lapses: nobody answers for it, and the feud goes on.
- **While a feud waits**, the grudge between its heads does not cool and there is no time limit: once the realm has been at peace with that foe for a while it takes up again where it left off, unless the houses made up, what it was fought over is gone, or it is now beyond their reach. If lords of both sides fight side by side against the invader meanwhile, the two houses may make it up and end the feud, more readily the more merciful and generous their heads; if they do not, it goes on. Making up ends the feud as a white peace.
- Heeding the crown does not lift a [felony](#felony): a vassal who rose against his lord still answers for it at the end.

### The host summoned for a war abroad {#the-host-summoned-for-a-war-abroad}

Just after the realm declares war on another kingdom, the crown **asks** the two heads of each feud whose hosts are in the field to set their quarrel aside and ride with the realm's host. It is a request, not a command: no days of grace, no traitors.

- Each head answers for its side. A head of the AI heeds less often than not: more if honourable or fond of the ruler, less if calculating or full of hatred for the other head.
- **Only if both heed** does the feud wait until that war is over, then take up again. If either refuses, the feud goes on; a head who refused loses some of the ruler's goodwill, and one who heeded loses nothing and fights on.
- At the head of a side, you get a window, **"The host is summoned against {kingdom}"**: **"Lay the quarrel aside for the war with {kingdom}"** or **"Keep to the quarrel"**. As ruler, the crown asks in your name, the same way.

**Wars and feuds.** A realm with feuds fought or paused is slower to go to war abroad: the more of its strength rides in them, the less its council (yours too) wants a new war, and less still while one is being fought; a lord may give it as his reason: *"our own houses are at each other's throats"*. While a feud runs, a realm restless after a long peace does not turn its eyes abroad; once the last one is over, it does, as if it had never waited.

## Asking a ruler to step down {#asking-a-ruler-to-step-down}

A [plot against the throne](civil-war.md) that has gathered a good part of the realm asks the ruler to lay down the crown before it rises. An AI ruler only thinks it over when the plot has most of the realm behind it, far more than it needs to ask (below that he always refuses, and the plot may ask again as soon as it is strong enough), and even then a ruler with a strong claim may well refuse; a weak claim makes him likelier to agree. A ruler newly crowned is left in peace for a while. If he does agree, the realm chooses a new ruler by its [own law](succession.md), and the plot's houses, its leader among them, vote together for the house the plot would crown, its [pretender](civil-war.md#who-it-would-crown). If he refuses, the lords hold it against him, and the plot goes on.

If you are the ruler, you are asked too, with no grace for a new reign and as soon as the plot is strong enough to ask (not again for a good while after you refuse). You can step down and stay as a vassal with your lands, or refuse and take what comes. Stepping down takes your whole house off the throne: none of it stands in the vote that follows (see [The crown and its claim](succession.md)).

If you lead the plot, you may ask the ruler yourself once it is strong enough, as an AI plot does; below the share at which an AI ruler would think it over, he refuses.

## Alliances between houses {#alliances-between-houses}

Two houses of a realm may become allies, the ruler's and yours as any other.

- **By marriage.** When a head of a house, or a child or a brother or sister of one, weds someone of another house of the same realm, the two houses are allied, if their heads were on good terms before the wedding. The alliance rests on that marriage: if one of the couple dies, it holds if another couple of the two houses is still wed, or as a sworn pact if their heads are good friends; otherwise it is over.
- **By a pact.** Heads of houses who are good friends may swear to stand together, now and then, rarely. A head of the AI may write to you to offer it; you may also ask a head of house who is your good friend in conversation (*(House alliance)*), or send an [envoy](envoys.md) to ask, from his page in the encyclopedia or from his house under **Fealty** in the Realm tab. If he says no, give it a couple of months: ask again sooner and he only tells you his answer has not changed.
- **What it does.** Allies are called to arms in a feud (see above), and weigh riding with an ally that [breaks with the realm](civil-war.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-the-realm). In a plot against the crown, a charter or a reckoning, a house leans more to join one an ally of it leads (or, for a plot, one that would crown an ally), and far less to join one against an ally of it: the crown, or the house a reckoning is after. Allied to both, neither counts.
- **When it ends.** When a house leaves the realm (also by riding over to the rebels of a civil war), when the two end on opposite sides of a feud or one joins a reckoning against the other, when the heads of a sworn pact fall out, or when an ally refuses a call to arms.
- **Weddings.** Lords of the AI marry within their own realm far more than abroad, and so do the matches offered to your house.

Your allies are listed under **Your standing** in the Realm tab, under *Your bonds*.

## You and the factions {#you-and-the-factions}

Talk to the head of another house of your realm:

These talks are under one line of the game's *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*: **"There are matters of the realm I would speak of, away from other ears. (Plots, factions and alliances)"**, shown only when one of them is open to you. Every line the mod adds to a lord's talk ends with a tag in brackets saying what it belongs to: *(Plot against the crown)*, *(Factions)*, *(Fief vote)*, *(Feud)*, *(Squire path)*, *(Manhunter path)*, *(Marshal)*, *(Royal court)* and so on.

- **What news from the realms of Calradia? (News of the realms)** Any lord, of any kingdom, unless he dislikes you: pick a realm and he tells you who rules it and how strong it is, its wars (with whom, for how long, who declared it and the reason its lords gave, and how it goes), its alliances, and its troubles within as far as they are known abroad: a civil war, a feud between two of its houses, a charter or a reckoning pressed in the open, lords demanding the ruler step down, talk of a plot (never who is in it), traitors being judged.
- **What is happening in the realm? (The realm's affairs)** and **Tell me of your house. (Their house)**: see [Asking how things stand](#asking-how-things-stand) below.
- **What news of the realm, my friend?** Any lord who is your friend, of any kingdom (not its ruler), tells you who is pressing whom: the factions, their aims, who stands with them, how far along they are, whether there is a darker plot against the throne, and what his own house holds against the crown. A good friend tells you more; an honourable one keeps the secrets of his own cause unless you are in it; a calculating one may remember more for a consideration. When a faction a friend is in comes to a head, he may write to you.
- **Join** a lord's charter or reckoning, or **leave** it.
- **Start a charter**, or **start a reckoning**: you choose the house, then the fief, which is for your house. (The houses of the AI may start one against your house too: you hear the rumour first, then the news when it comes out in the open.) Win other lords over one by one, in person or by an [envoy](envoys.md); how likely they are to agree is said in words.
- **Tell the ruler.** If you know of a charter still in whispers, you can take it to the ruler. He may strike at its head or keep watch, tells you which, and thanks you. Whoever told you about it will find out. A reckoning is not a plot against the crown, and is not told.
- **Ask the ruler to step down**, if you lead a plot strong enough to ask it.

When you lead a charter, you bring its laws before the council yourself, from the kingdom screen; its lords vote with you. A reckoning you lead goes to the assembly. Your factions, and those that put their case to you as ruler, have their own entry in the journal.

## Asking how things stand {#asking-how-things-stand}

Two questions sit under the game's *"There is something I'd like to discuss."*, beside the news of the realms. You can ask them of any lord of a kingdom who is not of your house, a sellsword or of a minor faction. A lord who dislikes you will not say (the same as for the news of the realms).

**"What is happening in the realm? I would know how things stand among its houses." (The realm's affairs).** The lord tells you about **his own** realm: yours, or another one if he serves another crown (then you ask *"How do things stand within {Kingdom}, among its houses?"*). He tells it page by page, the gravest first; *"Go on."* turns the page, *"That is enough for now. My thanks."* ends it. Everything he says is how things truly stand:

- **A civil war**: who raised the banner and what for (whom they would crown, a charter, or to break away with their lands), which houses rose with them, how long it has lasted, which side has the better of it and by how much, whether the two sides may now treat for peace, and where his own house stands. A lord of the rebels tells it from their side. After a civil war, he says the realm is still healing.
- **Houses marked as traitors** and waiting for the crown's judgment.
- **Feuds** between houses of the realm, over which fief, and who rides with each side.
- **Charters, reckonings and a demand to step down** that are pressed in the open (never those still in whispers), with their houses.
- **Talk of a plot**: only once there is talk of one, never with names, and every lord says the same, even one who is in it.
- **Vacant titles**: a duchy (or a theme or an ulus) that the crown has yet to grant, and whether his house could hope for it.
- **Beyond the borders**: the realm's wars, and a war or a peace the council is voting on.

If nothing troubles the realm, he says so, in his own manner.

**"Tell me of your house." (Their house).** The lord tells you about his house, page by page: who leads it, its standing, its grown lords and ladies, whom it serves and what it holds; its titles, its lord and its vassals and knights (in a kingdom of [titles](titles.md)); its [rights](fief-rights.md) to the fiefs of other houses and the rights of others to its own; a claim to the throne, and by what right; its allies, friends and foes, and the grudges it bears; and where it stands with the crown: how its head gets on with the ruler, the wrong it holds against the crown if any, its oath, its offices at court, the cause it leads or is in, a traitor's mark. A house never tells you of a plot or a cause it still keeps in whispers.

## Turn it off

In the [settings file](../settings.md), set to `false`:

- `FactionsEnabled`: all of it (the plots and civil wars of before stay, alone);
- `FactionGrievancesEnabled`: the wrongs houses remember; `FactionGrievanceRelationsEnabled`: wrongs between AI houses souring their heads; `FactionGrievanceRelationsWithPlayer`: the same against your house; `FactionCrownGrievanceRelationsEnabled`: wrongs against the crown costing goodwill toward the ruler; `FactionAbandonedGrievanceEnabled`: armies that came near a besieged fief and did nothing are blamed (off: any lord of the realm near the fief when it falls spares the crown, as before); `FactionMarshalBlameEnabled`: the Marshal sharing the crown's blame; `PleaToRelieveEnabled`: the pleas of the lords of your host;
- `FactionCharterEnabled`, `FactionReckoningEnabled`, `FactionAbdicationEnabled`, `FactionConfidantsEnabled`: each part on its own;
- `FiefRightsReckoningNeedsRight` (on the [Rights to fiefs](fief-rights.md) page): a reckoning needs a right;
- `ReckoningUnheardEnabled` (on the [assembly](assembly.md) page): a reckoning left unheard counts as refused;
- `FiefDefyEnabled`: houses of the AI defying a fief taken from them (off: they always yield; you still choose); `BlindEyeRecordEnabled`: a ruler who looks the other way at a defiance losing some of his reign's record; `RevokeDefectToPatronEnabled`: a house named traitor going over to a foreign king;
- `FactionFeudsEnabled`: reckonings taking up arms; `FactionFeudKingHeedsRightEnabled`: the crown respecting a firm right in a feud (off: it may forbid any feud); `FactionFeudPressRightEnabled`: a right pressed by the sword; `FactionFeudPressBondWeightEnabled`: the AI weighing its kin and friends before pressing a right, bringing a reckoning or forging a claim against them (off: it never does; you stay free); `FeudWarningEnabled`: rumours and an ultimatum before a feud by the sword (off: it is declared at once); `ReckoningJudgedEnabled`: a right judged by the crown when it leaves the fief with its house (off: no right is judged); `FactionFeudPlayerJoinEnabled`: your sword in a feud (letters, offers, hire); `FactionFeudGiveBackEnabled`: fiefs given back after a feud; `InternalWarWeaknessEnabled`: a realm weak in its wars being less ready for quarrels within (off: no realm counts as weak); `FeudWarRestraintEnabled`: feuds frowned on in a war; `FactionFeudBlindEyeEnabled`: the crown looking the other way after its call to arms (off: those who refuse are named traitors); `FeudSummonsEnabled`: the host summoned for a war abroad; `WarDuringFeudPenaltyEnabled`: feuds making a war abroad less likely; `WarWaitsForFeudsEnabled`: the restlessness of a long peace waiting while feuds run; `FelonyJusticeEnabled` (on the [Dukes, counts and castellans](titles.md) page): felony; `TitlesReliefEnabled`: asking one's own lord for aid; `FeudAllyCallEnabled`: allies called to a feud; `FeudCourtEnabled`: courting houses into a feud; `FeudAiDefiesBanEnabled`: an AI house defying the crown's ban; `FeudPartySizeEnabled`: bigger companies in a feud; `FeudStrengthCountsHiresEnabled`: what a purse could hire counted in a feud's strength;
- `HouseAlliancesEnabled`: alliances between houses; `AlliesInCausesEnabled`: allies in plots and causes; `MarriageOtherRealmEnabled`: fewer weddings across realms;
- `FactionsInAiRealms`: factions in the AI kingdoms;
- `ReckoningSmallRealmEnabled`: a small realm sparing its houses in a reckoning's hearing;
- `RealmAffairsTalkEnabled`: asking a lord what is happening in the realm; `HouseTalkEnabled`: asking a lord about his house.

With Mod Configuration Menu, the warning before a feud is **Rumours and an ultimatum before a feud** and the crown's judgment **Rights judged by the crown**, under **Mod Options > Kingdoms Alive**.
