# Mercenary contracts and the Unsworn

Selling your swords is a career, not a single oath. The ruler who pays you sends you work by messenger, calls your company to the realm's armies and signs you for a set number of days. Every crown remembers how you served it, and your company earns a name that raises its price, brings offers from other rulers and tempts the enemy to buy you. The **Unsworn**, a company of sellswords who answer to no crown, can also be hired in the taverns.

## The contract

A few hours after your clan enters a realm's service as mercenaries (by any of the game's ways), the ruler's messenger finds you on the road and asks how long you will ride for the crown:

- **"Put us down for 30 days."**
- **"60 days, then."**
- **"100 days, and the larger advance in my hand."**

Each contract pays an **advance** the moment you sign: **1,000 denars × your clan tier**, times 0.75 for 30 days, 1.5 for 60 days and 2.5 for 100 days. A tier 3 clan gets 2,250, 4,500 or 7,500 denars. Longer contracts pay the same per day; the advance grows with your company's name (see below). The contract's days served show as a bar in the journal entry **"The name of your company"**.

If the messenger cannot reach you for 5 days, the paymaster sends again later. No contract is ever forced on you.

### Renewing or riding on

**7 days before the end**, another messenger asks whether you sign again (**"Another 30 days."**, 60 or 100, with a new advance) or **"No. When my time is up, my company rides on."** On the last day the paymaster settles with you and your clan leaves the realm's service. If you never answer, the contract counts as served, and you stay in the crown's pay without one until the paymaster asks again. Meanwhile you may leave whenever you like, without blame.

If a task of the crown is still under way when the contract ends, the release waits for it, at most 10 days.

### Serving it out

A contract served to its last day:

- **+2 to +8 relation** with the ruler (+3 for every 40 days of contract);
- adds to your company's name;
- mends the crown's trust if you once broke your word to it.

The ruler sends you off with their thanks, and remembers it when you meet again.

### Breaking it

Leaving the service before the end, without a just cause, breaks the contract:

- **-8 relation** with the ruler, who remembers it;
- the crown will not trust your word until you serve a whole contract with it again, and its messenger greets you coldly;
- your company's name falls;
- the part of the advance you did not serve goes back to the crown.

**Just causes** let you leave early without blame, giving back only the unserved part of the advance:

- a new ruler has taken the throne since you signed;
- the realm has lost half or more of the towns and castles it held when you signed;
- the realm has been at peace with every kingdom for 7 days.

Any early end asks for the unserved advance. **If you cannot pay it all back, the crown counts the contract as broken**, whatever the reason.

Swearing fealty to the crown you serve ends the contract without blame (as served, once half of it is done). Swearing to another crown in the last 7 days also counts as served. Becoming a lord's [squire](squire.md) ends the contract without blame, and the unserved advance goes back.

### Leave in peacetime

When the realm makes peace with every kingdom, you are told at once. If the peace holds for **7 days**, the ruler sends word: **"Then we ride on, with the crown's thanks."** ends the contract as served (give back the unserved advance), or **"We will stay. Peace never lasts."** keeps you on. While the peace lasts, you may also leave on your own without blame. A peaceful release counts the days actually served and does not mend a lost trust.

## The name of your company

Every crown keeps a record of the tasks you did for it and the contracts you kept, for good, even if you serve it again years later. Your company also earns a name across Calradia, shown in the journal entry **"The name of your company"** as a bar toward the next rank:

| Points | Your company is | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | a company few have heard of | — |
| 10 | a company with a name | +10% pay; other rulers send for you |
| 25 | a company of renown | +20% pay; the enemy tries to buy you |
| 50 | a company famed across Calradia | +30% pay; the enemy tries more often |

| Gains | Losses |
|---|---|
| +1 for every task done | -1 for a task failed (refusing costs nothing here) |
| +2 for every 40 days of a contract served | -3 for a contract broken |
| +1 for reporting an enemy's purse to your ruler | -3 for being dismissed by the crown |
| | -3 for being found out standing aside at a siege |
| | -3 for changing sides |

Without failures, that is roughly 90 days of service to make a name, 225 to be renowned and 455 to be famed. The name never falls below nothing. A rank lost is told in the taverns' words, and the pay falls with it at the game's next renewal of your pay.

The extra pay applies to the game's mercenary wages, to the contract's advance and to the enemy's purse. The entry closes when you swear fealty to a realm or come to rule one.

## Offers from other rulers

Once your company has a name, other crowns want it. Only rulers at war with some kingdom send for you, never one at war with the crown you serve, never one you betrayed, and not if you have fallen too low with them. Honourable rulers will not send for a company that ever changed sides. A crown that knows your work, or a generous ruler, sends more readily.

- **Near the end of a contract** (12 days before it), a messenger may ask you to come when your time is up: **"Tell {ruler} I will come when my time is up, for 30 days."** (or 60, or 100), or **"Not this time."** If you give your word, the crown you serve sends its farewell. You may still change your mind: **"...We will stay for another 30 days."** costs -3 with the ruler you let down. Otherwise, when the contract ends, your company rides for that crown at once.
- **While you serve nobody**, a messenger comes every 6 to 15 days. Sign on the spot (**"30 days. Tell {ruler} my company is on its way."**) or answer **"Not now. My company has other plans."** After a refusal the next one waits 20 to 40 days. After two refusals in a row, none comes until you serve a crown again or your name grows. Not while you carry [the King's Writ](kings-writ.md).

A crown that sent for you pays **25% more** for that whole service.

## The enemy's purse

A company of renown is worth buying.

**On the road.** From the 10th day of a contract, a messenger of a ruler at war with your crown may find you: about 1 chance in 70 a day (1 in 40 once you are famed), once per contract, and never within 45 days of the last offer. An honourable ruler is less likely to be the one who sends it. The purse is **3,000 denars + 40 per man in your party**, raised by your name. Your answers:

- **"Give me the purse. Tell {ruler} my company is hers/his."** You change sides at once (see below).
- **"My company is not for sale. Go, while you still can."**
- **"Seize him. {your ruler} will want to hear of this."** Your ruler sends **1,000 denars**, +5 relation, honour, and your name grows. The ruler who sent the purse: -8.

**On the eve of an assault.** Riding in an army of your crown that besieges an enemy town or castle (not one you lead), if your company is at least a tenth of the army's strength, a hooded man may come to your fire in the night: **"A visitor in the night"**. About 1 chance in 3 per siege, less if the besieged ruler is honourable. The purse is 1.5 times larger. Your answers:

- **"Take the purse, and change sides tonight"**.
- **"Take half the purse, and be gone before the assault"**: you slip out of the army, at a small cost in honour. The other half comes if the walls hold. About 1 chance in 3 that the crown hears of it a day or so later: -15 with the ruler and the army's commander, the crown no longer trusts you, your name falls, and your contract is torn up as soon as you are free.
- **"Send him back the way he came"**.
- **"Have him bound and taken to {commander}"**: as reporting above, and +5 with the army's commander too.

**Changing sides** pays the purse and signs you to the buyer for **100 days**, with the purse as the advance. You give the silver advanced by your old crown back for the days you did not serve. The betrayed crown never trusts you again and never sends for you: its ruler falls to -30 with you or lower, its lords remember, its ruler's house holds a **blood feud** with yours (not on game version 1.4.8), you lose honour and your name falls. Other parties leave you alone for a day while you ride out.

## Gifts of the crown

Serve one crown well and long enough and, one day, it shows its thanks (more than once, if you keep serving it; tasks for the same crown count over all your services with it). Its ruler sends a rider, and a journal entry opens, **"Summoned by {ruler}"**, with the ruler marked on your map. Go and say **"Your rider found me, my lord/my lady. I came as soon as my road allowed."** The ruler gives you the full harness of a noble warrior of their realm, finer each time: arms, armour, and a horse if that warrior rides.

Never from a crown you betrayed, nor while you are at war with it. A new ruler on the throne keeps the crown's word.

## How tasks arrive

- Only while your clan serves a kingdom **as mercenaries**.
- A messenger of the ruler finds you on the map. The first comes about **5 days** after you enter the service. After that, one every **15 to 25 days**, counted from the last messenger, and never sooner than **5 days** after your last task ended.
- **One task at a time.**
- The messenger waits until you are out on the map and free: not while you are a prisoner, at sea, in a town, castle or village, in a battle, a siege or a menu. If they cannot reach you for **5 days**, they give up, with no penalty. The days you spend riding in an army do not count towards those 5.
- If there is nothing to ask of you nearby, the ruler looks again **3 days** later.
- If the ruler is a prisoner, their house speaks for them: see [The ruler's rescue](#the-rulers-rescue).

The messenger describes the task, its deadline and reward. Answer **"Tell her/him my company is on its way. It will be done."** (it goes into your quest journal, with the places marked on your map) or **"Tell her/him my men are in no state for this. I cannot take it on."** (a refusal). Close the talk without answering and they come back 12 hours later.

## "In the service of the crown"

Your first contract or task in each service opens a journal entry: **"In the service of the crown of {KINGDOM}"**. It is the record of that service: every task given, done, failed or refused, contracts signed and renewed, offers taken or sent away. It no longer leads to vassalage. If you want land, swear fealty the game's own way.

## The tasks

| Task | Deadline | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Raid an enemy territory | 60 days | 5,000 denars, 5 renown, 20 influence, honour, +10 with the ruler (or the lord of their house who gave it) |
| Escort a caravan | 30 days | 2,000 denars, 3 renown, +5 with the ruler |
| Guard a border village | 30 days | 3,000 denars, 3 renown, +5 with the ruler |
| Bring the crown a captive lord | 30 days | 2,000 denars + the captive's ransom (up to 10,000 more), 5 renown, +5 with the ruler |
| Rescue a lord | 50 days | 8,000 denars (12,000 for one of the ruler's house), 5 renown, +3 with the ruler, +5 with the lord |

### Raid an enemy territory
The game's own **"Raid an Enemy Territory"** quest, given by messenger: burn **one village** of the enemy kingdom whose villages lie nearest to you (the game's own quest asks three; here one is enough, for a third of its gold). The reward is paid the moment it burns. A village burnt in a fight as time runs out still counts. It is given in the name of the ruler or, if the ruler already has a quest of the game open, of a lord of their house; it is not offered if nobody of that house is free.

### Escort a caravan
**"Contract: escort {caravan} to {town}"**. One of the realm's caravans waits in a town near you, its wagons loaded for another town one to three towns' ride away. Both towns are marked on your map.

- Reach the caravan's town within **4 days**, or its master hires other swords and the task fails.
- Then it sets out with you, never faster than you can follow. **Be close by when it enters the town.**
- If you fall out of sight, it halts on the road until you come back (you get the game's warning, *"You are about to lose sight of the caravan."*). If nobody comes back for it within **2 days**, the task fails.
- If it reaches the town ahead of you, its master waits at the gates for **12 hours**. Arrive in time and it counts. If not, the task lapses with no penalty.
- If the town closes to it (war, a siege), or every road ahead is closed, the escort lapses with no penalty.
- If an enemy destroys it, the task fails.

### Guard a border village
**"Contract: guard {village}"**. A village of the realm near you and close to an enemy town or castle. Keep it **in sight for 3 days in all** within 30 days, like the game's quest to scout enemy garrisons: every hour it is within your party's sight counts. A message marks each day (for example *"Another day of watch over {VILLAGE} is done (1/3)."*). If the village is looted first, it fails, unless it was looted before you could have got there: then it lapses with no penalty. Beating an enemy lord's party at or near the village fulfils it at once.

### Bring the crown a captive lord
**"Contract: bring the crown a captive lord"**. Within 30 days, take **any lord** of a kingdom at war with the crown (only lords your own party takes after the task is given count; lords of minor clans and mercenary bands do not). Then hand them over:

- in the dungeon of any **town or castle of the ruler's house**, with the keep's **"Donate prisoners"**. Those places are marked on your map, and the marks follow if one changes hands;
- or to **the ruler in person**: **"I bring the crown a captive of the enemy, as you asked: {lord}."**

The reward is 2,000 denars plus what the game's ransom for that lord is worth, up to 10,000 more: a great lord pays better than a young knight. If you lose the captive, take another. If the war ends with no captive in your hands, the task lapses with no penalty.

### Rescue a lord
A lord of the realm held in the **dungeon of an enemy town** within a few days' ride (never a castle, which a sellsword cannot slip into, and never one carried by a party). One of the ruler's own house is picked first, then the nearest. Where they are held is marked on the map. Free them:

- with the game's own **prison break** in that town: the full reward;
- by **taking the town** in an assault you lead: the full reward;
- by being **among the besiegers** when the realm's host takes it, led by someone else (in the camp, in the army or in the assault): **40% of the reward**;
- if the enemy moves them meanwhile, by **beating the party that carries them** (the full reward if you lead the fight, 40% if someone else does). You are told where they were taken, and the new place or party is marked.

While the task is under way, the lord almost never escapes on their own (about 1 in 8 over the 50 days, instead of nearly always in the base game). If they go free some other way (ransom, peace, someone else) or die, the task lapses with no penalty.

### The ruler's rescue
If the ruler themselves lies in the dungeon of an enemy town within a long ride of you, someone of their house sends the messenger in their place: their spouse, or failing that their eldest child, or the eldest lord of the house. Bringing the ruler home is then the only task offered. It works as a rescue of a lord of the ruler's house (12,000 denars), and the ruler pays and thanks you once free. If the ruler is held somewhere you cannot reach, or carried by a party, the house waits and looks again every few days.

!!! note "The Unsworn tasks moved to the King's Writ"
    Clearing an Unsworn hideout and sweeping three Unsworn bands are no longer mercenary tasks: they are now contracts of [the King's Writ](kings-writ.md#the-kings-contracts), for a free man-hunter. With the writ's contracts turned off (`WritContractsEnabled` or `WritEnabled` set to `false`), they are mercenary tasks again:

    - **Clear an Unsworn hideout**: 4,000 denars, 3 renown, +5 with the ruler, within 30 days. A hideout near the realm's villages, marked on your map as **"Unsworn Hideout"**, with **35 to 40** Unsworn on foot inside. The last fight is against the **Unsworn Captain**.
    - **Sweep of the Unsworn bands**: 2,000 denars, 3 renown, +5 with the ruler, within 50 days. **Three bands** of about **20 to 40** Unsworn roam around villages of the realm, each with an arrow on the edge of the map. They will not talk or surrender, and come after you if they are clearly stronger.

## How the task is chosen

Each task has its weight in the draw, and **the kind you were given last is much less likely to come again** next time.

- **In war**, a lord to rescue comes first: when there is one, the rescue weighs 4 times as much as a raid (8 times for one of the ruler's house). The caravan escort and the border village weigh a little more than a raid each; a captive lord weighs as much as a raid.
- **In peace**, there are no raids, border villages, captive lords or rescues: you get the caravan escort.

## Summons to the host

When a lord of the realm you serve raises an army within a long ride of you, the ruler may call your company to it: about **2 times in 5**. It comes by its own messenger, even while you have a task of the crown in hand, but not when you already ride in an army or your contract has less than 12 days left.

- **"Tell {ruler} my company rides to {lord}'s banner."** opens **"Summons: ride in {lord}'s host"**. Join the army within **7 days** (the game's own *"I want to join your army"*) and stay until it is dismissed, or for at most 40 days.
- **"My company is bound elsewhere this time."** costs nothing. A summons is not a contract.

**Pay**, counted by the paymaster and paid when the host is dismissed:

- for every day in the army, your company's daily wages **and half as much again**;
- a purse for every man fit to fight, counted when you join, by tier: **8, 15, 30, 50, 80 or 110 denars** for tiers 1 to 6 (companions as tier 5; men of a hired company not counted). 100 tier 3 men: 3,000 denars.

Done, it counts as a task for the crown, with 4 renown, +5 with the ruler and +3 with the army's leader.

- **Ride off before the host is dismissed** and you lose it all: the task fails.
- **Never join** within the 7 days: the task fails.
- **Taken prisoner** from the army: you are paid everything owed, purse included, with no blame.
- **Left on the shore** when the army sails and you have no ships: no blame, but nothing is owed.
- The army breaks up or **lays siege before you reach it**, or you join another army: the summons lapses with no blame.

**While you ride with the host** (not on the way there), the crown's task in hand and the game's own quests with a time limit wait: their clocks stop. If the crown's task goes wrong meanwhile, nobody blames you. Once the host is dismissed, you have at least a day to get back to it. The mod's other journal entries (feasts, duels and the like) keep their own clocks.

## Failing and refusing

A refusal or a failed task (time ran out, the caravan lost, the village looted, the army left):

- your **mercenary pay is cut by half for 12 days**;
- **-2 relation** with the ruler, but never more than **-5** in all with the same ruler;
- a failed task (not a refusal) takes 1 point from your company's name;
- **three in a row** and the ruler ends your contract: *"A rider brings a sealed letter: the crown of {KINGDOM} no longer wants your swords. Your contract is ended, and you are free to sell them elsewhere."* That counts as a dismissal: your name falls by 3, and the unserved advance goes back. You are warned after the second. Any task done resets the count.

A task **lapses**, with no penalty, through no fault of yours: you were a prisoner when time ran out, the ruler is no longer on the throne, the caravan has nowhere left to go, the village changes hands, or it went wrong while you rode in the realm's host.

## Mercenary pay without influence

In the base game a mercenary is paid in denars for the influence they earn. With `MercInfluencePayEnabled` set to `false`, your clan earns no silver for influence while it serves as mercenaries: your pay is the contract's advance, the tasks and the hosts. The ruler says so in their own words when you ask for mercenary work, and so does the game's offer of service on the map. There are no wages to cut, so failures cost no pay, and a better price from a crown that sent for you goes into the advance. AI mercenaries are not affected.

## The Unsworn troop tree

Sellswords of no culture. Their names in the party screen:

| Tier | Troop | Upgrades to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unsworn Recruit | Footman, or Outrider (needs a horse) |
| 2 | Unsworn Footman | Wilder or Trained Bowman |
| 2 | Unsworn Outrider | Raider or Horse Archer |
| 3 | Unsworn Wilder | Warmonger or Hellion |
| 3 | Unsworn Trained Bowman | Longbowman |
| 3 | Unsworn Raider | Battle Rider (needs a war horse) |
| 3 | Unsworn Horse Archer | Mounted Master Archer (needs a war horse) |
| 5 | Unsworn Warmonger, Hellion, Longbowman, Battle Rider, Mounted Master Archer | — |

Where to get them:

- **Taverns.** When a town's tavern changes the mercenaries it offers (every two days, as in the base game), about **1 time in 5** they are Unsworn instead, mostly of the lower tiers. You hire them from the tavern's mercenary like any other.
- AI lords and caravans hire them from the same taverns, so you will also meet Unsworn in other parties.
- **Prisoners.** Like any regular troop of **tier 2 or higher**: every Unsworn except the Recruit.

!!! tip
    Need hundreds of men at once, for a few weeks? Hire a whole company instead: see [Companies for hire](free-companies.md).

## Turn it off
In the settings file, set the switch to `false`. See [Settings](../settings.md).

- `MercContractsEnabled`: no tasks by messenger, no summons to the host, no "In the service of the crown" entry, and none of the company's career. A task under way still ends as usual.
- `MercContractRaid`: no raid tasks.
- `MercContractProtect`: no caravan escorts or border villages to guard.
- `MercContractCapture`: no captive lords to bring in.
- `MercContractRescue`: no rescues of lords.
- `MercContractRescueRuler`: no rescue of a captured ruler; no task comes while the ruler is a prisoner.
- `MercContractArmy`: no summons to the realm's armies.
- `MercContractUnswornHideout`, `MercContractUnswornSweep`: only matter with the King's Writ contracts turned off (see above).
- `MercInfluencePayEnabled`: no silver for influence while you serve as mercenaries (see above). On by default.
- `MercCareerEnabled`: none of the career: no record of service, name, contracts with a term, offers, purses or gifts. The tasks go on.
- `MercFamePayEnabled`: your company's name no longer raises its pay.
- `MercTermsEnabled`: no contracts with a term or advance; you serve and leave as in the base game. Leave in peacetime, offers from other rulers and the enemy's purse on the road need it.
- `MercPeaceReleaseEnabled`: no word from the ruler when peace comes.
- `MercBidsEnabled`: no offers from other rulers.
- `MercGearRewardsEnabled`: no gifts of the crown.
- `MercTurncoatEnabled`: no enemy messenger with a purse on the road.
- `MercSiegeBribeEnabled`: no visitor in the night at a siege.
- `MercTurncoatBloodFeud`: changing sides no longer brings a blood feud.
- `UnswornTroopsEnabled`: taverns no longer offer the Unsworn, and the two Unsworn tasks are not given.
- `FixMercHideoutMenEnabled`: the hideouts of the Unsworn and of [your rival](rival.md) hold only as many men as the game's own hideouts.
