# The faiths of Calradia

Every people of Calradia now keeps a faith of its own. Each faith grows out of what the game already tells about it, in the encyclopedia and in the stories of its companions, and out of the real people that culture is drawn from in the years 600 to 1100. Priests live in every town, each faith holds a great festival once a year (every second year in fast mode), and the Aserai keep their sacred months. You can take a blessing home from a festival, or even change your faith.

A town or castle keeps the faith of its people, not of its lord: one taken by another realm comes round to that realm's faith only slowly (see [The faith and ways of a fief](faith-and-ways.md)).

None of it is shown as a number. How the faithful see you is told in words: *unknown to the priests*, *known at the shrines*, *one of the faithful*, or *a pillar* of your faith.

## The seven faiths

| Faith | Kept by | Drawn from | Holy place |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Imperial Church** | the three empires | the Byzantine Church | Diathma, "the city of the Divine Decree" |
| **The Church of Paravenos** | Vlandia | the Latin Church of the Normans | Pravend, the old capital of the West |
| **The Forest Gods** | Sturgia | the old Slavic gods | the sacred oaks of Omor |
| **The Gods of the North** | the Nords (War Sails) | the Norse gods | the Thing-stones of Thronderlag |
| **The Old Ways** | Battania | the old Celtic faith | Dunglanys, above the dark Llyn Tywal |
| **The Faith of the Sacred Months** | the Aserai | the Arabia of the sacred months | the well of Asera at Hubyar |
| **The Sky Father** | the Khuzaits | the faith of the steppe | Mount Iltan, above Tyal |

- The two churches pray to the same Heaven and quarrel over how. The Forest Gods and the Gods of the North are cousins.
- A lord keeps the faith of their house; a house keeps the faith of its people. Whoever marries into a house takes up its faith. A kingdom keeps the faith of its ruling house.
- Towns, castles and villages keep the faith of their people. The encyclopedia shows the faith of every lord, house, kingdom and settlement.

## Priests

Every town has a priest of its people's faith: a Father or Mother of the churches, a Volkhv of the forest, a Godi or Gydja, a Druid, a Kahin or Kahina, a Kam of the steppe. They stand in the square with the town's notables, and **Visit the church** (or the sacred grove, the hof, the shrine...) in the town menu takes you straight to them.

- Most priests are men. The churches keep their altars almost wholly for men; the older faiths have their seeresses, druidesses and shamans, though fewer than their men.
- They greet you by your faith and their own character: warmly, sternly, shrewdly or coldly.
- **Tell me of your faith**: the story of their gods, in their own voice.
- **What does your faith ask**: the two virtues their faith holds dear, and the deeds it weighs.
- **Is there some service I could do**: the priest's own tasks (see below).
- **An offering**: give to the faith, once a season (and once more at each festival).
- **Taking up their faith**: if you do not (see below).
- **Their recruits are for the most devout.** A priest gives men to no lord and to no garrison, and to you only once you are *a pillar* of their faith, whatever your friendship with the priest. Until then they are not among those you can recruit from.

### Warriors of the faith

A priest's recruits are not village lads: they are the sworn warriors of the faith, a line of their own for each faith, as the nobles have theirs. A priest gives only the first of them, already a veteran; the next two ranks are earned in battle.

| Faith | Veteran | Seasoned | Elite |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Imperial Church | Cloister Guard | Warden of the Decree | Champion of the Decree |
| The Church of Paravenos | Sergeant of Paravenos | Brother of Paravenos | Knight of Paravenos |
| The Forest Gods | Oak-Sworn | Oak Warden | Keeper of the Grove |
| The Gods of the North | Stone-Sworn | Bear-Shirt | God-Touched |
| The Old Ways | Woad-Painted | Lake-Sworn | Champion of the Deep |
| The Faith of the Sacred Months | Well Guard | Rider of the Truce | Sword of Asera |
| The Sky Father | Kam's Rider | Sky-Sworn | Rider of Mount Iltan |

They are few and slow to come: a priest has only a handful waiting at most, and a long while passes before another answers the call. They fight for their gods more than for silver, and ask far less pay than other soldiers of their rank.

The Gods of the North are kept in the Nord towns of **War Sails**. With War Sails, their warriors are Nords and seafarers, in the berserkers' helms and cloaks and the Ulfhednar's wolf; without it, they wear what the old Nords left in Calradia.

## Priests' missions

A priest asks the faithful for help, as the townsfolk do: ask what service you can do for the church (or the grove, the hof, the shrine...). The task goes into your quest log, with the place marked on the map.

| Mission | What the priest asks |
|---|---|
| **Pilgrims to the holy place** | See a band of pilgrims safely to the holy town of the faith. Raiders know the road too. |
| **Alms of grain** | Bring grain to a village of the faith that is going hungry. |
| **The stolen relic** | Bandits carried off a relic to their hideout near a village of the faith. Clear the hideout and bring it home. |
| **The heretic** | A preacher of false words goes from village to village gathering followers. Talk him round, break his band, or let him go, and the priest will not forget it. |
| **The shrine** | Raiders burned a village of the faith. Bring tools and timber to rebuild its shrine. |

- What the faithful think of you rises with every task done, and the priest thinks better of you. A task left undone or abandoned costs you both.
- The priest offers silver for your trouble. You may leave it to the temple instead, and the faithful will think the more of you.
- You may send a companion with some of your men, as with the townsfolk's tasks. The priest is grateful and pays in full, but the faithful would rather have seen you there yourself. If the place is held by your enemies, the priest will not let you go in person; then whoever you send serves just as well.
- A priest of another faith may ask a stranger who is thinking of joining it for help, one task at a time. It wins only the priest's friendship.

## Festivals

Each faith holds one great festival a year, always at the same time of year, in its holy town. In a fast mode campaign, where a year lasts only 24 days, each faith holds it **every second year**:

| Festival | Faith | When |
|---|---|---|
| The Spring Thing | the Gods of the North | the first day of spring |
| The Feast of Renewal | the Church of Paravenos | the middle of spring |
| The Great Gathering | the Sky Father | the first day of summer, wherever the khan is |
| The Midsummer Fires | the Forest Gods | the middle of summer |
| The Opening of the Sacred Months | the Faith of the Sacred Months | the first day of autumn |
| The Night of the Open Door | the Old Ways | the first day of winter |
| The Feast of the Divine Light | the Imperial Church | the middle of winter |

- The lords of that faith ride to it from every realm that is not at war with the town's owner, unless their own wars keep them too far away. The lords of different realms who pray together leave as friends more often, and their realms think a little better of each other: the three empires meet at Diathma.
- If the holy town is in the hands of another faith, the festival is held **in exile** in a town of the faithful, and every prayer ends with the same plea.
- **If it is your faith**, word comes a few days before and a quest log entry marks the town. There, **Join the festival**: keep the rites day by day, make an offering, speak with the priest. Each day you keep raises your standing and wins the lords of your faith who are there, the more so the better the faithful think of you. When it ends you take home its **blessing**. The quest log entry tells you beforehand which blessing the festival gives. When it ends, it tells you where you now stand among the faithful, which lords you prayed beside, and what the blessing does and how long it stays with you (half a year).
- **If it is not**, you may **Watch the festival** from the edge of the crowd.

## The sacred months

Every autumn the Aserai keep the sacred months, as the clans of Asera's sons have always done.

- No Aserai realm goes to war: its council cannot vote a war, it will not answer an ally's call to war, and its lords raid no villages. An Aserai overlord comes to the defence of a vassal once the season turns, and the vassal holds no grudge for it.
- **Whoever strikes the Aserai in those months is cursed.** Declaring war on an Aserai realm, raiding one of its villages or laying siege to one of its towns or castles turns every Aserai house against the ruler of the realm that did it, and more against the lord who led it. The Aserai realms keep it as a grievance, even after the war, and a grievance is a reason for war.
- If you keep the faith of Asera, raiding in the sacred months costs you your standing.

## Blessings

Each festival gives the blessing of its faith, for half a year:

- **of valour** (the Church of Paravenos and the Gods of the North): your men's morale is higher;
- **of mending** (the Imperial Church and the Old Ways): your company heals faster;
- **of the road** (the Forest Gods, the Faith of the Sacred Months and the Sky Father): your company marches faster.

You carry one blessing at a time.

## Your standing with your faith

How the faithful see you follows how you live and what you do, more than where you go.

**Virtues.** Each faith holds two virtues of character dear, the game's own traits, and their opposites are its sins. With every season the priests weigh yours: the more of its virtues you have, the higher you stand; the more of its sins, the lower. Your traits change with what you do in the game, as always: sparing or executing prisoners, keeping or breaking your word, standing or fleeing, giving or keeping.

| Faith | Its virtues |
|---|---|
| The Imperial Church | mercy, generosity |
| The Church of Paravenos | honour, valour |
| The Forest Gods | generosity, valour |
| The Gods of the North | valour, honour |
| The Old Ways | honour, generosity |
| The Faith of the Sacred Months | generosity, mercy |
| The Sky Father | honour, calculation |

**Deeds.** Your standing rises when you clear a bandits' hideout near a village of your faith, when you beat off a raid on a village of your faith, and above all when your army takes your faith's holy town back from another faith. Clearing bandits and defending villages only counts so much in one season. It falls when you raid a village of your own faith, or when you execute a lord who keeps it.

**And the rest.** Festivals and offerings raise it too. Among the most devout, it fades a little with every season, so it has to be kept up.

### What it is worth

- **The lords of your faith.** One of the faithful is better liked by the lords who keep the same faith, and a pillar more still; the most devout of them, those whose own character holds to the faith's virtues, warm to you most of all. It weighs wherever their goodwill does: whether a house will join your realm or stay in it, the fiefs they would give you, the matches they will arrange with your family, and how they hear you out. If you are a lord of some name and the priests do not know you, the most devout of the lords you have met think less of you.
- **Your towns and castles of your faith** hold more loyally to a lord they see at their altars.
- **The priests' recruits** follow only a pillar of the faith.
- At a festival, the lords of your faith warm to you faster, and the priests greet you as you stand.

It is not kept: if your standing falls, so does their esteem.

## Changing your faith

Ask a priest of another faith to take you in. They will tell you plainly what it costs, and you can still step back.

- Your whole house changes with you. Your companions keep their own.
- The lords of your old faith take it ill: most of all those of your own realm, and a little those of other realms who know you.
- The faithful of your new faith watch you for a year: what you do for it counts for less until they trust you. No priest will take you again for a year.
- If you rule a kingdom, your kingdom now keeps your faith.

## Faith among the lords

- Houses of the same faith quarrel a little less, and houses of different faiths a little more (the two churches, and the two northern faiths, neither more nor less).
- Lords marry across faiths less often.
