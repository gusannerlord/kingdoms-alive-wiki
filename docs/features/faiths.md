# The faiths of Calradia

Every people of Calradia now keeps a faith of its own. Each faith grows out of what the game already tells about it, in the encyclopedia and in the stories of its companions, and out of the real people that culture is drawn from in the years 600 to 1100. Priests live in every town, each faith holds a great festival once a year, and the Aserai keep their sacred months. You can go on pilgrimage, take a blessing home, or even change your faith.

None of it is shown as a number. How the faithful see you is told in words: *unknown to the priests*, *known at the shrines*, *one of the faithful*, or *a pillar* of your faith.

## The seven faiths

| Faith | Kept by | Drawn from | Holy place |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Imperial Church** | the three empires | the Byzantine Church | Diathma, "the city of the Divine Decree" |
| **The Church of Paravenos** | Vlandia | the Latin Church of the Normans | Pravend, the old capital of the West |
| **The Forest Gods** | Sturgia | the old Slavic gods | the sacred oaks of Omor |
| **The Gods of the North** | the Nords (War Sails) | the Norse gods | the Thing-stones of Thronderlag |
| **The Old Ways** | Battania | the old Celtic faith | Dunglanys, above the dark Llyn Tywal |
| **The Faith of the Sacred Months** | the Aserai | the Arabia of the sacred months | the well of Asera at Hubyar |
| **The Sky Father** | the Khuzaits | the faith of the steppe | Mount Iltan, above Tyal |

- The two churches pray to the same Heaven and quarrel over how. The Forest Gods and the Gods of the North are cousins.
- A lord keeps the faith of their house; a house keeps the faith of its people. Whoever marries into a house takes up its faith. A kingdom keeps the faith of its ruling house.
- Towns, castles and villages keep the faith of their people. The encyclopedia shows the faith of every lord, house, kingdom and settlement.

## Priests

Every town has a priest of its people's faith: a Father or Mother of the churches, a Volkhv of the forest, a Godi or Gydja, a Druid, a Kahin or Kahina, a Kam of the steppe. They stand in the square with the town's notables, and **Visit the church** (or the sacred grove, the hof, the shrine...) in the town menu takes you straight to them.

- They greet you by your faith and their own character: warmly, sternly, shrewdly or coldly.
- **Tell me of your faith**: the story of their gods, in their own voice.
- **An offering**: give to the faith, once a season (and once more at each festival).
- **A pilgrimage**: if you share their faith (see below).
- **Taking up their faith**: if you do not (see below).
- **Their recruits are the faithful.** A priest gives men only to those of their faith, and to you as many as your standing with the faith earns, not your friendship with the priest.

## Festivals

Each faith holds one great festival a year, always at the same time of year, in its holy town:

| Festival | Faith | When |
|---|---|---|
| The Spring Thing | the Gods of the North | the first day of spring |
| The Feast of Renewal | the Church of Paravenos | the middle of spring |
| The Great Gathering | the Sky Father | the first day of summer, wherever the khan is |
| The Midsummer Fires | the Forest Gods | the middle of summer |
| The Opening of the Sacred Months | the Faith of the Sacred Months | the first day of autumn |
| The Night of the Open Door | the Old Ways | the first day of winter |
| The Feast of the Divine Light | the Imperial Church | the middle of winter |

- The lords of that faith ride to it from every realm that is not at war with the town's owner, unless their own wars keep them too far away. The lords of different realms who pray together leave as friends more often, and their realms think a little better of each other: the three empires meet at Diathma.
- If the holy town is in the hands of another faith, the festival is held **in exile** in a town of the faithful, and every prayer ends with the same plea.
- **If it is your faith**, word comes a few days before and a quest log entry marks the town. There, **Join the festival**: keep the rites day by day, make an offering, speak with the priest. Each day you keep raises your standing and wins the lords of your faith who are there, the more so the better the faithful think of you. When it ends you take home its **blessing**.
- **If it is not**, you may **Watch the festival** from the edge of the crowd.

## The sacred months

Every autumn the Aserai keep the sacred months, as the clans of Asera's sons have always done.

- No Aserai realm goes to war: its council cannot vote a war, it will not answer an ally's call to war, and its lords raid no villages. An Aserai overlord comes to the defence of a vassal once the season turns, and the vassal holds no grudge for it.
- **Whoever strikes the Aserai in those months is cursed.** Declaring war on an Aserai realm, raiding one of its villages or laying siege to one of its towns or castles turns every Aserai house against the ruler of the realm that did it, and more against the lord who led it. The Aserai realms keep it as a grievance, even after the war, and a grievance is a reason for war.
- If you keep the faith of Asera, raiding in the sacred months costs you your standing.

## Pilgrimage

Ask a priest of your faith to send you on pilgrimage, once a year and never from the holy place itself. A quest log entry marks the holy place and how long your vow allows. There, **Go up to** the shrine and choose your prayer:

- **for victory**: your men's morale is higher;
- **for the wounded**: your company heals faster;
- **for the road**: your company marches faster.

The blessing lasts half a year, the faithful think better of you, and so does the priest of the holy place.

## Your standing with your faith

- It rises with every festival kept, every offering and every pilgrimage. Among the most devout, it fades a little with every season, so it has to be kept up. Raiding a village of your own faith costs you dearly.
- It decides how many of the priests' recruits will follow you, how much the lords of your faith warm to you at a festival, and how the priests greet you.

## Changing your faith

Ask a priest of another faith to take you in. They will tell you plainly what it costs, and you can still step back.

- Your whole house changes with you. Your companions keep their own.
- The lords of your old faith take it ill: most of all those of your own realm, and a little those of other realms who know you.
- The faithful of your new faith watch you for a year: what you do for it counts for less until they trust you. No priest will take you again for a year.
- If you rule a kingdom, your kingdom now keeps your faith.

## Faith among the lords

- Houses of the same faith quarrel a little less, and houses of different faiths a little more (the two churches, and the two northern faiths, neither more nor less).
- Lords marry across faiths less often.

## Settings
