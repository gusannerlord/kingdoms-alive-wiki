# Dukes, counts and castellans (very soon in 2.0.0)

In **Vlandia, Sturgia, Battania, the Aserai and Nordvyg**, the land is held in a chain of titles: a castellan holds his castle of a count, a count his town of a duke, and a duke answers to the crown. The empires and the Khuzaits keep to their own ways: in a new campaign, the empires have their [themes](#themes-of-the-empires), commands the emperor gives and takes back, and the Khuzaits their [ulus](#the-ulus-of-the-khuzaits), lands the khan gives to a house to pass down. Both are built on the same pairs of towns as the duchies, and work like a duchy except where said below.

## Counties and duchies

- **County:** each town, with the castles nearest to it on the map. The counties never change, whoever holds the land.
- **Count:** whoever holds the town. **Castellan:** whoever holds a castle.
- **Duchy:** two neighbouring counties, named after its chief town. When a campaign begins (or the first time you load a game with these titles), every town of each of these kingdoms is placed in a duchy, by the map:
    - The towns are paired two by two, so that the two towns of each duchy lie as close together as the whole kingdom allows.
    - **A town that stands alone**, with no other town anywhere near it (an isle, like Hvalvik), is a duchy of its own. With an odd number of towns, the one left over is a duchy of one county.
    - **The duke** is the house that holds the duchy's most prosperous town, the ruling house like any other: the ruler is duke only where he holds that town, never just for being ruler.
    - Campaigns whose duchies were set by an earlier version keep them.
- One house can be castellan, count and duke at once.

Each culture names them its own way:

| Kingdom | Duke | Count | Castellan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vlandia | Duke / Duchess | Count / Countess | Castellan / Chatelaine |
| Sturgia | Knyaz / Knyaginya | Posadnik / Posadnitsa | Voivode |
| Battania | Rí / Rígan | Mormaer / Banmormaer | Toísech / Bantoísech |
| Aserai | Malik / Malika | Emir / Emira | Qa'id / Qa'ida |
| Nordvyg | Jarl / Jarlinna | Lendmann | Hersir |

A lord of these kingdoms with no town or castle has a title of no rank: Baron or Baroness in Vlandia, Boyar or Boyarynya in Sturgia, Flaith or Banflaith in Battania, Sheikh or Sheikha among the Aserai, Hauld in Nordvyg.

A title counts only under a kingdom that knows it: a duchy whose house goes over to an empire lies dormant.

## Lords and vassals

Every house of the realm has **one lord**, as in the great feudal kingdoms of old, and never two:

- **The ruling house** has none: it is the crown.
- **A duke** holds of the crown (in the empires a strategos, among the Khuzaits a noyan).
- **Any other house** holds of the lord of its **chief seat**. The first time it is chosen, the seat is the house's most prosperous town, or, with no town, its most prosperous castle; from then on it **stays its seat for as long as the house holds it**, as a capital does in Crusader Kings, so a house never changes lords because another of its towns grew richer. A castle seat gives way to a town the day the house comes to hold one. If the house loses its seat, the town or castle it has held the longest becomes its seat (a town before a castle).
    - A castle is held of the count of its county, if he is of the same kingdom and another house; otherwise of the duke of the county's duchy. In the empires there are no counts: of the strategos.
    - A castle of a county whose town the ruler holds is held of the ruler, as its count, whoever holds the duchy.
    - A town is held of the duke of its duchy.
    - Where the duke is the ruler's own house, or no duke holds the duchy, the house holds of the crown.
- **All a house holds** is held of that one lord, wherever it lies.
- A house that holds no town or castle is shown as sworn to the crown, but owes no dues and is owed nothing for land it does not hold.

Each house pays its lord **dues**: a small share (2.5%) of what all its towns and castles yield a day. What its own vassals pay it is not counted. The ruling house pays none, and the crown takes dues from every house that holds of it directly. You see them in your daily gold, as "Dues to your lord", "Dues to the crown" or "Dues from your vassals".

**Land in another's province.** A duke bears it against any house of his kingdom that holds land in his duchy without holding of him, or of one of his own vassals (*"they hold Pravend in the heart of our own duchy, and answer to another for it"*). It is reckoned **by county**: a county whose town such a house holds is one wrong, its castles with it; a castle such a house holds counts on its own, a lesser wrong, only in a county whose town is the duke's own, another kingdom's or a sellsword's, or waits for the council's share-out (in the county of a vassal of his, its castles are that count's to resent: see below). It is a grudge against that house, the kind that moves a [reckoning](factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house); it costs no goodwill, stands for as long as it lasts, and is gone the day it ends. The same for a noyan in his ulus; not for a strategos, who commands the emperor's land rather than holding it. Never against the ruler's own house, nor a house freed of the duke, and never for a county whose town the ruler holds (that is the duke's [grievance against the crown](#loyalty-and-protection)).

A **count** bears the same against a house of his kingdom that holds a castle of his county and does not hold of him: never against his own lord or anyone above him, never in a theme or in the ruler's county. Where the count and the duke are the same house, it counts once.

## Rights of a duchy and of a county

Where a duke bears that grudge, he also holds a **weak [right](fief-rights.md)** *"of our duchy"* to the land: to the town of a county held by another house (its castles are not claimed apart), or to a castle that counts on its own. A noyan the same in his ulus. A count holds a weak right *"of our county"* to each castle of his county held by a house that does not answer to him. As in Crusader Kings, the land of a title is the title-holder's by right:

- **They last exactly as long as the case**: born the day such a house comes to hold the land, gone the day it passes to the duke or count, or to a house that answers to him. Where the house already has another right to the place (an old claim, a forged one), that one counts instead.
- **Pressed like any right**: in a reckoning, put before the council, or [by the sword](factions.md#three-ways-to-press-a-right), by you and by the AI alike. On its own, such a right adds nothing to what moves a house to begin a reckoning: the grudge above is that already.
- **A county takes its castles with it.** When a duke wins the town of a county by the right of his duchy, in a feud or by the council's vote, the castles of that county held by the house that lost the town pass to him with it; castles of other houses stay with them.
- You hear of it when such a right is born for your house, or over your land. Both kinds show among the rights in the Factions tab and the encyclopedia, with where they come from.

## Loyalty and protection

A title gives the dues, the loyalty of the vassals, who are owed protection in return, and a weight in the [wars between kingdoms](#titles-in-the-wars-between-kingdoms).

- **Plots, charters and reckonings:** a vassal leans hard to follow its lord (among the Khuzaits, not into a cause against the khan: see [the ulus](#the-ulus-of-the-khuzaits)). It joins a cause its lord leads or is in (into a plot against the crown it leans less: a count usually follows his duke, but not blindly: it needs some reason of its own as well), and holds back from a plot or a charter its lord stays out of. A house that holds of the ruler as its count or duke stands by the crown; one that holds of the crown only as the crown (a duke, a strategos, a noyan, a house freed of its lord) is free to weigh a cause against it like any other. A lord shields a vassal from a reckoning aimed at it. A vassal who hates its lord, or bears a grudge against him, follows him less, or not at all. When a lord joins a plot because his own lord is in it, he says so.
- **A town of the crown in a duke's duchy:** a duke whose duchy holds a town of the ruler's bears it against the crown for as long as it lasts (*"the crown keeps {town} for itself in the heart of our own duchy, and it answers to us in nothing"*), which makes his house readier for a plot or a charter. It is gone the day it ends: the ruler loses or gives up the town, the duchy changes hands, or the duke's house leaves the realm or comes to the throne. The ruler's own kin never hold it. You too, as such a duke.
- **Civil war:** the houses in a plot rise with it, so a lord's vassals usually ride with him. A duke is also likelier than other lords to start a [plot to break away](civil-war.md#plots-to-break-away) from the realm with the lands around his duchy.
- **Feuds:**
    - **The lord's call:** in every feud each head calls his own vassals, and so does a lord who comes to shield his vassal; each must choose a side, with his lord or against him, and riding against one's lord is [felony](factions.md#felony). See [The lord's call](factions.md#the-lords-call).
    - **The lord of the house attacked:** comes to shield it, with all his own vassals called to his side, so whoever means to take its fief weighs him and them before taking up arms, as they weigh its allies. He stays out only when he is caught up in another feud or in the quarrel itself, is of the ruler's family, or when his vassal has turned against him. When the lord is the ruling house, the ruler deals with the feud as the ruler, not as a lord. His own lord does not come unasked: he may [send a rider for aid](factions.md#aid-from-the-lord-above).
    - **Two vassals of the same lord:** they may fight it out between them; he stays out.
    - **A lord against his own vassal:** a lord bound to shield a house never takes up arms against it unprovoked, nor rides to win him its fief. He may [take it back by the council's vote](#a-lord-takes-back-a-vassals-fief); only if the vassal bars the gates does it come to a feud between them.
    - **A vassal of another lord attacking:** when the attacked house's lord rides to its side, the attacker's own lord may ride with his vassal. He never rides against his own lord. Then each lord's other vassals follow him, and a quarrel between two vassals can become one between their lords. Neither side can court a house its lord binds to the other side.
    - **You:** you are asked to stand by your lord or your vassal like anyone else. As a lord who rides in, the other side's lord may come against you and your own vassals follow you. As the lord of the house attacked, standing aside is remembered.
- **The duty to protect:** when a vassal's town or castle falls to a siege, it holds it against its lord if his host lingered near and did not fight, or if no party of his house came near though he was in the field and not too far. When the lord is the ruling house, the crown answers for it as for any fief left undefended. If you are that lord, the vassal sends word; and in a feud, as a vassal or a lord, you are asked to stand by yours like anyone else.

## A lord takes back a vassal's fief

Like the ruler, a duke or a count may put before the council the taking of a town or castle of a house that holds of him (the **Propose** button on the Fiefs tab of the kingdom screen, or the AI's own proposals). Only that house's own lord, only a town or castle in his own duchy (a count: a castle of his own county), and only over a house of lesser title; nobody else gains the button. In the empires nobody but the emperor puts another house's fief before the council (see [The Empire's own ways](empire.md)). A lord who is also part of a [reckoning](factions.md) against that vassal puts it to the vote as the reckoning's, with the reckoning's share-out.

- **If the council agrees,** the fief passes straight to that lord, with no share-out.
- **The vassal may refuse.** How likely depends on its head, as when the ruler takes a fief, but weighed against the lord who asks rather than the ruler. Refusing means barring the gates: a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses) between the lord and his vassal, the lord's other vassals riding with him. If no feud can be had then (the realm too torn already, a foe at the walls), the vassal yields after all.
- **If the vassal holds out until the lord gives up or is beaten** (a feud called off by the crown's peace or made up does not count), it owes that lord nothing more: it holds of the crown from then on, for as long as both houses stay in the realm. If the lord wins, the fief is his.
- The same for you: as a lord you may take back a vassal's fief; as a vassal you are asked whether to bow or bar the gates.

## A title without its land

As in Crusader Kings, a duke (or a noyan) **keeps his title even when he holds none of its land**. Losing the towns of a duchy does not lose the duchy; another house of the kingdom can take it from him only in a [feud for the title](#a-feud-for-a-title).

- **A sleeping title.** While no town of the duchy is held by a house of the duke's kingdom (another kingdom took them, or his house left the kingdom without them), the title sleeps: no dues, no vassals, no rights to its land, and nobody can challenge for it. It is still his, shown among his titles, and wakes when a town of it is back in his kingdom's hands. A house that leaves its kingdom keeps its duchy, sleeping if the towns stay behind (an ulus or a theme is lost instead: see below).
- **Vacant** only when the house dies out, or when it holds no town or castle anywhere for **96 days** (a lord with no seat at all has nowhere to hold a title from). The house keeps a firm claim to it.
- **Granted again.** If a house with a claim to a vacant title holds most of its towns, the title is its at once: there is nobody to fight. Otherwise the ruler of the kingdom that holds most of the duchy's towns grants it to one of the houses holding them: the one he likes best and with the most towns there, a house with a claim to the title weighing much more (a firm claim more than a weak one; the grant says so beside each house). If you rule, you choose. The houses passed over will remember it.

## Claims to a title

A house may hold a claim to a duchy or an ulus, as to a fief:

- **The house that loses a title** keeps a firm claim to it, weak under its next head and forgotten after, as with a fief it lost.
- **A forged claim**: a companion or kinsman of yours left at the duke's seat can forge one, as at any court (see [rights to fiefs](fief-rights.md#a-claim-to-a-title-forged-at-its-seat)). The AI's houses do the same now and then, for a duchy near their own lands.

A claim to a title held by another house of the same kingdom is pressed **only by the sword**, in a feud for the title, in any of the [three ways](factions.md#three-ways-to-press-a-right) a right is pressed. Never against the ruler's own house. A vassal may challenge his own duke, as a count may in Crusader Kings, but if he loses it is [felony](factions.md#felony). Your claims to titles, and those of others to yours, are in the Factions tab with your rights to fiefs, each with its **Press by the sword** button.

## A feud for a title

It is declared and fought like any [feud between houses](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses): the same rules for declaring it, the ruler's answer, allies, the [lord's call](factions.md#the-lords-call) and courting. What differs is what is fought over:

- **The land of the duchy:** every town and castle of its counties. A **town counts 2, a castle 1**. Only what the two sides hold counts: land of houses outside the quarrel, of another kingdom, of sellswords, or waiting for the council's share-out is left out, and a house that joins a side brings its land into the count. The duke's vassals who answer his call ride with his side.
- **Winning:** a side that holds **60%** of that land and **keeps it for 10 days** wins. The claimants win as soon as they do; the duke's side when the **120 days** of the feud are up. When the time is up, a side that holds the share fights on until its days are full or it loses it; if neither side holds it, the feud ends in a **white peace**: everything taken in it goes back, and the title stays where it was. A feud that ends any other way (made up, ended by the crown's peace, waited out too long) goes to the side that holds the share at that moment, or to a white peace.
- **If the claimants win**, the title is theirs. The old duke keeps a firm claim to it and a deep grievance against them (against the crown, if the ruler decreed it).
- **If the duke wins**, the claimant loses what it holds in the duchy, as a fief taken from it (it may refuse and break with the realm, as any house may; if it is you, you are asked). If the claimant was his vassal, it is also [felony](factions.md#felony). Vassals of the duke who rode against him answer for it the same way. Made up, or ended by the crown's peace: no punishment.
- **An AI house** declares one only when its side already holds the share, or is at least as strong as the duke's side with his vassals.
- **The journal** says all this when the feud begins, for the claimant, the duke and their allies alike, and shows how it stands: a bar for each side's share against what it needs, the days the side that holds it has held it, the time left, a line naming each town and castle, its worth and who holds it whenever that changes, and the whole duchy marked on the map.

## Where to see it

- **Clan screen, House tab, "Oaths":** your titles, your lord (or the crown) and the seat you hold of him by, the houses that hold land of you, and the dues paid and received today.
- **Clan screen, Fiefs tab:** every town and castle shows whom it is held of; its county, its duchy and the knights on its villages are in the tooltip.
- **Kingdom screen, Clans tab:** choose a house to see its first title, its lord and how many houses hold land of it, under its tier. The full lists are on its encyclopedia page.
- **Kingdom screen, Fiefs tab:** choose a town or castle to see its duchy and whom it is held of; its county and its knights are in the tooltip.
- **Kingdom screen, Court tab, "Titles":** every duchy of the kingdom with its duke and counts (or every theme with its strategos, every ulus with its noyan), and the kingdom's landed knights. An emperor or a khan has its buttons here (see below).
- **Kingdom screen, Factions tab, "Fiefs and rights":** your house's claims to titles beside its rights to fiefs, and the claims of others to your titles, each of yours with its **Press by the sword** button.
- **Encyclopedia:** a house's page lists its titles, the houses it holds its land of, and its vassals; a head of house's page their titles; a town's or castle's page its county and duchy, and whom it is held of.

## Themes of the empires

In the **Northern, Western and Southern Empires**, each pair of towns is a **theme**, and every house whose chief seat lies in it holds of its **strategos**, who commands it in the emperor's name. There are no counts under him. The titles: **Strategos** (Strategissa) of the theme, **Archon** (Archontissa) for a town, **Kastrophylax** for a castle, and **Patrikios** (Patrikia) for a lord with neither.

- **Named, not inherited.** The emperor names the strategos among the houses of the realm that hold a town or castle in the theme, never from his own house. An AI emperor picks the house he likes best and that holds most there (a town counts more than a castle) and of higher tier. Nobody has a right to a command, so nobody is passed over.
- **The command ends** when the strategos dies or no longer leads his house, when his house leaves the realm or comes to the throne, or when it holds no town or castle in the theme for **96 days**. The emperor then names another. A theme with no strategos stays in the emperor's hand until he does.
- **Disgrace.** An AI emperor relieves a strategos whose head is on bad terms with him, whose house was marked as traitor after a rising, or who is known to lead a plot. One who rides with the rebels of a civil war loses his command at once.
- **A new emperor** looks at every theme once: he keeps the strategos he finds unless another house suits him clearly better.
- **Relieved is not stripped.** No vote, no refusing, no breaking with the realm: the house keeps its towns and castles. It does remember being relieved, as a grievance against the crown.
- **What a strategos has:** the dues of the houses that hold of him while he commands, and everything a duke has (his vassals follow him, he shields them, he calls them to his banner, oaths, a claim to win back a lost town of the theme in the wars between kingdoms), except that he may not ask the council for a fief held of him: the land is not his. The emperor's own towns and castles in a theme wrong no strategos. There are no feuds between houses in the empires, so a strategos never leads his vassals into one; a strategos may instead plot to seize the throne itself (see [The Empire's own ways](empire.md)).
- **As emperor**, the Court tab's **Titles** entry gives you **"Name a strategos"** (pick the theme, then the house; one you replace remembers it) and **"Relieve a strategos"** (the theme stays in your hand until you name another). A theme with no strategos is put to you every week, as a vacant duchy is. You choose freely.

## The ulus of the Khuzaits

Among the **Khuzaits**, each pair of towns is an **ulus**, held by a house and passed down within it like a duchy. The titles: **Noyan** (Khatun) of the ulus, **Bey** (Begum) for a town, **Tarkhan** for a castle, held of the bey of its county, and **Bahadur** for a lord with neither.

- **Given first to the khan's blood.** When the campaign begins, the khan gives each ulus to a house of his own blood (one of its living members is his kin by blood, from grandparents and grandchildren to first cousins, never by marriage; never his own house) that holds a town or castle in it; failing that, to the **great house of the place**, the one with the most towns there, then the most land; his own house keeps one only where no other holds land. A vacant ulus goes to the houses with a town in it, or of his blood with a castle there, his blood first; the houses passed over remember it. An AI khan passes over traitors; you, as khan, choose freely.
- **All owe the khan first.** A vassal does not follow its noyan or its bey into a plot or a charter against the khan (it still stands by its lord in a reckoning or a feud). The khan's own towns in an ulus are no wrong to its noyan.
- **Taken back for treason.** The khan takes back the ulus of a house he knows to lead a plot in his realm (one not yet risen), and with it the best of its land in it (a town before a castle), as any fief taken: its plot may rise over it, or the house may refuse and break with the realm, as any house that loses a fief may; if it yields, the fief is shared out. With no land there, the title alone, and a grievance against the crown. A house marked as traitor after a rising keeps or loses its ulus with the [crown's judgment](realm-houses.md#houses-marked-as-traitors): it loses it with land taken or banishment, and keeps it with a pardon or a rebuke.
- **In a civil war,** the ulus of a house riding with the rebels sleeps (no dues) until the war is over. It keeps it after an amnesty or a rebel victory; if its house breaks away as a realm of its own, the ulus falls vacant.
- **If you are the noyan** whose ulus the khan takes back with a fief, a window **"The khan's judgment"** asks you: **"Bow to the khan"**, **"Raise the plot's banner"** (when you are in a plot that can rise) or **"Renounce your oath"**.
- **As khan**, the Court tab's **Titles** entry gives you **"Take back an ulus"**, greyed until a noyan betrays you (a house waiting for your judgment after a rising is judged in that window instead).

A theme or an ulus belongs to the realm that gave it: a house that leaves that realm loses it at once. One that falls vacant and is granted by another kingdom becomes what that kingdom knows (a theme taken by Vlandia becomes a duchy). In a campaign begun before the themes and the ulus came to the mod, the empires and the Khuzaits stay without titles.

## Oaths and duties

When your house comes to hold a town or castle of a lord, or another house comes to hold one of yours, or a landed knight swears to you (or you to a lord as one), the next time you speak with the head of that house the talk opens with the oath. He says, in his own manner, what each of you owes the other: the dues, the sword when the banners are called, the lord's shield when a fief is besieged. You swear or accept it warmly or coldly, and he remembers which. After that, lords and vassals greet you as what you are to them, and a vassal who holds a grudge against you lets it show, and says so when you failed to defend him.

When a town or castle you hold of a lord is besieged, you may send him a rider. He answers in his own words when the rider comes back: he promises, or tells you truly why he cannot (a siege of his own, his own walls besieged, a captive, too far), or, if he cannot stand you, refuses. A lord who promises is likelier to march to it. When a house holding a fief of yours is besieged, its head writes to you. You may promise to come (it goes in your journal), send word that you are held fast if you truly are, or refuse, which costs you their regard. A promise kept is remembered well, and a promise broken worse than a refusal, whichever of you broke it.

When your lord raises a host near you, his rider calls you to the banner. Answer and you have a few days to join it, or its siege if it has laid one (it goes in your journal, its leader marked on the map). Send your regrets, or never come, and your lord will think less of you. Calling a vassal or a landed knight of your own to your host costs less influence, for you as for any lord; a ruler pays less only for the houses that hold of him as their count or duke, not for a duke or another house that holds of the crown only as the crown.

Turn each of these off in the in-game options under *The houses of a realm*, or in the settings file: `TitlesOathsEnabled`, `TitlesGreetingsEnabled`, `TitlesReliefEnabled`, `TitlesServiceEnabled`, `TitlesVassalCallEnabled`.

## Titles in the wars between kingdoms

A town of a duchy held by a house of your kingdom, or a castle of a county whose town your kingdom holds, that a house of your kingdom held and lost to another kingdom, is land to take back: the kingdom goes to war for it more readily, a town of a duchy more than a castle. The claim lasts as long as that house keeps its [right](fief-rights.md) to the place and stays in your kingdom (riding out in a [feud](factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses) is leaving it). A vacant duchy counts for nobody. In the vote on a war, a duke or count whose lands border the enemy, or who has lost a place to it, is more eager for it, and one far from it a little less. See [War and peace](war-and-peace.md#dukes-and-counts-in-the-vote).

## Turn it off

In the [settings file](../settings.md), set `TitlesEnabled` to `false`, or in the in-game options under *The houses of a realm*. `TitleDuchyRightsEnabled` and `TitleCountyRightsEnabled` turn off the rights of a duchy and of a county; `FelonyJusticeEnabled`, a lord's sentence of death for felony (the felon then only loses his land). `ImperialThemesEnabled` and `KhuzaitUlusEnabled` turn off the themes and the ulus; they count only when a campaign begins, and switched off later the titles stand frozen (no dues, no changes) until switched on again.
