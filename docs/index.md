# Kingdoms Alive

**Kingdoms Alive turns your Bannerlord campaign into a living medieval realm, in the spirit of Crusader Kings:** a court to fill, oaths to demand, houses that scheme and feud, and kingdoms that remember what you did.

A campaign mod for Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord. Kingdoms go to war for a reason, wars last long enough to matter, peace is agreed by both sides, and peace is no longer empty: lords feast, hunt and duel, clans quarrel and make up, great realms split in civil war, and your own household gives you something to do.

Every feature can be switched off on its own (see [Settings](settings.md)).

!!! warning "Start a new campaign with it"
    Play the mod from the start of a campaign and keep it until the end. Removing it from a campaign in progress can break that save. See [Getting started](getting-started.md#new-or-existing-campaign).

## What is new in 2.7.1
- **[Traitors forfeit land and titles](features/realm-houses.md#houses-marked-as-traitors)**: for a while after a house loses a rising, whatever the crown's judgment, it is given no town or castle when the realm shares one out, and no house holds of it: its vassals hold of the lord above it, or of the crown. Left with no town or castle, it loses its duchy or ulus at once, and a judgment that takes its land takes its duchy too. **[The fief it broke with the realm over](features/realm-houses.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm)** goes, whenever the crown has it back, to the house it was being taken for.
- **[The khan bides his time](features/titles.md#the-ulus-of-the-khuzaits)**: before he takes back the ulus of a lord who leads a plot, an AI khan weighs that house and its whole plot against his own strength, and may wait and watch for a year before he weighs it again.
- **[A crown at war with itself answers few calls](features/civil-war.md#alliances-in-a-civil-war)**: while it fights a civil war, an AI crown answers an ally's call to war by how that war stands for its own realm, not for gold, and mostly refuses. Any crown fighting a civil war may refuse a call to war at no cost, yours too.
- **[A rising settles its wrongs](features/assembly.md#the-cases)**: once the crown has judged or pardoned a rising, or it has won, the desertions it rose over no longer go to the assembly; the wrongs between its own houses are settled too once they are back under the crown.
- **[A blind eye from a crown losing its wars](features/factions.md#the-crowns-call-to-arms)**: a crown losing abroad now looks the other way more often when a house defies it, its call to lay down arms among them: a realm bleeding abroad cannot afford a war at home.

## What is new in 2.7.0
- **[Recruitment by the land](features/recruitment.md)**: men come from the villages and towns and are missed there. A lord raises freely on his own lands; anyone else takes only the cheapest men and pays the lord for the right. Young nobles come from the knights' manors, the castles' musters and the richest townsfolk; every castle has a master of its muster; higher ranks cost more to pay and to raise. On by default.
- **[Every war is won on the war score](features/war-and-peace.md)**: captive lords count in it, and in a war within a realm an **Objective** line shows which side is getting what it fights for. Who is ahead, and by how far, sets what each side can ask at the peace table, in wars between realms and within them alike.
- **[Peace at the table](features/war-and-peace.md#terms-of-peace)**: every peace, between realms and within them, goes through the Terms of Peace screen and the councils of both sides; each ruler can overrule only his own council, at a cost in influence. No talk of peace in the first weeks of a war unless one side is crushed, no peace for nothing until the war has dragged on, and the losses of the lords' hosts make a realm weary. The AI asks only what the war has earned it: land and fealty only from a side that is crushed.
- **[Civil wars and feuds on the war score](features/civil-war.md)**: no set end any more, and their peace is agreed term by term at the table: a pardon, an heir or the crown, the charter, independence, the fief or the right in dispute, a vassal freed, silver paid once. A side well ahead keeps what it holds of the stake and asks for more the further ahead it is. A house that breaks with its realm always rises in a civil war of that realm, and its kin, allies and sworn houses may ride with it. [The rebels swear no alliance](features/civil-war.md#alliances-in-a-civil-war) with another kingdom, and a crown fighting them is loath to answer its allies' calls.
- **[Warning before arms](features/factions.md)**: a right pressed by the sword brings rumours, then an ultimatum the crown answers, and only then the swords; feuds are far rarer while the realm is at war. The AI very seldom turns on its own kin or friends, by the sword, a reckoning or a forged claim; you are free to. A right the crown has judged may not be raised again while that ruler reigns.
- **[Reckonings at the assembly](features/assembly.md)**: the ruler judges them in his hall, the first assembly comes soon after each peace, before any feast or hunt. **Yield or defy**: a house that loses a fief may give it up or defy the crown, which then looks the other way, at a cost to the ruler's reign, or names it traitor. Amends are paid as a feud's peace would pay them; a proud house may refuse, and the crown mostly lets it pass, or rarely distrains a fief for it.
- **[The Realm tab](features/royal-court.md#the-realm-tab)**: a new tab in the kingdom screen with every house of the realm under **Fealty**, **How it stands** for every step you could take, what waits on your word under **Pending**, and your **Envoys**. Windows that ask your word on the realm's affairs offer *"Let me look into it first"*. It takes the place of the Factions tab and the tree in the Crown tab; the House tab of the clan screen keeps your faith, claims and grievances.
- **[Envoys and offices](features/envoys.md)**: a mounted soldier of your party can carry some messages; offices of the court and commands are offered in person or by envoy; a landed knight never sits on the council. **[Marshals of the Fronts](features/command-your-armies.md)** lead the realm's other wars beside the Marshal.
- **[Smarter warlords](features/command-your-armies.md)** are now on by default.
- **And more**: plots are easier to join in a very large realm, and there a house in one holds on to it longer; AI rulers seize fiefs for themselves more rarely; houses seldom go over to far-off kingdoms; [a captive's family](features/captives.md) holds its hired blades back while you ride in an army; a new campaign starts with no old claims of a line; [companies for hire](features/free-companies.md) take real ships across the sea; [map views](features/titles.md#the-political-map) of realms, provinces or borders only; and each faith keeps [its own festival](features/faiths.md).

## What is new in 2.6.0
2.6.0 also brings everything prepared for 2.5.0, which was never released on its own (the last eight points below).

- **[The Marshal leads the hosts](features/command-your-armies.md#one-marshal)**: the realm's Marshal is always the head of a house, named at once when the seat falls empty, and the lord most eager to raise the realm's hosts, the ruler next; the other lords are slower to raise theirs while either of them can. The Marshal rides in no host but the ruler's or his own. As Marshal in your ruler's own host, no new aim comes to you and the time of yours stands still.
- **[Only lords of rank raise hosts](features/command-your-armies.md#who-raises-the-realms-hosts)**: the ruler and the ruling house, the Marshal, a captain, the holder of a province and the lord of a town; the lords of castles ride in the hosts of others, and nobody is called to a host by a lord of lower rank. **Princes lead**: a prince of the ruling house may raise a host at the head of his own company, the more readily when the ruler and the Marshal cannot, and a duke's kin when no one higher can. The same for you and for the AI.
- **[Captains of the Host](features/command-your-armies.md#marshals-of-the-fronts)**: in war a ruler may give the lord of a town or of a province (a count or a duke) command of the realm's lords against one enemy realm, under the title of his people: King's Lieutenant, Strategos Autokrator, Bearer of the Paiza, Voivode, Amir al-Juyush, Lendmann, Penteulu. As such a lord, you may ask your ruler for it in person or by envoy, or be offered it; as ruler, give it in the Crown tab.
- **[Feuds frowned on in a war](features/factions.md#feuds-frowned-on-in-a-war)**: while the realm is at war with another kingdom, its ruler is readier to forbid a feud between its houses, even against a firm right, and the lords are slower to start one or defy a ban; a house that takes one up all the same loses standing with the ruler. **A forbidden quarrel returns with peace**, and a ruler may turn **[a blind eye to a defied ban](features/factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses)**. Forbidding a feud costs a ruler less influence again, and the head of a house that bows to a ban thinks a little less of the ruler. A house stripped by a reckoning's vote [goes over to another king](features/factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house) only rarely.
- **[The lord's hall](features/feasts.md#the-lords-hall)**: the lords keep to the great hall instead of wandering the keep, nobody is left crowded at the door, and a full hall holds many more of them. **[The feast in the hall](features/feasts.md#the-feast-in-the-hall)**: every guest has a place by standing and friendship, families come in, musicians play, the tables are laid, servants go round, the host rises to welcome you and the hall cheers your toast.
- **[The assembly in the hall](features/assembly.md#the-assembly-in-the-hall)** sits as a court before the throne, each side of the case behind its head, a lord who changes his mind crossing the hall; **[court in the hall](features/royal-court.md#court-in-the-hall)**, with the petitioners in a line before your seat; **[oaths in the hall](features/royal-court.md#oaths-in-the-hall)** to a new ruler; **[the trial](features/kings-writ.md#the-trial)** of an outlaw chief under the King's Writ; and **[the festival in the square](features/faiths.md#the-festival-in-the-square)**. Each part has its own switch, and the waits and their days go on as before.
- **[Clearer titles](features/titles.md#marks-of-rank)**: a mark of rank on every house's banner wherever the screens list houses, houses grouped by rank in the realm's own words, and **[Liege and Vassals](features/titles.md#liege-and-vassals)** drawn as a tree.
- **[The political map](features/titles.md#the-political-map)**: draw the map back and the land takes the colours of its realms, their duchies, themes and ulus in shades of them, with borders and each realm's name over its land. Changes to it take effect at once.
- **[Aims for your army as a vassal](features/command-your-armies.md#aims-for-your-army-as-a-vassal)**: leading an army of a realm at war as one of its vassals, the Marshal (or your liege if there is none) now and then gives you one aim your army can really carry out: **break a siege** of one of the realm's towns or castles, **take** or **win back** an enemy fortress on the border, or **bring a weaker enemy army you can catch to battle**. Refusing costs a little of their regard; carrying it out wins their favour and some influence; accepting and then failing is remembered. It counts as yours only if your army had a hand in it, and besiegers who lift their siege rather than face you are yours to chase. Each aim is in your journal and marked on your map.
- **[A fief taken for nothing](features/royal-court.md#a-fief-taken-for-nothing)**: a ruler who takes a fief into the crown's own hands with no cause the realm would own (no traitor, no felon, no leader of an open charter, no firm right of the ruling house) pays far more influence for it, the house is far readier to break with the realm, and a plot it is sworn to rises over it more readily and draws more houses. A lord who demands a vassal's fief with no such cause pays that price too. You and the AI alike, so AI rulers do it more rarely.
- **[Backing a claim the council never voted for](features/factions.md#the-reckoning-of-a-house)**: backing a reckoning by decree without the council's vote, or a [lord's plea](features/royal-court.md#a-lords-plea), now costs the ruler's house influence; with the council's vote behind it, nothing.
- **[Vassal realms](features/vassal-realms.md#annexation)**: demanding fealty from the Diplomacy tab costs more influence. Demanding that a vassal realm give up its crown costs influence when you demand it, whatever it answers, and the war that may follow no longer does; taking its crown at the end of that war costs nothing.
- **[The feud panel](features/factions.md#the-feud-panel)** now also brings you, as ruler, a reckoning that lays its case before you (the house that leads it against the holder, the rights of the houses behind it, who would get the fief, whether the council voted) and a lord's plea against his vassal.
- **[Granting a vacant title](features/titles.md#a-title-without-its-land)**: as ruler, a vacant duchy, theme or ulus comes in a panel with one card for each house that may have it: its head, banner, traits and your relation, the land it holds in the title, its claim, its ties to you, and whether it will hold being passed over against you.
- **[Translations](getting-started.md#translations)** now cover the mod's settings in Mod Configuration Menu too, in your language from the first start.
- **[Made for Bannerlord 1.4.8 again](getting-started.md#requirements)** (the default Steam branch), as well as 1.5.7, with everything since 2.2.0; it needs UIExtenderEx there too. A few things differ there: no blood feuds, the game's own waiting for lords riding to relieve a siege, and free companies that do not cross the sea (see [Compatibility](getting-started.md#compatibility)). The same translation files serve both, and Arms of the Faithful has its own file for each game (on 1.4.8 without the tabards of the Order of Paravenos).

## What is new in 2.4.0
- **[The Marshal's aims](features/command-your-armies.md#your-lieges-aims)**: as Marshal of an AI ruler, you no longer get a new order every few days. Your liege gives you aims for the war instead: **take**, or **win back**, a fortress on the realm's border, within a time he sets. An aim is met the moment the realm holds the place, whoever of the realm takes it. When the realm turns to the defence, he charges you with **the realm's defence** for a while and judges it at the end; meanwhile any other aim waits, its time standing still. Each one is in your journal with the days left, and its place is marked on your map.
- **[Laying down an office](features/royal-court.md#laying-down-an-office)**: any office you hold at an AI ruler's court can be laid down, face to face with the ruler, at a little of their regard. They will not offer that office to your house again until you ask for it. The Warden's seat still goes back with the [King's Writ](features/kings-writ.md#when-the-writ-ends).
- **[The feud panel](features/factions.md#the-feud-panel)**: whenever you are asked to take a side in a feud between houses of your realm, a panel like the game's marriage offer shows both heads face to face: who attacks and who holds, your relation and ties with each, their allies, kin and vassals, each house's right to the fief, and the fief itself, every name a link to the encyclopedia. You no longer hear beforehand which houses will really ride: you see their allies, as anyone in the realm would.
- **[No third front lightly](features/war-and-peace.md#less-piling-on)**: a kingdom already at war with two realms is very slow to declare a third war; being the stronger side barely counts then, and only a strong cause carries it. And a kingdom much stronger than a [dominant](features/war-and-peace.md#a-kingdom-that-grows-too-much) one fears it only as much as its real strength says, not for the number of its fiefs.
- **[The ruler's answer to a feud](features/factions.md#when-a-reckoning-takes-up-arms-feuds-between-houses)**: forbidding a feud now costs a ruler more influence and taking a side less, you and the AI alike, so AI rulers take a side more often.
- **[The hunters' castle](features/kings-writ.md#the-hunters-castle)**: its Hunters' Lodge, Gaol Tower and Watchtower are never torn down. Their room for prisoners and their security go on whoever holds the castle; the manhunters, the crown's cart, the outlaw marks and the early warning of the outlaws' host come only to the castle of a writ in force, and nothing of them is built without one. Levels taken away in an older campaign come back once.
- **Fixed**: dialogue lists that came up empty or stuck: *"My vassals will see to it."* with no house to choose (only the houses that can go are listed now), the knights for a notable's task stuck on the first page, and the offer of a fief of your own to a house riding in a feud's host.

## What is new in 2.3.2
- **[Fewer wars ended within days by a feud](features/war-and-peace.md#wars-that-stop-and-start-sensibly)**: when a kingdom's border towns and castles are held by the hosts of a feud between its own houses, they still count as its border. Before, the game could find no border with the enemy and force a peace a few days into the war.
- **[A war counts as decided](features/war-and-peace.md#peace-needs-both-sides)** only with a clearer lead, so a kingdom needs the other side's consent for longer before it can make peace. By the war's score alone, an emperor [yields the purple](features/empire.md#one-empire-one-emperor) only when he is far further behind.
- **[No ally is called into a lost war](features/war-and-peace.md#allies-called-to-war)**: a kingdom clearly losing a war calls no ally into it, yours included if the rules bind it.
- **[Rich kingdoms are easier to attack](features/war-and-peace.md#less-piling-on)**: how much a lord must stand to win before he proposes a war is drawn toward that of a kingdom of middling wealth: less against a rich kingdom, more against a poor one.
- **[Lords shun a foreign crown](features/civil-war.md#who-joins-and-why)**: a lord is slower to join, and quicker to leave, a plot or a strategos's coup that would crown a house of another people than his own.
- **[The fief a house broke with its realm over](features/realm-houses.md#a-house-that-breaks-with-its-realm)**, retaken during that war, goes at once to the house it was promised to, if one was named, instead of being shared out by the council and passed on again at the end of the war. A fief lost by a decision of the realm (a vote or a decree) now leaves only a [weak right](features/fief-rights.md#how-a-house-comes-by-a-right).
- The houses the laws of blood and the Kurultai name as claimants to the throne are now called **among the mightiest of the realm**: they are the houses with the best claim to rule after the crown's.

## What is new in 2.3.0
- **[Laws of succession](features/succession.md#the-laws-of-succession)**: each realm has its own few laws of succession, drawn from the history of its people (seventeen in all, from Salic Primogeniture and the Veche to the Kurultai and Cognatic Designation), and lives under its custom. Only the ruler brings another before the council; a law brought in against the custom is resented by the houses until it grows old enough to be the custom itself, and a charter may demand the custom back. Vlandia now starts under Salic Primogeniture, and whoever founds a realm chooses its law.
- **[Laws of the Crown](features/crown-laws.md)** stand together at the top of the game's Policies tab, in three groups (Succession, Crown Authority, Taxes), each with one law in force marked by the crown; Autonomous Vassals and No Tax Obligation are laws of their own, and the realms of the Empire keep High Crown Authority as their custom. AI rulers bring High Crown Authority, or drop all taxes, only very rarely.
- **[Liege and Vassals](features/titles.md#liege-and-vassals)**: the tree of lords and vassals, as in Crusader Kings, with landed knights in it: your liege up to the crown and your vassals in the House tab (it takes the place of Oaths), every house of the realm under **Fealty** in the Realm tab of the kingdom screen, and a lord's liege and vassals in the encyclopedia. The mightiest houses are marked.
- **The kingdom screen**: the Court tab is now the **[Crown](features/royal-court.md#the-kingdom-screen)** tab, and your vassal realms, your overlord and an enemy demanding your fealty are under its **Tributaries**. **[Demand fealty](features/vassal-realms.md#demanding-it-yourself)** of a beaten enemy from the war on the Diplomacy tab, for influence; face to face or by an envoy it stays free.
- **[A lost battle is blamed](features/memories.md#lords-remember-each-other)** on whoever led it, and on the realm's ruler and Marshal, by the lords who fought in it, you included when it is yours.
- **[The court](features/royal-court.md#naming-your-court-as-ruler)**: an office warms a house to its ruler only while it holds it, losing one stings for a while, and a house that holds more than one is resented.
- **The houses' regard for their ruler** no longer climbs because of the mod: taxes weigh more on it, help and rescue between lords count once at a time, and the game's lodging perks bring no goodwill at the mod's feasts and assemblies.
- **[Plots](features/civil-war.md#the-rising)**: an AI plot needs half the realm's strength to rise; a plot to break away a little less than before.
- **Kingdoms Alive no longer replaces the game's screens**: it adds to them, so mods that add to the same screens work beside it. It now needs [UIExtenderEx](getting-started.md#requirements); delete the old mod folder before installing.
- **Fixed**: a crash of a quest of the base game when a faith's priest was taken for one of a town's notables.
- **Built for Bannerlord 1.5.7** (with or without War Sails). On 1.4.8, Kingdoms Alive stayed at 2.2.0 until 2.6.0.

## What the mod does

### War and diplomacy
- **[War and peace](features/war-and-peace.md)**: wars have a cause you can read, are won on the war score, and end only at the peace table, when both rulers and their councils agree; set terms of peace (tribute, towns and castles, fealty) on their own screen and watch both councils vote, house by house.
- **[Events between kingdoms](features/events-between-kingdoms.md)**: raids, insults and gifts between neighbours build tension that can lead to war, or prevent it.
- **[Winning the council over](features/council.md)**: talk lords into backing your war or your peace, or your house when a fief is shared out (now with five candidates, the conqueror and the ruler always among them); council votes bring modest, fair goodwill.
- **[Vassal realms and annexation](features/vassal-realms.md)**: a beaten kingdom can bend the knee instead of being wiped out. A vassal realm keeps its ruler, pays tribute and marches in its overlord's wars, and may in time be joined to the overlord's realm for good, or rebel. Your own realm can take vassals, or become one: demand it of a beaten enemy from the Diplomacy tab, and see your ties under Tributaries in the Crown tab.
- **[Command your armies](features/command-your-armies.md)**: the Marshal of your court leads the realm's armies. As ruler, give the war its aims through your Marshal or order one army to besiege, stand guard or come home; as a vassal, become Marshal, take up your liege's aims for the war and carry them to the lords, or take the Marshal's aims for the army you lead. Only lords of rank raise the realm's hosts, the Marshal first, and each war beyond the Marshal's own may have its Marshal of the Front. Riders take your word anywhere. AI realms that are losing turn to the defence. On by default: smarter AI lords who see as you do, lift lost sieges, take refuge and hunt armies they can catch.

### Life at court
- **[Feasts and the grand tournament](features/feasts.md)**: lords gather in peacetime; you can attend or hold your own. Every feast in a town has its tournament, a ruler's is the grand tournament of the realm, and in the hall, full of guests in their places, musicians and laid tables, you can raise a toast, hear the talk, seek a match or argue war and peace.
- **[Hunts](features/hunts.md)**: in peacetime lords invite friendly houses to hunt in the woods of their fiefs; ride along or host your own, win friends, have a quiet word about a plot, and once in a long while someone does not come back.
- **[The kingdom assembly](features/assembly.md)**: soon after each peace the houses gather at the ruler's hall to bring the realm's real quarrels before the crown, reckonings over a fief among them; win voices in the hall for gold, friendship or a favour, plead, bow or defy, and a house that stays away is marked for it.
- **[Events between clans](features/clan-events.md)**: the clans of a kingdom fall out and make up.
- **[The crown and its claim](features/succession.md)**: every kingdom follows its own law of succession, drawn from history: the Senate, the army, the dynasty, the eldest of the house, the eldest son, the strongest chief, the sheikhs, the kurultai or the Thing, among others; only its ruler may bring in another, against the custom of the realm, and a charter may ask the custom back. Every lord has a claim to the crown, and a ruler with a weak one faces plots, grudging oaths and hungry neighbours.
- **[The faiths of Calradia](features/faiths.md)**: seven faiths drawn from history, a priest in every town, a great festival for each faith once a year (every second year in fast mode) where lords of every realm meet, the Aserai sacred months, blessings, and the choice to change your faith.
- **[The faith and ways of a fief](features/faith-and-ways.md)**: a conquered town or castle slowly takes up the faith and customs of its new lords; leave it its temple and priest, or push and risk brawls, old preachers and a rising.
- **[Civil war](features/civil-war.md)**: any kingdom can crack, and the larger it grows the more ambitious and rancorous its lords; clans plot for the throne, and you can join, oppose or lead the plot from the Realm tab of the kingdom screen. A realm can hold several plots at once, each behind its own pretender, a house with a claim to the throne by blood, marriage, an old crown or the law of the realm, and the first to rise ends the rest. Houses on bad terms with the crown may instead plot to break away with their lands.
- **[Factions and grievances](features/factions.md)**: lords remember every wrong the crown does them and band together behind a cause: a charter to bind the ruler's hands, a fief taken from a house by someone with a better right, or a ruler asked to step down. The crown hears them in council or at the assembly, and when the law fails them, houses give warning and then take up arms in private feuds over a fief. Houses also bind themselves to each other by marriage or a sworn pact and answer each other's call to arms; friends tell you what is afoot, and any lord will tell you how things stand in the realm or in his house.
- **[Your house at a glance](features/house-tab.md)**: the **Realm** tab of the kingdom screen with every house of the realm, what you can do with each and how it stands, what waits on your word and your envoys; and a **House** tab in the clan screen with your faith, your claims and the grievances held against you.
- **[Laws of the crown](features/crown-laws.md)**: the law of succession, High Crown Authority and the tax obligation, at the top of the Policies tab; only the ruler brings them before the council.
- **[Traitors and the houses of a realm](features/realm-houses.md)**: the leader of a failed rising pays with his life and the rebel houses are at the crown's mercy for a while; a house that breaks with its realm rises in a civil war; AI kingdoms raise new houses when they have more land than lords, rich houses take knights into their service, and a kingdom with more lords than land lets some go.
- **[Landed knights](features/landed-knights.md)**: houses of a single knight, holding the land of one castle and sworn to its lord (one knight to a castle); they follow their lord, have no vote and no castle, and rise to full houses when the kingdom has land to spare. You can become one, or make one of a companion, and send yours to the troubles of your fiefs.
- **[Works in a village](features/village-works.md)**: whoever holds a village, you or the AI, may raise a well, a palisade, a mill and a storehouse there, each helping it a little; raids undo them.
- **[Dukes, counts and castellans](features/titles.md)**: in five kingdoms each town is a county and every two neighbouring towns a duchy; every house has one lord, by its chief seat (dukes the crown), pays him dues and must choose a side when he calls it to a feud, and a vassal who rides against his lord risks his head for felony. Dukes and counts claim the land of their titles, a duke keeps his title without its land, and another house takes it only in a feud for the title. The empires have their themes under a strategos the emperor names, and the Khuzaits their ulus, handed down in a house.
- **[The Empire's own ways](features/empire.md)**: petitions to the emperor, pronoia granted by the throne, strategos coups and the dream of one Empire.
- **[Sparring with nobles](features/sparring.md)**: practice bouts with the nobles of your realm, best of three falls, each fought until someone really falls.
- **[Rights to fiefs](features/fief-rights.md)**: every house holds its fiefs by right and remembers the ones it lost; marriages bring claims, and a companion left at another house's court can forge one for you, even to a duke's title. Press a right by the sword in person, by an envoy or from the kingdom screen.
- **[Envoys](features/envoys.md)**: from any lord's page in the encyclopedia or the Realm tab, send a family member, a companion or a mounted soldier with a message: a meeting, a gift, a marriage, the council, a plot, a fief, a cause, a pact, terms of peace and more. They argue it as you would, with their own skills and character.
- **[Your rival](features/rival.md)**: beat a lord in a tournament final, or earn their hatred, and they may swear a blood feud against you: taunts, hired blades on your trail, ambushes in town streets, and at the end a duel to the death.
- **[Duels](features/duels.md)**: your rival challenges you to a duel in the arena, and AI lords who hate each other fight their own. The loser dies, unless it is you: you are only badly hurt.
- **[Lords who remember you](features/memories.md)**: lords bring up what you did, good and bad; small things fade in days, a dead kinsman never. They remember each other too, and it warms or sours their houses.
- **[Captives](features/captives.md)**: the house of a lord you hold may bargain, hunt you or send knives; and your noble prisoners talk, bring news of the world, offer ransom or their word, remember how they were kept, and may swear vengeance.

### Your realm and household
- **[The royal court](features/royal-court.md)**: offer the offices of Marshal, Steward, Master of Works, Chancellor and Warden of the King's Peace in person or by envoy, and see them in the Crown tab; choose **Hold court** in one of your own towns or castles to hear the petitioners, then the notables with tasks for you; win the oath of every house when a new ruler takes the throne; take a fief by decree, at a price, and set the laws of the crown.
- **[Affairs of your kingdom](features/realm-affairs.md)**: in peacetime a messenger stops you on the road with news of sellswords, a revolt, a famine, a feud, a great work or, for a vassal, hired killers sent against the crown. Deal with it yourself, send a companion or one of your kin with men you pick, or, if you rule, hand it to one of your vassals' houses. Whoever you send shows in your quest log until they are back.
- **[Recruitment by the land](features/recruitment.md)**: men are raised from the land and missed there; lords recruit freely on their own lands and pay for the right elsewhere, nobles come from the manors and the castles' musters, and pay and training grow dearer with rank.
- **[Companion errands](features/companion-errands.md)**: companions ask for leave and a few men you pick to settle matters of their own.
- **[Alayen](features/alayen.md)**: help a Sturgian village generously and a young smith's son asks to ride with you; his past, and his quest *The Grey Wolf*, come out by the campfire.
- **[Squire service](features/squire.md)**: serve a lord as their squire: errands by messenger, their armies, their battles, talks by their fire, bouts in their yard, lessons in their army, and in the end their household knight's arms, the accolade from their hand, a life in their service, or a rival if you walk out.
- **[The Blackhand's knives](features/lords-enemies.md)**: hired killers sent against your lord or your ruler, and five ways to stop them: in a hall, a street, an arena, a village or a tavern.
- **[Mercenary contracts and the Unsworn](features/mercenary-contracts.md)**: while your clan serves a realm as mercenaries, its ruler sends you tasks (raids, escorts, guarding a village, rescues, a captive lord, riding in its armies). Sign contracts of 30, 60 or 100 days, make a name for your company, take offers from other rulers or the enemy's purse, and earn the crown's gifts. If the ruler is captured, their house asks you to bring them home. Taverns now also hire out the Unsworn, a new tree of sellswords.
- **[The King's Writ](features/kings-writ.md)**: without a realm of your own, carry a ruler's writ and hunt outlaws for the price on their heads, in their lands only: wanted chiefs, trials, manhunters, ranks, and a castle at the end.
- **[Companies for hire](features/free-companies.md)**: hire one of nine sellsword companies, hundreds of men at once, for a term of their own; they march to you on the map, take ship to cross the sea, cost a fortune and leave when the term is up.

### Campaign
- **[Fast mode families](features/fast-mode.md)**: in fast mode, noble houses no longer die out.
- **[Other changes](features/other-changes.md)**: longer sieges, less peacetime training for AI troops, fairer gear prices.
