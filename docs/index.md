# Kingdoms Alive

**Kingdoms Alive turns your Bannerlord campaign into a living medieval realm, in the spirit of Crusader Kings:** a court to fill, oaths to demand, houses that scheme and feud, and kingdoms that remember what you did.

A campaign mod for Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord. Kingdoms go to war for a reason, wars last long enough to matter, peace is agreed by both sides, and peace is no longer empty: lords feast, hunt and duel, clans quarrel and make up, great realms split in civil war, and your own household gives you something to do.

Every feature can be switched off on its own (see [Settings](settings.md)).

!!! warning "Start a new campaign with it"
    Play the mod from the start of a campaign and keep it until the end. Removing it from a campaign in progress can break that save. See [Getting started](getting-started.md#new-or-existing-campaign).

## New in 0.95

Wanted chiefs roam the realm, outlaws are bigger and look like it, a chief's purse is richer, and your captains only take manhunters.

## New in 0.94
- **[The King's Writ](features/kings-writ.md)**: a king's sheriff in every town, a notable you talk to for your pay, your prisoners and your men. A crossbow branch of manhunters, a shock line on foot with two-handed maces, and wages like regular soldiers. No limit of the writ's own on your company, only sworn writ-men. New contract: a raid on a gang hiding inside a town, fought in its streets among the townsfolk. A chief sentenced to the rope may draw his gang to the gallows. The sheriffs' master-at-arms gives you a manhunter's kit, and better ones as your works add up; the crown's armoury sells the manhunters' gear. Bars in the journal show how far you are from the next rank and from the king's summons, and a High Justice of the Roads may already name a captain.

## New in 0.76
- **[Companies for hire](features/free-companies.md)**: four sellsword companies of 170 to 250 men, each with troops of its own (pikes and crossbows, heavy horse, horse archers, axes), quartered in the towns of Calradia. Ask a tavern keeper where they are, strike a deal with their captain or send a rider, and watch them march to you across the map: dear, slow to come, and worth it when a siege must be broken. - **[Captives](features/captives.md)**: a captive's house cares more when the captive matters (a head of a house or the ruler's kin), captives speak of what happened in the world while they sat in your camp, and a well-treated one can name their own ransom or go free on their word, which not all of them keep.

- **Everything in the journal**: affairs of your kingdom, duels, feasts, hunts, envoys, petitions, oaths and the rest now have their own quest log entry, with places marked on the map; the texts of the whole mod speak in the voice of whoever says them.

## What the mod does

### War and diplomacy
- **[War and peace](features/war-and-peace.md)**: wars have a cause you can read, last weeks instead of days, and end when both sides agree.
- **[Events between kingdoms](features/events-between-kingdoms.md)**: raids, insults and gifts between neighbours build tension that can lead to war, or prevent it.
- **[Winning the council over](features/council.md)**: talk lords into backing your war or your peace, or your house when a fief is shared out (now with four candidates, and the conqueror always among them); council votes bring modest, fair goodwill.
- **[Vassal realms and annexation](features/vassal-realms.md)**: a beaten kingdom can bend the knee instead of being wiped out. A vassal realm keeps its ruler, pays tribute and marches in its overlord's wars, and may in time be joined to the overlord's realm for good, or rebel. Your own realm can take vassals, or become one. Everything shows in the Vassals tab of the kingdom screen.
- **[Command your armies](features/command-your-armies.md)**: the Marshal of your court leads the realm's armies. As ruler, give the war its aims through your Marshal or order one army to besiege, stand guard or come home; as a vassal, become Marshal and carry your liege's orders to the lords. Riders take your word anywhere. Optional, off by default: smarter AI lords who see as you do, lift lost sieges, take refuge and hunt armies they can catch.

### Life at court
- **[Feasts and the grand tournament](features/feasts.md)**: lords gather in peacetime; you can attend or hold your own, and a ruler can add a grand tournament.
- **[Hunts](features/hunts.md)**: in peacetime lords invite friendly houses to hunt in the woods of their fiefs; ride along or host your own, win friends, have a quiet word about a plot, and once in a long while someone does not come back.
- **[Events between clans](features/clan-events.md)**: the clans of a kingdom fall out and make up.
- **[The crown and its claim](features/succession.md)**: every kingdom follows its own law of succession, drawn from history: the Senate, the army, the dynasty, the eldest of the house, the eldest son, the strongest chief, the sheikhs, the founder's blood or the Thing. Every lord has a claim to the crown, and a ruler with a weak one faces plots, grudging oaths and hungry neighbours.
- **[The faiths of Calradia](features/faiths.md)**: seven faiths drawn from history, a priest in every town, a great festival for each faith once a year where lords of every realm meet, the Aserai sacred months, pilgrimages, blessings, and the choice to change your faith.
- **[Civil war](features/civil-war.md)**: large kingdoms crack, powerful clans plot for the throne, and you can join, oppose or lead the plot from the Plots tab of the kingdom screen.
- **[Factions and grievances](features/factions.md)**: lords remember every wrong the crown does them and band together behind a cause: a charter to bind the ruler's hands, the fall of an overmighty house, or a ruler asked to step down. They try the council first; friends tell you what is afoot.
- **[Envoys](features/envoys.md)**: send a family member or companion to a lord with a message.
- **[Your rival](features/rival.md)**: beat a lord in a tournament final, or earn their hatred, and they may swear a blood feud against you: taunts, hired blades on your trail, ambushes in town streets, and at the end a duel to the death.
- **[Duels](features/duels.md)**: your rival challenges you to a duel in the arena, and AI lords who hate each other fight their own. The loser dies, unless it is you: you are only badly hurt.
- **[Lords who remember you](features/memories.md)**: lords bring up what you did, good and bad; small things fade in days, a dead kinsman never.
- **[Captives](features/captives.md)**: the house of a lord you hold may bargain, hunt you or send knives; and your noble prisoners talk, bring news of the world, offer ransom or their word, remember how they were kept, and may swear vengeance.

### Your realm and household
- **[The royal court](features/royal-court.md)**: from the Court tab of the kingdom screen, name a Marshal, Steward, Master of Works, Chancellor and Warden of the King's Peace; choose **Hold court** in one of your own towns or castles to hear the petitioners, then the notables with tasks for you; win the oath of every house when a new ruler takes the throne; and think twice before taking a fief from a vassal, or quietly win the votes first.
- **[Affairs of your kingdom](features/realm-affairs.md)**: in peacetime a messenger stops you on the road with news of sellswords, a revolt, a famine, a feud, a great work or, for a vassal, hired killers sent against the crown. Deal with it yourself, send a companion or one of your kin with men you pick, or, if you rule, hand it to one of your vassals' houses. Whoever you send shows in your quest log until they are back.
- **[Companion errands](features/companion-errands.md)**: companions ask for leave and a few men you pick to settle matters of their own.
- **[Alayen](features/alayen.md)**: help a Sturgian village generously and a young smith's son asks to ride with you; his past, and his quest *The Grey Wolf*, come out by the campfire.
- **[Squire service](features/squire.md)**: serve a lord as their squire: errands by messenger, their armies, their battles, talks by their fire, bouts in their yard, lessons in their army, and in the end their household knight's arms, the accolade from their hand, a life in their service, or a rival if you walk out.
- **[The Blackhand's knives](features/lords-enemies.md)**: hired killers sent against your lord or your ruler, and five ways to stop them: in a hall, a street, an arena, a village or a tavern.
- **[Mercenary contracts and the Unsworn](features/mercenary-contracts.md)**: while your clan serves a realm as mercenaries, its ruler sends you tasks (raids, escorts, guarding a village, rescues, a captive lord, riding in its armies). Sign contracts of 30, 60 or 100 days, make a name for your company, take offers from other rulers or the enemy's purse, and earn the crown's gifts. If the ruler is captured, their house asks you to bring them home. Taverns now also hire out the Unsworn, a new tree of sellswords.
- **[The King's Writ](features/kings-writ.md)**: without a realm of your own, carry a ruler's writ and hunt outlaws for the price on their heads, in their lands only: wanted chiefs, trials, manhunters, ranks, and a castle at the end.
- **[Companies for hire](features/free-companies.md)**: hire one of four sellsword companies, hundreds of men at once, for 30, 40 or 50 days; they march to you on the map, cost a fortune and leave when the term is up.

### Campaign
- **[Fast mode families](features/fast-mode.md)**: in fast mode, noble houses no longer die out.
- **[Other changes](features/other-changes.md)**: longer sieges, less peacetime training for AI troops, fairer gear prices.
