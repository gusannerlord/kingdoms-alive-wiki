# Kingdoms Alive

**Kingdoms Alive turns your Bannerlord campaign into a living medieval realm, in the spirit of Crusader Kings:** a court to fill, oaths to demand, houses that scheme and feud, and kingdoms that remember what you did.

A campaign mod for Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord. Kingdoms go to war for a reason, wars last long enough to matter, peace is agreed by both sides, and peace is no longer empty: lords feast, hunt and duel, clans quarrel and make up, great realms split in civil war, and your own household gives you something to do.

Every feature can be switched off on its own (see [Settings](settings.md)).

!!! warning "Start a new campaign with it"
    Play the mod from the start of a campaign and keep it until the end. Removing it from a campaign in progress can break that save. See [Getting started](getting-started.md#new-or-existing-campaign).

## What is new (very soon in 2.0.0)
- **[The Empire's own ways](features/empire.md)**: the three empires keep no reckonings, feuds or vassal annexations; houses petition the emperor instead, the throne alone grants land as pronoia, a strategos may seize the throne in a palace coup or with the army at the gates, and a civil war between emperors can end with one Empire again.
- **[Landed knights](features/landed-knights.md)**, **[works in a village](features/village-works.md)** and **[dukes, counts and castellans](features/titles.md)**: knights holding the land of one castle (raise one yourself from the House tab every 20 days), small works in your villages, and one lord for every house, with dues, duchies, feuds for a title and felony.
- **[Factions and grievances](features/factions.md#quarrels-in-a-time-of-war)**: plots, charters, reckonings and feuds no longer wait for peace, but a weak realm at war is far less prone to them. The crown may call both sides of a feud to arms or decree the king's peace, look the other way at those who refuse, or summon the houses to its host for a war abroad.
- **[War and peace](features/war-and-peace.md)**: set terms of peace on their own screen and watch each council vote; hatred between rulers weighs less than friendship; tribute weighs against a war instead of forbidding it.
- **[Civil war](features/civil-war.md)**: plots for independence, a peace that mends the realm, and your own lord calling you to ride with him.
- **[Sparring with nobles](features/sparring.md)**: practice bouts with the lords of your realm in a castle yard or the arena, best of three falls, for skill, friendship and now and then a wager.
- **[Lords who remember you](features/memories.md)**: lords remember each other too.

## What the mod does

### War and diplomacy
- **[War and peace](features/war-and-peace.md)**: wars have a cause you can read, last weeks instead of days, and end when both sides agree; set terms of peace (tribute, towns and castles, fealty) on their own screen and watch each council vote, house by house.
- **[Events between kingdoms](features/events-between-kingdoms.md)**: raids, insults and gifts between neighbours build tension that can lead to war, or prevent it.
- **[Winning the council over](features/council.md)**: talk lords into backing your war or your peace, or your house when a fief is shared out (now with five candidates, the conqueror and the ruler always among them); council votes bring modest, fair goodwill.
- **[Vassal realms and annexation](features/vassal-realms.md)**: a beaten kingdom can bend the knee instead of being wiped out. A vassal realm keeps its ruler, pays tribute and marches in its overlord's wars, and may in time be joined to the overlord's realm for good, or rebel. Your own realm can take vassals, or become one. Everything shows in the Vassals tab of the kingdom screen.
- **[Command your armies](features/command-your-armies.md)**: the Marshal of your court leads the realm's armies. As ruler, give the war its aims through your Marshal or order one army to besiege, stand guard or come home; as a vassal, become Marshal and carry your liege's orders to the lords. Riders take your word anywhere. AI realms that are losing turn to the defence. Optional, off by default: smarter AI lords who see as you do, lift lost sieges, take refuge and hunt armies they can catch.

### Life at court
- **[Feasts and the grand tournament](features/feasts.md)**: lords gather in peacetime; you can attend or hold your own, and a ruler can add a grand tournament.
- **[Hunts](features/hunts.md)**: in peacetime lords invite friendly houses to hunt in the woods of their fiefs; ride along or host your own, win friends, have a quiet word about a plot, and once in a long while someone does not come back.
- **[Events between clans](features/clan-events.md)**: the clans of a kingdom fall out and make up.
- **[The crown and its claim](features/succession.md)**: every kingdom follows its own law of succession, drawn from history: the Senate, the army, the dynasty, the eldest of the house, the eldest son, the strongest chief, the sheikhs, the founder's blood or the Thing. Every lord has a claim to the crown, and a ruler with a weak one faces plots, grudging oaths and hungry neighbours.
- **[The faiths of Calradia](features/faiths.md)**: seven faiths drawn from history, a priest in every town, a great festival for each faith once a year (every second year in fast mode) where lords of every realm meet, the Aserai sacred months, blessings, and the choice to change your faith.
- **[The faith and ways of a fief](features/faith-and-ways.md)**: a conquered town or castle slowly takes up the faith and customs of its new lords; leave it its temple and priest, or push and risk brawls, old preachers and a rising.
- **[Civil war](features/civil-war.md)**: any kingdom can crack, and the larger it grows the more ambitious and rancorous its lords; clans plot for the throne, and you can join, oppose or lead the plot from the Factions tab of the kingdom screen. A realm can hold several plots at once, each behind its own pretender, a house with a claim to the throne by blood, marriage, an old crown or the law of the realm, and the first to rise ends the rest. Houses on bad terms with the crown may instead plot to break away with their lands.
- **[Factions and grievances](features/factions.md)**: lords remember every wrong the crown does them and band together behind a cause: a charter to bind the ruler's hands, a fief taken from a house by someone with a better right, or a ruler asked to step down. They try the council first, and when the law fails them, houses take up arms in private feuds over a fief. Houses also bind themselves to each other by marriage or a sworn pact and answer each other's call to arms; friends tell you what is afoot, and any lord will tell you how things stand in the realm or in his house.
- **[Your house at a glance](features/house-tab.md)**: a **House** tab in the clan screen with your faith and standing, your claims, the crown, the mark of treason, your court offices and the grievances held against you, with an explanation on every line.
- **[Traitors and the houses of a realm](features/realm-houses.md)**: the leader of a failed rising pays with his life and the rebel houses are at the crown's mercy for a while; AI kingdoms raise new houses when they have more land than lords, rich houses take knights into their service, and a kingdom with more lords than land lets some go.
- **[Landed knights](features/landed-knights.md)** (very soon in 2.0.0): houses of a single knight, holding the land of one castle and sworn to its lord (one knight to a castle); they follow their lord, have no vote and no castle, and rise to full houses when the kingdom has land to spare. You can become one, or make one of a companion.
- **[Works in a village](features/village-works.md)** (very soon in 2.0.0): whoever holds a village, you or the AI, may raise a well, a palisade, a mill and a storehouse there, each helping it a little; raids undo them.
- **[Dukes, counts and castellans](features/titles.md)** (very soon in 2.0.0): in five kingdoms each town is a county and every two neighbouring towns a duchy; every house has one lord, by its chief seat (dukes the crown), pays him dues and must choose a side when he calls it to a feud, and a vassal who rides against his lord risks his head for felony. Dukes and counts claim the land of their titles, a duke keeps his title without its land, and another house takes it only in a feud for the title. The empires have their themes under a strategos the emperor names, and the Khuzaits their ulus, handed down in a house.
- **[The Empire's own ways](features/empire.md)** (very soon in 2.0.0): petitions to the emperor, pronoia granted by the throne, strategos coups and the dream of one Empire.
- **[Sparring with nobles](features/sparring.md)** (very soon in 2.0.0): practice bouts with the nobles of your realm, best of three falls.
- **[Rights to fiefs](features/fief-rights.md)**: every house holds its fiefs by right and remembers the ones it lost; a few AI houses carry old claims of their line, marriages bring claims, and a companion left at another house's court can forge one for you, even to a duke's title. Press a right by the sword in person, by an envoy or from the kingdom screen.
- **[Envoys](features/envoys.md)**: send a family member or companion to a lord with a message.
- **[Your rival](features/rival.md)**: beat a lord in a tournament final, or earn their hatred, and they may swear a blood feud against you: taunts, hired blades on your trail, ambushes in town streets, and at the end a duel to the death.
- **[Duels](features/duels.md)**: your rival challenges you to a duel in the arena, and AI lords who hate each other fight their own. The loser dies, unless it is you: you are only badly hurt.
- **[Lords who remember you](features/memories.md)**: lords bring up what you did, good and bad; small things fade in days, a dead kinsman never. They remember each other too, and it warms or sours their houses.
- **[Captives](features/captives.md)**: the house of a lord you hold may bargain, hunt you or send knives; and your noble prisoners talk, bring news of the world, offer ransom or their word, remember how they were kept, and may swear vengeance.

### Your realm and household
- **[The royal court](features/royal-court.md)**: from the Court tab of the kingdom screen, name a Marshal, Steward, Master of Works, Chancellor and Warden of the King's Peace; choose **Hold court** in one of your own towns or castles to hear the petitioners, then the notables with tasks for you; win the oath of every house when a new ruler takes the throne; and think twice before taking a fief from a vassal, or quietly win the votes first.
- **[Affairs of your kingdom](features/realm-affairs.md)**: in peacetime a messenger stops you on the road with news of sellswords, a revolt, a famine, a feud, a great work or, for a vassal, hired killers sent against the crown. Deal with it yourself, send a companion or one of your kin with men you pick, or, if you rule, hand it to one of your vassals' houses. Whoever you send shows in your quest log until they are back.
- **[Companion errands](features/companion-errands.md)**: companions ask for leave and a few men you pick to settle matters of their own.
- **[Alayen](features/alayen.md)**: help a Sturgian village generously and a young smith's son asks to ride with you; his past, and his quest *The Grey Wolf*, come out by the campfire.
- **[Squire service](features/squire.md)**: serve a lord as their squire: errands by messenger, their armies, their battles, talks by their fire, bouts in their yard, lessons in their army, and in the end their household knight's arms, the accolade from their hand, a life in their service, or a rival if you walk out.
- **[The Blackhand's knives](features/lords-enemies.md)**: hired killers sent against your lord or your ruler, and five ways to stop them: in a hall, a street, an arena, a village or a tavern.
- **[Mercenary contracts and the Unsworn](features/mercenary-contracts.md)**: while your clan serves a realm as mercenaries, its ruler sends you tasks (raids, escorts, guarding a village, rescues, a captive lord, riding in its armies). Sign contracts of 30, 60 or 100 days, make a name for your company, take offers from other rulers or the enemy's purse, and earn the crown's gifts. If the ruler is captured, their house asks you to bring them home. Taverns now also hire out the Unsworn, a new tree of sellswords.
- **[The King's Writ](features/kings-writ.md)**: without a realm of your own, carry a ruler's writ and hunt outlaws for the price on their heads, in their lands only: wanted chiefs, trials, manhunters, ranks, and a castle at the end.
- **[Companies for hire](features/free-companies.md)**: hire one of nine sellsword companies, hundreds of men at once, for 30, 40 or 50 days; they march to you on the map, cost a fortune and leave when the term is up.

### Campaign
- **[Fast mode families](features/fast-mode.md)**: in fast mode, noble houses no longer die out.
- **[Other changes](features/other-changes.md)**: longer sieges, less peacetime training for AI troops, fairer gear prices.
